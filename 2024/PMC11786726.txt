Introduction
The evaluation of chick quality after hatchling is a crucial step in ensuring the success of poultry production. Improvements in chick quality lead to more efficient and profitable flock production (
Narinç and Aydemir, 2021
). During chick embryonic development, several crucial metabolic changes occur (
Givisiez et al., 2020
), and situations that may compromise embryo metabolism will result in a decrease in the quality of the hatched chicks (
Uddin and Hamidu, 2014
).
Early assessment of chick quality is important for both the hatchery and the farm. For the hatchery, chick quality is a valuable parameter for evaluating the efficiency of the incubation process, as abnormalities in chicks result in financial losses due to a reduction in the number of sellable chicks (
Ogunmoyo and Odunsi, 2018
). On the farm, the presence of low-quality chicks is associated with poor performance. Low-quality chicks are more prone to infections, consequently leading to losses in animal welfare, worsening feed conversion, and reducing bird uniformity (
Van De Ven et al., 2012
).
Defining a method to assess chick quality is a challenging task, given the subjectivity in judgements among evaluators (
Willemsen et al., 2008
;
Yeboah et al., 2019
). Current methods for assessing quality rely on visual inspection or weight parameters, and although more standardized methods, such as those of Tona or Pasgar (
Molenaar and Reijrink, 2011
), are utilized, they still involve a certain degree of subjectivity. In addition to evaluating chicks at the hatchling stage, another approach involves promoting actions that aim to improve embryonic development, resulting in high-quality chicks. In this regard, in addition to recommended management practices and improvements in incubation equipment,
in ovo
nutrition practices can be beneficial. For both approaches, assessing the quality of day-old chicks and endeavours to enhance chick quality during embryonic life, the use of biomarkers is a relatively unexplored yet potentially valuable tool.
Several variables can influence hatchery outcomes, including breeder age (
Nasri et al., 2020
;
Yamak et al., 2023
). In this context, an approach to better understand biomarkers that vary according to breeder age is valuable for researching methods that mitigate the effects of age.
Omics technologies (genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics) aim to comprehensively assess biomolecules in tissues, cells, fluids, or organisms. Specifically, metabolomics has emerged as a powerful tool for understanding complex biological systems, enabling the detection and analysis of thousands of metabolites in a single sample (
Manjula and Gowda, 2010
). In the broader field of poultry research, these "omics" technologies play promising roles in revealing new insights into pathology and physiology, as well as assisting in the identification of crucial biomarkers for bird performance (
Goossens et al., 2022
).
Given the significance of continuous improvements in hatchery processes and defining the quality of hatched chicks, the limitations of visual techniques, the need to identify quality biomarkers, and the versatility of metabolomics, this study sought to identify potential biomarkers associated with laying hen age and the quality of the meconium of laying hen chicks.
Materials and methods
Animals and samples
This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee, protocol SEI n° 23117.010799/2022-54. Sample collection took place at the "Novo Mundo" commercial hatchery located in Uberlândia, Brazil. For analysis, chicks from the "Lohmann" lineage were assessed during the regular routine of the hatchery. In summary, breeder eggs were disinfected and stored at a controlled temperature of 18°C for 4-7 days prior to incubation. The eggs were then incubated in "CASP®" incubation machines and transferred to hatchery machines of the same brand, with an average total of 504 h of incubation. After the end of the hatching window, which averaged 26 h for the tested flocks, the chicks were sexed, and only females were included in this study. Following sexing, the birds were transferred to the sorting room for quality assessment. Therefore, the chicks were not subjected to subsequent management steps in the hatchery routine, including subcutaneous vaccination and infrared beak treatment.
For the study design, three categories of breeder hen age were adopted: young breeder (26-32 weeks), middle-aged breeder (41-48 weeks), and old breeder (76-82 weeks). For each age group, chicks from three (3) flocks with different ages within each range were sampled, totalling nine (9) flocks. The sampled ages within each age category in this study were chosen based on the day of birth at the hatchery. Chicks from the young category originated from breeder flocks aged 26, 30, and 32 weeks; middle-aged flocks had breeder ages of 41, 42, and 48 weeks; and old flocks had breeder ages of 76, 80, and 82 weeks.
For each flock, individual meconium samples were collected from 22 chicks; 11 of them were classified as good quality, and the other 11 were classified as poor quality. This classification was performed by two independent and trained evaluators via visual inspection. To be classified as poor quality, a chick had to exhibit at least one of the following 10 characteristics: drowsy behaviour, crusted feathers, joint injury, very white feathers, a pasted abdomen and/or cloaca, presence of the umbilical cord, sticky eyes, dehydrated legs, sparse feather covering, and greenish feathers. Chicks showing no or slight alterations in their eyes, feathers, beak, neck, or locomotor system and those with normal navels, well-hydrated or mildly dehydrated legs, and firm and nonpasted abdomens were considered to be of good quality.
The chicks underwent manual collection of meconium, involving gentle pressure on the abdominal region of the chick to facilitate the release of its entire contents. Meconium was stored in cryotubes and frozen in liquid nitrogen during transportation for subsequent storage in a freezer at −80°C.
Laboratory analyses
Samples were analysed at the Poultry Incubation Laboratory and the Nanobiotechnology Laboratory Dr. Luiz Ricardo Goulart Filho, both at the Federal University of Uberlândia. Only samples where the presence of uric acid could be visually observed were used in the laboratory to standardize the analysis. Thus, out of the 198 collected samples (22 × 9), 16 chicks per age group were utilized, with 8 chicks per category, except for the 26-week age group, where only five (5) samples of poor-quality chicks were used due to content loss during transport through the opening of the Eppendorf tubes. For the 26-week age group, 10 samples met the quality standard. In total, only 143 studies were analysed, comprising 74 of good quality and 69 of poor quality.
Upon arrival at the laboratory, each meconium sample was macerated and homogenized to obtain a uniform specimen. Subsequently, the meconium was weighed, and a 1:10 dilution with spectroscopic-grade methanol was performed, followed by vortex homogenization and centrifugation for 15 min at 13,000 × g. The supernatant was transferred to another microtube and subjected to vacuum concentration (miVac duoconcentration, Genevac™) for approximately 2 h until drying was complete. The material was reconstituted in 400 µL of methanol (J.T.Baker®) and filtered through a 0.22-micrometer filter (Merck®).
Metabolite analyses were conducted using high-performance liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry (HPLC/MS) on an Agilent Infinity 1260 HPLC system coupled with an Agilent® 6520 B high-resolution quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer (Q-TOF) with an electrospray ionization source. Separation was achieved using an Agilent Poroshell 120 column (2.1 × 50 mm, 2.7 μm). The mobile phase consisted of water acidified with formic acid (0.1 % v/v) (A) and methanol (B), with a gradient of 10 % B (0 min), 98 % B (0-10 min), and 98 % B (10-17 min). The ionization parameters were set as follows: nebulizer pressure of 20 psi, drying gas at 8 L/min and 220°C, and capillary voltage of 4.5 kV. The injection volume was 4 µL.
MassHunter Qualitative software version 10.0 was used to process the raw data. A Molecular Feature Extractor (MFE) tool was utilized to extract mass spectra and convert them to the CEF extension. The Agilent Mass Profiler Professional (MPP) software version B.13.1.1 was then used to filter and analyse the extracted molecular compounds. The applied filters included a minimum absolute abundance of 5,000 counts, with all charges allowed. The analysis parameters consisted of a retention time tolerance of 0.15 min and a mass window of 15 ppm + 2 mDa.
Statistical analyses
The molecular compounds considered were those present in 75-100 % of at least one group. Statistical analyses of the data generated in the metabolomic analysis were performed with log
2
-transformed values. Mass Hunter Qualitative software version 10.0 was used for the unpaired t-test, with significance set at
p
< 0.05 and a fold change (FC) equal to or greater than 2.00. An overall analysis of metabolites with significant differences and FC >2 was conducted to assess the validity of multivariate analysis using the sphericity test and KMO in the R program. Once the validity of the multivariate test was confirmed (
p
< 0.05), heatmaps were constructed for better visualization, and principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) were performed.
PLS-DA utilizes the multivariate regression technique of partial least squares (PLS) with cross-validation, allowing better visualization of components and predicting the ability of different categories to be correctly classified based on excreted metabolites. Metabolites were identified using the METLIN 2019 database (included in the MPP software). Afterwards, a simple regression test was conducted to identify the metabolites that individually interfered the most with chick categorization based on the area under the curve (AUC) value using GraphPad Prism (10.0) software. For sensitivity and specificity testing for age category, each age group was compared with the others collectively.
Results
Breeder age influences the expression of metabolites in chick meconium
A total of 1531 metabolites were identified in meconium, and the sphericity test validated the application of multivariate analysis for their discrimination. For the evaluation of the age of the parent stock, out of the 1531 identified metabolites, a 100 % frequency filter was applied, revealing 21 metabolites that were differentially expressed in chicks classified as good quality and 15 in chicks classified as poor quality. For the good-quality chicks, a total of 16 metabolites had FC > 2 between chicks that originated from new, middle-aged, or old breeder hens. Discussion
The metabolites PI, TO, D3T, and MG16 exhibit different excretion profiles in chick meconium depending on the age of the parent stock
The age of the breeder influenced the expression of the metabolites PI, TO, D3T, and MG16 in both chicks classified as poor quality or good quality (
Fig. 1
A and
1
C). A previous study demonstrated the buffering effect of TO on chicken embryonic metabolism (
Sisken et al., 1985
). Our study is the first to report this compound in chick meconium. One possible explanation for the lower quantity of TO in the embryos of older birds may be related to the eggshell. As the embryo advances during embryonic development, the CO
2
concentration in the blood increases, causing the subsequent accumulation of bicarbonate for acid‒base balance (
Decuypere and Bruggeman, 2007
). Shells from younger breeders are less porous (
Araújo et al., 2017
) and may hinder gas exchange between the chorioallantoic membrane and the external environment (
Christensen et al., 2005
), requiring greater internal control with buffering substances such as TO.
Meconium samples from chicks originating from older breeders expressed more monoglyceride MG16 in both poor- and good-quality categories (
Fig. 1
A and
1
C). Glycerides are important energy sources for the embryo because yolk glucose levels are low (
Pulikanti et al., 2010
;
Oliveira et al., 2008
), and the monoglyceride metabolic pathway is crucial for triglyceride synthesis in the chicken embryo (
Speake et al., 1998
). The higher expression of M16 in chicks from older breeders suggests that these embryos consumed more energy than did those from younger breeders. This hypothesis is supported by studies showing that embryos from older breeders (
Nangsuay et al., 2013
;
Wagt et al., 2020
) and heavier eggs (
Wagt et al., 2020
) have greater amounts of available energy due to their larger yolk size, which contributes to increased energy consumption by the embryo.
Good- and poor-quality chicks originating from younger breeders excreted less D3T in their meconium than did those from middle-aged and older breeders (
Fig. 1
A and
1
C). This metabolite is a product of vitamin D metabolism (
Kim et al., 2022
), which is crucial for embryonic osteogenesis, adipogenesis, myogenesis and antioxidant activity (
Gu et al., 2017
). There is a direct relationship between vitamin D consumption, hen age and the deposition of the nutrient in eggs (
Saunders-Blades and Korver, 2015
). As older breeders are larger and consume more feed (and consequently more vitamin D), they may deposit a greater amount of the vitamin in the eggs; thus, chicks from older breeders end up excreting greater amounts of its metabolites.
PI excretion followed a consistent pattern across age groups; it was lower in chicks from older breeders, regardless of their quality. However, it was more abundant in good-quality chicks from younger breeders or in poor-quality chicks from middle-aged breeders (
Fig. 1
A and
1
C). This metabolite is classified as a glycerophospholipid, a compound resulting from glycerol esterification, and acts as an essential component of cell membranes (
Valentine et al., 2020
;
Wang et al., 2022b
). We do not have scientific literature available to understand the variation in this metabolite in meconium according to the age of the breeder.
The results emphasize the importance of the repeated metabolites TO, D3T, MG16, and PI in both chicks classified as good quality and those classified as poor quality, showing that these compounds are mother age-dependent excreted metabolites. Among them, D3T appears to be the most strongly associated with breeder age, as it exhibited a high AUC (96.39 %) for good-quality chicks (
Table 3
).
Good-quality chicks from young breeders can be discriminated from other age groups by their metabolic profile in meconium with high sensitivity and specificity, highlighting the metabolites GPL, SB, CR, D3T, and TRH
Interestingly, some metabolites differed only between age groups in good-quality chicks. There was greater excretion of PS, SB, OAG, GPL, PI NI
1
and NI
2
in chicks originating from young breeder hens. Similar to PI, PS is a glycerophospholipid and was more highly expressed in good-quality chicks from young breeders. There is limited information about PS in the literature, but it is found in plants (
Vuko et al., 2022
) and in the plasma of rats fed chicken protein (
Onuh et al., 2016
).
The presence of tripeptides, such as GPL, in the meconium of the embryo may be associated with a low maturation of intestinal epithelial cells, as the absorption of these compounds is dependent on a transporter protein (Pept1, SLC15A1) located in the apical membranes of enterocytes (
Tokutake et al., 2021
). Although we do not have a basis to better understand our results, GPL is a metabolite that discriminates chicks according to age with a high AUC (93.75 %,
Table 3
) and should be the subject of further studies.
SB and OAG are glycoside compounds. Glycosides constitute a variety of secondary metabolites of plants with diverse structures that are useful in pharmacology due to their proven bioactivities (
Bartnik and Facey, 2024
). However, we have no basis to understand our results; in particular, SB had a high AUC (93.91 %,
Table 3
) in good-quality chicks from young breeders, reinforcing the importance of further studies of this metabolite.
The expression levels of the metabolites TRH, NN, MG18, HCG, 3R and CR in the meconium of good-quality chicks from middle-aged and older breeders were lower than those in the meconium of good-quality chicks from young breeders. TRH is a hormone that activates thyroid-stimulating hormone, which, in turn, promotes the release of the hormones T3 and T4 (
Nijsten et al., 2021
). We cannot understand our results, but factors that inhibit the effects of TRH can delay embryo growth and metabolism (
Chelnokova et al., 2023
). Again, these results may be related to the weight of eggs from young breeders, which are smaller (
Wagt et al., 2020
). Supported by the fact that this metabolite showed a strong association with breeder age with a high AUC (93.23 %,
Table 3
), further studies are needed to better understand the role of this hormone.
The literature does not provide enough reports on the function of 3R in birds, but in humans, 3R has a protective effect as an antioxidant (
Xavier et al., 2021
;
Raduan et al., 2022
). Due to the lack of available information, we cannot determine why this compound appeared less often in chicks from young breeders.
Similar to MG16, MG18 is also a glyceride with lower expression in the meconium of chicks from young breeders. The interpretation of this result follows the same discussion concerning MG16, emphasizing the need for further studies.
CR is a protein of significant energy value deposited in the yolk by the hen (
Reicher et al., 2020
). Chicken embryos can synthesize this metabolite to meet their energy needs (
Walker, 1979
;
Liu et al., 2021
) starting from the 11th day of incubation, at which point it serves as an energy source (
Dayan et al., 2023
).
In ovo
inoculation of CR improves the hatchability rate of broiler chicks by providing a greater energy supply at the end of incubation (
Firman et al., 2023
). As previously discussed, it is conceivable that birds from younger breeders may exhibit lower expression of metabolites related to energy sourcing, given their lower energy utilization due to their smaller size. However, specific studies are needed to validate this observation, particularly for CR, which exhibited a high AUC (95.42 %,
Table 3
).
HCG is classified as a lipid ether (
Rangholia et al., 2021
). These compounds have important biological functions, such as membrane formation, differentiation and cellular signalling regulation, and antioxidant activity (
Dean and Lodhi, 2018
). Finally, NN was expressed at lower levels in the meconium of good-quality chicks from younger breeders. There are no recent studies on these metabolites that support our results.
The PLS-DA showed better separation between components from chicks classified as good quality from young breeders (
Fig. 1
B), which was confirmed by the high accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity values (
Table 1
). The AUCs were high (>90 %) for the metabolites TRH, CR, SB, GPL, and D3T, indicating that these metabolites are strongly associated with age and are efficient at classifying breeders as either young or not young.
Our study is the first to associate metabolites excreted in chick meconium with breeder age. Although our work raises many questions to understand why certain metabolites are more or less excreted by chicks from young, middle-aged, or old breeders, it is evident that differences exist among these age groups.
The literature robustly supports findings demonstrating differences in hatching window and hatchability among chicks from breeders of different ages (
Nasri et al., 2020
;
Rebolledo et al., 2023
;
Yamak et al., 2023
). Our study reinforces these metabolic differences. Our work provides insight for further research, as we introduce metabolites that potentially contribute to improving incubation outcomes depending on breeder age.
Another noteworthy point is the weaker association between the identified metabolites in poor-quality chicks and breeder age. This might be because our selection was based on poor-quality chicks without considering the cause of this condition. Thus, future studies should aim to separate good- and poor-quality chicks based on the specific defects that lead to the classification of poor-quality chicks. Additionally, the PLS-DA results for good-quality chicks (
Fig. 1
B) showed a clear separation for the young breeder age group. When separating breeder age categories, we used a classification commonly used for poultry farms. However, concerning the physiological classification of the breeder and the embryo, there is justification to reconsider this classification.
In summary, among the metabolites that differed with a high AUC between chicks from breeders of different ages, those in the literature are related to energy metabolism (CR), hormonal activity (TRH), vitamin D synthesis (D3T) and peptide constitution (GPL).
There is a difference in the metabolic profiles of chicks classified as good or poor quality, but this difference has low accuracy
Good-quality chicks exhibited greater excretion of the metabolites HV, PI, and NN and lower excretion of N6 and 7 M (
Fig. 3
A). HV is a dipeptide derived from the incomplete breakdown of proteins during digestion or catabolism (
Wishart et al., 2022
). There are no studies on the specific function of this metabolite in chicken embryos. However, two amino acids, hydroxyproline and valine, play roles in the metabolism of bird embryos. The injection of valine into embryonated eggs increases the weight of hatched chicks, reduces the hatching window, and enhances myoblast production (
Kita and Makino, 2014
;
Kita et al., 2015
), and hydroxyproline stimulates the growth of blood vessels and cartilage in broiler chicken embryos (
Beck et al., 2015
). Our results indicate the importance of these metabolites for embryo metabolism, as their levels were greater in good-quality chicks.
There are no reports in the literature regarding 7 M compounds in birds. N6 is classified as a purine nucleoside (
Wishart et al., 2022
) that acts as a nucleic acid constituent in all living organisms. We have no basis in the literature to help interpret our results.
Disregarding breeder age, overall, we found low accuracy (61.26 %), sensitivity (59.45 %), and specificity (67.64 %) for classifying chicks as good or poor quality. This is likely due to factors observed in this study, such as the need for a detailed stratification of the type of defects in chicks classified as poor quality.
Regarding the differences in the expression levels of metabolites in good- and poor-quality chicks originating from young breeders, PE, D3, BIS, OXO, DI, PS, NST, SB, and OAG were excreted more in good-quality chicks.
PE is a phospholipid, and as discussed earlier, this class of compounds constitutes cellular membranes and plays roles in the metabolism of bird embryos (
Valentine et al., 2020
;
Wang et al., 2022a
). The compound DI is an alkaloid (
Siddiqui et al., 1987
) that acts as an antioxidant (
Liu et al., 2023
). Our work reinforces the importance of DI, as it is strongly associated with chick quality, with 70 % < AUC > 80 % (
Table 5
).
Like D3T, D3 is also classified as a participant in vitamin D metabolism (
Saunders-Blades and Korver, 2015
;
National Center for Biotechnology Information, 2024a
). As discussed earlier, our study supports the idea that this metabolite is beneficial to bird metabolism, as it was detected at higher levels in the meconium of good-quality chicks than in that of poor-quality chicks.
We did not find enough information about OXO and NST. OXO is a metabolite of linoleic acid degradation that can increase energy metabolism (
Kim et al., 2017
). NST is a derivative of amino acid metabolism, especially tryptophan metabolism (
Da Cunha et al., 2022
). The injection of tryptophan into embryonated eggs contributes to an increase in muscle fibres in the breast muscle, has a positive effect on the immunomodulation of birds (
Nayak et al., 2018
) and stimulates the intestinal growth of chicken embryos (
Nayak et al., 2015
). Given the beneficial effects of tryptophan on embryo metabolism, the higher expression of the metabolite NST in higher-quality chicks from younger breeders may be related to these pathways being more active.
The metabolites CR, LB, and 7 M were more highly expressed in chicks of poor quality (
Fig. 3
A). CR is a protein with significant energy value that is beneficial to the embryo (
Firman et al., 2023
). Although there is a clear role for CR as an indicator of chick quality, we do not have a basis to support a good understanding of our results.
As discussed previously, the peptide LB is part of a class of metabolites for which more studies are needed to understand their roles in enterocyte maturation. The literature on 7 M, a metabolite, was not sufficient to explain our findings.
The accuracy for classifying chick quality according to the metabolic profile at a young breeder age was 74.46 %, with good sensitivity (80.74 %), indicating that good-quality chicks can be classified more accurately than poor-quality chicks, given the specificity of 66.64 %. Among the metabolites that most influenced this classification, CR and DI stand out (
Table 5
) as potential biomarkers for the quality of chicks from young breeders and should be further explored in future tests.
The metabolites HV, KA, NN, 4OX, AP, HCG, AF, HC2, PTN, and MG16 were more highly expressed in good-quality chicks than in poor-quality chicks.
Concerning HV, in chicks from middle-aged breeders, the beneficial effects of valine and hydroxyproline on chick quality can explain our results, as HV is composed of these amino acids. Considering that 70 % < AUC < 80 %, HV is a potential candidate biomarker for quality. NN was found to be more highly expressed in good-quality chicks, but information in the literature is lacking to support our discussion. However, with 70 % < AUC < 80 %, it appears that NN can be considered a potential metabolite marker for chick quality.
Although we lack a basis for understanding why AP was more highly expressed in chicks of good quality from middle-aged breeders, the occurrence of the amino acids constituting this peptide (asparagine and phenylalanine) is more frequent in the literature. Asparagine concentrations increase in the amniotic fluid of embryonated chicken eggs, which is related to an increase in embryo weight (
Zhu et al., 2022
). In turn, phenylalanine is involved in the production of hormones and neurotransmitters (
Liu et al., 2021
).
Other metabolites related to amino acid metabolism include AF, which participates in tryptophan metabolism (
Liu et al., 2023
), and PTN, which is involved in methionine metabolism (
Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes, 2024
). Methionine supplementation in broiler embryos had positive effects on feathering (
Chen et al., 2020
) and intestinal formation in broiler chicks (
Chen et al., 2021
) and laying hens (
Farias et al., 2023
). We adopt the same interpretation for AF and PTN as for NST: while there is no specific reference in avian embryos, we consider the importance of their precursors, tryptophan and methionine, in embryo metabolism. Just as NST was more highly expressed in the meconium of good-quality chicks from young breeders, AF and PTN had greater expression levels in those from middle-aged breeders, suggesting that the embryo's better utilization of amino acid metabolism pathways positively influences chick quality.
HC2 is a nitro compound (
National Center for Biotechnology Information, 2024b
) isolated from plants, fungi, bacteria, and mammals that exhibits antibacterial and cellular signalling properties (
Parry et al., 2011
). To the best of our knowledge, our study is the first report of its presence in any form in an avian organism; thus, we cannot discuss the reasons for its expression in chick meconium.
The glyceride MG16 also occurred in greater quantities in the meconium of chicks from good-quality middle-aged breeders. As this class of compound is related to energy metabolism (
Pulikanti et al., 2010
;
Oliveira et al., 2008
), it might be excreted at higher levels in good-quality chicks due to more efficient metabolism in these birds.
The accuracy for classifying chicks from middle-aged breeders in relation to quality was 70.83 %, and the AUCs were higher for NN, AP, AF, HC2, N6, PTN, and HV (70 % < AUC > 80 %). Regarding the difference in the expression levels of metabolites in good- and poor-quality chicks originating from old breeders, PI and OAM were more highly expressed in good-quality chicks. There are limited reports on OAM as a metabolite in the literature. As it is also a glycoside, we can discuss it in the same manner as OAG. It is possible that the occurrence of OAM in chick meconium occurs through maternal nutrition, as this class of metabolites is found in plants (
Bartnik and Facey, 2024
).
As previously discussed, PI may be related to energy storage in the embryo (
Liu et al., 2021
). Again, although we cannot explain the exact reason for our result, our study highlights the importance of PI in distinguishing embryos classified as good or poor quality from old breeders.
Among older breeders, the accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity for classifying chicks according to metabolites that showed differences were low. These results suggest that although there were differences in the excretion of metabolites in meconium, there must be more variables interfering with chick quality at this age. In any case, PI and OAM influenced chick quality, especially OAM, which had an AUC < 80 % (70 % < AUC < 80 %) (
Table 5
).
In addition to the relationship with the age of the hen, for the first time, we have presented metabolites related to the quality of the laying chick. Both overall and in different age categories, there were differences in the metabolites of birds classified as good or poor quality. However, when evaluating the associations between chick quality and the identified metabolites, the accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity were lower than those of the classification of good-quality chicks according to breeder age. One hypothesis for this has already been raised, which is an overall classification of poor-quality chicks without stratifying by the causes of this quality determination.
Conclusions
Age biomarkers of laying hens can be identified in the meconium of chicks with high accuracy (>90 %). The potential metabolites that can mitigate the effects of hen age on hatchability were investigated, with a focus on SB, CR, D3T, and TRH metabolites related to energy metabolism, hormonal activity, vitamin D synthesis and peptide constitution. Although there are biomarkers related to chick quality, such as DI, CR, HV, NN, AP, AF, HC2, PTN, N6 and OAM (related to energy and growth metabolism, tryptophan and methionine metabolism, and antioxidation, with some having no known functions in embryos), the accuracy was lower (70-80 %).
