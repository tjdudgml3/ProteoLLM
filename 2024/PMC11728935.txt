Introduction
The anterior pituitary gland, a crucial part of the endocrine system, generates and secretes the growth hormone (GH). GH stimulates the liver to produce insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), which affects target tissues, including fat, muscle and bone. The GH-IGF-1 axis significantly influences protein synthesis, glucose metabolism and lipid metabolism (
1
). Insufficient production or secretion of GH by the pituitary gland leads to growth hormone deficiency (GHD).
Various congenital and acquired conditions, including malignancies in the sellar area and pituitary adenoma, cause adult growth hormone deficiency (AGHD). Intracranial germ cell tumors (iGCTs) are a group of rare tumors that primarily affect children and adolescents, with 90% of cases occurring before the age of 20 (
2
,
3
). Although the majority of iGCT patients have an optimistic prognosis after radiotherapy, chemotherapy and surgical resection, there is a high incidence of metabolic disease due to the potential structural damage of the hypothalamic energy center. The presence of GHD further increases the likelihood of metabolic disorders in these patients.
The main metabolic features of AGHD include increased central adiposity, decreased lean body mass, dyslipidemia, insulin resistance and increased risk of cardiometabolic disease (
4
,
5
). Dyslipidemia is a prominent clinical issue. Elevated triglycerides (TGs) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c) are associated with an increased risk of atherosclerosis and cardiovascular events (
6
,
7
,
8
). AGHD patients also have an increased risk of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) as GH directly reduces hepatolipogenesis (
9
,
10
,
11
). GH treatment can improve dyslipidemia and NAFLD (
9
). Although it is well known that GH plays a vital role in lipid metabolism, including its impact on the expression of specific enzyme genes, many unanswered questions remain in this field. Lipids are essential signaling molecules in various pathological and physiological processes. Because of the limited resolution of detection methods, a comprehensive lipidomics evaluation for AGHD still needs to be done.
Metabolomics, an emerging discipline that has evolved after genomics and proteomics, holds immense potential in identifying disease-specific metabolic changes (
12
). Our early nontargeted metabolomics research found that AGHD patients have distinct metabolic profiles (
6
). The major disrupted pathways were the biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids, sphingolipid metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, fatty acid elongation, degradation and biosynthesis (
6
). State-of-the-art lipidomic methods show promise in providing a more profound understanding of the pathophysiological mechanisms and therapeutic targets of AGHD.
In this cross-sectional study, we further applied high-coverage targeted lipidomics methods to investigate the lipidomic patterns in AGHD patients with iGCTs. We discovered an AGHD-specific dynamic change in the serum lipidomic profile and comprehensively correlated novel lipid metabolic dysregulation in AGHD patients to explore potential biomarkers of metabolic disorders.
Participants and methods
Participants
Thirty consecutive AGHD patients were enrolled in this study at the outpatient clinic of  the Peking Union Medical College Hospital. The American Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline criteria were followed in diagnosing AGHD (
13
). All patients had multiple pituitary hormone deficiencies and IGF-1 levels below the lower limits of normal ranges. Replacement therapy with glucocorticoid, levothyroxine and testosterone undecanoate/estrogen and progesterone had been maintained for at least 1 year.
Thirty healthy volunteers matched in age, gender and body mass index (BMI) were enrolled as controls. Volunteers were recruited through advertising and gave written informed consent. Inclusion criteria as health controls included not having any of the defined criteria of metabolic syndrome (
14
), endocrine disease, chronic disease or routine medication. This study was performed following the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (no. I-23PJ1713). All participants consented to participate in the study. All data were de-identified before analysis.
Clinical evaluation
All patients received follow-up care at the outpatient clinic of the Peking Union Medical College Hospital’s outpatient clinic. All anthropometric measurements, including height, weight and blood pressure, were obtained after overnight fasting while wearing light clothing.
Biochemical and hormonal assays
Blood samples were collected in the early morning after overnight fasting. Liver and kidney function, blood lipids, blood glucose and other indicators were tested on the day of the blood draw. Blood samples for lipidomic testing were collected in serum separator tubes, centrifuged at 1,500
g
for 10 min at room temperature and stored at −80°C at the Clinical Biobank, Peking Union Medical College Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences. The lipidomics test was completed within 6 months.
The following tests were performed using standard procedures in the Clinical Laboratory Department of Peking Union Medical College Hospital: complete blood count, liver function, kidney function, lipid profile (total cholesterol (TC), LDL-c and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), TGs, apolipoprotein A1 (ApoA1) and apolipoprotein B (ApoB)), fasting blood glucose (FBG), fasting insulin (FINS), pituitary hormone, beta-human chorionic gonadotropin (β-HCG) and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels. Insulin sensitivity was calculated using the insulin resistance index (HOMA-IR  =  FBG  ×  FINS/22.5) and the TyG index (TyG = Ln(TG × FBG/2)). NAFLD was diagnosed on established ultrasound criteria for fatty liver in the absence of any secondary cause for fatty liver (
15
). An entirely automated, two-site, solid-phase, chemiluminescent enzyme immunometric assay (Immulite 2000, Siemens Healthcare Diagnostics, Germany) was used to assess the serum IGF-1 level. The IGF-1 SD score (SDS) was determined based on data from healthy Chinese people (
16
).
High-coverage targeted serum lipidomics and data analysis
Lipids were extracted from 50 μL serum using a modified version of the Bligh and Dyer method as described previously (
17
). Briefly, 750 μL chloroform:methanol (1:2) (v/v) were added to serum samples and incubated at 200
g
for 30 min at 4°C. A total of 350 μL deionized water and 250 μL chloroform were then added and centrifuged to extract the lower organic phase containing lipids. Lipid extraction was repeated once by adding 450 μL chloroform to the remaining aqueous phase, and the lipid extracts were pooled into a single tube and dried in a SpeedVac under OH mode.
Lipidomic analyses were conducted at LipidALL Technologies (China), using Jasper high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) coupled with Sciex Triple Quad 4500 MD as reported previously (
18
). The separation of individual lipid classes by normal phase (NP)-HPLC was carried out using a TUP-HB silica column (i.d. 150 × 2.1 mm, 3 μm) under the following conditions: mobile phase A (chloroform:methanol:ammonium hydroxide, 89.5:10:0.5) and mobile phase B (chloroform:methanol:ammonium hydroxide:water, 55:39:0.5:5.5). Multiple reaction monitoring transitions were set up for comparative analysis of various lipids. Individual lipid species were quantified by referencing to spiked internal standards. d9-PC32:0(16:0/16:0), PE 34:0, dic8-PI, d31-PS, C17:0-PA, DMPG, CL-14:0, C14-BMP, C12-SL, C17-LPC, C17-LPE, C17:1-LPI, C17:0-LPA, C17:1-LPS, C17-Cer, C12-SM, d17:1-S1P, d17:1-Sph, C8-GluCer, C8-LacCer, Gb3-d18:1/17:0, d3-16:0 carnitine and diacylglycerol (DAG) (18:1/18:1)-d5 were obtained from Avanti Polar Lipids. GM3-d18:1/18:0-d3 was purchased from Matreya LLC (USA). Free fatty acids (FFAs) were quantitated using d31-16:0 (Sigma-Aldrich). d6-CE 18:0 and TAG (16:0)3-d5 were obtained from CDN isotopes.
Statistical analysis
The metabolite data were analyzed using unsupervised principal component analysis and supervised orthogonal partial least square discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) to create a statistical classificatory model. The corresponding variable importance in projection (VIP) values were also calculated in the OPLS-DA model. Variables with a VIP value ≥1.0, fold change  > 1.2 (or  < 0.83) and
P
value (analysis of variance) adjusted for a false discovery rate threshold of 5% (
q
value) in the OPLS-DA model were selected as potential biomarkers.
Continuous variables are presented with median and interquartile ranges (Q1, Q3) according to data distribution. Categorical variables were described with percentages. The differences between groups were compared by the
t
-test or the Wilcoxon rank sum (Mann–Whitney) test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. Linear regression was conducted to estimate the mean difference. A forest plot of significant variables was constructed, and a box plot of a single significant variable was generated. The correlation analysis for each altered lipid and clinical metabolic parameters was implemented and plotted with the R package corrplot (
http://cran.r-project.org/package=corrplot
). Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS for Windows (version 27.0), and Prism 9 software was used for mapping. The difference was considered statistically significant when
P
was less than 0.05.

presents the baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of 30 patients with iGCT. The median age of patients was 23.5 years (Q1, Q3: 22.0, 28.0) with an average BMI of 24.3 kg/m
2

Of the patients, 67.9% received pathological diagnosis, while the rest were clinically diagnosed; 73.3% of patients received radiotherapy and chemotherapy, 23.3% received radiotherapy, and 3.3% received chemotherapy. Correlation between differential lipids and clinical profiles
We then assessed the association of altered serum lipids with clinical parameters in AGHD patients with iGCT (
Fig. 3A
). The results showed that TAGs were significantly positively correlated with BMI, glucose metabolism index (FINS, HOMA-IR and TyG index), adverse lipid profile (TC, TG, LDL-c and non-HDL-c) and liver enzymes (ALT, AST and GGT). CE22:0, CE20:2, PC40:1 and PC40:2 displayed significant positive correlations with TG, TyG index and FINS. PE-FA (PE34:1 (18:1_16:0)) was also positively associated with glycolipid metabolic indexes and liver enzymes, while polyunsaturated phosphatidylethanolamines (PUFA-PE) (PE38:7 (16:1_22:6)) were significantly and negatively associated with these clinical indicators. This indicates that the increase in serum TAGs and the decrease in PUFA-PE are related to the abnormal occurrence of glycolipid metabolism. Notably, PE-O, PC-O and LPE-O, but not PEs, were negatively correlated with FINS levels, insulin resistance and TG. In addition, we also observed strong negative correlations with TAGs, FT4 and IGF-1 SDS.

Based on the lipid alterations observed in the AGHD group and the fact that many clinical parameters are associated with specific lipid subclasses, a schematic of the metabolic pathways of the lipids involved was generated to show how the lipidome is affected by AGHD-associated chronic metabolic disorders (
Fig. 3B
). Many lipid subclasses including the significant components of adipose tissue TAGs, DAGs and critical molecules in phospholipid metabolic pathways, such as PE, PC, PG and PI, were predictably elevated. Interestingly, ether-linked PCs, PEs and LysoPEs (PC-O, PE-O and LysoPE-O) exhibited significant differences within their subclasses compared with general molecules.
Our study further provided insights into the lipid profiles of AGHD patients with or without NAFLD. These insights, as depicted in
Fig. 3C and D
, have significant implications for managing of metabolic diseases in these individuals. Our results revealed substantial increases in total DAGs, three specific DAGs and two TAG lipid molecules among AGHD patients with NAFLD, reflecting the elevated TG levels commonly seen in clinical settings. Conversely, PE species PE38:7 (16:1_22:6) and one PE-O, shown negatively correlated with metabolic dysfunction, were notably diminished in the NAFLD cohort. Furthermore, PI species PI36:2 (18:1_18:1) and PI36:4 (18:2_18:2) also showed significant decreases in this group, highlighting potential targets for therapeutic interventions.
Discussion
In this study, we applied state-of-the-art lipidomics methods to investigate the serum lipidomic profile of AGHD patients with iGCTs. Our data identified highly AGHD-specific clinical metabolic alterations and lipid subclasses, such as TAGs, DAGs and critical molecules in phospholipid metabolic pathways. The reversal change of phospholipids vs alkylated phospholipids is one of the prominent features of the lipid profile in AGHD. We also demonstrated the association between various lipids and clinical parameters in AGHD patients. In addition, significant sex differences in lipid profiles in AGHD patients and lipid outlier signatures that may predict NAFLD were also found.

20

Phospholipids are vital in mammalian cells, regulating membrane proteins, membrane trafficking, cell growth, apoptosis and intracellular signaling. Disruptions in their metabolism can trigger pathological conditions (
32
). Our data showed significantly elevated levels of phospholipid subclasses in AGHD patients, such as PEs, PCs and PIs. Several lysophospholipids, including LPCs, LPAs and LPIs, were also upregulated, reflecting dysregulated phospholipid metabolism in the AGHD group. This further confirms our previous work in nontargeted serum metabolomics of AGHD (
6
). We also observed the opposite effect, with an increase in PE-FA (PE34:1 (18:0_16:0)) and a decrease in PUFA-PE (PE38:7 (16:1_22:6)) associated with poor metabolism-related indicators. Furthermore, there was a significant reduction in alkylated phospholipids in the AGHD group. Alkylated lipids have been considered beneficial for metabolism, and rich ester- and ether-linked PEs (PE-O and PE-P) were found to be associated with higher HDL-c levels and lower FINS levels (
33

33
,
34
). Our data also found new associations, including an association of ester-linked PE-O, PC-O and LPE-O, but not PEs, with low FINS levels, insulin resistance index, and TG levels. The change in the ratio of phospholipids to alkylated phospholipids is a characteristic of AGHD metabolic changes. The polyunsaturated ether phospholipids synthesized by peroxisomes are critical in controlling susceptibility to ferroptosis (
35
). Further research on the expression and activity changes of alkylating enzymes under GHD may provide new targets for treating metabolic disorders in AGHD.
Increased prevalence of NAFLD was reported in AGHD patients, associated with visceral obesity and insulin resistance (
8

In AGHD patients with NAFLD, we discovered that total and three DAG and two TAG lipids were significantly elevated, consistent with clinically noted high TG levels in their lipid profiles. DAGs can act as a lipid signaling molecule to assemble and activate signal complexes, such as interacting with proteins with GTPase activity (
41
). This is crucial for regulating cell membrane dynamics, endocytosis and cytoskeleton remodeling. At the same time, one PE-O lipid and PUFA-PE (PE38:7(16:1:22:6)), which have been shown to be beneficial for metabolism, were significantly reduced in NAFDL individuals. In this context, studying specific lipid subclasses known to be detectable in plasma in future studies will also provide ideas for promising predictors of NAFLD. Further research is warranted to investigate how changes in these lipid molecules affect liver cell function in AGHD.
Our study highlighted a significant finding – strong sex-specific signatures in lipid subclasses, such as LPAs, PCs and PC-FAs within the AGHD group sample, despite the comparable clinical lipid profiles between the two groups (Supplementary Table S1, see section on
supplementary materials
given at the end of this article). In contrast, the lipid composition of the healthy control group showed minimal sex disparities. This finding underscores the critical importance of accounting for sex as a potential confounding factor in investigations of lipid metabolism studies in AGHD. The testosterone/estradiol/progesterone replacement regimen may have a corresponding impact on lipidomics. However, additional research is warranted to validate these findings.
Although the study was based on age-, sex- and BMI-matched AGHD populations with iGCT versus healthy controls, there are some limitations. This dataset was generated from a small sample size and needs validation for a broader population. Although this study is based on the characteristics of metabolic changes observed in the AGHD population in previous small sample size studies, targeting validation and further revealing changes in lipidomics in the enlarged sample size and homogeneous causes, future more extensive longitudinal cohort studies should be designed to confirm and extend our observations. Second, our patients had various degrees of intracranial invasion, received surgery, chemotherapy and irradiation and probably had not only pituitary deficiency but also hypothalamic damage. These may also affect lipid metabolism, and further studies are needed to evaluate these impacting factors. In addition, the etiology of AGHD is diverse, and our findings need to be proven relevant to patients with other causes of AGHD. Finally, our earlier, nontargeted metabolomics study preliminarily analyzed the metabolic profile changes of 11 AGHD patients before and after 12 months of rhGH treatment, and the results showed that rhGH treatment increased the level of specific glycerophospholipid compounds and decreased the level of fatty acid ester compounds (
5
). Therefore, further studies on lipidomics after GH treatment would also help elucidate the mechanism of GH replacement therapy beneficial to metabolic improvement. Finally, the roles of complex lipids are diverse, and the observed dysregulation of lipid metabolic networks awaits further mechanical validation.
Conclusions
Our results suggested significant serum lipidomic profile changes in AGHD patients with iGCTs. This dataset offers a valuable resource for a better understanding of the impact of the GH-IGF-1 axis on lipid metabolism.
