INTRODUCTION
The primary cilium is a solitary, nonmotile, microtubule-based organelle that protrudes from the surface of nearly all vertebrate cells (
Anvarian
et al.
, 2019
). It serves as a critical sensory antenna, detecting chemical and mechanical signals in the cell's environment (
Kopinke
et al.
, 2020
). Primary cilia are involved in various cellular processes, including cell cycle regulation, differentiation, and maintaining tissue homeostasis (
Mill
et al.
, 2023
). Defects in ciliary structure or function are linked to a group of disorders known as ciliopathies, which include conditions such as polycystic kidney disease, retinal degeneration, and Bardet–Biedl syndrome (
Hilgendorf
et al.
, 2024
). Structurally, the primary cilium is composed of a microtubule axoneme, surrounded by a specialized ciliary membrane that is distinct from the plasma membrane (
Nachury and Mick, 2019
). The specialized ciliary membrane is characterized by its unique lipid and protein composition (
Hilgendorf
et al.
, 2016
). Unlike the general plasma membrane, the ciliary membrane is enriched with specific transmembrane receptors such as G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), ion channels such as polycystins, single-pass transmembrane protein Fibrocystin, and membrane-associated lipidated proteins (
Hilgendorf
et al.
, 2016
). The unique composition of the ciliary membrane is essential to process extracellular signals and coordinate appropriate cellular responses (
Hwang
et al.
, 2021
;
Truong
et al.
, 2021
;
Hwang
et al.
, 2023
). Therefore, understanding the fundamental mechanisms underlying protein trafficking into the cilium holds significant potential for development of treatments for a range of cilia-related diseases (
Reddy Palicharla and Mukhopadhyay, 2024
).
Intraflagellar transport (IFT) is a crucial process for the assembly and maintenance of cilia, involving the bidirectional movement of molecular complexes along the ciliary axoneme (
Rosenbaum and Witman, 2002
;
Taschner and Lorentzen, 2016
). IFT is mediated by two main protein complexes, IFT-A and IFT-B. In addition to IFT-A's critical role in retrograde transport of proteins in cilia (
Wren
et al.
, 2013
;
Lechtreck
et al.
, 2022
), IFT-A also plays a significant role in the import of cargoes into the cilium (
Lee
et al.
, 2008
;
Mukhopadhyay
et al.
, 2010
;
Ocbina
et al.
, 2011
;
Qin
et al.
, 2011
;
Liem
et al.
, 2012
;
Picariello
et al.
, 2019
). TULP3 (tubby like protein 3) is an adaptor protein that specifically interacts with IFT-A to mediate the trafficking of both transmembrane and membrane-associated proteins into the cilium (
Mukhopadhyay
et al.
, 2010
;
Badgandi
et al.
, 2017
;
Han
et al.
, 2019
;
Palicharla
et al.
, 2023
). TULP3 cargoes include transmembrane proteins such as GPCRs (GPR161, MCHR1, SSTR3, etc.), polycystins, Fibrocystin, and multiple membrane-associated lipidated proteins including ARL13B, and ARL13B-dependent lipidated cargoes such as INPP5E, NPHP3, and CYS1 (
Badgandi
et al.
, 2017
;
Han
et al.
, 2019
;
Hwang
et al.
, 2019
;
Legue and Liem, 2019
;
Palicharla
et al.
, 2023
). TULP3 homologues in other organisms are also critical in ciliary trafficking. For example, the
Drosophila melanogaster
homologue dTULP, regulates TRP channel localization in auditory neuronal cilia (
Park
et al.
, 2013
), whereas the
Caenorhabditis elegans
homologue TUB-1 determines localization of GPCRs and cyclic nucleotide gated channels to olfactory neuron cilia (
DiTirro
et al.
, 2019
). TULP3 functions redundantly with the founding tubby family member TUBBY (TUB) in trafficking of cargoes in the neural tube and brain neurons (
Sun
et al.
, 2012
;
Badgandi
et al.
, 2017
;
Ferent
et al.
, 2019
). Disruptions in IFT-A components or TULP3 or the related founding member TUB can lead to defective ciliary protein trafficking in multiple tissue contexts (
Sun
et al.
, 2012
;
Loktev and Jackson, 2013
;
Chavez
et al.
, 2015
;
Garcia-Gonzalo
et al.
, 2015
;
Hirano
et al.
, 2017
;
Dateyama
et al.
, 2019
;
Hilgendorf
et al.
, 2019
;
Barbeito
et al.
, 2021
;
Wu
et al.
, 2021
;
Fagan
et al.
, 2024
). In line with a role of TULP3 in trafficking multiple cargoes to cilia, TULP3 and IFT-A have been implicated in neural tube patterning (
Tran
et al.
, 2008
;
Norman
et al.
, 2009
;
Qin
et al.
, 2011
;
Liem
et al.
, 2012
;
Mukhopadhyay
et al.
, 2013
;
Legue and Liem, 2020
), renal and liver cystogenesis (
Hwang
et al.
, 2019
;
Legue and Liem, 2019
;
Wang
et al.
, 2021
;
Devane
et al.
, 2022
;
Wang
et al.
, 2022b
), adipogenesis (
Hilgendorf
et al.
, 2019
), insulin and glucagon secretion (
Wu
et al.
, 2021
) and spina bifida (
Patterson
et al.
, 2009
;
Kuang
et al.
, 2022
;
Wang
et al.
, 2022a
).
TULP3 serves as a bridge between IFT-A and ciliary cargoes, with an N-terminal helix of TULP3 that binds to IFT-A and a C-terminal tubby domain that binds to PI(4,5)P
2
and membrane cargoes (
Mukhopadhyay
et al.
, 2010
;
Badgandi
et al.
, 2017
). We previously proposed a three-step process during trafficking of proteins into the cilium by TULP3 and IFT-A. These steps include: 1) Capture of cargoes by the tubby domain of TULP3 at the plasma membrane in a phosphoinositide PI(4,5)P
2
dependent manner, 2) Transport of cargoes into the cilium via interaction of TULP3 N-terminus with the IFT-A core, and 3) Release of cargoes in the PI(4,5)P
2
-deficient ciliary membrane (
Badgandi
et al.
, 2017
). We also showed that short motifs called ciliary localization signals (CLSs) from multiple GPCRs and Fibrocystin were necessary and sufficient for TULP3-mediated ciliary trafficking (
Badgandi
et al.
, 2017
). Understanding the precise mechanisms by which IFT-A and TULP3 capture diverse cargoes is critical for elucidating the molecular basis of ciliary function and its related diseases.
Recent structural studies on the intraflagellar transport complex A (IFT-A) and its interaction with TULP3 have provided significant insights into their roles in ciliary protein trafficking and ciliary function (
Reddy Palicharla and Mukhopadhyay, 2024
). IFT-A is composed of six subunits: IFT43, IFT121, IFT122, IFT139, IFT140, and IFT144 (
Hesketh
et al.
, 2022
;
Meleppattu
et al.
, 2022
;
Jiang
et al.
, 2023
;
Ma
et al.
, 2023
). Most of the IFT-A subunits, except for IFT43, feature WD domains and TPR repeats. Structural analysis reveals that all the WD domains are positioned on one side of the complex, while the TPR repeats are aligned on the opposite side, suggesting a functional segregation within the complex. Additionally, multiple zinc-binding domains have been identified within the structure, which are likely crucial for maintaining the integrity of the IFT-A complex (
Meleppattu
et al.
, 2022
;
Jiang
et al.
, 2023
;
Ma
et al.
, 2023
). Furthermore, although the tubby domain of TULP3 is not resolved due to its flexibility, the high-resolution structure of the N-terminus of TULP3 in complex with IFT-A has identified critical amino acid residues involved in this interaction and highlighted their significance in ciliary protein trafficking (
Hesketh
et al.
, 2022
;
Jiang
et al.
, 2023
). While these structural studies provide molecular details of TULP3 N-terminus—IFT-A interactions, the mechanisms by which the tubby domain of TULP3 recognizes a wide variety of ciliary cargoes are not known.
Here we identify critical residues in the tubby domain of TULP3 that mediate ciliary transport of diverse ciliary cargoes. These residues, along with recently identified mutations in patients with hepatorenal fibrocystic disease and spina bifida, are localized to one side of the tubby domain β-barrel. The tubby domain surface mutants affect TULP3’s proximity with diverse ciliary cargoes without affecting phosphoinositide binding, ciliary trafficking, or hydrodynamic properties of TULP3. These findings indicate a specific surface of the β-barrel of TULP3 tubby domain in ciliary targeting of diverse cargoes relevant to pathogenesis of ciliary trafficking diseases.

Proximity biotinylation–mass spectrometry screen for identifying TULP3 tubby domain residues that mediate cargo interactions
We previously demonstrated that multiple cargoes are in proximity to the C-terminal tubby domain of TULP3. The cargoes tested included transmembrane cargoes (
Badgandi
et al.
, 2017

Palicharla
et al.
, 2023

Badgandi
et al.
, 2017

Motley
et al.
, 2003

To identify potential residues on TULP3 tubby domain that mediate likely cargo interactions, we performed proximity based biotinylation analysis of
LAP(GFP-TEV-Stag)
TULP3 by cotransfecting C-terminal-BirA* tagged CLSs of the GPCR GPR161 along with the corresponding nonciliary mutant (CD8α-GPR161-CLS
cBirA*

5A

Figure 1A

The GPR161 CLS is in the intracytoplasmic loop 3 (IC3) and part of an extended TM5 helix in the recently solved cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) structure (

A characteristic feature of the tubby domain is a 12 stranded β-barrel that is positioned around a C-terminal hydrophobic α−helix (H12) (
Boggon
et al.
, 1999
). There are two cooperative and adjacent PI(4,5)P
2
binding sites in the tubby domain (
Santagata
et al.
, 2001
;
Thallmair
et al.
, 2022
). The originally described site is in a distinct pocket nestled by positive residues projecting from β-strand 5 (
Santagata
et al.
, 2001
). A second PI(4,5)P
2
binding site has been recently shown to be present in a positively charged cluster of residues between β-strands 3 and 4 (
Thallmair
et al.
, 2022
). Interestingly, almost all of the TULP3 residues (except K333) that we find biotinylated by cargoes are on one surface of the β-barrel close to the adjacent β-strands 8-10 or the central H12 α−helix, distinct from the PI(4,5)P
2
binding sites (
Figure 1, C and D
). This suggests a TULP3 tubby domain interface involved in the recognition of diverse cargoes.

Conserved phenylalanine residues inside the β-barrel close to the surface regulate ciliary trafficking of TULP3 cargoes
We next examined conserved residues within the β-barrel close to the surface for their role in ciliary trafficking. We identified three highly conserved phenylalanine residues (F391, F406, and F414) structurally stacked in β-strands 10–12 and facing the interior of the β-barrel (
Figure 4, A and B
). While F391 and F414 are close to the surface of the β-strands 10 and 12, respectively, the F406 residue is in the middle of β-strand 11 (
Figure 4A
). Another phenylalanine (F435) was located on the outer edge of the H12 α-helix, facing these β-strands (
Figure 4, A and B
). These phenylalanine residues are located close to the K389 residue that we showed to be crucial for ciliary protein trafficking (
Figure 4B
). We mutated residues F391, F414, and F435 to arginine or alanine and upon equivalent stable expression of these mutated residues in
Tulp3
ko IMCD3 cells, F391R and F414A variants showed a significant reduction in ARL13B and GPR161 levels in cilia, while F435A had no effect (
Figure 4, C–F
). Unlike residues close to the surface, the F406A mutant did not express well likely from defects in folding and lack of stability of the TULP3 protein. Overall, the conserved phenylalanine residues in β-strands 10 and 12 that are close to the surface are crucial for ciliary cargo trafficking.

TULP3

TULP3
have recently been reported in patients suffering from congenital hepatorenal fibrocystic disease (

and

R408

TULP3
including

TULP3
L235F
(

, 2022

DISCUSSION
The mechanisms by which TULP3 captures diverse transmembrane and membrane-associated cargoes are poorly understood. Our results suggest that cooperative interactions between the clustered residues on one half-surface of the TULP3 tubby β-barrel ensure transport of diverse cargoes into cilia (
Figure 8, A and B
). We propose the importance of this specific tubby domain region based on the following observations. First, human TULP3 tubby domain residues R319, R382, K389 (or equivalent K407 residue in mouse), and R408 that affect ciliary trafficking and proximity of diverse cargoes are positively charged and predominantly cluster in one half-surface of the tubby β-barrel (β-strands 8, 10-11). Second, at least two phenylalanines in a triad that may stabilize hydrophobic contacts in one half of the β-barrel close to the surface, F391 in β-strand 10 (very close to K389), and F414 in β-strand 12 also regulate cargo trafficking and cargo proximity. These results suggest that the β-barrel hydrophobic residues close to the surface are critical for cargo capture. Third, ciliary localization of TULP3 that is mediated by IFT-A core binding to TULP3 N-terminus (
Mukhopadhyay
et al.
, 2010
) was not affected in these β-barrel mutants. Additionally, PI(4,5)P
2
binding was unaffected. Thus, this β-barrel surface that mediates cargo proximity is distinct from the PI(4,5)P
2
and IFT-A binding properties of TULP3. The two cooperative and adjacent PI(4,5)P
2
binding sites in the tubby domain are positioned perpendicular to this β-barrel surface (
Santagata
et al.
, 2001
;
Thallmair
et al.
, 2022
) (
Figure 8B
). Such an orientation would place this β-barrel surface in the lower half of the tubby β-barrel and not directly in contact with the membrane.

, 2001
;
Thallmair
et al.
, 2022
).
We also describe ciliary cargo trafficking defects of TULP3 variants—from human patients with hepatorenal fibrocystic disease and spina bifida (
Devane
et al.
, 2022
;
Jafari Khamirani
et al.
, 2022
;
Kuang
et al.
, 2022
), and from a
Tulp3
mutant mouse model of embryonic renal cystogenesis (
Legue and Liem, 2019
)—located at this specific tubby domain surface. Thus, defects in cilium-generated signaling arising from aberrant trafficking of TULP3 cargoes could mediate the pathogenesis of these diseases. TULP3
K389
is at the beginning of β-strand 10 at this specific tubby domain surface. Mice homozygous for
Tulp3
K407I
are hypomorphic compared with
Tulp3
ko (
Norman
et al.
, 2009
) but show renal cysts during embryogenesis (
Legue and Liem, 2019
). Of the patient mutations studied here, only the residues in β-strand 11 (R408H) and in the adjacent loop (R382W) significantly reduced cargo trafficking to cilia. These mutations were seen in patients suffering from hepatorenal fibrocystic disease (
Devane
et al.
, 2022
;
Jafari Khamirani
et al.
, 2022
) and spina bifida (
Kuang
et al.
, 2022
). However,
TULP3
T372S
and
TULP3
L235F
mutations seen in patients with spina bifida rescued ciliary trafficking defects in
Tulp3
ko like WT (
Kuang
et al.
, 2022
). The heterozygous nature of the spina bifida associated alleles in

and the multifactorial nature of the disease (

et al.
, 2021
), makes it currently difficult to assign pathogenicity to the
TULP3
T372S
and
TULP3
L235F
variants that do not affect ciliary trafficking in our assays. Alternatively, these residues might impact on unknown functionalities of the TULP3 tubby domain.
The specific ciliary cargoes regulating these phenotypes is currently speculative, but mice models of cargo knockouts and nonciliary cargo mutants provide important clues. For example,
Tulp3
K407I
homozygous mice show defects in ARL13B trafficking to renal collecting duct cilia (
Legue and Liem, 2019
), and lack of ARL13B in these cilia precedes cyst formation from TULP3 loss (
Hwang
et al.
, 2019
), suggesting a predominant role of ARL13B and ARL13B-dependent lipidated cargoes in renal pathogenesis in juvenile mice (
Palicharla
et al.
, 2023
). Lack of ARL13B specifically from cilia starting from embryogenesis also causes renal cystogenesis (
Van Sciver
et al.
, 2023
). Similarly,
Gpr161
knockouts show fully penetrant spina bifida (
Mukhopadhyay
et al.
, 2010
;
Kim
et al.
, 2023
), whereas a GPR161 C-terminal truncation results in strain-specific partially penetrant spina bifida (
Matteson
et al.
, 2008
). Mutations in the ECL2 domain of GPR161 that promotes self-activation (
Hoppe
et al.
, 2024
), are also seen in human patients of spina bifida (
Kim
et al.
, 2019
). Lack of GPR161 specifically from cilia in a knock-in mouse model (
Gpr161
mut
1
) mutated at the TULP3 binding IC3 CLS without impacting on constitutive cAMP signaling activity also causes spina bifida (
Hwang
et al.
, 2021
). Thus, it is likely that the relevant TULP3/TUB ciliary cargo in patients with spina bifida is primarily GPR161.
There are critical positive charged residues in the CLSs of GPR161, and Fibrocystin that mediate ciliary trafficking (
Badgandi
et al.
, 2017
;
Hwang
et al.
, 2021
). These CLSs are membrane-proximal and generate secondary helical structures. In fact, the CLS in GPR161 IC3 (VKARK) is shown to be part of an extended TM5 helix in the cryo-EM structure (
Hoppe
et al.
, 2024
). Similarly, the CLS sequence KTRKIKP of Fibrocystin likely follows an extended TM domain (
Follit
et al.
, 2010
). The 19 aa palmitoylated amphipathic helix in ARL13B that regulates TULP3-mediated ciliary trafficking (
Palicharla
et al.
, 2023
) strongly suggest this CLS to be a membrane-proximal sequence as well. However, many of the residues in the tubby β-barrel surface are positively charged and are most likely not directly mediating binding with the short peptide sequences in these CLSs. Rather, we predict that the membrane-proximal cargo CLSs orients the linked BirA* to have proximity with the β-barrel surface in the tubby domain.
There could be multiple ways by which the specific β-barrel surface residues functions in cargo trafficking. First, the β-barrel surface residues could allosterically regulate membrane-proximal interactions between cargo CLSs and TULP3. Second, the β-barrel surface residues could facilitate cooperative interactions between additional cargo interfaces and/or in IFT-A. The presence of CLSs in the C-term cytoplasmic tails of GPCRs SSTR3, HTR6 (
Raleigh
et al.
, 2018
), and µ-opiod receptor (
Fagan
et al.
, 2024
), suggests that these C-terminal nonmembrane proximal regions could also be important in interacting with diverse cargoes for trafficking with TULP3. Recent cryo-EM studies of soluble IFT-A complexes (
Hesketh
et al.
, 2022
;
Meleppattu
et al.
, 2022
;
Jiang
et al.
, 2023
;
Ma
et al.
, 2023
), along with cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET) of IFT trains captured in situ help in orienting the IFT-A complex toward the ciliary membrane (
Jordan
et al.
, 2018
;
van den Hoek
et al.
, 2022
;
Lacey
et al.
, 2023
). This orientation shows that the WD domains of IFT-A subunits IFT140 and IFT121 (WDR35) face toward the membrane while the TPR regions are on the opposite face (
Hesketh
et al.
, 2022
;
Meleppattu
et al.
, 2022
;
Jiang
et al.
, 2023
;
Ma
et al.
, 2023
). In addition, recently solved cryo-EM structure (
Jiang
et al.
, 2023
) and structural modeling (
Hesketh
et al.
, 2022
) of insertion of the TULP3 N-term α-helix into the IFT-A1 core by our group and others suggest orientation of the tubby domain towards membrane. In contrast, the recently described cryo-ET of the retrograde IFT trains is predicted to block tubby domain access toward the membrane (
Lacey
et al.
, 2024
), conferring specificity of membrane orientation of IFT-A and tubby domain in the anterograde trains. Therefore, additional IFT-A subunits could contribute to cargo-specific interactions (
Ye
et al.
, 2018
), especially for cargoes such as polycystins for which precise TULP3-regulated CLSs are currently unknown. In fact, the core subunit IFT140 and the peripheral subunit WDR35 might provide interface with TULP3 cargoes. A deletion of the WD repeats of IFT140 (
Picariello
et al.
, 2019
) that should otherwise not impact on the IFT-A complex (
Jiang
et al.
, 2023
) causes defects in trafficking of Arl-13 and multiple farnesylated or myristoylated proteins, presumably Arl-13–regulated, into
Chlamydomonas
flagella (
Picariello
et al.
, 2019
). The IFT-A2 peripheral subunit WDR35 does not bind or regulate trafficking of TULP3 to cilia (
Mukhopadhyay
et al.
, 2010
), but
WDR35
mutants affect ciliary localization of multiple TULP3-dependent cargoes (
Fu
et al.
, 2016
;
Takahara
et al.
, 2018
;
Picariello
et al.
, 2019
;
Kobayashi
et al.
, 2021
;
Quidwai
et al.
, 2021
). IFT-A subunits are also detected in pulldown of a SSTR3 CLS located in an unstructured part of the IC3 that is not membrane-proximal (
Ye
et al.
, 2018
). The oligomeric nature of the anterograde IFT trains (
van den Hoek
et al.
, 2022
) could also be relevant for low-affinity TULP3–cargo interactions in membrane proximity. However, lack of IFT-A train formation on existing IFT-B trains upon IFT139 loss (
Jordan
et al.
, 2018
), without affecting steady-state membrane cargo trafficking (
Hirano
et al.
, 2017
), suggests that cargo transport into cilia through formation of tripartite cargo-TULP3–IFT-A complex can occur even in the absence of IFT-A trains (
Reddy Palicharla and Mukhopadhyay, 2024
).
While future structural studies are needed to precisely reveal the atomic organization of TULP3 cargo binding sites, our results, by providing a distinct function of TULP3 in cargo interface beyond its known functions in PI(4,5)P
2
and IFT-A binding, could be insightful in understanding the impact of
TULP3
mutations in patients (
Devane
et al.
, 2022
;
Jafari Khamirani
et al.
, 2022
;
Kuang
et al.
, 2022
). We are hopeful that in the future, the cargo-TULP3-IFT-A cryo-EM structures would be able to address the model we have started to build in this study and simplify the heterogeneity and complexity in membrane cargo trafficking by TULP3 and IFT-A (
Cilleros-Rodriguez
et al.
, 2022
). Finally, targeting TULP3–cargo interactions could provide therapeutics in ciliary trafficking-regulated diseases.

Single colonies of Rosetta cells transfected with the desired plasmid were grown in 3 ml LB broth containing ampicillin and chloramphenicol for 8 h and then transferred to 25 ml and grown overnight. Overnight culture is transferred to 1 l LB broth and grown until the OD reached 0.5–0.8. Cells were grown at 37°C and 200 RPM. Protein expression was induced overnight at 18°C, 200 RPM using 0.25 mM IPTG. Cells were pelleted by spinning at 6000 RPM for 10 min at 4°C. Cells were lysed by resuspending and incubating in 25 ml lysis buffer (50 mM Tris pH7.4, 450 mM NaCl, 5 mM EDTA, 1 mM DTT, 0.5% Triton X 100, and 10 µg/ml each of Leupeptin, Pepstatin, and Chymostatin and 0.1 mM AEBSF) for 30 min at 4°C in a rotator. Cells were sonicated until the solution is nonviscous and spun at 20000 ×
g
for 20 min at 4°C to remove insoluble material. GST- and MBP-tagged proteins were incubated with washed Glutathione beads and Amylose beads respectively for 3 h at 4°C. Beads were washed three times with lysis buffer and two times with low salt buffer (50 mM Tris pH7.4, 150 mM NaCl, and 1 mM DTT). GST-tagged protein were eluted with elution buffer containing 10 mM reduced glutathione pH 7.5 in low salt buffer. MBP-tagged proteins were eluted using 10 mM Maltose in low salt buffer. Proteins were dialyzed into storage buffer 20 mM HEPES pH 7.4, 100 mM NaCl, 1 mM DTT and 5% Glycerol.

Mass spectrometry
For proximity biotinylation experiments, CD8-Gpr161 IC3
WT
-BirA* or CD8-Gpr161 IC3
5A
-BirA* were cotransfected with
LAP
TULP3 in TREx-293 cells. After LAP tandem affinity purification, excised gel bands were submitted for posttranslational analysis at Taplin proteomics core for biotinylated peptides. In the Taplin proteomics core excised gel bands were cut into ∼1 mm
3
pieces.  Gel pieces were then subjected to a modified in-gel trypsin digestion procedure (
Shevchenko
et al.
, 1996
).  Gel pieces were washed and dehydrated with acetonitrile for 10 min followed by removal of acetonitrile.  Pieces were then completely dried in a speed-vac.  Rehydration of the gel pieces was with 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate solution containing 12.5 ng/µl modified sequencing-grade trypsin (Promega, Madison, WI) at 4°C.  After 45 min, the excess trypsin solution was removed and replaced with 50 mM ammonium bicarbonate solution to just cover the gel pieces.  Samples were then placed in a 37°C room overnight.  Peptides were later extracted by removing the ammonium bicarbonate solution, followed by one wash with a solution containing 50% acetonitrile and 1% formic acid.  The extracts were then dried in a speed-vac (∼1 h).  The samples were then stored at 4°C until analysis. On the day of analysis, the samples were reconstituted in 5–10 µl of high performance liquid chromtaography (HPLC) solvent A (2.5% acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid).  A nanoscale reverse-phase HPLC capillary column was created by packing 2.6 µm C18 spherical silica beads into a fused silica capillary (100 µm inner diameter × ∼30 cm length) with a flame-drawn tip (
Peng and Gygi, 2001
).  After equilibrating the column each sample was loaded via a Famos auto sampler (LC Packings, San Francisco, CA) onto the column.   A gradient was formed, and peptides were eluted with increasing concentrations of solvent B (97.5% acetonitrile, 0.1% formic acid). As peptides eluted, they were subjected to electrospray ionization and then entered an LTQ Orbitrap Velos Pro ion-trap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA).  Peptides were detected, isolated, and fragmented to produce a tandem mass spectrum of specific fragment ions for each peptide.  Peptide sequences (and hence protein identity) were determined by matching protein databases with the acquired fragmentation pattern by the software program, Sequest (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA) (
Eng
et al.
, 1994
).  Post translational analysis was performed using probability-based scoring (
Beausoleil
et al.
, 2006
). All databases include a reversed version of all the sequences and the data were filtered to between a 1 and 2% peptide false discovery rate.
