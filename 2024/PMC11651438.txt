1. Introduction
Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is the most common malignancy of the kidney in adults.
[
1
]
Clear cell RCC (ccRCC), which is highly fatal and is the most common type of RCC,
[
2
]
has seen a rising incidence rate in recent years.
[
3
]
Emerging evidence indicates new favorable subsets of cancers of unknown primary, including RCC cancers of unknown primary. This newly recognized clinical entity of RCC may contribute to the recent rise in RCC incidence.
[
4
]
The incidence of RCC varies around the world, and the probability of RCC is higher in people who smoke or those with obesity or hypertension.
[
5
]
RCC is estimated to cause >100,000 deaths annually.
[
6
]
As an immunogenic tumor, immunotherapy is a key component of RCC treatment.
[
7
]
For example, from a therapeutic standpoint, tyrosine kinase inhibitor monotherapy remains a suitable first-line option for many patients who are ineligible for immunotherapy, and the advanced clear cell RCC (STAR) findings
[
8
]
are directly applicable to these patients. Although treatment breaks could be considered for these patients, caution is advised because the patients generally experience shorter progression-free survival than patients on first-line tyrosine kinase inhibitor therapy.
[
9
]
The dynamic interaction between immune modulators in TME and tumor cells can promote a positive response to immunotherapy.
[
10
,
11
]
For example, reduced expression of lactotransferrin, a key protein in the innate immune system, has been found to promote metastasis in RCC. Interestingly, this reduction also makes RCC tumor cells more responsive to mammalian target of rapamycin inhibitors, suggesting that lactotransferrin has potential as a predictive marker for treatment efficacy.
[
12
]
However, heterogeneity and inhibitory changes in the TME have a significant impact on the occurrence, development, drug resistance, and therapeutic differences of tumors.
[
11
,
13
]
Macrophages are cells comprising a heterogeneous population with functional properties and complex phenotypes in the TME.
[
14
]
Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are defined as macrophages, including M1 and M2 macrophages, which accumulate in or infiltrate the solid TME.
[
15
,
16
]
TAMs are prominent immune cells in the TME,
[
17
]
and a further analysis of TAMs is of great significance.
Recently, macrophage-related research has intensified in several fields of clinical medicine,
[
18
–
21
]
and the number of articles in the field of macrophages and RCC continues to rise. However, the breadth and depth of these studies vary considerably.
[
22
]
Bibliometrics aims to analyze and classify a body of literature to produce in-depth summaries,
[
23
]
including descriptions of the hotspots, research status, and predictions of future trends, which is difficult to achieve using traditional review or meta-analysis methods. Bibliometric analysis has become increasingly popular recently and has been utilized in several fields of clinical medicine.
[
24
]
However, in the field of RCC and macrophages, no specific bibliometric studies have been published. The aim of this study was to visualize and analyze the hotspots and future research directions of RCC-macrophage research using bibliometric analysis.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Data sources and retrieval strategies
We performed a bibliometric analysis based on the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). The inclusion criteria were as follows: listed in the WoSCC SCI-EXPANDED database; published in the English language; reviews or research articles; published between January 1, 2004, and December 13, 2023; and relevant study topic (with irrelevant studies manually excluded). The search terms were as follows: TS = ([renal OR kidney] NEAR/2[tumor* OR cancer* OR tumor* OR neoplasm* OR oncology OR adenocarcinoma* carcinoma*]) AND TS = (macrophage OR macrophages). Following the search, the full record was exported with its references in the form of a text file and a tab-delimited file. Eligible publications, including 656 articles and 122 reviews, were subsequently analyzed (Fig.
1
).
Figure 1.
Detailed search flowchart.
2.2. Data analyses and visualization
From the WoSCC studies, we extracted data on the number of papers, citation times, author, publication year, country/region, journal, references, author keywords, and other data. In addition, synonym merging was conducted to eliminate duplicates. Among all the items in the research network, some nodes were not connected to each other and may not be represented in the analysis. Sets of connected items may consist of only nodes with interconnections to make the image display more visible. The tools used for network analysis were CiteSpace 6.2.4, VOSviewer 1.6.20, and an online bibliometric analysis platform (
http://bibliometric.com/
).
Citespace can be used to analyze and visualize developments and trends in scientific research and is continuously updated and actively maintained with new theoretical developments and visual analysis features.
[
25
]
Similarly, VOSviewer facilitates the visualization and construction of bibliometric maps; moreover, the ease of image interpretation is a major advantage of VOSviewer for bibliometric mapping.
[
26
]
Finally, this study utilized the bibliometric analysis platform to conduct a national/regional collaborative network analysis.
2.3. Ethics statement
This study involved the analysis of papers published in a public database and did not involve conducting human or animal studies. Therefore, ethical approval was not required for this study.
3. Results
3.1. Publication and citation trends
From the WoSCC database, between January 1, 2004, and December 13, 2023, 778 papers were cited 25,826 times, with an average of 33.2 citations. Furthermore, 32,955 references from 3427 different journals were cited by publications in the field of “RCC and macrophages.” We found that the number of publications per year and citation frequencies have shown a rising trend (note that the number of publications in 2023 was lower than in previous years, likely because publications for that year have not yet been fully included in the database). 4. Discussion
This study represents the first attempt to apply bibliometrics to RCC-macrophage research. We utilized bibliometric tools to search for macrophage-related studies in the field of RCC. Subsequently, 778 articles were included. We found that the number of publications per year and the citation frequencies have generally shown an increasing trend.
China produces the most articles, but it does not rank very highly in the corresponding article citation trends. Among the top 10 authors and institutions by publication count, 8 authors and 6 organizations were from China, explaining the relatively rapid development of the field in China in recent years. This suggests that China has produced a significant amount of research in the field of RCC and macrophages; however, its findings have garnered relatively limited attention and impact within the international academic community. Thus, there is still considerable room for growth, and future research in China should aim to be more comprehensive and in-depth. The United States ranks second in the number of published documents but leads in total citations (Table 1, Supplemental Digital Content,
http://links.lww.com/MD/O178
) and also has a relatively high average of citations per document. As shown in Table 3, Supplemental Digital Content,
http://links.lww.com/MD/O178
, among the top 10 most co-cited authors, 6 are based in the United States, suggesting that researchers in the United States have a substantial impact on academic research in this field.
Research journals play a crucial role as a medium for scholars to access academic information in a timely manner; as such, their reputations wield a significant influence. Frontiers in Oncology was the most published journal in Table
3
. There was a prevalence of oncology journals among the top-ranked journals in both publication and citation counts, suggesting that oncology journals were particularly effective in promoting RCC-macrophage research.
The clustering results for the co-cited literature (Table 5, Supplemental Digital Content,
http://links.lww.com/MD/O178
) were consistent in the general direction with the keyword analysis results. Based on the results, the research directions most likely to be of interest to scholars include the individualization of immunotherapy, in-depth exploration of the TME, development of macrophages as therapeutic targets, and the use of bioinformatics tools for the discovery of new biomarkers and therapeutic targets.
Keyword analysis, an important part of bibliometrics, revealed that prognosis, immunotherapy, TME, and immune infiltration were research hot spots in this field. Prognosis was a relatively broad concept. Recent advancements in medical diagnostic technology have greatly enhanced early detection rates and improved prognoses for RCC. Exosomes, for instance, have emerged as a promising source of noninvasive tumor biomarkers. The unique double-layer membrane structure of exosomes provides protection for external RNases and proteases, enhancing the stability of enclosed mRNAs, miRNAs, and functional proteins. This structural advantage makes exosomes highly sensitive markers for disease diagnosis. The molecular contents of tumor-derived exosomes, especially the range of miRNAs, serve as biomarkers for RCC, detectable in the patient serum and urine, providing valuable targets for early diagnosis and disease monitoring.
[
31
]
On the other hand, Liu et al
[
32
]
developed and validated a prognosis model for 6 genes related to macrophage differentiation, namely, ABCG1, HAVCR2, CD14, TGFA, KDF1, and KITLG. Xing et al
[
33
]
applied a combination of radiomics and machine learning to formulate a prognostic prediction model for RCC patients, where radiomics could be used to predict overall survival and for the individualized treatment of RCC patients. Schulz et al
[
34
]
evaluated and developed a multimodal deep learning model for the prediction of prognosis in patients with ccRCC, while Mahootiha et al
[
35
]
applied a multimodal deep learning method to predict the survival rate of patients with RCC based on CT imaging and clinical data, both achieving good prediction results. Chen et al
[
36
]
identified methyladenine-related lncRNAs and explored the cellular infiltration characteristics of the 2 risk groups using a prognostic risk model; M0 macrophage scores were significantly higher in the high-risk group although M1 macrophages of the low-risk group were considerably higher than those in the control group. This observation is similar to the findings of Guo et al,
[
37
]
who reported M1 macrophages in the high-risk group that were notably lower than those in the low-risk group; the 3 genes screened in their prognostic model were PNKD, SLC16A8, and SLC5A8. However, few studies have explored the role of these genes in the development and progression of RCC. This may offer a promising direction for immunotherapy and prognostic prediction in patients with ccRCC. Feng et al
[
38
]
established a prognostic model for RCC based on immunogenomic analysis, while Yang et al
[
39
]
applied urine proteomics to explore diagnostic biomarkers and constructed a prognostic model for ccRCC. These findings hold significant clinical value. In conclusion, the use of artificial intelligence and multiomics data to build prognostic models may be a trend for future research.
Cancer immunotherapy has been a subject of exploration for several years. Immunotherapy has been used as an important treatment for cancer and has provided a new direction for the treatment of RCC. By enhancing and regulating the host immune system, immunotherapy aids in eliminating tumor cells, thereby reducing tumor recurrence risk and improving patient survival rates.
[
40
]
Targeted immune checkpoint therapy (cluster 1, APY:2021.286) was identified as an important research focus, and its appearance signals the onset of the immunotherapy era.
[
41
]
However, only a subset of patients benefit from this treatment,
[
42
]
and in many cases, resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) develops over time.
[
43
]
Therefore, it is essential to understand the underlying causes of ICI resistance. ICI resistance is often associated with the TME, where macrophages are one of the components of the TME,
[
44
]
and macrophage depletion can enhance the efficacy of ICIs.
[
45
]
Xiang et al
[
46
]
showed that TAM can directly inhibit effective T cells through various checkpoint molecules and immunosuppressive cytokines, suggesting a significant interaction between TAM and checkpoint inhibitors. Exploring macrophage-ICI interactions and ICI resistance holds promise for future research. In parallel, Chevrier et al
[
42
]
utilized mass spectrometry flow cytometry and a wide range of antibodies to perform an in-depth analysis of the immune infiltration of patients with ccRCC. Their study, published in Cell, identified 17 TAM phenotypes, shedding light on the immune profile of ccRCC. Future work should focus on determining the roles of these phenotypes and increasing the sample size in the cohorts. Addressing the challenges of immunotherapy, Yao et al
[
47
]
proposed that elucidating resistance mechanisms in RCC could guide the development of future therapies.
Figure
8
D depicts the increasing interest in the TME over the past 3 years, with numerous scholars focusing on the interactions between immune cells and tumors. Immune cells are closely related to tumor progression, particularly those in RCC. The composition of the TME is complex and includes various secreted factors, stromal cells, the extracellular matrix, and immune cells, with macrophages constituting a large portion.
[
48
]
While most studies predominantly focus on M2 macrophages, several characteristic markers of M1 macrophages warrant in-depth exploration. For example, macrophages isolated from RCC can produce pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interleukin-6, C-C motif chemokine ligand 2, and interleukin-1 beta
[
49
]
M1- and M2-type macrophages exhibit plasticity and can be transformed into each other under specific conditions.
[
50
]
Chai et al
[
51
]
found that absent in melanoma 2 prompts the transformation of M2 TAMs to the M1 phenotype by activating inflammasome signaling, exerting an interventional and therapeutic role in RCC. Similarly, the transition of macrophages from the M1 to the M2 phenotype may also affect the pathogenesis of tumor progression and metastasis,
[
52
,
53
]
which should be further explored.
Immune infiltration was at the forefront of research from 2020 to 2023, revealing that tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIICs) shaping an ecosystem within the TME regulate tumor progression and hold potential prognostic value.
[
54
]
TIICs are also likely to be effective targets for drug interventions to improve clinical outcomes.
[
55
]
RCC exhibits robust immune infiltration and strong immune characteristics,
[
56
]
with macrophages representing the most abundant TIIC subpopulation in RCC.
[
57
]
The term “biomarkers” appeared frequently during the keyword co-occurrence analysis. Su et al
[
58
]
showed that YTHDF2 might serve as a biomarker because its expression significantly correlated with macrophage infiltration levels and even more significantly with ccRCC immunoinfiltration. Thus, YTHDF2 potentially serves as an independent prognostic predictor in patients with ccRCC. These findings suggest the importance of understanding the immune infiltration status for individualizing immunotherapy, identifying biomarkers, and conducting detailed studies on their associated mechanisms. This approach holds promise for improving the prognosis of certain patients, and identifying new predictive biomarkers is an important research hotspot.
Based on the existing literature, we scrutinized the frontiers and research hotspots in this field, pointing to various avenues for future research. However, our study relied solely on the WoSCC database, and the possibility of incomplete datasets cannot be ruled out. Moreover, our analysis was restricted to articles and reviews, and the language was restricted to English, which may have introduced a selection bias.
5. Conclusion
RCC-macrophage research has become a focus, with China as a major producer in terms of quantity and the United States exerting substantial academic influence. In the fields of RCC and macrophages, the use of multiomics data and artificial intelligence in prognostic modeling shows great promise. Furthermore, our study found that the resistance mechanism of ICIs and the interaction between macrophages and ICIs will be research hotspots in the future.
