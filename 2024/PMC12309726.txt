INTRODUCTION
KRAS is the most frequently mutated oncogene in human cancers and is a critical driver of high-mortality cancers such as lung, colon, and pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
1
Drugging RAS has long been a priority for cancer therapeutics. Recent advances in drug design have led to the development of multiple strategies to inactivate KRAS, including direct inhibitors that target specific oncogenic mutations, several now approved by the US Food and Drug Administration.
2
Unfortunately, as with other targeted therapies, monotherapy with currently available KRAS inhibitors universally fails due to the emergence of drug-resistant clones that drive relapse of disease. Further understanding the biology of KRAS will be critical to identifying additional susceptibilities that can supplement the emerging KRAS treatment strategies.
KRAS
pre-mRNA is alternatively spliced to generate two mature mRNAs,
KRAS4A
and
KRAS4B
.
3
,
4
The two
KRAS
transcripts are identical save for an alternatively spliced fourth exon that encodes the 3′ portion of the α5 helix and the hypervariable region (HVR) that mediates membrane targeting. As a result, the two
KRAS
transcripts encode nearly identical G domains that precede distinct C-terminal membrane-targeting regions. Hotspot mutations in
KRAS
occur in exons 1 and 2, which are included in both transcripts, resulting in the production of two constitutively activated oncoproteins. Despite sharing oncogenicity, KRAS4B has been considered the primary product of the
KRAS
locus due to its increased expression relative to KRAS4A across a variety of tissues.
5
However, interest in KRAS4A has recently been heightened because several studies have demonstrated increased expression of this isoform in human colorectal adenocarcinomas.
4
,
6
,
7
The evolution of
KRAS4A
475 million years ago with the emergence of cartilaginous fish and its subsequent conservation strongly suggest non-overlapping functions of the two splice variants.
8
Indeed, KRAS4A but not KRAS4B interacts with hexokinase 1 (HK1)
9
and SIN1.
10
In the case of HK1, KRAS4A regulates the enzyme on the outer mitochondrial membrane as a consequence of its unique membrane-targeting properties.
9
RAS proteins are regulated and perform their functions through direct protein-protein interaction (PPI) with a variety of effectors, exchange factors, guanosine triphosphatase (GTPase)-activating proteins, processing enzymes, and chaperones.
11
Therefore, mapping the differential interactomes of RAS proteins can reveal novel properties and functions of these oncoproteins. A variety of approaches have been used to map differential interactomes, including affinity capture
9
and stable isotope labeling with amino acids in cell culture (SILAC) coupled with mass spectrometry.
12
,
13
Due to the covalent nature of the labeling, BirA-based proximity ligation allows for sensitive identification of PPIs that include transient and/or weak PPIs that would not be identified by alternative methods. In this work, we generated TurboID-KRAS-expressing cell lines and used these tools to perform proximity labeling and proteomic mapping of the differential interactomes of the KRAS splice variants. We identified baculoviral inhibitor of apoptosis (IAP) repeat-containing protein 6 (BIRC6), an essential member of the IAP protein family with a unique E2/E3 hybrid ubiquitin ligase domain, as a KRAS4A-specific interactor. We characterized the biochemical properties of the interaction and establish BIRC6 as a previously unidentified E3 ubiquitin ligase for KRAS4A.

Among the previously unreported protein interactors, BIRC6 was the most isoform-specific with ≥19 peptide-spectrum matches in each of three replicates for KRAS4A and none for KRAS4B. We therefore sought to study the interaction between KRAS4A and BIRC6, an extremely large dimeric protein with 4,857 amino acids in each protomer that functions as an E2/E3 ubiquitin ligase and limits apoptosis by ubiquitinating several pro-apoptotic proteins, including caspase-3 and caspase-7.

We validated the interaction between KRAS4A and BIRC6 by co-immunoprecipitation. GFP-tagged RAS proteins were expressed along with 3×FLAG-BIRC6 in HEK293 cells and immunoprecipitated. FLAG-BIRC6 was detected in immunoprecipitates of constitutively active (G12V) GFP-KRAS4A and, to a much lesser extent, GFP-NRAS, but not in those of GFP-KRAS4B and GFP-HRAS (
Figure 2A

Figure 2D

Figure 2D

To determine to what region of BIRC6 KRAS4A binds, we repeated the co-immunoprecipitation assay with full-length BIRC6 or N- or C-terminal fragments. Whereas both the N-terminal and full-length proteins interacted with FLAG-KRAS4A, the C-terminal fragment did not (
Figure 2E

Figure 2F

Figure 2F

in vitro

Figure S2

To validate the GTP dependence of the interaction and determine whether the interaction is direct, we employed recombinant proteins. We loaded rKRAS4A with guanosine diphosphate (GDP)βS or GTPγS, mixed them with rFLAG-BIRC6, and immunoprecipitated BIRC6 with anti-FLAG beads. rFLAG-BIRC6 captured KRAS4A loaded with GTPγS twice as efficiently relative to that loaded with GDPβS (

BIRC6 mono- and di-ubiquitinates KRAS4A on lysines 128 and 147
The interaction of BIRC6 with KRAS4A suggests that the latter could be a substrate for the ubiquitin ligase activity of BIRC6. To test this hypothesis, we utilized an
in vitro
ubiquitination assay using recombinant BIRC6, UBA6 (the E1 activating enzyme that charges BIRC6), and ubiquitin.
17
Upon addition of ATP, activated caspase-7 and LC3B, previously established BIRC6 substrates, were ubiquitinated, but recombinant pyruvate kinase M2 was not (
Figure 4A
). Using these conditions and recombinant KRAS4A or KRAS4B, we observed mono- and di-ubiquitination of the two KRAS proteins (
Figure 4B
). The lack of isoform specificity in this
in vitro
assay without cellular membranes demonstrates that the G domain of either splice variant is capable of interacting with BIRC6
in vitro
and that the isoform restriction in intact cells is driven by subcellular localization. We repeated this assay with previously characterized mutants of BIRC6
17
and found that, whereas Δhelix (amino acids [aa] 1,616–1,666 removed) and ΔUbl (aa 3,821–4,067 replaced with GSGSG linker) retained KRAS ubiquitination activity, a BIR mutant (C328S/C331S) did not (
Figure 4C
).
17
Thus, BIRC6 is a KRAS ubiquitin ligase, and its
in vitro
activity depends on the BIR domain that is known to bind substrates and, as we have shown, is sufficient for KRAS4A binding (
Figure 2F
).
Mono- and di-ubiquitinated proteins are difficult to detect and quantify
in vivo

To confirm KRAS4A ubiquitination by BIRC6 in intact cells, we co-transfected BIRC6-deficient HEK293 cells with HA-KRAS4A-12V and 8×His-Ub, with or without BIRC6, and analyzed the electrophoretic mobility of KRAS4A by anti-HA immunoblot before and after enrichment of ubiquitinated proteins with cobalt affinity beads. HA-KRAS4A-12V was observed at the expected molecular weight of 23 kDa. In addition, immunoreactive bands were observed at 30, 38, and 46 kDa, consistent with mono-, di-, and tri-ubiquitinated KRAS4A (
Figure 4D

Figure 4D

Figure 4D
). The most abundant species was di-ubiquitinated KRAS4A. To confirm the ability of endogenous BIRC6 to modify KRAS4A, we silenced BIRC6 in HEK293 cells with CRISPR-Cas9, expressed in these cells HA-KRAS4A-12V and 8×His-Ub, and analyzed KRAS4A modification after 8 M urea lysis and cobalt affinity capture of ubiquitinated proteins. When BIRC6 was silenced, we observed diminished modification of KRAS4A that could be rescued by re-expression of BIRC6 (
Figure 4E
). To establish the preference for KRAS4A over KRAS4B in ubiquitination by BIRC6 in intact cells and to avoid non-specific bands caused by the exogenous expression of 8×His-Ub, we expressed 6×His-KRAS4A-12V or 6×His-KRAS4B-12V in HEK293 cells with or without GFP-BIRC6, lysed the cells in 8 M urea, affinity captured the KRAS proteins on cobalt beads, and analyzed the modified proteins by immunoblots quantified by Li-Cor scanning. Overexpression of BIRC6 enhanced the ubiquitination of KRAS4A but not KRAS4B (
Figure 4F
). Importantly, this assay allowed us to determine the stoichiometry of KRAS ubiquitination. Nine percent of 6×His-KRAS4A-12V expressed in HEK293 cells was ubiquitinated by endogenous ligases, and this rose to 15% upon overexpression of BIRC6. Although 6×His-KRAS4B-12V was modified to a similar extent, there was no significant increase upon overexpression of BIRC6, suggesting involvement of other ligases. These data establish BIRC6 as an E3 ligase for KRAS4A and demonstrate that a physiologically relevant pool of the GTPase is modified.

To enrich for ubiquitinated proteins, we lysed with 8 M urea and performed affinity capture of 6×His-KRAS4A-12V using magnetic cobalt beads. BIRC6 is required for sustained GTP loading of KRAS4A and its maximal proliferative effect
Because ubiquitination has been previously reported to affect the GTP/GDP cycling of KRAS,
21
,
22
,
24
we measured the GTP loading of GFP-KRAS4A and GFP-KRAS4B, with or without BIRC6 knockdown (KD), in cells serum starved and then stimulated with epidermal growth factor (EGF). As expected, EGF induced GTP loading of both KRAS4A and KRAS4B, but whereas BIRC6 KD had no effect on EGF-induced GTP loading of KRAS4B, it significantly diminished GTP loading of KRAS4A after 15 min (
Figure 5A
), suggesting a more rapid return to baseline, consistent with enhanced GTPase-activating protein (GAP) activity. To determine the effect of BIRC6 on oncogenic KRAS, we studied the growth of 293 Flp-In T-REx cells that conditionally express either FLAG-KRAS4A-12V or FLAG-KRAS4B-12V upon doxycycline induction. The boost in proliferation upon doxycycline induction in cells expressing FLAG-KRAS4A-12V was eliminated in cells in which BIRC6 was silenced (
Figure 5B
). In contrast, silencing BIRC6 had no effect on the proliferation of cells induced to express FLAG-KRAS4B-12V. The greater capacity of oncogenic KRAS4A relative to KRAS4B to stimulate growth that we observed in this assay is consistent with previous reports.
25
Our data suggest that BIRC6 is required for the full proliferative effect of activated KRAS4A but not KRAS4B. Because FLAG-KRAS4A-12V is insensitive to GAP proteins, the mechanism whereby BIRC6 enhanced signaling is likely via a mechanism independent of GAP. Interestingly, silencing BIRC6 had an effect on mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling in response to EGF neither in cells expressing KRAS4A nor KRAS4B (
Figure S6
), suggesting dissociation of MAPK signing from sustained GTP loading of KRAS4A.
DISCUSSION
Using proximity labeling with an optimized biotin ligase, we labeled 14 proteins well known to interact with RAS, including direct interactors (ARAF, BRAF, CRAF, NF1, and RASA3) as well as indirect interactors (EGFR, MET, and ERBB2), demonstrating the sensitivity of the assay. The ten KRAS4B-specific interactors included six not previously reported as interacting with any RAS protein. Among the ten are three proteins involved in lipid transport (NPC1, STARD3, and OSBPL9), suggesting that KRAS4B differentially regulates this process. A greater number (24) of KRAS4A-specific interactors were identified, including ten proteins not previously reported to interact with any RAS protein. Among these was SIRT2, which, although not previously detected in PPI assays, has been reported to have deacetylating activity toward KRAS4A acetylated on lysines in the HVR.
26
There is surprisingly little overlap between the KRAS splice-variant-specific interactors we identified and those reported by Lin et al. using stable isotope labeling with amino acids in cell culture (SILAC) and affinity-purification mass spectrometry.
12
,
13
These two SILAC studies identified more than 4,000 RAS-interacting proteins with surprisingly little overlap between the two studies. The discrepancy may result, in part, from our use of a doxycycline-inducible system that allowed expression of tagged KRAS at endogenous levels.
The protein identified by our screen as the most stringent isoform-specific interactor was BIRC6, which was labeled by V5-TurboID-KRAS4A in each of three assays but not at all by V5-TurboID-KRAS4B. BIRC6 (also known as BRUCE and Apollon) is a member of a family of seven IAPs first identified in baculovirus and therefore designated baculovirus IAP repeat (BIR)-containing (BIRC) proteins.
27
–
29
BIRC proteins use their BIR domains to bind and inactivate caspases. Some IAPs contain ubiquitin association (UBA) domains that mediate interaction with ubiquitinated proteins, and most possess a RING domain that acts as an E3 ligase for caspases. BIRC6 does not contain a RING domain but possesses a ubiquitin-conjugating (UBC) domain that confers both E2 and E3 ligase activity. This activity allows for polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of pro-apoptotic proteins such as activated caspases and thereby keeps apoptosis in check. When the intrinsic or extrinsic pathways of apoptosis are activated, SMAC released from mitochondria binds to BIRC proteins with high affinity and frees caspases to mediate apoptosis.
17
,
30
–
32
Elucidation of this pathway spawned the development of SMAC mimetics as anticancer agents, several of which have entered clinical trials.
33
BIRC6 has been implicated in biological processes other than inhibiting apoptosis, including the regulation of autophagy
33
,
34
and cytokinesis.
19
BIRC6 is a huge protein of 4,857 aa. It is unique among BIRC proteins in being essential for mouse development
27
and in possessing, in addition to the defining N-terminal BIR domain stabilized by a WD40 propeller, a C-terminal UBC domain.
35
We and others recently solved the structure of BIRC6 using cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM), which revealed the mechanism whereby SMAC displaces caspases. BIRC6 exists as a megadalton, antiparallel homodimer that uses a series of armadillo repeats to mediate dimerization and form a horse-shoe-shaped scaffold that creates a large substrate binding pocket and supports several functional domains.
17
,
31
,
32
Our data indicate that KRAS4A binds to the BIR domain of BIRC6 and thereby becomes a substrate for mono- and di-ubiquitination. Many, but not all, substrates for BIRC6 ubiquitination bind to its BIR domain via a 4-aa N-terminal IAP-binding motif (IBM).
36
–
38
KRAS4A lacks an IBM. The GTP dependence of the interaction suggests that the effector-binding domain of KRAS4A binds to the BIR domain of BIRC6. Interestingly, HRAS was shown to bind to the E3 ligase β-transducin repeat-containing protein (β-TrCP) through its WD40 domain.
39
Our data do not rule out a contribution of the BIRC6 WD40 domain in binding to KRAS4A.
Isoform specificity of RAS protein interactions and function are driven by differential subcellular localization, which is mediated by the various HVRs. For example, in intact cells, HK1 exclusively binds KRAS4A because this is the only RAS isoform that visits the outer mitochondrial membrane where HK1 resides.
9
In vitro
, in the absence of membranes, all RAS isoforms engage HK1 via their G domains.
9
These features were also evident in the interaction between BIRC6 and KRAS4A, which was exquisitely isoform specific in proximity labeling and co-immunoprecipitation but not
in vitro
in ubiquitination assays. This suggests that the subcellular location of the interaction with BIRC6 is unique to KRAS4A. BIRC6 has been reported to exist on the Golgi apparatus
18
as well as on lysosomes
34
and the cytokinetic midbody.
19
Using indirect immunofluorescence to detect endogenous BIRC6, we found the protein on the
cis
and
trans
Golgi as well as on small, diffuse cytoplasmic vesicles that were neither endosomes, lysosomes, nor peroxisomes. How this giant protein associates with membranes is unknown, and the recent cryo-EM structures were uninformative in this regard. Although at 37°C we observed GFP-KRAS4A predominantly on the plasma membrane as we have previously reported,
4
when vesicular transport was slowed by cooling the cells, KRAS4A but not KRAS4B accumulated on the Golgi. Thus, KRAS4A transits the Golgi, where it encounters BIRC6. The fact that neither NRAS nor HRAS, which also transit the Golgi, were able to efficiently pull down BIRC6 by co-immunoprecipitation suggests that a subdomain of the Golgi is the site of interaction.
RABGEF1
40
and β-TrCP
39
have been reported to have ubiquitin ligase activity toward HRAS. NEDD4 can polyubiquitinate ectopically expressed KRAS4B.
41
LZTR1/CUL3 has been reported to mono- and di-ubiquitinate HRAS
42
and polyubiquitinate KRAS4A.
43
However, others have found LZTR1/CUL3 activity toward RIT1 but not RAS.
44
Our demonstration of KRAS4A mono- and di-ubiquitination by BIRC6 identifies an E3 ligase that can modify KRAS in this fashion; however, our data suggest that other ubiquitin ligases for KRAS exist because silencing BIRC6 could not eliminate modification.
Ubiquitination can alter RAS protein function in at least three ways: subcellular localization, PPI, and degradation.
45
Despite the various reports of E3 ligases active against overexpressed RAS proteins, there is little evidence of regulation of endogenous RAS levels by the ubiquitin-proteasome system. In contrast, mono- and di-ubiquitination of both HRAS and KRAS are well documented. In the case of HRAS, di-ubiquitination increases association of the GTPase with endosomes.
46
The mechanism by which mono-ubiquitination affects RAS function is site specific.
47
,
48
We detected BIRC6-dependent ubiquitination of KRAS4A on K128 and K147. Moreover, we detected 15% of the total pool of KRAS4A modified in this manner, a fraction likely to be sufficient to impact signaling.
The 128
th
codon is lysine in KRAS and NRAS, but arginine in HRAS. Based on publicly available proteomic databases, ubiquitination of K128 is the most prevalent post-translational modification of KRAS outside its HVR.
22
Lysine 128 lies in the α4 helix on the so-called allosteric face of the molecule, diametrically opposite the switch region that engages effectors (
Figure S5C
). At least one mutation in this region is associated with a RASopathy, suggesting that modification at this site can result in gain of function.
49
,
50
Sablina and colleagues recently reported that K>R substitutions at codon 128 of KRAS and NRAS diminished the binding of RASA1 and NF1 GAP proteins.
22
Molecular dynamic modeling predicted that mono-ubiquitination at K128 created an additional binding interface for these GAPs.
22
Because cells and tumors grew faster with KRASG12D-K128R than KRASG12D, the authors concluded that mono-ubiquitination at K128 is a loss-of-function modification mediated by enhanced GAP binding.
22
However, K>R substitutions can have effects other than blocking ubiquitination such that confirmation of the signaling consequences of ubiquitination of K128 await
in vitro
analysis analogous to that applied to KRAS modified at K147.
24
Lysine 147 resides in the G5 region of KRAS that participates in nucleotide binding. Ubiquitination of K147 was reported to enhance GTP loading and interaction with effectors as a consequence of faster GDP/GTP cycling.
21
Subsequent analysis of
in vitro
modified KRAS demonstrated that mono-ubiquitination at lysine 147 blocked GAP-enhanced GTP hydrolysis by KRAS.
24
Thus, regardless of which mechanism comes into play, ubiquitination of lysine 147 of KRAS is a gain-of-function modification analogous to class I oncogenic mutations.
50
Our identification of K128 and K147 as the sites of modification suggests that ubiquitination of KRAS4A by BIRC6 could be a gain- or loss-of-function modification, depending on which of the two sites predominate. Our observation that BIRC6 is required for sustained GTP loading of KRAS4A (
Figure 5A
) is consistent with a gain of function mediated by diminished GAP activity and is therefore consistent with ubiquitination of K147. Because oncogenic KRAS mutants are insensitive to GAPs, the requirement for BIRC6 for optimal KRAS4A-12V-stimulated proliferation indicates that other, GAP-independent, mechanisms are also in play. Our failure to detect altered MAPK signaling in KRAS4A-expressing cells knocked down for BIRC6 dissociates sustained GTP loading from signaling and will require further study.
The primary function of BIRC6, like all IAP proteins, is to keep low-level caspase activity in check, thereby preventing unwanted apoptosis, but to also act as an SMAC-regulated switch that allows apoptosis to proceed once the intrinsic mitochondrial pathway is activated. Activated RAS proteins promote cell survival through a variety of mechanisms, including inhibiting apoptosis.
51
KRAS4A has the unique function of interacting with HK1 on the outer mitochondrial membrane.
9
It is well established that HK1 on mitochondria promotes retrotranslocation of BAK and BAX and thereby protects cells from the intrinsic pathway of apoptosis.
52
,
53
Thus, enhanced activation of KRAS4A by mono- and di-ubiquitination by BIRC6 suggests crosstalk between two systems that regulate programmed cell death. Inhibiting IAP proteins is an attractive approach to anticancer drugs,
54
and the new structures of BIRC6 may be exploited to develop better inhibitors. Such drugs may also block ubiquitination of KRAS4A and thereby diversify their mechanism of action.
Limitations of the study
As with all proximity labeling screens, there are undoubtedly both false positives and false negatives among the proteins we identified, or failed to identify, as interacting differentially with the two splice variants of KRAS. Although our data that support the ubiquitination of KRAS4A by BIRC6 are very strong, low expression of KRAS4A in many cultured cells and lack of isoform-specific immunoprecipitating antibodies have precluded a demonstration of ubiquitination of endogenous KRAS4A. The effects of ubiquitination of KRAS4A on signaling are uncertain. Determining the functional consequences of mono- or di-ubiquitination of a signaling molecule such as KRAS is difficult because the K>R substitutions typically used to eliminate modification can have functional consequences unrelated to ubiquitination, because E3 ligases are promiscuous with regard to the lysines modified and because ubiquitin ligases often have multiple substrates that can impact a biological pathway. The literature supports both gain and loss of function of KRAS as a consequence of mono- and di-ubiquitination and, although our data suggest that modification by BIRC6 is a gain of function for KRAS4A, further studies will be required to verify this feature and thereby determine whether BIRC6 is a target for anti-KRAS drug discovery.
