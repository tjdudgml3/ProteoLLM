Background
Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer death worldwide, with small cell lung cancer (SCLC) accounting for approximately 15% of all lung cancer diagnoses and causing more than 200,000 deaths per year [
1
]. SCLC is a highly metastatic and recalcitrant malignant disease, remaining extraordinarily poor prognosis with a 5-year survival rate of less than 7% [
1
]. Although surgical resection has been shown to potentially offer improved long-term outcomes in patients with localized SCLC confined to the lung, it is applicable to only a small fraction of patients since the vast majority of patients present with metastatic disease at diagnosis when curative-intent multimodality therapy is no longer indicated [
2
].
Tissue-destructive metastasis is one of the main causes of morbidity and mortality for cancer patients, which seriously impairs organ function [
3
]. Despite the widespread application of adjuvant chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy following surgery, the majority of patients with SCLC will still experience recurrence and metastases [
4
]. Accumulating evidence suggested that metastasis could be an extremely early event, with cancer cells dissemination occurring in the phase of disease initiation [
3
]. For SCLC, rapid proliferation, apoptotic imbalance, high vascularity, strong predilection for early metastasis and the high mutational burden caused by cigarette smoking exposure support a model in which the metastatic process begins during the early tumorigenesis [
1
,
5
]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to explore the mechanisms of SCLC metastasis and to find biomarkers for identifying patients who are prone to experience early disease recurrence and metastases.
Nonetheless, tumor metastasis is widely recognized as an inefficient process, as the vast majority of cancer cells failled to survive the many steps of the metastatic cascade and form distant dissemination [
6
]. Furthermore, even though harboring large primary tumors, some SCLC mouse models had no detectable metastases or even disseminated tumor cells after 7–11 months of tumor growth, indicating that these tumors may be deficient in invasion and migration [
7
]. Primary tumor cells require a great number of traits to succeed in all the steps of metastatic cascade [
3
]. Some of these traits originate from the primary tumor and are the result of genetic alterations that silence tumor suppressor genes or activate oncogenes, enabling uncontrolled cell proliferation, survival, invasion and migration [
8
]. However, few metastasis-specific genetic alterations were identified when comparing metastatic tumors and corresponding primary tumors [
9
]. Although an almost universal inactivation of the
TP53
and
RB1
tumor suppressor genes, as well as
NOTCH
family tumor suppressors inactivation and
MYC
family oncogenes amplification, no driver alterations have yet been distinguished from passengers [
5
,
10
]. While, metastatic tumor cells usually exhibit a unique gene expression signiature [
11
]. It has been reported that increased expression of the transcription factor NFIB and the neuronal lineage markers ASCL1 and NEUROD1 were required to the acquisition of metastatic capability in SCLC [
12
–
14
]. In addition,
YAP
silencing induced by
RB1
mutation was also identified as a pro-metastatic switch [
15
].
Overall, the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying the ability of SCLC to metastasize remain relatively poorly understood. In addition to cancer cell intrinsically genetic and transcriptional mechanisms, interactions between different cell populations in tumor microenvironment may also influence the metastasis of SCLC. Recent proteomic studies in cell lines and human tissue specimens have provided significant insight into the biological understanding of SCLC [
16
,
17
]. Here, we performed an unbiased proteomic analysis of archival tumor specimens to comprehensively explore potential mechanisms of SCLC metastasis and recurrence. This study revealed the critical role of lipid metabolism, especially phospholipid metabolism, in impeding SCLC metastasis and further identified the phospholipase A2 group IIA (PLA2G2A), which also participates in innate immune response, as an important predictive biomarker for SCLC recurrence.
Methods
Study cohort
All primary tumor samples were retrospectively obtained from patients diagnosed with SCLC who underwent surgical resection during September, 2013 to September, 2022 at Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and had received no previous treatment for their disease, including chemotherapy or radiotherapy. Paired lymph node samples with histologically confirmed SCLC metastases were collected from the same patient during tumor resection. In addition, brain metastatic samples were retrospectively obtained from SCLC patients who underwent palliative surgery, with no history of chemotherapy or radiotherapy within the past 6 months.
Histological sections obtained from each sample were reviewed by an experienced pathologist to confirm the tumor percentage according to cell nuclei. The clinicopathological parameters were collected and summarized, including age, gender, smoking status, date of surgical resection, tumor site, tumor size, histologic type, TNM staging, Veterans Administration Lung Study Group staging and so on. This study was approved by the Ethics Commitee of National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences & Peking Union Medical College (23/032-3771). Written informed consent was exempted by the Institutional Review Board.
Sample preparation
Formalin-fixed parrafin embedded (FFPE) samples were collected in 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes, then deparaffinized with 1 mL environmental dewaxing dip wax transparentize solution (Leagene, P/N: DZ2011) three times, washed with 1 mL percutaneous ethanol twice, and 1 mL 75% ethanol twice. After deparaffinization, sections were placed in grind tube, then homogenized in ice-cold lysis buffer (6M Guanidine Hydrochloride, 50 mM Tris–HCl, 0.5% protease cocktail [v/v], 1% phosphatase inhibitors [v/v]). The remaining debris was removed by centrifugation at 15,000
g
at 4 ℃ for 10 min. The supernatant was collected and incubated at 90 ℃ for 3 h, then protein concentration was determined with BCA kit (Thermo Scientific
™
, P/N: 23,225) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For digestion, proteins were reduced with 1 M DTT for 1 h at room temperature and alkylated with 1 M IAA for 1 h at room temperature in darkness. The protein solution incubated with SP3 beads (Cytiva, P/N: 45152105050250, 65152105050250) for 15 min at room temperature, then trypsin (Promega, P/N: V1062) was added at 1:50 trypsin-to-protein mass ratio for 16 h at 37 ℃ for on beads digestion. After trypsin digestion, peptide concentration was determined with BCA kit (Thermo Scientific
™
, P/N: 23225) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Then peptide was desalted by ziptip column (Merck millipore, P/N: ZTC18S960), and dried by vacuum centrifuging.
Mass spectrometry analysis
All Liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) experiments were performed on a Orbitrap Exploris 240 (Thermo Scientific) equipped with an Vanquish_Neo UPLC system (Thermo Scientific). Peptides were loaded onto a 75 mm id × 2 cm trap column (Acclaim PepMap 100, Thermo Scientific), then separated onto a 75 mm id × 20 cm C18 column packed with reversed phase silica (Reprosil-Pur 120 C18-AQ, 1.9 μm, Dr. Maisch GmbH). The peptides bounded on the column were eluted with a 120-min linear gradient. The solvent A consisted of 0.1% formic acid in water solution and the solvent B consisted of 80% acetonitrile and 0.1% formic acid. The segmented gradient was 5.8–6.3% B, 2 min; 6.3–23% B, 74 min; 23–36% B, 35 min; 36–55% B, 1.5 min; 55–99% B, 0.5 min; 99% B, 7 min at a constant flow rate of 400 nL/min.
The MS analysis was performed with Orbitrap Exploris 240 mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific). With the data-independent acquisition (DIA) mode, the MS scans were acquired at a high resolution 120,000 (m/z 200) across the mass range of 400–1100 m/z. The AGC target value was 3.00E + 06 with a maximum injection time of 22 ms. The MS/MS scans were acquired with forty variable overlapping precursor isolation windows and centered at: 405, 415, 424, 432, 440, 448, 456, 464, 472, 480, 488, 496, 504, 512, 520, 528, 536, 544, 552, 560, 568, 576, 584.5, 593.5, 602.5, 611.5, 620.5, 629.5, 638.5, 647.5, 656.5, 665.5, 674.5, 683.5, 687.5, 702.5, 717.5, 732.5, 747.5, 762.5, 777.5, 792.5, 807.5, 822.5, 837.5, 852.5, 867.5, 882.5, 905, 935 and 965 m/z. Programed normalized collision energy was set to 25%, 27% and 30%. The AGC target value was 1.00E + 06 with a maximum injection time of 40 ms in MS2. For nano electrospray ion source setting, the spray voltage was 1.8 kV; no sheath gas flow; the heated capillary temperature was 310 ℃.
Database search
The DIA data were processed using DIA-NN (version 1.8) software [
18
]. Protein sequence database was comprised of reviewed human preotome (uniport _UP000005640_9606.fasta, 20588 entries, download date: December 2, 2021) and common contamination proteins [
19
]. Trypsin (full) was specified as proteolytic enzyme allowing up to 2 missed cleavages. The mass tolerance for both precursor and product ions was set to 20 ppm. Carbamidomethyl on Cys was specified as fixed modification, oxidation on methylation and acetylation on protein N-term were specified as variable modifications. The range of peptide length was set from 7 to 50. The run-specific false discovery rate (FDR) of identifications at both peptide and protein level were estimated by Percolator [
20
]. For DIA-NN, all the proteome data analyses were performed using a project-specific DIA spectral library building from the peptides of all the MS runs. Precursor FDR was adjusted to < 1%. All the other parameters in DIA-NN were set to default values.
Preprocessing of proteomic datasets
For each protein, the number of unique peptides was required as above 1. The protein quantity was calculated as summed intensities of top three peptides, that were quantified in over half of samples. Then we employed python (version 3.10.4) for quantification data preprocessing,including median normalization and missing value imputation which was the half minimum method to fill. To evaluate the quality of standardized data, we conducted several analyses, which included principal component analysis (PCA), Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), clustering analysis, and sample correlation analysis. The PCA analysis utilized the prcomp function for statistical analysis. Hierarchical cluster analysis was performed using the hierarchical cluster algorithm, and sample correlation analysis utilized the Pearson’s correlation coefficient method.
Differentially protein analysis
To extract differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) between groups, Wilcoxon rank-sum test was performed on DIA-based global proteomic data. Multiple testing correction with the Benjamini–Hochberg method was applied to calculate the FDR. Proteins with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR < 0.01 and log2 transformed fold change (log2FC) > 1 were considered to have a significantly differential expression between groups.
Functional enrichment analysis
Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and over-representation analysis (ORA) of gene ontology (GO) terms were performed for pathway enrichment analysis on the background of quantified proteins using clusterProfiler (version 4.10.0) [
21
] and org.Hs.eg.db (version 3.18.0). Terms were considered significant with a Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted
p
< 0.05. Proteomaps were constructed based on Kyoto Encyclopedia Genes and Genomes (KEGG) annotations using a online program based on the log2FC values of significantly up- or down-regulated proteins [
22
]. DEPs were assessed for interaction relationships using STRING database (version 12.0) [
23
]. Confidence relationships (combined score > 0.4) were extracted and visualized in Cytoscape [
24
] and colored according to changes in expression and participated pathways. Only connected nodes were shown.
Weighted gene co-expression network analysis
Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was performed on the dataset of all 85 primary samples using R package WGCNA [
25
]. To group proteins into modules, a dissimilarity measure (dissTOM = 1—TOM) was used to perform average linkage hierarchical clustering with the following settings: power = 7, TOM type = unsigned, minimum module size = 30, merge cut height = 0.4. Module eigengene (ME), a summary measure for each gene module, was calculated as the first principal component of the scaled module expression profiles. The correlation between the MEs and clinicalpathological parameters was evaluated using Spearman's correlation coefficient analysis.
Estimation of immune infiltration
Single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) [
26
] was performed to accurately determine the absolute abundance of 27 immune cell populations, including activated B cell, activated CD4 T cell, activated CD8 T cell, activated dendritic cell, CD56bright natural killer cell, CD56dim natural killer cell, central memory CD4 T cell, central memory CD8 T cell, effector memory CD4 T cell, effector memory CD8 T cell, eosinophil, gamma delta T cell, immature B cell, immature dendritic cell, macrophage, mast cell, myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSC), memory B cell, monocyte, natural killer cell, natural killer T cell, neutrophil, plasmacytoid dendritic cell, regulatory T cell, T follicular helper cell, type 1 T helper cell, type 17 T helper cell, type 2 T helper cell. The immune cell feature genes were obtained from the publication of Charoentong et al. [
27
].
Machine learning approach for the identification of signature proteins
We utilized recursive feature elimination algorithm in Perseus (version 2.0.10.0) [
28
] to determine the signature protein combinations. For classification, the support vector machine (SVM) was performed with radial basis function kernel. For feature ranking, ANOVA was performed with S0 of 0.01. To avoid overfitting, feature selection was embedded in a cross-validation procedure (random sampling using 85% of the samples for training and the other 15% for testing) and was repeated 250 times. Finally, the minimal signature was selected based on the diagnostic performance (determined according to the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) for the combination of the top-ranked proteins.
Transcription factor enrichment analysis
Transcription factor enrichment analysis was performed using the ChIP-X Enrichment Analysis Version 3, which predicted the acticity of specific transcription factors according to the signature of differentially expressed proteins based on human and mouse data from 6 annotated transcription factor target libraries, including ENCODE, ReMap, ARCHS4, GTEx, Enrichr, and literature curation [
29
]. Then, cluster grams were plotted to show the regulation relationship between differentially expressed proteins and the transcription factors that regulate them. For a slim presentation, only proteins in the top 100 signature proteins ranked by the machine learning approch were displyed in the cluster grams.
Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence
4 um consecutive FFPE sections were mounted on glass slides and dried overnight at room temperature, followed by deparaffinization, antigen retrieval and blockade of endogenous peroxidase. Then, slides were stained with PLA2G2A antibody (1:50, Abcam, ab23705), CD8 antibody (ready to use antibody, Abcarta, PA577), CD15 antibody (ready to use antibody, Zsbio, ZM-0037), CD38 antibody (ready to use antibody, Zsbio, ZM-0422), CD68 antibody (ready to use antibody, Roche, KP-1), ASCL1 antibody (1:100, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-374104), NEUROD1 antibody (1:300, Abcam, ab60704), POU2F3 antibody (1:100, Cell Signaling Technology, #36,135) and YAP1 antibody (1:80, Abcam, ab52771), respectively. Tissue sections were processed by a fully automated protocol on the Roche BenchMark ULTRA system (Roche Diagnostics Inc., USA). Immunostained slides were scanned using the NanoZoomer S210 digital slide scanner (Hamamatsu Photonics Inc., Japan). Staining quantification was performed using the QuPath software (version 0.5.0) [
30
]. For PLA2G2A, Immunostained slides were scored using the H-score: the sum of intensity of positive staining × corresponding staining intensity cell percentage.
Statistical analysis
Data processing and statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.2.1). All statistical tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was all defined as
p
< 0.05 unless otherwise indicated. Fisher’s exact test was adopted for the comparison of categorical variables versus categorical variables. Wilcoxon test or Kruskal–Wallis test was adopted for the comparison of continuous variables versus categorical variables. Pearson’s or Spearman’s correlation coefficient was used to measure the correlation between continuous variables. Multiple comparisons were adjusted by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. The optimal cutpoint for survival analyses was determined using the survminer (version 0.4.9) R package. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were then constructed using survival R package and the log-rank test was used to determine the differences between different groups based on the optimal cutpoint. The univariate Cox regression analysis was used to identify which variable is significantly associated with disease-free survival (DFS). And the prognostic factors with
p
< 0.1 were all included in multivariable Cox regression analysis. Hazards ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated and forestplot R package was used to provide the visualization of each factor. DFS was defined as the time from surgical resection to disease recurrence or death from any reason, whichever occurred first. More details of statistical analyses can be found in figure legends.
Results
Proteomic characterization of SCLC
To gain insight into biological mechanisms associated with disease recurrence after surgery and identify candidate markers predictive of metastasis, mass spectrometry based proteomics was performed on 120 SCLC tumor samples from surgical resection. Of these tumor samples, 90 are primary tumors from treatment-naive limited stage or extensive stage SCLC patients, 15 are patient-matched lymph node metastatic tumors, and 15 are brain metastatic tumors from extensive stage SCLC patients. The clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in Fig.

LC–MS/MS analysis was conducted based on the DIA method for all tumor samples. Overall, 6,304 non-redundant proteins were quantified and the intensities of quantitative proteins spanned 6 orders of magnitude (Fig.

Histological assessment of hematoxylin and eosin stained sections indicated an average percentage of 65% epithelial cancer cells, 10% stroma, 0% necrosis, and 15% adjacent normal tissues (Fig. S1A and S1B). On the basis of this assessment, a certain proportion of samples showed no more than 50% epithelial cancer cells in the histological assessment (Fig. S1A and S1B). Therefore, we performed unsupervised clustering and PCA to confirm whether the data suffered from any clustering bias based on cancer cell percentages. As a result, 9 samples with lower percentage (< 30%) of epithelial cancer cells, including 5 primary tumors and 4 brain metastatic tumors, were clustered together and far away from other tumors based on the euclidean distance matrix (Fig.
1

Unsupervised proteomic analysis of SCLC primaries and metastases identifies biologically distinct clusters
Hierarchical clustering based on the maximum distance matrix were performed again on the retained 111 samples. As a result, 31 primary tumors were segregated exclusively as cluster A, while the other 54 primary tumors were clustered with all 26 metastatic tumors as cluster B, C, or D (Fig.
2
A). This observation was supported by the results of the UMAP dimension reduction (Fig.
2

In light of these findings, we assigned primary tumors in cluster A, primary tumors in other clusters (cluster B, C and D), and metastatic tumors in other clusters (cluster B, C and D) to non-potential metastatic (NMp) group, potential metastatic (Mp) group, and metastatic (M) group, respectively (Fig.
2

It was worthy of noting that most (11/15) of the patient-matched primary tumors were grouped together with their patient-matched lymph node metastatic tumors in the Mp group, while the other four primary tumors in the NMp group may indicate potential transformed protein expression profiling compared with their patient-matched lymph node metastatic tumors. In addition, the only sample from extensive stage SCLC patient was in the Mp group.

The co-expression network consisted of 14 protein modules, the size of which ranged from 32 to 1507 proteins (Fig.

Identification of signature proteins for SCLC metastasis
To generate a minimal protein signature for classification of different groups and identify key molecules involved in regulating SCLC metastasis. A recursive feature elimination algorithm based on a SVM model and a cross-validation procedure was applied for the 85 primary tumors to select protein biomarkers. This machine learning approach resulted in a signature protein combination containing the following ten proteins: ACAN, HAPLN1, LYZ, MFGE8, LECT2, SLPI, PLA2G2A, FMOD, COL10A1 and MUC5B; all of those were higher in the NMp group than in the Mp group and the M group (Fig.
5
A,
B
; Table S4A). The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve of this ten-protein signature achieved an area under the curve (AUC) value of 0.990 (95% CI 0.976–1) in the primary tumor dataset (NMp vs. Mp), an AUC value of 1 (95% CI 1–1) in the metastatic tumor dataset (NMp vs. M), and an AUC value of 0.981 (95% CI 0.959–1) in the whole dataset (primary vs. metastatic; Fig.
5
C). The signature proteins provided excellent separation between the groups according to the first component in the PCA (Fig.
5
D). Additionally, the unsupervised clustering of these protein combinations showed relatively high accuracy in the classification of the NMp, the Mp and the M groups (Fig.
5
E). The discrimination performance of each biomarker for different groups was shown in Fig. S5. According to these findings, this protein combination may have clinical value in predicting possible metastasis.

The proteomic pathway enrichment and WGCNA results correlated the lipid metabolism and immune response with SCLC metastasis. Thus, we focused on the phospholipase PLA2G2A, which also participates in innate immune response [

Given that bulk proteomic analyses might have averaged expression patterns from different histological tissues and cell populations, we examined the in situ expression of PLA2G2A on the tissue level by immunohistochemistry staining on consecutive slides. Immunohistochemistry showed staining of PLA2G2A both on epithelial cancer cells and stroma cells (Fig.

In multivariable Cox regression analysis, both lack of stroma in tumor and PLA2G2A low expression in stroma remained significantly associated with inferior DFS in a model adjusting for smoking history and adjuvant radiotherapy (hazard ratio > 1;

Discussion
SCLC is an aggressively metastatic cancer characterized by high degree of heterogeneity and plasticity. Thus, it has become much clinically valuable to develop effective therapies preventing metastatic spread of SCLC. Not only is there a need to protect patients from disease recurrence and developing metastases, but to improve treatment strategy for metastatic SCLC and early cancer detection methods. The identification of therapeutically targetable oncogenic driver alterations and an increase in knowledge of disease biology has dramatically improved survival and decreased mortality in non-small cell lung cancer [
35
]. In contrast, the decrease in SCLC mortality has been entirely explained by declined incidence over recent decades, with no improvement in survival [
35
]. This result has a close relation with the limited therapeutic advances for SCLC during the same period. Consequently, a greater understanding of the underlying biology mechanisms of metastatic SCLC and translating the knowledge into better therapies are urgently required. Recent advances in single cell sequencing and liquid biopsy have uncovered transcriptional and epigenetic changes as well as cell fate plasticity that promote metastatic potential and immunosuppression during metastatic growth [
36
,
37
]. These novel understandings of this disease could be critical steps in addressing preventive and therapeutic challenges in SCLC metastases.
In this study, we collected surgical tumor samples from both primary and metastatic tumors of SCLC patients. Protein-level analysis was performed to give insight into potential mechanisms and novel biomarkers for the prediction of SCLC recurrence and metastasis. Here, we observed two groups of primary tumors with different protein expression profiles, one of which clustered with lymph node and brain metastatic tumors. Thus, we assigned the primary SCLC samples to NMp group and Mp group, respectively, according to their “metastatic potential”. In comparison to the NMp group, we identified 74 downregulated and 72 upregulated proteins in the Mp group, while 368 downregulated and 195 upregulated proteins in the M group. Additionally, functional enrichment analyses confirmed that the Mp and the M groups were dominated by similar categories, mainly related to chromosome, cell cycle, DNA replication and cell division. In contrast, the NMp group in both comparisons were enriched for cell adhesion, extracellular exosome, immune response and lipid metabolism. Furthermore, there was a strong correlation between the two comparisons based on the 2D enrichment analysis, indicating that the protein profile of Mp group has the ability to reflect the high metastatic potential of SCLC, on behalf of metastatic tumors in M group.
Moreover, we constructed 14 protein modules using WGCNA and classified both module eigengenes and clinicopathological phenotypes into two clusters using hierarchical clustering analysis. The module clusters were significantly correlated with the clinical phenotype clusters, suggesting potential distinct biological mechanisms in each cluster. GO enrichment analysis further demonstrated immune response and lipid metabolism pathways in NMp group related cluster, while chromosome, cell cycle and cell division pathways in Mp group related cluster. The recent multiomic study of SCLC also revealed the changes of lipid metabolism in SCLC [
17
]. Therefore, we retrieved plasma lipid data of SCLC patients and found that the levels of all 7 plasma lipids were associated with DFS, in which patients with higher levels of CHOL and LDL-CHOL had a shorter DFS. Both CHOL and LDL-CHOL were negatively associated with MEred and MEturquoise, which was correlated with local recurrence and early stage disease, respectively. However, the positive correlation of CHOL and MEblack perhaps due to the higher levels of both CHOL level and MEblack value in female patients. What’s more, HDL-CHO and LDL-CHO were independent prognostic factors in multivariable Cox regression analysis, indicating the significance of lipid metabolism in the control of SCLC metastasis. In previous studies, high ApoA1 decreased lung cancer risk in both gender, whereas ApoB was associated with an increased lung cancer risk [
38
]. In addition, LDL-CHO was demonstrated to promote proliferation, metastasis and angiogenesis of cancer cells in breast cancer, anaplastic thyroid cells, prostate cancer and ovarian cancer, while HDL-CHO inhibited the proliferation of cancer cells through reduction of cholesterol content [
39
]. Patients with higher HDL-CHO had reduced risk of death and disease relapse compared with lower HDL-CHO in a pan-cancer meta-analysis [
40
].
Furthermore, we applied a SVM-based machine-learning approach to identify a biomarker combination of ten proteins that provided accurate predictions of different sample groups. High degree of sensitivity and specificity were observed in each biomarker. All of these biomarkers showed a significantly favourable correlation with the infiltration levels of multiple antitumor immune cells, while negative correlation with suppressive immune cells. Given the close relationship between anti-tumor immunity and lipid metabolism in SCLC according to prior findings, we focused on the only phospholipase PLA2G2A, which simutaneously involved in phospholipid metabolism and innate immune response. PLA2G2A was expressed in cancer-associated fibroblast in breast cancer and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, promoting immune cell infiltration [
41
,
42
]. And it has been demonstrated that increased proliferation and cancer stemness of gallbladder carcinoma was correlated with overexpression of PLA2G2A in gallbladder cancer cells [
43
]. However, in this study, Kaplan–Meier curves and Cox regression analysis indicated that high expression of PLA2G2A in stroma strongly predicted a decreased risk of disease recurrence for limited stage SCLC patients. And the results of immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence suggested that B cells may contribut to most of the PLA2G2A expression in the stroma, or as the main type of immune cells interact with PLA2G2A positive stromal cells.
Taken together, our work highlighted the critical significance of lipid metabolism on the disease recurrence and metastasis of SCLC. Further investigations were needed to gain insights into the role of lipid metabolism in SCLC anti-tumor immunity. This study also indicated the importance of proteomic analysis in the SCLC biological research, which integrates the effect of the cellular microenvironment into the genetic information. Importantly, these findings may provide a valuable glimpse into the mechanisms of resistance to immunotherapy and considerations for the management strategies employed in the long-term management of SCLC.
