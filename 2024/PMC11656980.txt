Introduction
Chlorophyll is an important pigment in plant photosynthesis [
1
], and a special trait mutation in higher plants has been observed in various crops such as rice [
2
], corn [
3
], tobacco [
4
], wheat [
5
], and tea [
6
]. Chlorophyll deficiency often affects the photosynthetic efficiency of mutants and can lead to weakened growth, reduced crop yield, and even plant death in severe cases, and previously considered a harmful mutation [
7
,
8
]. However, chlorophyll-deficient mutants are ideal materials for studying plant photosynthesis, chlorophyll synthesis, degradation [
9
,
10
], chloroplast development [
11
], hormone physiology [
12
], possess excellent germplasm and marker traits, and can be used for the breeding of superior varieties. The chlorophyll content of the chlorophyll b reducing mutant of rice “Zhenhui 249” was significantly decreased, but its ratio of chlorophyll a to chlorophyll b and saturation light intensity are high, the ratio between the duration of high photosynthetic rate and leaf lifespan is large, and the photosynthetic ability is strong. These traits provide this variety with high application value in the context of heterosis [
13
]. Anji White Tea is a temperature-sensitive mutant with a stage-wise whitening effect. The total amount of amino acids in the whitening stage is much higher than that in the control variety, and it possesses high nutritional and economic value [
14
]. The tobacco chlorophyll-deficient variant Burley tobacco possesses a chlorophyll content of only one-third of that of normal green varieties, exhibits a strong aroma, possesses strong growth potential, and has developed into a new type of tobacco.
The mechanism of leaf colour mutation is complex and is typically caused by mutations in genes related to chlorophyll synthesis metabolism or chloroplast development that hinder chlorophyll synthesis, chloroplast development, chlorophyll degradation, cytoplasmic nuclear signal transduction pathways, haem feedback regulation disorder, chloroplast-encoded protein synthesis obstruction, and photosystem abnormalities [
12
,
15
–
19
], ultimately leading to chlorophyll deficiency and leaf colour variation. If a mutation occurs in the
OsChlH
gene encoding the Arabidopsis Mg-chelatase H subunit on chromosome 3 of rice, it will hinder the synthesis of chlorophyll in the mutant, and the rice leaves will become white during the seedling stage [
20
]. The down regulation of the expression levels of
rbcL
,
ndhB
, and
ndhD
in the st13 rice mutant leads to imbalanced energy metabolism within chloroplasts, impaired chloroplast development, and reduced pigment content [
21
]. The yellowing phenotype of the sorghum YX-yl mutant is due to changes in gene expression patterns between YX-yl and the wild-type, ultimately resulting in differences in chloroplast structure and enzyme activity in chlorophyll biosynthesis pathways [
22
]. Changes in the expression of genes and transcription factors involved in chlorophyll synthesis and chloroplast development in Huangye tea lead to yellowing of leaf colour. To date, multiple genes such as
CHLH, CHLI, CHLP, HEMA, pbdg, NtClpRI, NtClpR4, alb3, NADP, NtEGY1
, and
NtEGY2
have been demonstrated to regulate chlorophyll content and leaf colour [
7
,
17
,
23
–
25
].
Tobacco is an important economic crop that is harvested from leaves and is grown in approximately 120 countries [
26
]. Due to its short growth cycle, clear genetic background, and ease of genetic transformation, it is a model plant for studying and revealing various biological phenomena. Leaf colour is an important indicator for judging the maturity and harvesting times of tobacco leaves, and during the long-term natural evolution process, tobacco has formed a “wildness” of mature leaves that become yellow from bottom to top. If the chlorophyll content of tobacco varieties is already low or if the chlorophyll content in all leaves decreases simultaneously at a certain stage of tobacco plant growth and development, this may promote concentrated leaf maturation and allow for mechanized harvesting. Chlorophyll deficiency mutations exhibit a special utilisation value in tobacco plants. In the early stages, we obtained a yellow leaf colour mutant in tobacco from emergence to maturity that possessed a chlorophyll content close to half that of the normal green variety. Using the yellow leaf K326 obtained from backcrossing as the material, we confirmed that this leaf colour mutation exerts no adverse effects on growth and development and exhibits the effect of improving the inconsistent “wildness” of tobacco leaf maturity [
27
]. Transcriptome analysis revealed that the differentially expressed genes between yellow leaf K326 and K326 were primarily enriched in processes such as copal-8ol diphosphate 3-degradation metabolism, cis-diphenol metabolism, magnesium chelatase activity, chloroplast mRNA processing, and porphyrin and chlorophyll metabolism. This indicates that the yellowing of mutant leaves is closely related to these metabolic processes, but the mutated gene responsible for leaf colour must be further explored [
28
]. This study used Huangye K326 (KY) and K326 (K) as parents to construct a four-generation genetic research population for P
1
, P
2
, F
1
, and F
2
. BSA-seq and proteome sequencing were used and combined with transcriptome sequencing results to identify key genes regulating Huangye traits to provide a theoretical basis and technical support for the molecular mechanism analysis of tobacco variety selection and leaf colour regulation suitable for machine harvesting.
Materials and methods
Experimental materials and population construction
In this experiment, K326 (K) and Huangye K326 (KY) were used as parents to obtain orthogonal F
1
(K × KY) and backcrossed F
1
(KY × K) generations through hybridisation. The F
1
generation was self-pollinated with both positive and negative crosses, and the resulting F
2
generation was used as the localisation population material. Huangye K326 is a yellow-leaf strain obtained by backcrossing six generations of recurrent parents with a yellow-leaf mutant as the maternal parent. The yellow leaf mutant is a yellow leaf material discovered by our research group during the anther culture process of the G28 × NC2326 hybrid combination that was obtained through multiple generations of self-crossing. The test materials used in this study were provided by the Key Laboratory of Tobacco Quality Research, Guizhou Province, China.
The experiment was conducted from 2021 to 2023 at the Tobacco Research Base of Guizhou University in Yangwu Township, Anshun City, and the Key Laboratory of Tobacco Quality Research in Guizhou Province. Floating seedling cultivation was adopted, and field management was conducted according to local high-quality tobacco variety cultivation and management measures. Parent and reciprocal F
1
were designed using a randomized block design with three replicates and 45 plants per community. The F
2
population consists of 1,859 plants.
Phenotyping and data analysis
On the 40th day after emergence, 30 parents from K, KY, and F
1
were randomly selected and mixed according to the material with three biological replicates. F
2
was obtained from a single plant. Fresh leaves (0.2 g) were placed in a mortar and pestle. A small amount of quartz sand, calcium carbonate powder, and an 80% acetone solution were each added until the solution became white. The tissue solution was transferred into a volumetric flask, mixed well, and filtered, and the absorbance values were measured at 470, 649, and 665 nm using a UV spectrophotometre (SPECROD 200 PLUS). Chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll, and carotenoid content were all analysed.
Differential analysis was performed on F
1
crosses to determine the genetic type of the yellow leaf traits. After determining the genetic type, a normal distribution test was performed on the F
2
segregation population. By observing the continuous distribution of the F
2
population, the skewness coefficient, kurtosis coefficient, skewness coefficient (skewness/standard deviation), and kurtosis coefficient (kurtosis/standard deviation) were calculated. If their absolute values were less than 1.96, F
2
conformed to a normal distribution.
Illumina sequencing
Based on the chlorophyll content in individual F
2
plants, 30 plants possessing the highest chlorophyll content and 30 plants possessing the lowest chlorophyll content were selected from the F
2
isolated population. DNA was extracted using the magnetic bead method. After equal mixing, green and yellow leaf-mixing pools were constructed. Additionally, two-parent mixing pools were added, resulting in four DNA-mixing pools for BSA resequencing. After sequencing was completed, quality distribution testing, database distribution testing, and pollution monitoring were performed on the original data. The sequencer generated image data, which was then converted into raw sequence data reads through base calling. Sequencing data containing adapter sequences or low-quality reads was removed to obtain high-quality clean reads for subsequent analysis. The alignment algorithm was used as the default parameter for bwa mem using the genome alignment software BWA. The clean reads of all samples were aligned to the K326 reference genome [
29
], Picard was used to perform Mark Duplicates, and the results were compared.
BSA analysis
The BWA method was used to analyse the variation information of resequencing results, identify significant difference markers between extreme mixing pools, and evaluate the regions associated with traits. First, homozygous but genetically different SNPs were screened in the two parental lines. The genome was divided into intervals of certain sizes, and the frequencies of SNP sites in the max- and min-pools were calculated separately. The SNP-index difference between the max-pool and the min-pool was computed, and the delta SNP-index (ΔSNP-index) graph was plotted accordingly. The regions where the peak values exceeded the threshold line CI95 or CI99 (confidence interval) were considered as the intervals at which the target gene was located. Subsequently, based on the Euclidean distance (ED) algorithm, significant differences in SNP sites between pools with high and low leaf numbers were identified, and the depth of each base in the different pools was recorded. This information was used to calculate the ED value for each site, with the ED value exceeding the significance association threshold, suggesting a potential association with the target trait. The ED algorithm possesses strong noise-reduction capabilities and does not require parental information for localisation.
RNA sequencing and analysis
We selected K and KY tobacco seedling leaves that have been present for 40 days and collected three biological replicates. We extracted RNA using the Trizol reagent kit, constructed a cDNA library, and sequenced them using the Illumina Hi Seq 6000 platform with a read length of 300 bp. Before bioinformatic analysis, low-quality readings or readings containing linker sequences were removed. Before proceeding with the next sequencing, we confirmed the quality of the transcriptome sequencing by sequencing saturation and single gene coverage. TopHat2 software was used to sequence align the clean data obtained after accession with Ntab-K326. Quantitative analysis of the transcriptome and gene expression levels was performed using RSEM software. The reference screening criteria were | log2FC |≥ 2 and padjust < 0.05, and we performed differential gene expression analysis using DESeq2 software.
Proteome sequencing
We selected K and KY tobacco seedling leaves that have been present for 40 days and collected three biological replicates, the total protein of the parent seedlings was extracted using the TCA/acetone method, and the protein concentration was analysed using a BCA proteomics kit. TMT labelling, high-pH reverse-phase liquid chromatography separation, and protein liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry detection were performed on tobacco leaves during the seedling stage using enzymatic hydrolysis in a super hotel. Proteome Discoverer software (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2.3) was used to perform a database lookup and quantitative analysis of the mass spectrometry data. We marked the proteins with a confidence level of higher than 99% and at least one treatment as “High”, and the peptides with a confidence level of higher than 95% and Exp Proteins with
q
< 0.01 were identified as high-reliability proteins. Proteins with differential expression multiples greater than 2.0 and a
P
-value < 0.05 were considered to be differentially expressed proteins (DEPs). We used gene ontology (GO) to annotate and enrich the functions of the differentially expressed proteins, and we used KEGG Automatic Annotation Server for KEGG pathway annotation of differentially expressed protein sets.
Gene cloning and sequence alignment
This study cloned the CDS region sequence and promoter region sequence of Nitab4.5_0008674g0010 from the DNA and cDNA of K and KY. Design gene specific primers using Primer 5 software based on the K326 reference genome sequence (Table
S1
). The sequence was cloned onto the TOPO vector, a recombinant plasmid was constructed, and sent to Beijing Qingke Biotechnology for sequencing. Using Snapgene for nucleotide sequence translation, alignment, and amino acid sequence alignment.
Statistical analysis
Using Microsoft Excel and IBM SPSS Statistics 26 software to statistically analyze the photosynthetic pigment content of parents of yellow and green leaves, as well as reciprocal F
1
and F
2
materials. Differential analysis was conducted on the reciprocal F
1
to determine the cytoplasmic inheritance type of leaf number traits. The significant difference between reciprocal F
1
indicates that the leaf number trait of Jiucaiping 2 belongs to cytoplasmic inheritance, while the insignificant difference between reciprocal F
1
indicates that the leaf number trait belongs to nuclear inheritance. After determining the genetic type of the nucleus, IBM SPSS Statistics 26 software was used to perform a normal distribution test on the F
2
segregating population. By observing the continuous distribution of the F
2
population, the skewness coefficient and kurtosis coefficient were calculated. If the absolute values of the skewness coefficient (skewness/standard deviation) and kurtosis coefficient (kurtosis/standard deviation) are both less than 1.96, then F
2
conforms to a normal distribution. Otherwise, it does not conform.

BSA-seq identification of candidate intervals for yellow leaf genes
Whole-genome resequencing was performed on K, KY, the green leaf mixed pool, and the yellow leaf mixed pool. The average alignment rate between the parents and the two mixed pools was 99.76%, with an average coverage rate of 87.91%. The sequencing depth of the parents was greater than 15, and the sequencing depth of the mixed pool was greater than 25. Through filtering, a highly reliable SNP dataset was obtained, with 3,696,828, 1,648,396, 1,910,082, and 1,912,768 SNP mutation sites in the K, KY, green leaf mixed pool, and yellow leaf mixed pool, respectively.
To obtain the genomic regions associated with the yellow leaf mutation Δ, the SNP index and ED algorithm were used to calculate the allele segregation of SNP and index between two extreme DNA libraries. The SNP index correlation interval results indicate that there is one interval between chromosome 8, (92,039,908–92,838,158) that exceeds the CI95 threshold. The interval size was 0.8 Mb (Fig.
3

3
B) that ranged from 148,548,485 bp to 151,882,522 bp with a total length of 3.3 Mb and 83 genes within the interval. The SNP index and ED algorithm reveal a total of two candidate intervals on chromosomes 8 and 16, with a total of 95 candidate genes within the intervals (Table

Folic acid content in yellow leaf materials
Based on the above results, we measured the folic acid content in tobacco K and KY plants to determine if folic acid is involved in tobacco leaf colour changes. The folate content of K was 5.80 µG/g, while the folate content in KY was 1.12 µG/g and was decreased by 80.69% compared to that in K (Fig.
5
F). To further verify the role of folic acid in tobacco leaf colour changes, we selected the mutant Y (Fig.
5
G) with a yellower colour than that of KY and determined that its folic acid content value was only 0.04 µG/g and was reduced by 99.31% compared to that in K (Fig.
5
F). Folic acid is an intermediate product of chlorophyll biosynthesis. Therefore, we speculated that the downregulation of
DHNA
gene expression in KY hinders the biosynthesis of folic acid and reduces its content, thus further hindering the biosynthesis of chlorophyll and resulting in a significant decrease in chlorophyll content and yellowing of leaf colour in KY.
Discussion
The colour change of plant leaves is caused by complex biological processes, and the yellowing of leaves is primarily related to the content of chlorophyll and carotenoids. For example, Xu et al. [
30
] identified a yellow leaf mutant Yl2 in cucumber that displayed a yellow colour throughout its lifecycle. Studies have demonstrated that the chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, and carotenoid contents in the leaves are lower than those in the wild-type leaves. Jiang et al. [
29
] discovered four yellow-leaf mutants in strawberries with significantly reduced chlorophyll and carotenoid content. Sun et al. [
28
] observed that a higher ratio of chlorophyll to carotenoids in Ginkgo biloba can lead to the yellowing of leaves. Our research indicates that the total chlorophyll content of KY is significantly lower than that of K, and the carotenoid content is significantly lower than that of K. Therefore, the yellowing of this mutant leads to a decrease in chlorophyll content, thus indicating that it is a chlorophyll-deficient mutant.
The changes in chlorophyll are typically related to photosynthesis and development, and certain studies suggest that the yellowing of leaves may affect the normal development of crops. For example, Zhao et al. [
31
] reported in their research investigating wheat that the grain length, grain width, and 100-grain weight of the yellow leaf line were significantly lower than those of the green leaf line, thus indicating that changes in leaf colour may lead to a decrease in grain yield. The study by Xu et al. [
30
] examining cucumbers indicated that the net photosynthetic rate (Pn) and transpiration rate (Tr) of the Yl2 yellow leaf mutant were significantly reduced by approximately 60% and 50%, respectively, thus indicating relatively weak and slow growth. Yuan et al. [
3
] observed abnormal chloroplast development and reduced photosynthetic efficiency in Oy2 yellow leaf mutants in maize that can lead to plant death when grown to the three-leaf stage. Fan et al. [
32
] determined that the height of the els yellow leaf mutant was lower in cotton. Based on the results of this study and previous research by our team, we observed that the tobacco yellow leaf mutant KY exhibits an abnormal chloroplast structure and a decreased net photosynthetic rate. However, the growth momentum of plants did not weaken throughout the growth period. At maturity, the yellow leaf phenotype can accelerate tobacco leaf maturation, improve the consistency of tobacco leaf maturation, and achieve mechanized harvesting, and this is of great significance for tobacco production.
Chlorophyll and carotenoid biosynthesis are determined by complex biological processes, and blocking any step in this process can lead to a decrease in chlorophyll or carotenoid content that in turn leads to changes in leaf colour [
31
,
33
–
35
]. Previous studies have revealed that chlorophyll biosynthesis in higher plants begins with l-glutamyl-tRNA (
Glu-tRNA
) and ultimately generates chlorophyll a and b [
36
]. Magnesium protoporphyrin IX is a key link in chlorophyll synthesis. Studies have demonstrated that mutations in any of the
CHLH, CHLD
, or
CHLI
genes encoding the three subunits of magnesium ion chelatase can hinder the synthesis of magnesium protoporphyrin IX, thus causing a decrease in chlorophyll content in the mutant and ultimately resulting in leaf colour changes [
37
]. For example, Jung et al. [
20
] reported that when the gene
OsCHLH
encoding the H subunit of magnesium ion chelatase is mutated, it can cause the synthesis of protoporphyrin IX in the mutant to be blocked, thus resulting in a decrease in chlorophyll synthesis and ultimately the whitening of rice leaves during the seedling stage. Fan et al. [
32
] determined in their study examining cotton that mutations in
GaCHLH
inhibit the normal function of the tetrapyrrole pathway and fail to exert Mg-chelatase activity, ultimately leading to the accumulation of
Proto-IX
and a decrease in
Mg Proto-IX
that results in impaired chlorophyll synthesis. In our study, it was demonstrated that the differential expression of proteins related to processes such as tetrapyrrole binding plays an important role in yellow leaf mutations. Based on our previous research [
38
], the content of various precursors in KY was significantly lower than that in K throughout the
Chlb
synthesis process of protoporphyrin IX (
Proto IX
). The inhibition of chlorophyll synthesis in this yellow leaf mutant was related to the biological synthesis of protoporphyrin IX and magnesium protoporphyrin IX.
In our study, the tobacco yellow leaf genes were localized by combining BSA-seq, RNA-seq, and proteomic sequencing, and it was observed that the key regulatory gene for folate synthesis (
DHNA
) was an important candidate gene for yellow leaf mutations. Folic acid plays an important role in the synthesis of chlorophyll by primarily participating in the synthesis and supply of S-adenosylmethionine and affecting the synthesis and enzyme activity of the key enzyme for chlorophyll synthesis, magnesium protoporphyrin IX methyltransferase (CHLM) that uses it as a substrate. Dhananjay K. Pandey et al. [
39
] discovered that bacterial DNA may act as a heterologous protein to regulate folate production. The author developed a genetically engineered independent transgenic tobacco line and used heterologous proteins for DHNA gene expression. It was found that the yield of folate increased by 2.5 times compared to the control plants. Weilong Xie et al. [
40
] identified dihydrofolate aldolase (DHNA) in the folate synthesis pathway of kidney beans and found that the isolated gene can be used for detecting and selecting soybean varieties with increased folate content in soybean breeding. Val é rie Van Wilder et al. [
41
] found that the use of methotrexate as a specific inhibitor of folate biosynthesis changed the folate status in the yellowing leaves of pea for 10 days. The methyl tetrahydrofolate observed in the experimental treatment is the folate derivative most affected by the treatment. The results indicate that reducing the activity of dihydrofolate reductase in yellowing leaves at 10 days of age can lead to a partial decrease in total folate content. The concentration of AdoHcy increases and the methylation index decreases. This study indicated that the efficiency of chlorophyll synthesis in yellowed leaves was significantly correlated with that of folate. Shohag et al. [
42
] observed a high correlation between folate content and chlorophyll in Chinese cabbage. The main function of CHLM is to catalyse the methylation and cyclization of magnesium protoporphyrin IX, thus forming magnesium protoporphyrin IX methyl ester and finally generating chlorophyll a. Combined with our previous research results, we determined that the precursor changes in chlorophyll occur during the biosynthesis of magnesium protoporphyrin I. Therefore, we speculate that this may be due to changes in the folate content in the leaves that lead to a decrease in the activity of CHLM, a slowdown in catalytic activity, a decrease in chlorophyll synthesis, and ultimately a yellowing of tobacco leaves. Although further research is required to verify this result, it provides an important reference for understanding the molecular mechanism of tobacco leaf colour yellowing.
