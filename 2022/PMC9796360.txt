1
Introduction
The global pandemic of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) has last lasted two and a half years and the SARS-CoV-2 continued to spread over the world
[23]
,
[49]
,
[58]
. To date, no drugs specifically for COVID-19 have been approved, therefore, effective disinfection and protective methods to prevent the transmission and infection of SARS-CoV-2 are still the main strategies
[17]
.
In the epidemic of SARS-CoV-2, several variants of SARS-CoV-2 namely Beta, Gamma, Delta, and Omicron, with increased transmission fitness, vaccination escape, and immune evasion were constantly identified
[18]
,
[42]
,
[59]
. At present, the main variant spreading over the world is the Omicron variant which was first identified in South Africa at the end of November 2021
[35]
,
[43]
,
[51]
. The previous studies demonstrated that the Omicron variant contains 34 mutations in the Spike protein, 3–4 times higher than other variants
[10]
,
[36]
. Especially the 15 mutations in the receptor binding domain (RBD) of the Spike protein that is responsible for the recognition and binding to the cell receptor—human angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (hACE2) contribute to the high affinity of the Omicron variant to the receptor
[10]
,
[36]
. The mutations in the Spike protein also lead to the vaccination escape of the Omicron variant
[1]
. In addition, a previous study compared the survival times of different variants and the Omicron variant exhibited the strongest stability with a median survival time of more than 8 days on the plastic surface
[12]
,
[22]
. Due to the strong transmissibility, long survival time, and vaccination escape, the Omicron variant traveled around the world and became the dominant variant barely several weeks after first identification
[24]
,
[26]
. Effective disinfection and protective methods became particularly important to prevent the infection of the Omicron variant. The most commonly used disinfectants are chlorine-containing disinfectants that are irritant to the skin and respiratory tract of humans, and medical alcohol (70–75% ethanol) that are inflammable and would bring potential safety risks
[11]
,
[20]
,
[46]
. Therefore, the development of an efficient and safe disinfection strategy is needed.
Gas plasma contains a variety of reactive species including hydrogen peroxide (H
2
O
2
), hydroxy radical (
•
OH), singlet oxygen (
1
O
2
), nitrite (NO
2
−
), and peroxynitrite (ONOO
−
)
[44]
,
[5]
. Gas plasma and its derived form—plasma-activated water (PAW) both exhibited strong bactericidal ability and were also potential for virus inactivation ((Filipic et al., 2020)
[30]
,
[33]
,
[16]
). An early study demonstrated that the treatment with surface plasma for 240 s reduced 6-log
10
adenovirus and inhibited both gene expression and replication
[64]
. Chen et al. used the plasma jet with the working gas of argon could efficiently inactivate SARS-CoV-2 on different surfaces that experience frequent human contact, such as plastic, metal, and cardboard
[11]
. Pulsed corona discharge plasma could efficiently inactivate by 4-log
10
spring viremia of carp virus in the water after 120-s treatment by inducing damage to the phosphoprotein and polymerase complex proteins
[48]
. Qin et al. found that the plasma jet with the working gas of argon efficiently inactivated SARS-CoV-2-like coronavirus GX_P2V and six SARS-CoV-2 variants by inducing spike protein damage
[41]
. The plasma-activated water activated by surface plasma with argon and 1% air for 2 min could inactivate more than 10-log
10
T4, Φ174, and MS2 bacteriophages
[19]
. Our group previously demonstrated that plasma-activated water prepared by the surface plasma with the working gas of air could potently inactivate the RBD domain and prevent the infection of SARS-CoV-2 pseudovirus
[20]
. These studies proved plasma-based technology is a promising strategy for sterilization, however, it is necessary to further develop the application of plasma technology for the sterilization of contaminated microorganisms in the epidemic.
In this study, the pseudovirus and the RBD of SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variants (Omicron-pseudovirus and Omicron-RBD) were used as models to evaluate the effects of the combined application of water spray and plasma-activated air. The molecular mechanism of the combined application was also studied. Further, the bactericidal effects of the combined application on both
Pseudomonas aeruginosa
and
Staphylococcus aureus
were determined. This study provided a potent strategy for the application of plasma technology to eradicate contaminated pathogenic microorganisms.

Materials and method
2.1
Preparation of water spray and plasma-activated air
The experimental setup contains a siphon nozzle and a gliding arc reactor as illustrated in
Fig. 1
. The deionized water was sprayed through a siphon nozzle driven by airflow at a flow rate of 20 L/min. The gliding arc reactor was similar to that in the previous study and consisted of two stainless steel blades with a length of 35 mm and a distance of 4 mm and an air nozzle with an inner diameter of 1 mm
[56]
. The blade electrodes are powered by a high-voltage transformer (Sinolift, TH5-NT-1530) with a digital power meter to monitor the discharge power, and the power of supply power and sliding arc together was controlled at approximately 80 W. The ambient air was introduced into the reactor at the flow rate of 0.8 L/min and discharged by the gliding arc to produce the plasma. The plasma was flown with the airflow and mixed with another airflow at a similar flow rate of 0.8 L/min in a mixing chamber to form the plasma-activated air (PAA) for the treatment. Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy (Bruker, Tensor II) was used to detect the long-lived gaseous components in the PAA in the wavenumber range of 600–3000 cm
−1
. For the combined treatment, the water spray and the PAA were introduced into the treatment chamber and the samples were first treated with water spray for 1 min and then with PAA for 3 min or 6 min sequentially. The treatment time of PAA for 3 min and 6 min was determined by the inactivation effects of bacteria and the methicillin-resistant
Staphylococcus aureus
(MRSA) was inactivated below the limit of detection after the combined water spray and 6-min PAA treatment (
Fig. S1
).

In the improvement of the combined treatment, the PAA preparation device was integrated and the treatment chamber was replaced by a funnel-shaped nozzle with diameters of 10 cm, 12 cm, and 15 cm. The treatment time of water spray for the funnel-shaped nozzles with different diameters was varied: 30 s for 10 cm, 40 s for 12 cm, and 50 s for 15 cm. The diameters of three different water sprays from the metal nozzle, medical atomizer, and commercialized electric atomizer were measured using the spray particle size meter Winner311XP (Winner particle, Jinan, China).
2.2
Pseudovirus-based inactivation assay
The pseudovirus incorporated with the S protein of SARS-CoV-2 Omicron strain and the reporter gene firefly luciferase were supplied by ARCO system (Beijing, China). The HEK-293 T cells with the overexpression of human ACE2 (hACE2) were cultured in a complete medium containing Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM, Thermo-Fisher), 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco), and 1% (v/v) penicillin-streptomycin (Beyotime) at 37 °C in 5% CO
2
. The quartz, metal, and cardboard flakes (10 mm × 10 mm) were sterilized with an autoclave, and the leather and plastic flakes (10 mm × 10 mm) were sterilized with 75% ethanol and dried. The pseudovirus (3 μL) was dropped on the quartz, metal, leather, plastic, and cardboard flakes and dried, then put on the support bracket. The samples were treated with water spray for 1 min and then with PAA for 3 min or 6 min. After the treatment, the treated and untreated pseudovirus was washed down using 600 μL complete medium constituted of DMEM, 10% FBS, and 1% penicillin and streptomycin, and each 200 μL of the pseudovirus was added to the HEK-293 T cells with the overexpression of hACE2. After culturing for 48 h, the infections of pseudovirus were measured using ONE-Glo™ Luciferase Assay System (Promega) and the cell viability was measured using CellTiter-Glo® Luminescent Cell Viability Assay (Promega) using a microplate reader (Thermo Scientific Varioskan Flash). The pseudovirus could infect cells and the reporter firefly luciferase gene expressed in the cells, which was reflected by the value of the luminescence unit (RLU) and the positive infection was determined as the RLU value of samples ten-fold relative higher than that of the cell background
[39]
.
2.3
Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)
The ELISA assay of the Omicron-RBD was performed as described previously
[20]
. The hACE2 protein (Arco system, Beijing, China) was diluted with coating buffer (15 mM Na₂CO₃, 35 mM NaHCO₃, 7.7 mM NaN₃, pH 9.6) to 1 μg/mL and added to the wells of 96-well plates (100 μL per well). The plates were incubated at 4 °C for 16 h and washed with PBS buffer with 0.05% triton (PBS-T). Then, the wells were blocked with PBS-T containing 2% BSA at 37 °C for 1.5 h and washed with PBS-T three times. Next, 100 μL of the biotinylated Omicron-RBD (3.90625–2000 ng/mL, diluted with PBS-T containing 0.5% BSA) were added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. The wells were washed as above and incubated with 100 μL of horseradish peroxidase-conjugated streptavidin (ThermoFisher) at 37 °C for 1 h. After washing, TMB substrate (100 μL, ThermoFisher) was added to the wells and incubated at 37 °C for 20 min, then 2 M H
2
SO
4
(100 μL) was added to the wells. The optical density (OD) at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Thermo Scientific Varioskan Flash).
2.4
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
The visualization of Omicron-pseudovirus was performed as described previously with slight modification
[20]
. The pseudoviruses treated with water spray for 1 min and PAA for 3 min or 6 min were washed down with PBS buffer, dropped onto carbon-coated grids, and incubated for 10 min at 22 °C. Then, the excess liquid was discarded, and the grids were covered with 1% uranyl acetate for 30 s at 22 °C. These stained samples were examined by using an FEI Talos F200C transmission electron microscope operating at 200 kV at 45,000 × magnification.
2.5
Mass spectrometry
Four peptides from the Omicron-RBD were synthesized by KareBay Biochem (Ningbo, China) and the sequences of peptides were as follows: YNFAPFFAFK, SNKLDSKVG, PLRSYQFRPT, and PTYGVGHQ. The peptides (1 mg/mL, 3 μL) were dropped on the quartz flakes (10 mm × 10 mm) and dried, then put on the support bracket. The dried peptides were treated with water spray for 1 min and then with PAA for 6 min. Then the peptides were resuspended in 0.1% formic acid solution and analyzed using an analytical column (750 µm × 150 mm, 3 µm particle size) on an Ultimate 3000 RSLCnano system connected to a Q-Exactive Plus mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific). The samples were eluted using a binary solvent system with 2% acetonitrile (ACN) to increase the solubility and 0.1% formic acid (phase A) used as ion pair to facilitate the ionization, and 80% ACN and 0.1% formic acid (phase B). The full-scan mass spectrometry (MS) mode was operated with the following parameters: automatic gain control (AGC) target, 1e6; resolution, 70,000; scan range, 350–1800
m/z
; and maximum injection time, 100 ms. Masses selected for MS/MS (AGC 1 × 10
5
) were the top 10 most intense parent ions with an isolation window of 2.0
m/z
. Fragmentation was performed in a higher-energy collisional dissociation cell with 24 NCE at 17,500 resolution. The results were analyzed by Xcalibur.

2.7
Bacteria inactivation assay
The single
Pseudomonas aeruginosa
PAO1 or methicillin-resistant
Staphylococcus aureus
ATCC 33591 (MRSA) colony was picked into 4 mL Luria-Bertani (LB) or tryptic soy broth (TSB), respectively, at 250 rpm and 37 ℃ overnight. Then, 40 μL culture was transferred into a fresh LB or TSB broth and further cultured for 3–4 h to the stationary phase. The bacterial cultures were collected by centrifugation at 3000g and suspended with saline at an optical density at 600 nm of approximately 50.0. The quartz, metal, and cardboard flakes (10 mm × 10 mm) were sterilized with an autoclave, and the leather and plastic flakes (10 mm × 10 mm) were sterilized with 75% ethanol and dried as described above. After that, the bacteria suspension (10 μL) was dropped on the quartz, metal, leather, plastic, and cardboard flakes and dried, then put on the support bracket. The samples were treated with water spray for 1 min and then with PAA for 3 min or 6 min. After the treatment, the bacteria were washed down using phosphate saline buffer (PBS). The bacteria suspensions were serially diluted, and 10 μL of each dilution was dropped onto LB (for
P. aeruginosa
) or TSB (for MRSA) agar plates for continued incubation at 37 ℃ overnight. The resulting colony-forming units were counted and statistically analyzed.

3.1
Prevention of the infection of the Omicron-pseudovirus
The air flew into the arc chamber and was discharged by gliding arc, then the plasma with the airflow was mixed with another airflow to generate the plasma-activated air (PAA) (
Fig. 1
A and B). The gaseous components in the PAA were detected by FTIR and the PAA contained NO
2
, NO, slight N
2
O
5
, HNO
2
, and HNO
3
(
Fig. 1
C). The PAA was then combined with water spray for the inactivation of experimentally contaminated microorganisms to evaluate the inactivation effects (
Fig. 1
A and B).
The pseudovirus incorporated with the Spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variants (Omicron-pseudovirus) was employed first as the model microorganism contaminations in this study. First, the RLU value of the cells infected with the Omicron-pseudovirus treated with PAA alone was 31,458.0, similar to that of cells infected with untreated Omicron-pseudovirus (32,380.5), which indicated that the PAA treatment alone exhibited little inactivation effects (
Fig. 2
A). Based on the previous study, the plasma-activated water exhibited excellent bactericidal effects
[56]
. It was speculated that the weak inactivation effects were due to the dryness of the samples, therefore, the water spray was used to increase the humidity of the samples. The dried Omicron-pseudovirus was treated with the water spray first for 1 min and then with the PAA for 3 min or 6 min and the RLU values of the cells infected with the Omicron-pseudovirus treated with the combined treatment were 12.5 and 6.6, indicating that the combined treatment efficiently inactivated the Omicron-pseudovirus (
Fig. 2
A). The treatment of water spray and untreated ambient air was used as a control and exhibited little effect (
Fig. 2
A). To investigate the effects of the sites of samples in the treatment chamber, the dried samples in four sites with the different distances of 10 cm, 20 cm, 30 cm, and 40 cm were treated and detected. The RLU values of the cells infected with the treated Omicron-pseudovirus samples in four sites were 59.8, 11.1, 10.2, and 8.0, respectively, demonstrating that the Omicron-pseudovirus samples in different sites were inactivated and lost the infection ability (
Fig. 2
B). The infection of both untreated and treated Omicron-pseudovirus did not affect the cell viability (
Fig. S2
). To evaluate the ratio of the Omicron-pseudovirus inactivated, the cells were also infected with a 10-fold serial dilution of untreated Omicron-pseudovirus solutions, and the RLU value of cells infected with 10
−4

Fig. S3

3.2
Inactivation of the Omicron-RBD by the combined treatment
The RBD is at the top side of the Spike protein and responsible for the recognition of the cell receptor and the binding with the cell receptor, therefore, the effects of the combined treatment of the water spray and PAA were also measured. The binding ability of the RBD to hACE2 was measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and the concentration for 50% of the maximal effect (EC50) of the untreated Omicron-RBD was approximately 228 ng/mL. The EC50 of the Omicron-RBD treated with the combined treatment of the water spray and PAA was at least 100 times higher, as calculated by a fitted curve (
Fig. 3
A). The Omicron-RBD in different sites of the chamber was all inactivated by the combined treatment, which was consistent with the treatment effects on pseudoviruses (
Fig. 3
B). The results proved that the combined treatment of water spray and PAA prevented the Omicron-pseudovirus infection through the inactivation of the Omicron-RBD.

Discussion
The SARS-CoV-2 variant Omicron with strong transmission ability spread to 128 countries within two months after the first identification in South Africa due to the strong transmission and survival ability, and the current global epidemic is mainly caused by the Omicron variant
[24]
,
[35]
,
[37]
. The Spike protein of the Omicron variant has more mutations and the mutations of the Omicron variant continue
[29]
,
[47]
. Therefore, the establishment of an efficient and nonspecific sterilization strategy for the SARS-CoV-2 variant is needed. This study developed a combined treatment of water spray and plasma-activated air (PAA) for the nonspecific and efficient inactivation of pathogenic microorganisms.
Compared with the single treatment of PAA that exhibited weak inactivation effects, the combined treatment of water spray and PAA with the step to increase humidity greatly improved the inactivation effect. The water spray could form a thin film of water on the dried microorganisms, then the reactive species dissolved in the water film to form PAA-activated water and acted on the microbial cell, which was proved by the reactive species detected in the water spray. FTIR analysis demonstrated that the gaseous reactive species in PAA contained NO, NO
2
, N
2
O, and N
2
O
5
(
Fig. 1
C). The nitrogen oxide gas dissolved in the water to generate nitrite and nitrate and could also generate peroxynitrite, hydroxyl, singlet oxygen, and superoxide anion through a series of chemical reactions, then the aqueous reactive species acted on the microorganisms and play biological roles
[25]
,
[28]
,
[32]
,
[62]

5
Conclusion
In conclusion, the application mode of the combined water spray and plasma-activated air could efficiently inactivate Omicron-pseudovirus and bacteria by causing nitration and nitrosative modification on the proteins. The improved device for surface disinfection could efficiently inactivate the microorganisms on the surface of different materials including quartz, metal, leather, plastic, and cardboard. The combined treatment exhibited the advantages of low water consumption, low cost, no selectivity, and no residue. Therefore, the combined water spray and plasma-activated air based on plasma technology provided a potent candidate for disinfection application during the epidemic.
Environmental implication
Contamination with microorganisms on the surface is one of the important ways of microbial transmission, especially during the epidemic. A combined treatment of waterspray and plasma-activated air based on plasma technology was proposed and used for the efficient inactivation of microorganisms contamination on the surface of different materials to prevent microbial transmission. The combined treatment also exhibited the advantages of no selectivity on microorganisms, no chemical residue, and low cost. Therefore, the combined treatment of waterspray and plasma-activated air provided a effective and environment-friendly strategy for the disinfection of contaminated microorganisms.
