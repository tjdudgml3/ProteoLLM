Research in context
Evidence before this study
Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive condition characterized by diffuse proliferative and obstructive pulmonary arteriopathy, for which there is no cure. Approved PAH-specific medications are predominantly pulmonary vasodilators with the potential to reduce cellular proliferation. They do not address the key pathobiological mechanisms underlying PAH such as inflammation, immune dysfunction etc. and significant occlusive arteriopathy and plexiform lesions remain in patients on PAH-specific drugs. While modern treatment has improved outcomes, mortality still remains high (median survival 6–7 years). Cardiosphere-derived cells (CDCs) are heart-derived progenitor cells with anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory and other potent effects to address PAH pathobiology. CDCs exhibit disease-modifying bioactivity in patients with Duchenne muscular dystrophy. The cells’ potential utility in PAH is supported by preclinical studies in rats in which CDC infusion exerted salutary effects on both the pulmonary vasculature and the right ventricle.
Added value of this study
In this Phase 1a and b study, CDCs were administered intravenously (IV) to patients with PAH on optimal and stable background combination PAH-specific medications. In addition to demonstrating short and long-term safety, several exploratory endpoints, including right ventricular end diastolic volume index, exercise capacity, diffusing capacity, hemodynamics and serum creatinine were significantly improved or stabilized by CDCs which was not found with placebo infusions.
Implications of all the available evidence
CDCs merit further study as adjunctive therapy to improve outcomes in PAH. The HOPE-2 trial of repeated IV infusions of CDCs to patients with Duchenne muscular dystrophy showed sustained improvement in cardiac and skeletal muscle function. Thus, next-phase studies will test repeated infusions of CDCs in patients with PAH with intermediate to high-risk characteristics.
Introduction
Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive condition characterized by diffuse proliferative and obstructive pulmonary arteriopathy, for which there is no cure.
1
Even with substantial pharmacologic advances, survival remains unacceptably poor.
2
,
3
Median survival of idiopathic (I) PAH in the pre-treatment era was 2.8 years from the time of diagnosis.
4
In the modern treatment era, the median survival at 3 different time ranges was 6.2 years.
5
Of importance, prominent features of adverse vasculopathy, including inflammation and plexiform lesions, were evident in 62 explanted lungs from patients with PAH despite being on PAH-specific therapies.
6
,
7
Finally, despite apparent clinical improvement on PAH-specific medications, progressive right ventricular (RV) dysfunction and failure has been reported
8
,
9
which is often fatal as no specific therapies are available and patients often fail to meet criteria for lung transplantation due to comorbid complications. Thus, a substantive unmet need for further effective therapies that address the complex PAH pathobiology are needed, to improve outcomes.
While the precise pathobiology of PAH is still being actively explored, several key principles include: genetic factors, epigenetic modifications, dysregulated immune function and inflammation, endothelial cell (EC) injury and dysfunction, imbalance between vasodilators and constrictors, transforming growth factor (TGF)β/bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) imbalance, up regulated growth factors, matrix aberrations, channelopathy and metabolic transitions.
10
,
11
,
12
Currently approved PAH-specific medications are predominantly pulmonary vasodilators which exhibit the potential to offset proliferative remodelling. However, a minority of patients with PAH are vasoreactive and, the drugs even in combination, fail to offset the occlusive arteriopathy or prevent right ventricular (RV) dysfunction and failure.
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
They also do not act on some of the major drivers of PAH pathobiology such as inflammation and immune dysfunction etc. highlighted above. Thus, therapies that can impact on several aspects of PAH pathobiology are needed to advance therapies for this dire condition.
Cardiosphere-derived cells (CDCs) are cardiac progenitor cells which are multipotent and clonogenic
13
Their therapeutic effects occur predominantly through the influences of secreted extracellular vesicles such as exosomes,
14
which contain cargos of bioactive nucleic acids.
13
,
15
CDC exosomes transfer their cargo into target cells to effect epigenomic, transcriptomic and phenotypic influences in target cells.
14
,
15
CDCs exhibit potent properties that could positively impact on PAH pathobiology (Anti-inflammatory, immune modulating, anti-fibrotic, anti-oxidative and anti-apoptotic). In our preclinical studies, CDCs administered intravenously (IV) to rats with PH reduced RV systolic pressures, RV hypertrophy, pulmonary arteriolar vessel wall thickening and inflammation in and around vessels.
16
The primary objective of this FDA-authorized Phase 1 study was to determine short and long-term safety profile of CAP-1002 (contains CDCs) administered by central intravenous infusion, using the maximum feasible dose (MFD), to patients with IPAH, Heritable PAH (HPAH), PAH associated with connective tissue disease (PAH-CTD) and PAH associated with HIV (PAH-HIV). A secondary objective was to evaluate for possible exploratory encouraging signals.
Methods
Ethics
This study was approved by the Cedars-Sinai Institutional Review Board's (IRB) Stem Cell Research Oversight Committee. The study is registered with
ClinicalTrials.gov
(
NCT03145298
) and is FDA authorized (IND #16686). All subjects underwent the full process of informed consent with IRB approved informed consent forms signed and retained.
Study design
Twenty-six (26) patients with PAH were studied. (Phase 1a, n = 6 and Phase 1b, n = 20). Patients with IPAH, HPAH, PAH-CTD and PAH-HIV on background PAH-specific agents for at least 4 months, were enrolled into the study. The choice of PAH subsets reflected inflammation and immune dysfunction as being a major pathobiologic contributor and prominent in these subsets, which accounts for about 75% of patients with PAH.
17
Phase 1a was an open label dose escalating paradigm in which half received 50 million CDCs with the remainder receiving 100 million CDCs which at the time of study development was the maximum feasible dose allowed from a
manufacturing perspective
. Of interest this dose did however correlate with our preclinical dosing, when normalized for biologic variables in the rats. Phase 1b was a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled study in which 10 subjects were randomized to receive 100 million CDCs while 10 subjects received vehicle (placebo) infusions. Randomization was determined by a computer algorithm. See
Fig. 1
for Flow Chart of participants. All research participants were blinded to study group in Phase 1b throughout the study apart from the quality assurance manager at Capricor. Recruitment measures began in July 2017 and follow up continued until June 30th 2022. Unblinding was shared with the PI and statistician on July 27th 2022.
Fig. 1
Flow chart of participants over 1st 4 months.
CDC manufacture
CAP-1002, the test article, is a frozen allogeneic product consisting of 25 million CDCs in 10 mL of vehicle solution contained within a cryobag Vehicle solution consists of 9 mL of a cryogenic cell preservation solution (CryoStor® CS10, BioLife Solutions) with 10% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and 1 mL of heparin (1000 USP units). CAP-1002 and vehicle for placebo was produced by Capricor at a cGMP facility.
Manufacture: donor hearts were obtained from an organ procurement organization (OneLegacy, Los Angeles, California), tissue dissected and explants cultured to create a master cell bank. Cardiospheres are formed in nonadherent cultures and expanded over several passages. CAP-1002 was cryopreserved for storage prior to use. All batches are thoroughly tested for identity, sterility, potency and viability.
A biorepository, (SriSai Biopharmaceutical Solutions, LLC), was used to distribute drug product in validated vapor phase LN2 shipping containers (Cryoport Express® Standard Liquid Nitrogen Dry Vapor Shipper, Lake Forest, CA). CAP-1002 was removed from its shipping container by a research-trained pharmacist and thawed at room temperature CAP-1002 was then delivered to the Investigator in the catheterization laboratory. The 50 and 100 million cell doses were administered along with an intermediate wash solution to ensure complete delivery.

Study procedures
Patients meeting inclusion criteria were considered for screening. Likely eligible patients were reviewed in person at the Cedars-Sinai Regenerative Medicine clinic whereby the investigator conducted the process of informed consent followed by a history and physical examination and all screening/baseline studies. Provided no contra-indications were identified, plans were the made for study infusion.
Infusion procedures
For the study infusion, patients were admitted to the hospital for a 23-h stay. They were transported to the cardiac catheterization laboratory where a dedicated cardiac interventionalist performed all procedures with cardiac anaesthesiology and PAH specialists available for assistance. Exclusionary data was a RAP >20 or a PCWP (or LVEDP) > 15 mmHg. For CDCs, sequential dose administration of 25 million cells each was given via the pulmonary artery catheter. This was a practical decision as we needed to monitor hemodynamics pre and after CDC infusion for safety reasons. Patients randomized to the placebo group received central intravenous placebo (i.e. CAP-1002 minus the active CDC constituent). Each 10 mL bag was infused at a rate of 1 mL/min. Defined stopping rules were in place after Wilson et al.
18
After 1 h if deemed stable, the patient was transferred to the post operative care unit and monitor bed for the remainder of the 23-h stay.

Exploratory end points
Exploratory signals were monitored over the course of the trial. See
Table 2
for breakdown over the study duration of 12 months. Data at 2 and 4 months were considered the key exploratory time frames for review of possible signals. These included: Transthoracic echocardiogram, cardiac MR (baseline and 4 months), right heart catheterization (baseline and 4 months), pulmonary function tests (PFTs), BNP, 6MWT, “Cambridge Pulmonary Hypertension Outcome Review” (CAMPHOR tool) and discovery proteomics.
Discovery proteomics
Plasma samples were obtained from 6 individuals (two placebo and 4 CDC-treated) at baseline and 2 weeks later were processed and analysed by mass spectrometry as outlined in (Mc Ardle 2022 PMID:
34687543
) Briefly, plasma processed on the BioMek i7 automated workstation (Beckman Coulter) was denatured, Cys residues reduced and alkylated, digested (1:25 trypsin:protein ratio) and desalted using a positive pressure apparatus (Amplius Positive Pressure ALP, Beckman Coulter). Mass spectrometry data was obtained using data independent acquisition on an Orbitrap Exploris 480 instrument (Thermo Fisher) coupled to an Ultimate 3000 ultra high-pressure chromatography system (Thermo Scientific). Fragmented ions were detected using 50 DIA non-overlapping 12 Da precursor windows. The raw spectra were searched using DIA-NN against a generated spectral library. Raw intensities were filtered by interquartile range and subsequently normalized by the median. Downstream data analysis and statistics was performed using the online MetaboAnalyst tool (
https://www.metaboanalyst.ca/MetaboAnalyst/home.xhtml
), as well as the in-house Protein Interaction Network Extractor (PINE) visualization suite (Sundararaman 2020 PMID:
32463229
). The mass spectrometry data are available in the Massive repository (MSV000093323).
Statistics
The study was an early Phase 1a and b study. The main objectives related to safety and dosing. For exploratory end points however, this was regarded as a pilot study. The main purposes of the statistical analyses for exploratory findings were therefore 1) to see whether changes in numerical variables were promising for future research and 2) to determine appropriate estimates for power analysis for future studies related to the results deemed to be promising.
Numerical variables and changes (delta) were summarized by mean and standard deviation (SD) and 95% CIs. Within group, CDC or Placebo (PB change between two time points was assessed for statistical significance by the paired t test and the Wilcoxon signed rank test. Between group differences (at specific times and deltas) were assessed by the independent samples t test and the Wilcoxon rank sum test. A 2-sided 0.05 significance level was used in all statistical testing. No adjustment was made for multiple comparisons. Statistical calculations were made using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina). As this is a pilot Phase 1 study, p-values and confidence intervals should be interpreted cautiously and should
not
be construed as evidence of effects.
When trying to glean the most insight from a phase 1 study, it is not uncommon to analyse all treated patients versus all placebo patients, regardless of randomization status. An influential FDA Guidance Document (“Enrichment Strategies for Clinical Trials to Support Determination of Effectiveness of Human Drugs and Biological Products”, March 2019) concludes that “The overall strategy (open trial followed by randomization of responders) is an efficient way to document effectiveness”. Further in Phase 1a as can be seen in
Supplementary Figure S1
, encouraging directions of change were noted for the 3 patients getting 50 million cells as well as the 3 patients receiving 100 million cells. We, together with our statistician felt comfortable combining all patients receiving CDCs as a comparator to placebo as stated above to determine any encouraging signals to justify moving forward with advanced phase testing.

CDC cell viability
CDCs were evaluated for cell viability, phenotype and size before and after passage through the distal port of a size 7 French Swan Ganz (SG) ®catheter (Edwards Lifesciences LLC). The data showed viable cells not altered by passage through the PA catheter, with our data far exceeding the FDA standards which are: a) Cell viability of CDC was greater than 70% for all conditions: T0 (after thawing), T45 (45 min of room temperature holding prior to catheter delivery) and post SG catheter delivery. b) Cell aggregation/clumping measured by size distribution using the Multisizer was unaffected by 45 min of room temperature holding and SG catheter delivery. Greater than 99% of CDCs were less than 50 μm and no particles larger than 150 μm were found. c) The phenotype of CDC before and after catheter delivery was greater than 90% CD105+ and less than 10% CD45+. CDC phenotype as measured by CD105+ and CD45+ was unchanged by SG catheter delivery. Extensive GMP quality control testing is done for all CDCs provided, including viability testing (ensuring >70% viability the FDA standard).

Immunologic safety monitoring
DSA testing to assess for class 1 and 2 HLA antibodies was performed at baseline and at 3 months post infusion. No clinical immunologic events were noted in any of the patients. No class 1 or 2 HLA antibodies were detected at baseline and on follow up in most patients. No patient negative at baseline developed antibodies at follow up. Those in whom low titers were detected at baseline showed the same patterns on follow up assays. Based on extensive safety documentation in multiple clinical CDC trials, routine immunologic testing has recently been omitted
19
(e.g. 19).

Quality of life measures
The CAMPHOR Questionnaire has 3 components: a symptom score, activity score and quality of life measure, in which lower scores are better. There were no significant changes for either group. No subjects in the CDC or placebo groups qualified for our definition of “clinical worsening” attributable to PAH. Only one CDC patient was downgraded from FC 3 to 2.

Discussion
No short- or long-term adverse safety events attributable to CAP 1002 (containing CDCs) were evident throughout the 12-month study. No subjects met criteria for clinical worsening due to underlying PAH. No adverse immunological events, either clinical or serological, were observed for the allogeneic product. Encouraging exploratory signals were evident across several domains, as described above.
In the original NIH registry, median survival of patients with IPAH (untreated) was 2.8 years from the time of diagnosis.
4
A recent study evaluated survival over three-time frames in the PAH treatment era (2005–2009, 2010–2014 and 2015–2019) and found that survival did not increase over time despite increased therapeutic options. The overall median survival was 6.2 years which is still sub optimal.
5
This is informative, as older registries such as the French registry were conducted when the treatment modalities were limited.
2
,
24
Thus, PAH in the modern treatment era remains a progressive and incurable condition. Many patients with PAH progress to develop significant RV dysfunction and failure despite maximum combination PAH-specific therapies
8
,
25
with many precluded from lung transplant because of co-morbidities. Thus, new more effective therapies to address the pathobiological drivers of occlusive arteriopathy in PAH are needed.
The pathobiology of PAH is complex and multifaceted.
10
,
11
,
12
CDCs are stromal/progenitor cells derived from cardiac tissue first reported in 2007.
13
Their therapeutic effects occur predominantly through the secretion of extracellular vesicles (EVs) such as exosomes.
14
,
15
CDC exosomes contain cargos of potent protein effectors as well as bioactive nucleic acids such as non-coding RNAs (nc RNA) such as microRNAs (miRs) and long nc RNAs.
13
,
15
Transfer of exosome cargo effects epigenomic, transcriptomic and phenotypic influences in target cells.
14
,
15
The fact that inflammation and immune dysfunction are important drivers of PAH pathobiology
11
motivated the use of CDCs in this early Phase trial. Further, in our preclinical studies, CDCs decreased inflammation in and around pulmonary blood vessels.
16
This is particularly cogent, as currently available PAH-specific agents do not address these and other pathobiological drivers.
In PAH, inflammatory infiltrates are present in peri-vascular spaces and plexiform lesions, with correlates between inflammation scores, vessel wall remodelling and pulmonary hemodynamics.
6
It is postulated that, as in animal models, inflammation precedes the genesis of vascular remodelling.
26
,
27
Further, freshly isolated human endothelial cells (EC) from patients with IPAH demonstrated a marked pro-inflammatory signature.
28
IL-1 and IL-6 induce fibroblast growth factor-2 (FGF-2), while IL-6 can promote pulmonary artery smooth muscle cell (PASMC) proliferation.
29
Further, loss of BMPR2 signalling is associated with increased pulmonary vascular cell expression of IL-6.
Immune dysfunction is linked to the genesis of perivascular inflammatory cells and cytokine and chemokine release. T regulatory cells (Tregs) are key cells that modulate adaptive immunity, activate monocyte/macrophages, regulate T-cells, B cells and dendritic cells and are dysfunctional in patients with IPAH, HPAH and PAH-CTD.
30
Peri-vascular lymphoid infiltrates, resembling tertiary lymphoid follicles have been described in IPAH
31
which can generate autoantibodies and contribute to the notion of autoimmunity as a factor in PAH.
CDCs exhibit potent anti-inflammatory properties as reported in several preclinical disease models which include myocardial infarction, Duchenne muscular dystrophy, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction and pulmonary arterial hypertension.
16
,
32
,
33
CDCs can inhibit proinflammatory cytokines including INFɣ, TNFα, IL-1 β, IL-6, IL-8, CXCL10, CCL2, CCL3, CCL5.
13
These effects may in part be attributed to the influences of miRs in the CDC EV/exosome cargo such as miR-146a,
14
miR-181b, and miR-148a.
32
Further, miR 146a and Mir-148a can target NF
K
B signalling pathways thought to play a role in PAH by attenuating NF
K
B phosphorylation.
32
Further CDCs have been described to polarize macrophages away from a pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype.
34
In addition, CDCs can augment two key anti-inflammatory cytokines, IL-10 and IL-1ra. The former may be related to a key exosome cargo fragment, Y RNA.
35
Lastly, CDCs have been reported to expand the T reg population.
36
PAH, and notably HPAH can be associated with dysfunctional signalling/imbalance downstream of the TGF-β superfamily of receptors resulting in down regulation of the BMPR2/pSMAD 1/5/8 pathway in vascular smooth muscle and endothelial cells. This results in an imbalance towards pro-proliferative pSMAD 2/3 signal transduction pathways.
10
Of note, CDCs express endoglin which can also be shed into the circulation and to which BMP9 and BMP10 have a high affinity. The latter can drive BMPR2/pSMAD 1/5/8 signalling and downregulate the TGFβ1/pSMAD 2/3 cascade.
37
Thus, CDCs have the potential to be potently antifibrotic and antiproliferative
33
and rebalance impaired activin signalling, as described with the ligand trap, sotatercept. Intracoronary CDCs significantly decreased RV fibrosis in an animal model of PH complicated by RV dysfunction.
38
Other properties of CDCs could also impact PAH pathobiology. CDCs attenuate oxidative stress. CDCs rescued the diminished nuclear content of Nrf2 and augmented expression of its target gene products (e.g. catalase, superoxide dismutase), mediated in part by increased phosphorylated Akt.
39
Further, CDCs are anti-apoptotic and can act on early EC injury to prevent apoptosis and emergence of apoptotic-resistant clones.
39
Several factors may play a role including reduction in oxidant-induced apoptosis, suppression of the caspase cascade and the elaboration of IGF-1 which is a potent anti-apoptotic factor. Thus, CDCs have the potential to target many different PAH pathobiological drivers simultaneously.
Regarding
safety and dosing,
no significant adverse events related to CDCs were noted in the short or long term the primary focus of this Phase 1 study. Similarly, no significant clinical or serological immunological adverse events were noted. As mentioned previously monitoring for this has now been abandoned. These safety outcomes mirrors our previous proven track record of safety for intracoronary and IV infusions of CDCs in other conditions.
13
With regard to dosing, similar encouraging exploratory endpoints were noted for 50 and 100 million CDCs. We thus felt comfortable pooling all CDC results to increase statistical power.
In this Phase 1 study, several encouraging exploratory signals were observed across several different domains. We interpret these as encouraging signals only, that motivate development of more advanced later stage studies in which true efficacy can be determined. We postulate that the mechanisms underlying our encouraging signals as related to the RV may reflect an impact of CDCs on pulmonary arteriolar remodelling and in the RV muscle, as described in our pre-clinical studies.
16
,
37
The increment in 6 MWD at 2 months in the CDC group likely reflects an improvement in RV muscle reserve, but was short lived, suggesting that repeated doses of CDCs may be necessary. (See rationale below for repeated dosing in future studies). The significant decrease in DLCO in the placebo group but not in the CDC subjects, likely reflects reduced pulmonary capillary blood volume in the placebo group. Significant reduction in serum creatinine was also noted in our pre-clinical study.
16
We speculate this may reflect a direct impact of CDCs on the kidney ± improved renal blood flow.
In terms of long-term outcomes, no further improvements were observed for encouraging exploratory endpoints measured through 12 months. We hypothesize that the impact of a single dose of CDCs are not sustained for prolonged periods which begs the question of repeated dosing which was shown to be both safe and efficacious for our recent study in Duchenne muscular dystrophy in which CDCs were infused intravenously every 3 months.
19
While slightly larger doses of CDCs were administered, we postulate that the repeated dosing was the main driver of success.
In terms of future paradigm testing, based on our current data and those of McDonald et al.
19
we believe future higher phase studies to meet several unmet PAH treatment needs should be conducted. These include: i) More frequent IV dosing of 150 million CDCs (current manufacturing limit) ii) Evaluating a patient population exhibiting at least one parameter of RV systolic dysfunction on either TTE or cMR as RV dysfunction is a key determinant of outcome with PAH
8
and iii) Comparing and contrasting the impact of the new treatment paradigm in patients with IPAH and scleroderma spectrum associated PAH, as the latter tradionally has been viewed as having a poorer prognosis and response to treatment as well as exhibiting distinct RV cardiomyocyte pathology and dysfunction.
40
We envisage 6 MWD being a primary outcome measure with indices of RV function as secondary endpoints. We aim to utilize some of the innovative trial designs concepts outlined in the Weatherald Lancet review in designing a robust advanced stage study.
41
An adjunctive product that can simultaneously impact several different aspects of the pathobiology of PAH (pulmonary vasculopathy, RV muscle), would be a great potential advance for this devastating condition.
