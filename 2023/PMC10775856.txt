Introduction
Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) is a systemic
autoimmune disorder
characterized by the occurrence of vascular morbidity-arterial or
venous thrombosis, and/or pregnancy morbidity that includes recurrent
miscarriages, fetal death, stillbirth, and early severe pre-eclampsia
related to placental insufficiency.
1
−
3
According to the Sydney
classification criteria, the clinical manifestations should be associated
with the persistent positivity for antiphospholipid antibodies (aPLs)
including lupus anticoagulant (LA), anti-β2-glycoprotein I (anti-β2GPI),
and anticardiolipin antibody (aCL).
4
,
5
The diversity
in aPLs’ profiles contributes to the complexity of understanding
the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying APS. In 2013, a new clinical
criterion was proposed to separate APS into thrombotic APS (TAPS)
characterized by thrombosis and obstetric APS (OAPS) characterized
by adverse pregnancy events.
6
OAPS has
been referred to as a severe acquired risk factor for recurrent abortions,
fetal losses, pre-eclampsia, and placental insufficiency and represents
an important health threat for women of childbearing age.
7
,
8
In the past few years, the study of the pathogenic mechanisms
leading
to OAPS has increased.
9
Previous studies
on the pathogenesis of OAPS suggest the roles of thrombotic, antiangiogenic,
and inflammatory factors in the pathological process of the disease.
1
The aPLs have been shown to have direct embryotoxic
effects, resulting in reduced implantation or embryonic growth.
10
Despite the canonical pathogenesis of OAPS,
there is still a lack of an unbiased protein map of this disease,
which will greatly extend the understanding of OAPS in another dimension,
as an important addition of pathogenesis induced by aPLs.
Data-independent
acquisition (DIA), known for its identification
depth, quantitative accuracy, and high reproducibility, has a great
potential for the large-scale proteomic analysis.
11
Here, we conducted a quantitative proteomic analysis of
serum samples from OAPS patients and healthy controls, with DIA mass
spectrometry method, to identify differentially expressed proteins
including functional annotation, enrichment, and pathway analysis
of differential proteins.
12
,
13
We aim to identify
new indicators for disease conditions and further explore the pathological
mechanism of OAPS.
Materials and Methodologies
Study Populations
and Samples Collection
A total of
31 patients with a disability were recruited in this study. All the
OAPS patients were diagnosed by Sydney APS criteria from June to October
in 2022 in Pecking University Third Hospital.
8
The participants were recruited from the Medical Examination Center
of the Peking University Third Hospital in 2022. Female participants
diagnosed with the Sydney criteria as follows: (1) three or more consecutive
spontaneous abortions prior to the 10th week of gestation; (2) one
or more unexplained deaths of a morphologically normal fetus at or
beyond the 10th week of gestation with normal fetal morphology documented
by ultrasound or by direct examination of the fetus; and (3) one or
more premature births of a morphologically normal neonate before the
34th week of gestation because of eclampsia or severe preeclampsia
or recognized features of placental insufficiency. Participants were
excluded if there were maternal anatomical or hormonal abnormalities
or if paternal and maternal chromosomal abnormalities caused abortion.
Meanwhile, 31 healthy age-approximately matched women were selected
as controls. None of the 31 women in the control group of this study
were pregnant. Furthermore, all participants in the obstetric antiphospholipid
syndrome (OAPS) group were enrolled in the study more than 1 month
subsequent to their last abortion. Blood samples were collected in
procoagulation vacuum tubes using standard venepuncture protocols.
Serum was extracted by centrifugation for 10 min at 3000 rpm and stored
in −80 °C for subsequent detection. The study was performed
in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration and was approved by the
Peking University Third Hospital Medical Science Research Ethics Committee.
Informed consent was obtained from all patients. The clinical characteristics
of OAPS patients and healthy control women are summarized in

Depletion of Highly Abundant Proteins from
Serum Samples
High-Select Top14 Abundant Protein Depletion
of serum samples was
conducted according to the manufacturer’s instructions for
the “Multi Affinity Removal Column, Human-14” (Agilent
Technologies). Briefly, 20 μL of serum and 60 μL of buffer
A were mixed evenly, transferred into a Spin-X centrifuge tube filter
0.22 μm cellulose acetate certified tube (COSTOR), and subsequently
centrifuged at 16,000
g
for 1 min. The filtrate was
transferred into preassembled plastic springs measuring 29 ×
5 mm for High-Select Top14 Abundant Protein Depletion using a liquid
chromatography system (Agilent Technologies 1290). The depleted samples
were collected, and the protein concentration was measured by using
a BCA protein assay kit (Thermo Scientific), following the manufacturer’s
instructions.
Sample Preparation
The sample preparation
for DIA followed
the previous laboratory protocol.
14
,
15
The depleted
serum sample (50 μg) was precipitated with 1/3 volume of trichloroacetic
acid (TCA) solution at 4 °C overnight and then centrifuged at
16,000
g
for 30 min at 4 °C. The precipitate
was washed three times with acetone and then dried with a vacuum concentrator
(Labconco, USA). The dried precipitate was dissolved in 40 μL
of 8 M urea (pH 8.5), incubated with 20 mM (2-carboxyethyl)phosphinehydro
chloride (TCEP), and alkylated with 40 mM IAA. The mixture was diluted
with 200 μL of 100 mM Tris–HCl buffer (pH 8.5) to a final
concentration of 1.3 M urea and then digested with 3 μg of trypsin
protease at 37 °C for 16 h. The sample was desalted by using
a Monospin C18 column (GL Science, Tokyo, Japan). The desalted peptides
were vacuum-centrifuged to dryness and then reconstituted in Milli-Q
water containing 0.1% formic acid (FA) for LC–MS analysis.
Indexed retention time (iRT) calibration peptides were spiked into
the samples prior to DIA analysis.
Sample Preparation for
Spectral Library Generation
Here, approximately 70 μg
of mixed peptide for spectral library
construction was fractionated using a high-PH reversed-phase HPLC
system (1290 Infinity, Agilent). Briefly, full resolution of the mixed
peptides in buffer A (20 mM ammonia, 2% ACN, pH 10.5) and loaded onto
an Accucore C18 column (2.1 × 150 mm, 2.6 μm particles;
Thermo Fisher Scientific) using 56 min linear gradient of 5–80%
of buffer B (20 mM ammonia, 98% ACN, pH 10.5) at a flow rate of 0.4
mL/min. Sixty fractions were collected along with the LC separation
and were concentrated into 10 fractions by combining fractions 1,
11, 21, 31, 41, 51, and so on. Then, the pool fractions were dried
completely under vacuum centrifugation. Dried peptides were dissolved
in 10 μL of Milli-Q water with 0.1% formic acid (FA) and then
spiked with iRT peptides to a final concentration of 1× for chromatographic
correction.
LC–MS/MS Analysis
The nLC-MS/MS
analysis was
performed on an EASY-nLC 1200 HPLC (Thermo Scientific) coupled to
a Q Exactive HF-X (Thermo Scientific) mass spectrometer. All samples
were separated within 120 min on a reversed-phase C18 column (25 cm
× 75 μm, 1.6 μm, IonOpticks). The elute gradient
of B was linearly increased from 5 to 30% within 109 min, followed
by an increase to 95% within 5 min and continued for another 6 min
at a flow rate of 300 nL/min at 50 °C (mobile phases A, 0.1%
formic acid in water; mobile phases B, 0.1% formic acid in 80% acetonitrile).
The mass spectrometer was operated in positive mode by using either
DDA or DIA modes.
DDA Mode

using a top 20 DDA
(data-dependent acquisition) method with the following settings: full

m
/

350–1200,

Generation of Spectral Libraries and DIA Data Analysis
Spectral libraries were generated using Spectronaut version 16.0
(Biognosys). The MS/MS spectra were matched against the human UNIPROT
database (20,421 human entries, downloaded in July 2019). All the
parameters were in the default setting. DIA files were processed in
the default mode. Briefly, searches used carbamidomethylation as the
fixed modification and acetylation of the protein N-terminus and oxidation
of methionines as variable modifications. The Trypsin/P proteolytic
cleavage rule was used, permitting a maximum of two missed cleavages,
a minimum peptide length of 7 amino acids, and a maximum peptide length
of 52 amino acids. The FDR for PSM and protein quantification were
both set to 0.01.
Serum Triglyceride Measurement
Triglyceride
(TG) levels
were quantified using colorimetric analysis kits on Beckman Coulter
AU5800 devices. The assay protocol transforms triglycerides in the
sample into free fatty acids and glycerol. The latter is phosphorylated
to glycerol-3-phosphate, which is subsequently oxidized, producing
hydrogen peroxide (H
2
O
2
) and dihydroxyacetone
phosphate. In the presence of peroxidase, H
2
O
2
reacts with 4-aminopyrine and
N
,
N
-bis(4-sulfobutyl)-3,5-dimethylaniline disodium salt (MADB), generating
a luminescent signal measurable at 660/800 nm wavelength. All procedures
adhered strictly to the manufacturer’s instructions, briefly
described as follows: 1.6 μL of serum sample was loaded into
the sample well, and the device handled the remaining procedures,
culminating in the luminescence readout.
Serum Glucose Measurement
Glucose levels in our study
were quantified using hexokinase (HK) analysis kits on Beckman Coulter
devices. The protocol entails the phosphorylation of glucose by hexokinase
to produce glucose-6-phosphate (G6P). Subsequently, glucose 6-phosphate
dehydrogenase (G6P-DH) oxidizes G6P, transforming NAD+ to NADH in
the process. The resulting color change is proportional to the glucose
level in the sample and is measured at a wavelength of 340 nm. All
experimental steps adhered strictly to the manufacturer’s instructions.
Briefly, a 1.2 μL serum sample was loaded into the sample well,
and the device handled the remaining procedures, culminating in the
luminescence readout.
Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay
Human protein ELISA
kits were used to quantify serum levels of endogenous protein according
to manufacturers’ instructions (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering
Institute). Briefly, serum samples were diluted according to the manufacturers’
dilution guideline. Then, a total of 50 μL of dilution plasma
sample was added to the antibody precoated plates, and then biotinylated
antigen. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. After washing,
50 μL of HRP conjugate-streptavidin was added to each well,
and the plates were incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. After washing,
a total of 100 μL of substrate solution was added and incubated
at 37 °C for 10 min followed by adding 50 μL of stop solution.
Finally, the OD value at 450 nm were determined. The standard curve
of each protein was generated by determination of OD values from serial
dilutions of the standard samples with known protein concentrations
provided by the manufacturers. The presented values are the average
of two technique replicates.

Discussion
OAPS patients were associated with significantly
higher risks of
pregnancy-induced hypertension, fetal loss, abortion, thrombosis,
and preterm delivery.
2
,
16
Therefore, research on the diagnosis
and mechanisms of the OAPS is of great importance. Advances in mass
spectrometry technologies have led to improvements in proteomics,
allowing for the rapid acquisition of significant amounts of biological
information from serum samples.
12
In this
study, we applied the DIA-MS approach to analyze differential proteins
between patients of OAPS and healthy controls with microvolumes of
samples, aimed to detect low abundance DEPs in our cohorts and investigate
the potential pathophysiologic mechanisms associated with OAPS. The
resulting quantitative proteomic data were subsequently analyzed for
enriched KEGG pathways. The significant differentially expressed proteins
were mainly associated with pathways related to autoimmune diseases,
allergic diseases, pathogen infection, metabolite pathways, and lysosomes.
Interestingly, our study emphasized the metabolic disorders of OAPS,
which required further attention. To our knowledge, no previous studies
have reported analyzing the mechanism of OAPS using DIA mass spectrometry
and achieved comparable identification depth.
Quantitative proteomic
analysis reveals disturbances in pathways
related to autoimmune diseases in the serum of the OAPS patients.
C-reactive protein (CRP) and the ferritin light chain significantly
increased. CRP is an acute phase response protein induced by interleukin
6 in hepatocytes, and plays an important role in immune processes
by activating complement and strengthening phagocytosis.
17
CRP has been used to monitor the autoimmune
condition for decades; previous studies supported that CRP as well
as hypersensitive CRP was elevated in OAPS patients, consistent with
our proteomic results. Ferritin light chain is a primary circulating
form of ferritin and mediates many of its immune functions with important
immune signaling actions.
18
Studies have
shown that in the setting of autoimmunity high levels of circulating
ferritin are associated with the macrophage activation, and whether
ferritin upregulation is a factor of underlying OAPS patients’
autoimmune dysregulation of OAPS patients remains to be elucidated.
We observed disturbances in protein levels related to the pathogenic
infection function, including Human papillomavirus, pathogenic
E. coli
infection, phagosome, and leishmaniasis.
Infectious agents are the main triggers for the formation of antiphospholipid
antibodies.
Molecular mimicry between structures of bacteria
or viruses and
β2-glycoprotein1-derived amino acid sequences are thought to
contribute to the formation of autoantibodies.
5
During the pathogenic infection process, β2GPI may facilitate
the involvement of apoptotic cells by promoting the release of bactericidal
peptide and assist in the phagocytosis of apoptotic cells.
19
Integrin alpha-2 and Integrin alpha-5 are two
of the DEPs in the pathogenic infection pathway. Infection may cause
the normally quiescent endothelium to acquire an inflammatory response.
Leukocyte-endothelial interactions and leukocyte extravasation are
orchestrated by selectins and cell adhesion molecules that facilitate
rolling of leukocytes at the endothelial surface, followed by stronger
integrin-mediated interactions that promote adhesion and eventual
exodus of leukocytes from vessels. Studies have detected endothelium-derived
microparticles in the circulation of APS patients as a surrogate for
endothelial activation, suggesting the vessel wall may be primed for
leukocyte interactions.
10
In KEGG
pathway analysis, the major differential proteins include
recombinant ribonuclease A3 (RNASE3), HLA class II histocompatibility
antigen DR alpha chain(HLA-DRA), tumor necrosis factor ligand superfamily
member 13B (TNFSF13B), matrix metalloproteinase-1(MMP1), CRP, integrin
alpha-2(ITGA2), integrin alpha-5 (ITGA5), and secreted phosphoprotein1(SPP1).
Among them, HLA-DRA, CRP, and TNFSF13B are key different proteins
in the autoimmune pathway. HLA-DRA is an alpha chain of antigen-presenting
major histocompatibility complex class II molecule, displays antigenic
peptides on professional antigen presenting cells for recognition
by alpha-beta T cell receptor (TCR) on HLA-DR-restricted CD4-positive
T cells, and guides antigen-specific T-helper effector functions,
both antibody-mediated immune response and macrophage activation.
20
Since there are antibodies against autoantigens
in the peripheral blood of OAPS patients, it is speculated that HLA-DRA
in serum mainly participates in regulating antigen presentation and
then plays a role in autoimmune response. TNFSF13B is a cytokine that
binds to the tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 13B(TNFRSF13B)
and Tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 17(TNFRSF17).
Tumor necrosis factor ligand superfamily member 13(TNFSF13) binds
to the same two receptors. Together, they form a two ligands-two receptors
pathway involved in the stimulation of B- and T-cell function and
the regulation of humoral immunity.
21
Our proteomic analysis reveals proteins with significant changes
in metabolism-related pathways including alanine aspartate glutamate
and tyrosine metabolism. There are no previous reports of abnormal
amino acid metabolism in OAPS patients, and the reason for these metabolic
disorder may be OAPS patients were treated with low-molecular-weight
heparin which may cause drug-induced liver damage.
22
Notably, our results indicated disturbance of pathways
related to fat acid deregulation in OAPS patients, and the levels
of the triglyceride in the two groups showed a significant difference.
Accelerated atherosclerosis is a recognized complication of APS, which
is not explained by traditional cardiovascular risk factors. It is
reported that the Ox-LDL/β2GP I/anti-β2GPI complex could
induce vascular endothelial dysfunction.
23
In addition, the lipid metabolism disorders in the OAPS patients
may be one of the reasons for the atherosclerosis. We also found that
proteins from OAPS patients were enriched in glucose metabolism as
well as in diabetes-related functions, including pentose and glucuronic
acid interconversion pathways, and diabetes pathway proteins; however,
there was no significant difference in fasting blood glucose levels
between the two groups.
Furthermore, we also found some apoptosis-
and lysosome-related
pathway proteins, suggesting that apoptosis and autophagy were activated
in the pathological process of the antiphospholipid syndrome. FAS
was found to have significant upregulation in autoimmune and pathogen
infection pathway. A recent research has shown that neutrophil extracellular
traps would promote the inflammation and apoptosis in trophoblast
cells, indicating that antiphospholipid antibodies aggravate the adverse
pregnancy by mediating apoptosis.
10
,
24
APLs from
OAPS patients may induce cellular stress evidenced by increased activation
of the mTOR, autophagic, and lysosome pathway proteins, including
alpha-galactosidase A (catalyzes the hydrolysis of glycosphingolipids
and participates in their degradation in the lysosome), arylsulfatase
(ARSA), cathepsin W (CTSW), beta-glucuronidase (GUSB)-cocaine esterase
(CES2), and acid ceramidase (ASAH1)-neutral ceramidase (ASAH2), which
may play a role in the pathogenesis of OAPS. APLs from women with
adverse pregnancy induce cellular stress evidenced by mitochondrial
hyperpolarization and increased activation of the mTOR and autophagic
pathways which may play a role in the pathogenesis of obstetric APS.
23
,
25
However, we acknowledge some limitations of this study at
present.
The serum level of proteins could not be simply corresponded to the
tissue level of the proteins and the exact mechanisms of these proteins.
Next, further studies are required to trace the origin of these proteins
and understand their functions under disease conditions, both in vitro
and in vivo, for future mechanism studies.
