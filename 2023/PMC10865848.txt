Introduction
Selenium (Se) is a trace element that participates in the structure of eukaryotic selenoproteins in the form of the twenty-first amino acid, selenocysteine (SeCys)
[1]
. Selenoproteins hold at least one SeCys
[2]
, being crucial for humans and animals to regulate their diverse biological functions, including antioxidant protection, immunity, and thyroid hormone production
[3]
. Fish, compared to other vertebrates, have a higher number of selenoproteins, among which salmonids have the highest number of selenoproteins, which could be related to the duplication event of their genome that occurred during the evolutionary transition from diploidy to tetraploidy
[4]
. Three groups among the 41 selenoproteins identified in teleost fish until now
[5]
are well-characterized. The first group of selenoproteins has antioxidant activity in the cell against the damaging impact of reactive oxygen species (ROS)
[6]
. Selenoenzymes, such as glutathione peroxidase (GPx) family and thioredoxin reductases (TR), regulate the antioxidant defense mechanisms by controlling the intracellular amount of organic hydroperoxides, as well as providing reducing agents required for the prevention of lipid peroxidation [
7
,
8
]. The second group of selenoproteins is iodothyronine deiodinases, essential for the success of thyroid hormone metabolism. Thyroid hormones are released predominantly as thyroxine (T
4
) into the bloodstream and then converted to the more active form, triiodothyronine (T
3
), by the selenoproteins type I or II deiodinases
[9]
. As the third type of selenoproteins, selenoprotein P (SePP) is synthesized in the liver and secreted to plasma to play a central role in the transport of Se into other body tissues
[10]
. In addition to transport activity, SePP has antioxidant properties and can actively reduce phospholipid hydroperoxides
[11]
.
On the market, Se products are available in three forms comprising organic, inorganic, and nanoparticles (nano-Se). It has been shown that the bioavailability and metabolism of these Se forms are significantly different in fish. According to the published literature, organic Se (e.g., selenomethionine: SeMet) has higher digestibility and bioavailability than inorganic forms (e.g., sodium selenite)
[12]
. However, published reports increasingly indicate that Se at the nanoscale level is more effective for the enhancement of the growth rate, reproductive performance, and overall health of fish than the other forms of Se
[13]
,
[14]
,
[15]
,
[16]
,
[17]
,
[18]
,
[19]
. Nanoparticles, due to exclusive characteristics such as tiny dimensions (1 to 100 nm), wide surface area, and hydrophobicity, have better dispersion in circulation, more interactions with cell membrane, and faster diffusion into cells [
7
,
8
]. The main advantages of dietary nano-Se over bulk Se are that Se nanoparticles have better gut absorption and lower toxicity to fish
[20]
.
Since Se is being included in the fish diet as a necessary trace element, an increasing number of aquaculture studies have used nutritional approaches to investigate the impacts of Se on fish performance. The supplementation of nano-Se in aquaculture diets has attracted significant research interest in the last few years, mainly due to its positive impacts on fish production
[20]
. Until now, almost all of these published papers report that supplementation of nano-Se in aquafeeds has resulted in increased growth performance, enhanced immune response, and reduction in lipid peroxidation and oxidative damage [
16
,
17
,
20
,
21
]. However, few researchers have studied the nano-Se metabolism and function in fish at the molecular level. Recent developments in high-throughput technologies and the increasing amount of “omics” data enable systematic and integrated approaches to studying nano-Se productivity in fish.
The suffix –ome means “complete” or “whole” and was first used in 1920 to name the whole genes of an organism as “genome.” Afterward, the suffix –Omics was applied to the term “genomics” for large-scale studies of all genes. Similarly, these suffixes have been included in the words “transcriptomics” for the high-throughput analysis of all transcripts (transcriptome), “proteomics” for analyzing all the proteins (proteome) as well as “metabolomics” for studying all the metabolites (metabolome) in a biological system under a particular condition (
Fig. 1
) [
22
,
23
]. Unlike the genome, which is a static entity, the proteome of any biological system has a dynamic nature and varies with time and the organism's state. The main goals of genomic studies are providing information about the location of genes on chromosomes, detecting single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), identifying candidate loci responsible for phenotypes, and analyzing the genetic structure of populations based on genomic data
[24]
. Thus, genomics cannot be used to recognize how the expression of genes changes in a given tissue or the whole organism in response to a specific condition. Gene expression patterns can be examined at two levels: (1) the mRNA molecules (transcripts) and (2) the proteins as the final products of gene expression. Both transcriptomic and proteomic analyses provide an opportunity to elucidate the differential regulation of genes within a particular developmental stage or in response to an altered condition. However, proteomics is more attractive for researchers because it provides information about the whole gene expression and post-transcriptional and post-translational modifications
[25]
. Metabolomics is also a rapidly developing field among Omics to analyze changes in the metabolite profile in a biological system induced by internal and external factors
[26]
. Like the proteome, the metabolome is also time sensitive but much more dynamic than the proteome
[27]
. It is well known that nutritional factors can affect gene expression patterns and metabolic processes in cells, tissues, or organisms [
6
,
28
]. Today, Omics tools have provided promising applications in fish nutrition, allowing scientists to understand the mechanisms of action of micronutrients like Se.

The data presented in this review highlights the potential of using these techniques for fish nutrition scientists in future research.
The status of nano-Se supplementation in aquafeeds
Nano-Se as a growth enhancer
Adequate Se supplementation in aquafeeds is essential for the growth and development of fish. Previous studies show that organic and nano sources of Se have higher bioavailability than inorganic Se sources for aquatic species
[29]
. However, numerous studies have shown that nutritional Se nanoparticle has even better effects than organic Se on fish growth performance. For instance, Saffari et al.
[13]
compared the biological effects of organic (SeMet), inorganic (sodium selenite), and nano forms of Se on common carp (
Cyprinus carpio
) at a concentration of 0.7 mg/Kg diet. Their results showed that nano-Se is a more efficient growth enhancer than other Se sources. Similar improvements in growth performance as an effect of nano-Se have been demonstrated by other authors in different species such as red seabream (
Pagrus major
)
[30]
, Nile tilapia (
Oreochromis niloticus
) [
31
,
32
], grass carp (
Ctenopharyngodon idella
)
[33]
, crucian carp (
Carassius auratus gibelio
)
[34]
, European seabass (
Dicentrachus labrax
)
[35]
, and striped catfish (
Pangasianodon hypophthalmus
)
[36]
. However, several other authors observed no significant beneficial effects related to nano-Se supplementation in a few species studied, such as rainbow trout (
Oncorhynchus mykiss
) [
19
,
37
] and yellowfin seabream (
Acanthopagrus latus
)
[38]
. These discrepant results in different studies may be related to species-dependent physiological responses, size and life stages, level of nano-Se supplementation, water quality, and husbandry conditions [
16
,
39
].
The growth-boosting effects of Se nanoparticles could be attributed to the biological roles of selenoproteins. For example, deiodinases are a group of selenoproteins participating in the regular metabolism of thyroid hormones. In fish, thyroid hormones are synthesized mainly as thyroxine (T
4
) in the thyroid gland and then released into the bloodstream. Type I and II deiodinases are involved in converting T
4
to its more active compound, triiodothyronine (T
3
)
[40]
. It is known that there is a positive correlation between T
3
level and growth hormone gene transcription in the pituitary gland cells of fish [
41
,
42
]. In this context, Khan, Zuberi
[43]
found that supplementing nano-Se in diet enhanced serum growth hormone levels in juvenile
Tor putitora
compared to the control group receiving no nano-Se supplementation. Besides, Izadpanah, Saffari
[15]
showed that larvae descended from the
Acanthopagrus arabicus
brooders fed on 2–4 mg/ Kg nano-Se diets had higher T
3
levels and enhanced length gain. Thus, these results confirm a close relationship between Se intake, T
3
level, and growth performance in fish.
Furthermore, previous studies have shown that Se enhances endogenous digestive enzyme production in fish
[44]
. Thus, the growth-promoting effect of nano-Se supplementation in fish could be partly attributed to the higher synthesis of digestive enzymes, resulting in better digestion, absorption, and metabolism of micronutrients. For example, it has been demonstrated that dietary nano-Se improved growth performance and enhanced digestive enzyme levels in Nile tilapia [
32
,
45
]. Besides, fish growth performance promoted by Se supplementation is also correlated to enhanced mucosal length/width, increased number of goblet cells, and augmented cell proliferation and protein synthesis in the intestinal epithelium, which are beneficial for higher nutrient assimilation in fish by enhancing the intestinal surface area [
32
,
45
,
46
]. A study by Saffari et al.
[47]
on common carp also detected that the best feed efficiency ratio and the tallest intestinal villi in the foregut and midgut were recorded in the group fed nano-Se diet compared to the inorganic (sodium selenite) and organic (SeMet) groups.
Effects of nano-Se on antioxidant status and immune function
Oxygen consumption in aerobic living organisms leads to the constant generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), such as hydrogen peroxide and lipid hydroperoxides, which can damage nucleic acids, proteins, and lipids. The role of the antioxidant defense system is to balance the ROS production and removal in cells. The antioxidant defense system consists of two parts, encompassing the enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant systems. The non-enzymatic antioxidant system includes a set of low-molecular-weight compounds such as vitamin C, vitamin E, and carotenoids. Superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GPx), catalase (CAT), glutathione S-transferase (GST), and thioredoxin reductase (TR) constitute the enzymatic part of the cellular antioxidative defense system
[48]
, among which Se is an integral part of the thioredoxin reductases and glutathione peroxidases
[49]
.
Numerous studies have shown that dietary nano-Se can significantly increase GPx activity in various fish species such as
Cyprinus carp
[
13
,
18
],
Carassius auratus gibelio
[34]
,
Dicentrachus labrax
[35]
,
Ctenopharyngodon idella
[33]
,
Tor putitora
[43]
,
Oreochromis niloticus
[
50
,
51
]. However, some of these studies found that dietary nano-Se enhanced GPx activity but had no positive effects on other antioxidant enzymes such as CAT and SOD. These data reveal that GPx as a selenoenzyme is more capable of responding to Se supplementation than other antioxidant enzymes. On the other hand, a study on
Oreochromis niloticus
[52]
showed that nano-Se supplementation had no significant effect on GPx activity but enhanced levels of SOD and CAT activities and decreased malondialdehyde (MDA) concentration. As MDA is the end product of lipid peroxidation, it can be concluded that nano-Se supplementation has reduced oxidative damage and increased antioxidant capacity by reinforcing antioxidant enzymes other than GPx.
Available reports in the fisheries and aquaculture world indicate that Se plays an immunomodulatory role in fish by triggering the synthesis of selenoproteins that are involved in the proliferation, differentiation, and activation of immune cells such as lymphocytes, natural killer cells, and neutrophils [
37
,
44
,
47
,
53
]. Nano-Se also positively influences innate and acquired immune responses by defending immune cells against oxidative damage and enhancing the synthesis of antibodies, total protein, globulin, lysozyme, total immunoglobulin, and complement pathways [
18
,
54
]. For example, a study conducted by Zahmatkesh et al.
[55]
revealed that supplementing nano-Se at 1 mg/Kg diet improved lysozyme, albumin, and globulin levels in
Rutilus caspicus
. In another study in rainbow trout, nano-Se at 0.3 mg/Kg diet improved lysozyme, total immunoglobulin, and albumin concentrations
[56]
. Several other studies on various species found that adequate dietary nano-Se improved humoral immune parameters in fish [
18
,
37
,
47
,
52
,
57
,
58
].
Challenging fish with pathogens is another efficient strategy to assess immune competence in addition to the direct evaluation of non-specific immune parameters. For example, a study demonstrated that nano-Se supplementation at 1 mg/Kg diet reduced cumulative mortality in
Pangasianodon hypophthalmus
after the
Aeromonas veronii
challenge
[59]
. In
Sparus aurata
, nano-Se at 0.5 and 0.75 mg/Kg diet increased the survivability of the fish exposed to
Vibrio parahaemolyticus
[60]
. Similarly, a study by Eissa et al.
[32]
reported that supplementing nano-Se at 1 mg/Kg diet decreased the mortality rate in
Oreochromis niloticus
following the
Aspergillus flavus
challenge.
Effects of nano-Se on reproductive performance and larval quality
Selenium is one of the essential micronutrients in animal reproduction, and Se-deficient diets can negatively influence the reproductive performance of both female and male breeders [
61
,
62
]. Selenium is required for the normal development of spermatozoa, and the effect of Se on male fertility could be connected to the presence of selenoprotein GPx4 in sperm
[63]
. A study by Wischhusen et al.
[64]
on rainbow trout broodstock found that a Se-deficient plant-protein diet with a Se level of 0.3 ppm reduced the number of females spawning, which can be increased by supplementing Se. Based on available reports, most studies conducted to assess the effects of dietary nano-Se on fish have been restricted to larval or juvenile stages, and information on the impacts of dietary nano-Se supplementation on broodfish and their offspring is scarce. In a study on female
Acanthopagrus arabicus
breeders, Saffari et al.
[14]
found that supplementing nano-Se at 2–4 mg/Kg in plant-protein diets improved reproductive performance by enhancing fertilization and hatching rates and survival and length of the larvae descended from the same group of broodfish. It was also reported that dietary nano-Se improved the antioxidant status of female broodfish and their progeny
[65]
. Working on the same species, Khademzade et al.
[66]
demonstrated that including nano-Se at 2–4 mg/Kg in plant-protein diets improved sperm kinetics and enhanced fertility potential in male broodfish. These results indicate that nano-Se is an efficient source of Se for improving the reproductive performance in male and female broodfish.
Application of quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) and transcriptomics in nano-Se research
Transcriptome profiling provides a foundation for studies targeting the genes expressed in a living organism under diverse developmental stages or environmental conditions. This type of study is essential for detecting transcripts related to molecular mechanisms ruling biological processes
[67]
. Recent progress in next-generation sequencing technology (NGS) enables whole transcriptome profiling, even in an organism without a genomic reference
[68]
. This new technique is more efficient for the comprehensive assessment of the complex transcriptome compared to its predecessors, such as expressed sequence tag (EST) technology and microarray
[67]
. The recent literature on the application of transcriptomics in various aspects of aquaculture, such as reproduction, developmental stage, nutrition, immunity, and toxicology, has already been reviewed by Chandhini and Rejish Kumar
[69]
. In this section, we review transcriptomic studies on nano-Se supplementation in fish (

Identification of molecular mechanisms underlying immunomodulation of nano-Se in diseased fish
Nano-Se exerts beneficial effects via the regulation of genes involved in antioxidant defense, lipid metabolism, and innate immunity
[132]
Non-specific humoral immunity plays a central role in the health and survival of fish against pathogenic invasions and stresses in husbandry conditions
[70]
. The immunomodulatory effects of dietary nano-Se in fish have been reported in numerous former studies [
16
,
18
,
71
]. In a trial conducted to compare the efficiency of two forms of Se (sodium selenite and nano-Se) on growth performance, health status, and resistance of Nile tilapia (
Oreochromis niloticus
) against
Streptococcus iniae
, the expression of immune-related (Lysozyme: LYZ and Immunoglobulin M: IgM) and antioxidant-related (Superoxide dismutase: SOD and Glutathione peroxidase: GPx) genes were analyzed
[50]
. The fish were fed the basal diet (control) or the basal diet fortified with 0.7 mg/Kg nano-Se or sodium selenite for ten weeks. The trial fish were challenged by
Streptococcus iniae
injection at the beginning of the ninth week. The quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis following the bacterial challenge revealed that the expression rates of immune and antioxidant genes were pointedly higher in nano-Se compared to in sodium selenite or control groups.
Dawood et al.
[52]
added nano-Se spheres produced by lactic acid bacteria to a basal diet at the level of 0.5, 1, 1.5, and 2 mg/Kg and studied its influences on growth and health performances, antioxidant competence, and immune-related genes in Nile tilapia after eight weeks. The analysis of tissue samples by qRT-PCR demonstrated that TNF-α (tumor necrosis factor-alpha) expression was up-regulated in the liver and spleen of the fish fed on nano-Se supplemented diets. The results also revealed that nano-Se up-regulated the liver IL1β (interleukin-1 beta) gene expression but has no significant effect on spleen IL1β.
In addition to the chemical form, size, and physical properties of Se, other factors such as feeding strategy and duration also influence its permeability and functionality. Regarding this premise, Abd El-Kader et al.
[57]
evaluated the impacts of nano-Se feeding regimens on the performance of European seabass (
Dicentrachus labrax
). The control group of this trial was fed the basal diet continuously. The R1 group received the nano-Se diet (1 mg/ Kg) daily. The R2 group fed the basal and nano-Se diets every other day, and the R3 group fed the basal diet for two days, then the nano-Se every two days. The results showed that the fish receiving nano-Se diets with different feeding strategies (daily, every other day, or every two days) had significantly higher growth rates, better feed conversion ratio (FCR), and improved immunity. Also, the relative expression of a couple of hepatic genes comprising growth hormone (GH), insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH), interleukin-8 (IL-8), interleukin-1 beta (IL1β), and HSP70 genes was assayed. The qRT-PCR tests showed that HSP70 was down-regulated in the R1, R2, and R3 groups, while the expression of GH, IGF-1, GAPDH, IL-8, and IL1β genes was up-regulated. The authors appropriately correlated the up-regulation of GH and IGF-1 genes to the decreased FCR and enhanced growth rate. Moreover, the up-regulation of IL-8 and IL1β and the down-regulation of HSP70 were attributed to the anti-inflammatory and anti-stressor effects of Se nanoparticles.
Some previous studies have shown that Se nanoparticles and other micronutrients such as vitamin C
[72]
, vitamin E
[37]
, and trace minerals
[71]
can synergize the beneficent effects of each other on fish performance, immunity, and antioxidant protection. In this context, several studies investigated the potential interactive effects of nano-Se and other nutritional factors on the expression of genes in Nile tilapia (
Oreochromis niloticus
). Dawood et al.
[73]
reported the synergistic effects of nano-Se and vitamin E on the expression of immune-related genes of Nile tilapia. The results indicated that dietary supplementation of nano-Se alone (1 mg/ Kg) or combined with vitamin E (100 mg/ Kg) led to the up-regulation of TNF-α and IL1β genes in the liver and spleen tissues. The same genes in the same tissues of Nile tilapia were also up-regulated in response to the supplementation of 1 mg/ Kg nano-Se or 1 mg/ Kg nano-Se plus 500 mg/ Kg vitamin C
[74]
. The highest expression of TNF-α and IL1β genes was observed in the group fed with combined Se nanoparticles and vitamin C. Furthermore, the individual or combination effects of nano-Se and
Spirulina platensis
(a blue-green alga) were studied on the gene transcripts of Nile tilapia
[75]
. The expression of hepatic SOD and TNF-α genes was up-regulated, and the transcription of HSP70 was down-regulated in fish fed on diets supplemented with nano-Se (1 mg/ Kg),
Spirulina platensis
(1 g/ Kg), and both nano-Se and
Spirulina platensis
. Finally et al.
[76]
investigated the potential of chitosan nanoparticles as a nano delivery system for Se in Nile tilapia nutrition. In this study, the control group was fed a basal diet containing organic Se, and the other groups’ diets were fortified with selenite-loaded chitosan nanoparticles (SeChNPs) at levels of 0.5, 1, and 2 g/ Kg. Enrichment with 2 g/ Kg SeChNPs up-regulated the liver expressions of glutathione peroxidase (1.12 fold), superoxide dismutase (4.9 fold), and catalase (2.31 fold), as compared to the control group. Also, the results indicated that supplementation with SeChNPs down-regulated the expression of the myostatin gene in the 0.5 and 1 mg/ Kg groups resulting in higher muscle mass and better growth performance. Besides, different levels of SeChNPs up-regulated the immune-related genes involving interleukin-10 (IL-10), IL-8, TNF-α, and IL1β in a dose-dependent manner. In response to the
Aeromonas hydrophila
challenge, the inflammatory caspase-1 gene was up-regulated in the 1 and 2 mg/ Kg groups for protection against microbial pathogens. The liver expressions of stress-related genes such as HSP70 and cytochrome P450 genes also declined in the SeChNPs groups following bacterial challenge.
The effects of four diets with different levels of nano-Se (0, 0.5, 1, and 1 mg/ Kg) on the expressions of immune-related selenoprotein genes and their synthesis factors were studied in the mono-sex Nile tilapia fingerlings for three months
[77]
. The obtained data revealed that the transcripts of immune-related genes (GPx2, Se1J, Se1L, Se1K, Se1S, Se1W, and Sepp1a) and their synthesis factors (SPS1 and Sc1y) increase in different tissues of the 1 mg/ Kg group in the second month of feeding. However, the authors concluded that the results of their study need to be evaluated in a model fish species to identify the mechanisms involved in the expression patterns of immune-related genes in response to dietary nano-Se.
Abd El-Kader et al.
[35]
conducted a study to examine the potential of dietary Se nanoparticles for enhancing the growth rate and immunity of European seabass (
Dicentrachus labrax
). After feeding fish with diets containing 0 (control), 0.25, 0.5, and 1 mg/ Kg nano-Se for 90 days, the highest expression levels of liver immune-related genes (GH, IGF-1, IL-8, and IL1β) were recorded in the 0.25 and 0.5 mg/ Kg groups followed by the 1 mg/ Kg group. Also, the expression of the HSP70 gene was down-regulated in the nano-Se-supplemented groups compared to the control group, demonstrating that Se nanoparticles have no stressful or inflammatory effects on European seabass.
Ghrelin is a peptide hormone synthesized by the stomach with crucial functions in fish, such as control of growth via the growth hormone (GH)/ insulin-like growth factor I (IGF-I) axis and regulating energy balance by affecting appetite and food intake
[78]
. Ghrelin treatment has different effects on food intake in various fish species. While ghrelin enhanced food intake in goldfish and tilapia, it reduced food intake in rainbow trout
[79]
. Selenium-deficient diets can reduce the appetite of fish
[80]
. In this context, Jahanbakhshi et al.
[81]
studied the effects of different sources of Se (selenomethionine and nano-Se) on the performance and expression of growth (IGF-1) and appetite-related (ghrelin) genes of goldfish (
Carassius auratus
). The experimental fish fed on diets supplemented with similar doses (0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9 mg/ Kg) of selenomethionine or nano-Se for nine weeks. The results indicated that selenomethionine and nano-Se promote liver IGF-1 and ghrelin expressions. However, the expression rates of the genes were higher in the nano-Se groups.
Production of resilient fish that can survive the harmful conditions induced by xenobiotics is vital. Heavy metals, such as cadmium (Cd), are among the most toxic substances found in aquatic ecosystems. Cd can accumulate in living organisms resulting in detrimental consequences, including the production of free radicals, peroxidation of lipids, and damage to cell membrane
[82]
. Selenium is an integral part of the antioxidant defense system to combat oxidative impacts caused by xenobiotics. Abu‐Elala et al.
[83]
studied the protective effects of nano-Se against sub-lethal Cd toxicity in Nile tilapia. In the first phase, the fish were fed a basal diet (control) or a nano-Se diet (1 mg/ Kg) for four weeks. In the second phase, the control fish were divided into two subgroups: G1 was fed the basal diet, and G2 was fed the basal diet and exposed to Cd (5 mg/ L). The nano-Se groups were also separated into two subgroups: G3 fed on the nano-Se diet and G4 fed on the nano-Se diet and exposed to Cd. The expression of growth, antioxidant, and immune-related genes was measured in the first phase. The results indicated that IGF-1, CAT, TNF-α, and IL1β genes up-regulated in the liver of fish fed on the nano-Se diet. Based on the results from the second phase, nano-Se alleviated the pathological damages induced by Cd.
4‑tert-buthylphenol (4-tBP), a phenolic monomer with endocrine disrupting effect, is extensively utilized in the synthesis of industrial substances, and is highly toxic to fish
[84]
. Cui et al.
[85]
investigated the potential of nano-Se in alleviating the harmful effects of 4-tBP on the hepatocytes of grass carp (
Ctenopharyngodon idella
). The results indicated that co-treatment of nano-Se with 4-tBP suppresses the ROS-PARP1 pathway to reverse the mitochondrial apoptosis and necroptosis caused by 4-tBP.
Dietary lipids play a central role in fish nutrition to supply energy, essential fatty acids, fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, and K), phospholipids, and cholesterol for typical health and growth. Also, lipids are known to act as a protein-sparing agent, and there is a tendency in aquaculture to replace proteins used for energy production with lipids
[86]
. However, excessive lipids in high-fat diets (HFD) can negatively impact the performance, body composition, and overall health of fish by inducing liver fat deposition and oxidative stress
[87]
. Regarding the protective function of Se against oxidative damages
[88]
, Liu et al.
[89]
investigated the effects of nano-Se supplementation on the intestinal health of the grass carp (
Ctenopharyngodon idella
) fed on HFD. The fish were fed a regular diet, a HFD, and a HFD enriched with 0.6 mg/ Kg nano-Se for ten weeks. Supplementation with nano-Se up-regulated the expression of genes related to the tight junction (ZO-1, claudin-3, and occludin), anti-oxidation (GPx4a and GPx4b), and also the protein of ZO-1 in the intestine tissue. Moreover, adding nano-Se to HFD suppressed the expression of genes involved in inflammation (IL-6, IL-8, IL1β, TNF-α, and IFN-γ), signaling molecules (TLR4, p38 MAPK, and NF-kB p65), as well as expression of TNF-α and NF-kB p65 proteins. These data indicated that nano-Se supplementation could relieve the intestinal damage of the fish fed on HFD by declining the inflammatory and oxidative substances.
As the bioavailability of different sources of Se differs in fish, Ghaniem et al.
[90]
compared the efficacy of inorganic, organic, and nano-Se in Nile tilapia. The fish were fed a basal diet and three diets supplemented with different sources of Se (1 mg/ Kg) for 65 days. The results revealed that the supplementation with Se improved growth performance and up-regulated the expression of immune-related genes (IL-1β and TNF-α), with the highest expression rates in the organic and nano-Se groups.
Global warming is an unceasing problem with irreversible harmful impacts on ecosystems and living creatures
[91]
. Ectothermic animals, including fish, are more susceptible to heat stress caused by global warming. Depending on the duration and severity, heat stress can result in physiological disorders and even death
[92]
. Supplementation of aquafeeds with additives such as Se is one of the main cost-effective measures to alleviate the damages of heat stress by reinforcing the defensive mechanisms of fish against stress-induced ROS. In this context, Li et al.
[93]
investigated the beneficial effects of nano-Se supplementation (0, 5, and 10 mg/ Kg) on rainbow trout under heat stress conditions (24 °C). The results revealed that supplementation with nano-Se (5 mg/ Kg) up-regulated the expression rates of the HSP70b, HSP90a1, GPx1a, and Trx genes to help stressed fish cope with oxidative damage. The same research team
[94]
, analyzed the intestinal gene expressions of two groups of rainbow trout fed on a basal diet and the basal diet supplemented with 5 mg/ Kg nano-Se to heat stress conditions. After heat stress, the expression levels of intestinal tight junction proteins (ZO-1, claudin-8d, and occludin) were lower in the un-supplemented fish. In the presence of nano-Se, the expressions of heat shock proteins (Hsp70b, Hsp90α, and Hsp30), selenoproteins (Gpx1a, Gpx1b1, and Trx), and anti-inflammatory cytokine (TGB-β) increased whereas the expression of two pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β and TNF-α) declined. These authors also reported that nano-Se mitigates heat stress-induced apoptosis via the activation of PI3K/AKT pathway
[95]
. This conclusion was supported by the higher expression rates of apoptosis-related genes (caspase3, caspase9, Cyt-c, Bax, and Bax/Bcl-2) and lower mRNA levels of P13K and AKT genes in the liver of heat-stressed fish without nano-Se supplementation. These researchers also added 5 μg/mL of nano-Se to cultured hepatocytes of rainbow trout and studied the response of hepatocytes to heat stress using transcriptome sequencing
[96]
. Transcriptome profiling of the nano-Se-treated hepatocytes demonstrated that most of the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were related to immune function and belong to five immune-related pathways, including FoxO signaling, RIG-I-like receptor signaling, C-type lectin receptor signaling, Toll-like receptor signaling, and Rachidonic acid metabolism. These pathways constitute a network enabling resistance to heat stress.
Alternative splicing (AS) is a post-transcriptional regulatory mechanism in eukaryotic organisms allowing the production of multiple mRNA isoforms from a single gene. These diverse mRNA isoforms enable the generation of proteins essential for eukaryotes in various developmental stages or environmental conditions
[97]
. Liu et al.
[98]
used RNA-seq datasets to depict AS events in rainbow trout hepatocytes after nano-Se supplementation and exposure to heat stress. The transcriptome analysis showed that nano-Se activated the phosphatidylinositol signaling pathway and enhanced the mRNA levels of the related genes (PI4KA, DGKH, ITPK1, and Ocrl). Also, nano-Se stimulated the plaque kinase-PI3K-Akt signaling system and calcium channels. These AS events lessen the inflammatory processes and support resistance to heat stress.
Ferroptosis, a nonapoptotic cell death induced by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, is involved in various pathological cell death and can be diagnosed by measuring the accumulated redox-active iron, the loss of antioxidant competence, and the peroxidation of phospholipid-containing fatty acids
[99]
. Zhu et al.
[100]
studied the antibacterial efficacy of the nano-Se extracted from
Bacillus subtilis
in
Onychostoma macrolepis
after a bacterial challenge by
Aeromonas hydrophila
. The results showed that
Aeromonas hydrophila
infection led to ferroptosis in the liver. Feeding with a diet supplemented with the biogenic nano-Se (0.9 mg/ Kg) up-regulated the expression of GPx4, Nrf2, and FPN1 genes. These findings indicated that nano-Se exerted its antibacterial effects by inhibiting ferroptosis in fish liver.

The effect of nano-Se on liver proteome modifications was studied in rainbow trout using two-dimensional gel electrophoresis (2DE) and MALDI-TOF/TOF mass spectrometry (MS)
[124]
. The fish were divided according to test diets (the basal diet without nano-Se supplementation and the 1 mg kg
−1
nano-Se- supplemented diet) and cultured in high rearing density (80 Kg m
−3
) for 60 days. Supplementation with nano-Se increased the expression of metabolic enzymes responsible for aerobic glycolytic pathways, amino acid metabolism, and energy-producing pathways. More interestingly, the expression of two enzymes with anti-tumor activity in humans, including glycine N-methyltransferase/hydroxypyruvate reductase (GNMT) and 10-formyltetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase (FDH), was shown to increase in the liver proteome profile of fish supplemented with nano-Se. In this study, the authors did not confirm the authenticity of mass spectrometry for protein identification by validating assays such as Western blot and qRT-PCR tests. However, the proteomic changes were correlated well with the physiological and metabolic parameters examined in the study.
The potentiality of nano-Se as an anti-heat stress micronutrient was investigated using non-gel-based proteomics in rainbow trout
[125]
. The fish were fed with a basal diet or basal diet enriched with 5 mg kg
−1
nano-Se. The proteomic comparisons of the liver of fish before (18 °C) and after (24 °C) heat stress was performed by Data-Independent Acquisition (DIA) and Sequential Windowed Acquisition of All Theoretical Fragment Ions (SWATH). The results revealed that dietary nano-Se alleviates the apoptotic effects of heat stress by increasing the abundance of heat shock proteins (HSPs) to retain cell function and homeostasis, declining the expression of proteins responsible for lipid metabolism, and up-regulating selenoproteins to reinforce the antioxidant defense under heat stress condition. In this study, the validation of proteomics data was tested at the mRNA levels of eight genes by qRT-PCR, showing that the mRNA expression trends were comparable to protein expression trends.
Application of metabolomics/ metabonomics in nano-Se research
Metabolomics is the study of all low-molecular-weight (<1000 Da) metabolites responsible for a phenotype expressed by the genome and altered by environmental parameters at a particular time point
[126]
. Metabonomics which is used interchangeably with metabolomics is a subset of metabolomics for studying metabolites produced in living systems in response to pathogenic factors, drugs, and mutations
[127]
. Two technologies consisting of nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) are used for metabolite profiling in biological samples
[128]
.
Until now, only one metabonomics study on the nutritional effects of nano-Se in fish is publicly available. Using LC-MS, Li et al.
[129]
investigated the metabolic modifications in the liver tissue of rainbow trout fed with nano-Se under heat stress conditions. The experimental groups were fed diets containing two levels of nano-Se (0 or 5 mg kg
−1
) at 18 °C for nine days. The water temperature was then raised to 24 °C and maintained for eight hours. The results indicated that heat stress adversely impacted the metabolism of glycerophospholipids and fatty acids. However, adding nano-Se to the diet mitigates heat stress by down-regulating galactose metabolism and activating pathways responsible for glutamate and glutamine metabolism.
Application of multi-omics in nano-Se research
To date, most molecular studies have applied a reductionist methodology. In this approach, a complex system breaks into its component pieces which are studied separately to solve a problem. In contrast to reductionism, systems biology is a comprehensive approach that maintains the constituent fragments together to obtain a larger picture of the system
[130]
.
Proteins are complex biomolecules responsible for almost all cellular tasks, and by nature, their abundance is related to mRNA abundance. However, the mRNA level sometimes does not correlate well with the protein level in a biological system
[131]
. Concerning this disparity, integrating transcriptomics and proteomics can give a more reliable image of a system
[130]
. Moreover, since the metabolome reflects all changes in the genome, transcriptome, and proteome, incorporating metabolomics into multi-omics approaches builds a systems biology framework to study a subject
[23]
.
Despite this benefit, only one recent research applied this approach. Using serum proteomic and tissue (liver and kidney) transcriptomic analyses on zebrafish fed with nano-Se in healthy or disease (injected with
Aeromonas hydrophila
) conditions, Xia et al.
[132]
searched for the molecular mechanisms underlying the immunomodulation of Se nanoparticles. The results showed that dietary nano-Se exerted its immunomodulation benefits via regulating genes involved in antioxidant defense, lipid metabolism, and innate immunity in both healthy and disease conditions. This study also revealed that the biological functions of nano-Se in both healthy and diseased zebrafish were almost the same, except for the ROS formation. While nano-Se suppressed ROS in healthy fish, it stimulated the formation of ROS in infected individuals, maybe as a defense against pathogens.
Species, tissue, Omics type, and country contributions
Four public databases, including Google Scholar, PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus, were searched for peer-reviewed research papers on dietary nano-Se supplementation in fish. The related articles were then screened for the Omics experiments for writing this review paper (
Table 1
). Analyzing data from the 24 selected papers shows that Nile tilapia (
Oreochromis niloticus
) and rainbow trout (
Oncorhynchus mykiss
) have been the main species for the Omics studies of nano-Se supplementation in fish (
Table 1
). It seems that the economic or ecological importance of the species in the region of the study has been the main factor in species selection. Despite the importance of zebrafish as a model for various biological studies, only one work has employed it in the Omics studies of nano-Se to date
[132]
. Based on the proteomic and transcriptomic reports reviewed in this article, some differentially expressed proteins or genes in the fish species of interest have been matched to previously identified sequences of other species. This problem can occur when working on species whose genome is not fully sequenced [
105
,
117
,
122
,
123
]. The accurate protein or transcript matching rate is predictable in the future as the number of aquaculture fish sequences in protein and gene banks increases.
Different tissue types such as liver, spleen, muscle, gill, kidney, intestine, and serum have been used for investigating nutritional nano-Se effects on fish. However, as the liver is a crucial organ for many metabolic processes
[133]
, most papers analyzed in this review (57 %) have focused on the liver tissue (
Table 1
).
The Omics approaches have been used solely or integrated to study the effects of nano-Se supplementation in fish. Incorporating more types of Omics datasets offers the ability to track the multifunctional role of nano-Se in a given system. Of the research papers reviewed above, only one study applied a muli-omics approach (
Table 1
). Despite the reduced cost of the Omics technologies, the funding for high-throughput multi-omics studies is still a problem. This obstacle has hindered many researchers from obtaining multiple Omics datasets on the same experimental samples.
Based on the author's institutional affiliation, only six countries, including China, Egypt, Iran, India, Thailand, and the USA, have contributed to the Omics studies of nano-Se supplementation. Among the participating countries (

Conclusion and future perspectives
The literature review of the studies related to nano-Se supplementation points toward the efficacy of this trace element for enhancing the growth and well-being of different fish species. The above Omics studies of nano-Se indicate that this trace microelement participates in the immunity, anti-apoptosis, and anti-inflammation chief pathways. These findings can be further analyzed using multi-omics studies on whole-genome sequenced model fish species to offer a more accurate picture of nano-Se physiological mechanisms. A few studies also revealed that co-treatment of nano-Se with xenobiotics and environmental endocrine disrupters could reverse the apoptotic and inflammatory consequences. These results can establish a bedrock for future Omics studies on nano-Se to combat global environmental problems such as microplastics.
