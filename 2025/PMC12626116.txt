Allylic systems bearing tertiary
and quaternary carbon centers are privileged frameworks widely present
in natural products and drugs (
Scheme
A).
Thus, not surprisingly,
numerous methods have continued to emerge for accessing these important
synthons.
Among them, the Pd catalyzed
Tsuji–Trost allylic alkylation reaction is the most prominent
method, which proceeds under mild conditions and offers excellent
regio- and stereoselectivity.
This protocol
operates via oxidative addition of Pd(0) to an allylic electrophile
to form a π-allyl-Pd­(II) key intermediate, followed by the nucleophilic
substitution by a carbon nucleophile (
Scheme
B-1).
In a similar
approach, decarboxylative allylic alkylation uses allyl carbonates
or β-ketoesters to in situ generation of a π-allyl-Pd­(II)-nucleophile
pair (
Scheme
B-2).
This transformation also proceeds under mild
conditions and has been highly effective for the facile construction
of tertiary and quaternary centers with high levels of regio- and
stereoselectivity.
However, the major
drawback of both of these approaches is the need for prefunctionalization
of allylic substrates, which adds extra synthetic steps. To solve
this shortcoming, a few methods have been developed for the direct
formation of the C–C bond from the allylic C–H bond.
Thus, Li and co-worker demonstrated the first
example of allylic C–H alkylation, employing a cobalt–copper/peroxide
system, which eliminated the need for prefunctionalization of allylic
component (
Scheme
B-3).
However, the use of strong oxidant
limits this protocol to nonfunctionalized cyclic alkenes and 1,3-dicarbonyl-containing
secondary nucleophiles. Later, White and co-workers reported Pd­(II)-catalyzed
allylic C–H alkylation of terminal alkenes in the presence
of 2,6-DMBQ oxidant (
Scheme
B-4).
In this approach, Pd­(II),
upon coordination to an alkene, activates the allylic C–H bonds
to form a π-allyl-Pd­(II) complex, the key Tsuji–Trost
reaction intermediate to subsequently provide reaction products via
reaction with carbon nucleophiles. Although, this protocol is applicable
to a wide range of terminal alkenes and nucleophiles, it is not applicable
to internal alkenes and electron-deficient alkenes, a common limitation
of the Pd­(II/0) allylic C–H substitution approach.
Furthermore, it predominantly produces linear
products and is limited to the use of relatively weak nucleophiles
(p
K
a
< 20). Herein, we report a mild
photoexcited Pd catalyzed allylic C–H alkylation reaction of
internal alkenes with carbon pronucleophiles that solves all of the
above limitations (
Scheme
C). This protocol enables the formation of tertiary and quaternary
centers at allylic position in a diverse range of cyclic and linear
alkenes with varied substitution patterns and electronic properties.
Furthermore, this protocol is also applicable to a wide range of carbon
pronucleophiles, including those with p
K
a
> 20.

Background and Methods for Allylic Alkylation
Recently, we developed a set of allylic C–H
amination, oxygenation,
and sulfonylation reactions via Pd­(0/I/II) photocatalysis (
Scheme
A).
In these transformations, excitation of Pd(0) by blue light
triggers a single electron transfer (SET) process with an aryl bromide
to generate an aryl radical.
The latter
undergoes hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) from a weak allylic C–H
bond. The resulting allyl–Pd­(I) species undergoes radical–polar
crossover (RPC) to form a π-allyl-Pd­(II) intermediate, capable
of reacting with a nucleophile (
Scheme
A). Naturally, we were eager to evaluate the possibility
to use this approach toward the allylic C–H alkylation reaction.
Although this method proved to be highly effective with heteroatom-based
nucleophiles, extending this approach to carbon pronucleophiles did
not seem straightforward. Thus, in addition to general pitfalls, such
as β-hydride elimination and homodimerization of alkenes, which
are common side reactions with a Pd­(0/I/II) approach, there are some
specific challenges associated with carbon nucleophiles (
Scheme
B).

In conclusion, we have
developed a broadly applicable strategy
for the allylic C–H alkylation of internal alkenes that operates
under photoinduced Pd catalysis. By harnessing a homolytic activation
pathway rather than the conventional electrophilic manifold, this
method significantly broadens the alkene scope with regard to substitution
patterns and electronics. The protocol also accommodates a wide range
of carbon pronucleophiles including those with p
K
a
> 20, enabling direct construction of tertiary and
quaternary
carbon centers from allylic C–H bonds. These findings establish
a versatile platform for C–C bond formation at the allylic
position, expanding the synthetic toolbox for accessing densely functionalized
molecular architectures. Initial studies on the enantioselective version
of this reaction support the feasibility for development of general
and efficient enantioselective allylic C–H alkylation reaction.
