1.
Introduction
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a systemic autoimmune disease characterized by chronic joint inflammation, involving complex interactions among genetic, environmental, and immunological factors [
1
,
2
]. Beyond articular manifestations, RA patients frequently develop extra-articular complications, among which interstitial lung disease (ILD) represents one of the more common and serious comorbidities [
3–5
]. RA-ILD not only leads to progressive respiratory impairment but is also associated with increased infection risks and reduced quality of life [
3
]. Epidemiological data reveal that RA-ILD patients face a 2- to 10-fold higher mortality risk compared to RA patients without ILD [
5
], with approximately 50% of cases progressing to progressive pulmonary fibrosis [
6
]. The median survival time ranges from merely 2 to 14 years [
7
]. However, effective early intervention strategies remain limited.
Current RA-ILD diagnosis primarily relies on high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT), though its utility for routine screening is constrained by radiation exposure and cost considerations [
8
]. While serological biomarkers including anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibody (anti-CCP) [
9
,
10
], Krebs von den Lungen-6 (KL-6) [
11–14
], and matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP7) [
15
] show some diagnostic potential, their sensitivity and specificity require further improvement. Although KL-6 has been incorporated into Japanese diagnostic guidelines for ILD [
16
], its applicability in Chinese populations warrants additional validation. Therefore, exploring more reliable biomarker combinations may contribute to enhanced early detection of RA-ILD.
Recent advances in proteomic technologies have provided new approaches for biomarker discovery [
17
,
18
]. Mass spectrometry-based quantitative proteomic methods, such as tandem mass tag (TMT) labeling, have been progressively applied to investigate molecular mechanisms in various diseases [
19
,
20
], though their utilization in RA-ILD research remains relatively underexplored. Existing studies suggest that RA-ILD pathogenesis may involve alveolar epithelial injury, dysregulated immune activation, and fibrotic progression [
21
,
22
], yet the complete molecular regulatory network awaits full elucidation.
Based on the aforementioned factors, this study aims to employ TMT-based quantitative proteomics to preliminarily analyze serum protein expression differences between RA-ILD patients and RA-non-ILD controls, with the goal of identifying potential diagnostic biomarkers. Through bioinformatic analysis, we seek to uncover key molecules associated with pulmonary fibrosis and immune regulation, followed by validation in an independent cohort. Ultimately, we hope to develop a potential supplementary approach for RA-ILD early screening and provide reference data for future investigations.
2.
Materials and methods
2.1.
Ethical approval
This single-center retrospective observational study was conducted with approval from the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University (BR/AF/SG-03/1.0; Approval Numbers: IIT-2022-105, IIT-2024-252), with waiver of informed consent. The research strictly adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, with particular emphasis on protecting patient privacy and rights.
2.2.
Study population
This study employed a three-phase design with the following cohorts:
(1) Discovery phase: 10 first-diagnosed RA inpatients (5 RA-non-ILD and 5 RA-ILD) admitted to the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University between November 2021 and November 2022. (2) Targeted validation phase: 27 treatment-naïve RA patients (14 RA-non-ILD, 13 RA-ILD) enrolled during the same period. (3) Clinical application phase: An expanded cohort of 110 first-diagnosed RA patients (59 RA-non-ILD, 51 RA-ILD) admitted from September 2022 to December 2024.
Inclusion Criteria: (1) The 2010 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for rheumatoid arthritis [
23
]; (2) RA-ILD group: Patients who met the diagnostic criteria for RA and were clinically diagnosed with RA-ILD by multidisciplinary discussion based on HRCT findings consistent with the 2013 ATS/ERS radiological features of interstitial pneumonia and the exclusion of other known causes. All patients in this group were initially diagnosed with interstitial lung disease by HRCT [
24
]; (3) RA-non-ILD group: Patients who fulfilled the RA diagnostic criteria but showed no evidence of interstitial lung disease on HRCT. All patients in this group were newly diagnosed and treatment-naïve; (4) Availability of complete clinical data, written informed consent for peripheral blood collection, and compliance with follow-up visits.
Exclusion Criteria: (1) Other autoimmune diseases (e.g. mixed connective tissue disease, systemic sclerosis, or systemic lupus erythematosus); (2) ILD attributable to alternative etiologies (e.g. drug-induced or environmental exposures); (3) Concurrent pulmonary comorbidities (e.g. lung cancer, tuberculosis, COPD, or asthma); (4) Recent major trauma/surgery or severe dysfunction of respiratory, circulatory, genitourinary, or endocrine systems.
2.3.
Study design
The study employed a three-phase analytical strategy, as illustrated in
Figure 1
. In the discovery phase, we performed TMT-based quantitative proteomics analysis to identify differentially expressed proteins using samples from 5 RA-ILD patients and 5 RA-non-ILD controls. The targeted validation phase subsequently enrolled 13 RA-non-ILD and 14 RA-ILD patients to verify the candidate proteins through enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Finally, the clinical application phase expanded the cohort to 59 RA-non-ILD and 51 RA-ILD patients to construct a predictive model by integrating the previously identified protein biomarkers with relevant clinical parameters.

2.4.
Sample size calculation
Discovery phase (
n
= 10): An exploratory design was adopted following the minimal sample size principle (5 per group) recommended by the MIAPE-Quant guidelines for proteomics studies, without formal power calculation.
Targeted verification phase (
n
= 27): Based on the smallest differential protein (AGT, 1.7-fold change) identified in the discovery phase and an assumed 20% inter-batch variation, a two-sample
t
test (α = 0.0125, Bonferroni-corrected) was performed, indicating that ≥ 12 samples per group were required. The actual enrollment included 14 versus 13 cases, achieving a post hoc power >85%.
Clinical application phase (
n
= 110): The sample size was determined using the Hanley-McNeil method, assuming a combined biomarker AUC of 0.90 with a 95% CI width ≤0.08. Ultimately, 59 versus 51 cases were included, meeting the precision requirement.
2.5.
Collection of clinical data and sample preservation
Eligible patients were identified through the hospital information system (HIS), with systematic collection of demographic characteristics (age, sex, and smoking history) and laboratory parameters measured within 24 h of admission, including C-reactive protein (CRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), immunoglobulin G (IgG), rheumatoid factor (RF), anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibodies (anti-CCP), alanine aminotransferase/aspartate aminotransferase (ALT/AST), uric acid (UA), and white blood cell count (WBC). Standardized joint assessments were performed to record the 28 tender joint count (TJC28) and 28 swollen joint count (SJC28), with disease activity evaluated using the Disease Activity Score 28 (DAS28) calculated from ESR values and categorized as remission (<2.6), low (2.6–3.2), moderate (3.2–5.1), or high (>5.1) activity. HRCT images were independently evaluated by two blinded radiologists. For biomarker analysis, 3 mL fasting blood samples were collected from all participants within 24 h of admission, with serum separated and stored at −80 °C until further processing.
2.6.
Major experimental equipment and reagents
The key instruments used in this study included a Q Exactive HF mass spectrometer and an EASY-nLC 1200 liquid chromatography system (Thermo Fisher Scientific), an SDS-PAGE gel electrophoresis apparatus (Beijing Liuyi Instrument Factory), a fully automated digital gel image analysis system (Shanghai Tanon), an eStain LG protein staining instrument (GenScript, Nanjing), and a high-pH reversed-phase liquid chromatography system (Agilent).
For sample preparation and analysis, the following major reagents were used: a high-abundance protein depletion kit (Millipore), a BCA protein assay kit, formic acid, unstained protein molecular weight marker, and TMT10plex isobaric labeling reagent (Thermo Scientific); phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF) and SDS lysis buffer (Beyotime Biotechnology); iodoacetamide (IAA, BBI Life Sciences); dithiothreitol (DTT, Damas-Beta); acetone and absolute ethanol (General-Reagent); trypsin (Worthington Biochemical); and triethylammonium bicarbonate (TEAB, Sigma-Aldrich). For biomarker validation, commercially available ELISA kits were used to measure matrix MMP3, VWF, angiotensinogen (AGT) (Hangzhou Lianke Biotechnology), and laminin subunit alpha-2 (LAMA2) (Shanghai Jianglai Biotechnology), and the sialylated carbohydrate antigen (KL-6) assay kit (Sekisui Medical, Japan).
2.7.
Sample analysis
2.7.1.
Proteomic profiling and data analysis
The TMT-based quantitative proteomic analysis of serum samples from 5 RA-ILD (tre1-tre5) and 5 RA-non-ILD (con1-con5) patients was performed in collaboration with Shanghai Lu Ming Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The experimental procedure was as follows: (1) high-abundance protein depletion using affinity chromatography; (2) protein quantification
via
BCA assay; (3) reduction (10 mM DTT, 37 °C for 1 h) and alkylation (55 mM IAA, dark for 30 min) of 50 μg protein; (4) tryptic digestion (1:50 w/w, 37 °C overnight); (5) TMT 10-plex labeling; (6) high-pH reverse-phase fractionation (Agilent 1260 Infinity II); and (7) nanoLC separation (EASY-nLC 1200) coupled to Q Exactive HF mass spectrometry(MS1 resolution: 60,000; MS2 resolution: 45,000; dynamic exclusion: 30 s).
The raw mass spectrometry data were processed using Proteome Discoverer 2.4 software, with searches performed against the UniProt human protein database (version February 2022). The false discovery rate (FDR) was set at <1%, with screening criteria of ≥1 unique peptide and Sequest HT score >0. For quantitative analysis, data reproducibility was assessed by inter-sample Pearson correlation coefficients, principal component analysis (PCA), and relative standard deviation (RSD). Differential protein screening criteria were defined as |fold change| ≥1.2 (or ≤0.83) with
p
< 0.05.And subjected to functional annotation (Gene Ontology [GO]: biological process, cellular component, molecular function), pathway enrichment (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes, KEGG), and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis (STRING v11.5, confidence score >0.7). All bioinformatics analyses were performed using R (v4.4.2).
2.7.2.
Serum biomarker quantification
Serum MMP3, VWF, AGT, and LAMA2 levels were measured by ELISA. All measurements were performed in triplicate. Prior to the formal experiment, the laboratory conducted preliminary tests using standard samples at high, medium, and low concentration gradients. These were measured 10 times within the same 96-well plate (intra-assay) and 5 times in each of three consecutive independent batches (inter-assay). The resulting coefficients of variation were <8% for intra-assay and <12% for inter-assay measurements. The detection procedure was as follows: after equilibrating reagents and samples to room temperature, standards were prepared by 2-fold serial dilution (MMP3/VWF/AGT) or used directly (LAMA2). Following plate washing (300 μL/well, 30 s), 100 μL samples were added and incubated (2 h at RT for MMP3/VWF/AGT; 1 h at 37 °C for LAMA2). Detection involved: (1) 50 μL 1:100 detection antibody (MMP3/AGT); (2) 100 μL 1:100 HRP-conjugated antibody (VWF); or (3) sequential 100 μL biotinylated antibody and enzyme conjugate (LAMA2). After TMB development (5–30 min) and reaction termination, absorbance was measured at 450/630 nm. Serum KL-6 levels were measured using a latex-enhanced immunoturbidimetric assay on an AU5800 automated biochemical analyzer, following the standard operating procedure.
2.8.
Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0, with data visualization conducted in R (version 4.4.2)
via
R-Studio. Categorical variables were expressed as percentages (%) and compared using χ
2
tests. For continuous variables, normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed with independent samples
t
tests (assuming equal variances), while non-normally distributed data were reported as median (M) with interquartile range (IQR; M25, M75) and compared using the Mann–Whitney
U
test.
To evaluate the diagnostic performance of MMP3, VWF, and KL-6 for RA-ILD, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed, with area under the curve (AUC), 95% confidence intervals (CI), sensitivity, and specificity calculated. Optimal cutoff values were determined using Youden’s index. Spearman’s correlation analysis assessed relationships between differentially expressed variables.
For risk factor analysis, univariate logistic regression was performed first, followed by multivariate logistic regression to identify independent predictors. Based on univariate results and biomarker cutoff values, variables were dichotomized to construct a nomogram. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test evaluated model calibration, while ROC analysis and calibration plots assessed internal validation. Decision curve analysis quantified clinical utility. A two-tailed
p
< 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

3.2.
Proteomic quality control and preliminary screening of differentially expressed proteins
Quality control analysis of the proteomic data revealed substantial inter-individual variability among the initial 10 samples. Notably, sample Con2 exhibited significant dispersion, while Tre1 and Tre2 demonstrated suboptimal clustering consistency with other samples within their respective groups (
Figure 2A

This process resulted in the retention of six samples (Con1/3/5 from RA-non-ILD and Tre3/4/5 from RA-ILD) for downstream analyses. Principal component analysis confirmed distinct intergroup separation among the filtered samples (
Figure 2B
), validating the appropriateness of our selection approach.

3.3.
Functional characterization of differentially expressed proteins and biomarker selection
Bioinformatic analysis of the 80 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) revealed distinct functional patterns (
Figure 3
). GO annotation classified these proteins into three major categories: (1) biological processes (BP, 30 proteins, 38%) enriched in complement activation (classical pathway) and lipoprotein metabolic processes; (2) cellular components (CC, 75 proteins, 94%) predominantly localized to extracellular regions and exosomes; and (3) molecular functions (MF, 39 proteins, 49%) involving signal receptor binding and immunoglobulin receptor activity (
Figure 3A
). KEGG pathway analysis identified 20 significantly enriched pathways (
p
< 0.05), including cholesterol metabolism (APOC1, APOA2) and complement/coagulation cascades (VWF) (
Figure 3B
). Notably, 17 upregulated proteins served as core components in these key pathways (
Table 3
). PPI network analysis highlighted VWF and AGT as central hubs (
Figure 3C
).

Four candidate biomarkers-VWF, MMP3, LAMA2, and AGT—were prioritized based on: (1) Magnitude of differential expression (fold change >1.5); (2) Pathway significance (
p

3.4.
Validation and analysis of candidate biomarkers
ELISA results demonstrated significantly elevated serum levels of MMP3 (
p
= 0.025) and VWF (
p
= 0.027) in the RA-ILD group (
n
= 13) compared to RA-non-ILD controls (
n
= 14), while AGT and LAMA2 showed no significant intergroup differences (
p
> 0.05) (
Table 4
). ROC curve analysis revealed diagnostic potential for both MMP3 (AUC = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.61–0.90) and VWF (AUC = 0.75, 95% CI: 0.60–0.91). At optimal cutoff values (MMP3: 71,597.15 pg/mL; VWF: 27,659.43 ng/mL), MMP3 exhibited higher specificity (92.9% at 46.2% sensitivity), whereas VWF showed superior sensitivity (100.0% at 50.0% specificity). Notably, combined detection enhanced diagnostic performance (AUC = 0.80, 95% CI: 0.67–0.93; Youden’s index = 0.56; sensitivity = 76.9%, specificity = 78.6%).

4.
Discussion
This study is the first to demonstrate that serum MMP-3 and VWF are potential biomarkers for rheumatoid arthritis-associated interstitial lung disease (RA-ILD). Through proteomic screening and multi-stage validation, we developed a nomogram that integrates MMP-3, VWF, KL-6, and clinical parameters (age, sex). This model demonstrated excellent performance (AUC = 0.89) in identifying ILD in newly diagnosed RA patients, providing an efficient, non-invasive tool for early risk stratification.
Our study reframes the pathological understanding of RA-ILD beyond alveolar epithelial injury to include the dual pillars of ‘matrix remodeling and vascular endothelial disruption.’ The significant upregulation of MMP-3 not only echoes its established role in RA-related joint destruction [
25–28
] but also underscores its critical function in pulmonary fibrosis. Evidence indicates that MMP-3 directly contributes to fibrogenesis by driving epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and activating pro-fibrotic pathways such as TGF-β [
29–33
]. Concurrently, the elevated level of VWF, a marker of vascular endothelial injury, reveals the disruption of the pulmonary vascular barrier as another core pathological feature of RA-ILD. This observation aligns with findings in other connective tissue disease-related ILD, such as systemic sclerosis [
34
], suggesting that endothelial dysfunction may be a common pathway in autoimmune-mediated lung involvement. Thus, MMP-3 and VWF complement the alveolar epithelial injury paradigm represented by KL-6 [
11
,
15
,
35
,
36
], offering two complementary dimensions—matrix remodeling and vascular damage—that together delineate a more complete pathological picture of RA-ILD.
The translational journey of this study, from discovery to validation, illustrates a central challenge in biomarker research. While highly sensitive proteomic technologies can identify a large number of candidate proteins, not all can be confirmed in independent cohorts using targeted methods like ELISA. The failure of AGT and LAMA2 to show significant differences in the validation phase underscores the impact of clinical sample heterogeneity and the variable sensitivity across technology platforms. This observation reinforces the critical importance of a multi-stage, rigorous validation strategy and confirms that the biomarkers which ultimately pass this process—MMP-3 and VWF in our study—possess superior robustness and reliability.
The most significant innovation of this study lies in proposing a multi-dimensional biomarker strategy for diagnosis. While KL-6 demonstrates high sensitivity, its specificity is limited [
37
,
38
]. We found that a subset of RA-ILD patients (15.7%, 8/51) who were KL-6 negative exhibited significantly elevated levels of MMP-3 and VWF. This suggests that a combined assay can effectively compensate for false negatives of KL-6 and potentially identify an ILD subtype characterized by predominant pathological features of matrix remodeling and endothelial injury. Consequently, the panel ‘KL-6 (epithelial injury) + MMP-3 (matrix remodeling) + VWF (endothelial injury)’ establishes a more comprehensive assessment framework from distinct pathological dimensions, thereby elevating the diagnostic performance to an AUC of 0.90. Consistent with established knowledge, advanced age was confirmed as a significant risk factor for RA-ILD [
39
]. Furthermore, multivariate analysis indicated that male sex might be a protective factor, which contrasts with some previous reports [
40–42
]. This discrepancy could stem from the unique characteristics of our cohort or might reveal a distinct pathophysiological mechanism in RA-ILD regarding sex, differing from other fibrotic lung diseases [
43
]. No significant association was observed with smoking history in our cohort, potentially implying that the driving mechanisms of RA-ILD are more reliant on intrinsic autoimmune abnormalities than on environmental exposure alone. Ultimately, we successfully developed a nomogram model by integrating clinical parameters with these biomarkers, enabling convenient and reliable individualized risk assessment. Although the absolute increase in the AUC for the combined detection (KL-6 + VWF + MMP-3) compared to KL-6 alone (ΔAUC = 0.03) did not reach statistical significance (
p
= 0.129) with the current sample size, reclassification analysis demonstrated that the nomogram correctly identified nearly 10% of missed high-risk patients, underscoring its clinical added value. Statistical power analysis indicates that a larger sample size (approximately 300–400 cases) is required to fully validate the incremental value of MMP-3 and VWF, thus directing future research. A prospective, multi-center validation study designed specifically to address this issue has now been formally initiated.
This study has several limitations. Firstly, in the biomarker screening phase, although 80 differentially expressed proteins were identified using highly sensitive TMT–LC–MS/MS technology, only four candidate biomarkers (MMP3, VWF, AGT, and LAMA2) were validated, and the manual selection process (based on FC >1.5,
p
< 0.05, etc.) may have introduced subjective bias. The limited sample size (
n
= 6) and exclusion of 40% of the original samples in the proteomics phase may also affect the representativeness of the findings. Secondly, as an exploratory-phase investigation, this study has inherent limitations in sample size that may affect the generalizability of findings, the sample sizes in each phase were relatively small (discovery phase
n
= 6, validation phase
n
= 27, clinical phase
n
= 110), and the study was a single-center, retrospective study. Additionally, important confounding factors (e.g. treatment history, gastroesophageal reflux) and known risk factors for RA-ILD (e.g. high-titer anti-CCP antibodies, RF) [
44
] were not included, and key clinical indicators such as pulmonary function were lacking. Thirdly, the cross-sectional design limited our ability to evaluate the dynamic changes in biomarkers and their prognostic value, as well as their predictive role in treatment response. Finally, the lack of an independent external validation cohort is a limitation that must be addressed before the model can be translated into clinical practice.
Despite the limitations mentioned above, the biomarker panel and predictive model established in this study hold clear potential for clinical translation. The model is practical, requiring only a single blood draw for assessment, making it highly suitable for use in rheumatology clinics as an initial screening tool prior to HRCT. This approach could optimize the identification and management of high-risk patients, potentially reducing unnecessary medical radiation exposure and costs. Future work should focus on validating the model’s efficacy in large-scale, multi-center prospective studies and elucidating the precise mechanistic roles of MMP-3 and VWF in the pathogenesis and progression of RA-ILD.
5.
Conclusion
This study aimed to address the challenge of early diagnosis for rheumatoid arthritis-associated interstitial lung disease (RA-ILD). Through a three-stage validation process, we established for the first time that serum MMP-3 and VWF serve as novel diagnostic biomarkers for RA-ILD and identified their combination with KL-6 as the optimal diagnostic panel. Building on this core finding, we developed a nomogram that integrates these biomarkers with key clinical parameters. The model demonstrated excellent discriminative performance, providing a user-friendly, non-invasive tool for early screening and risk stratification. Although future multi-center validation is warranted, this work undeniably offers a novel perspective and a powerful instrument for managing RA-ILD, holding clear promise for clinical translation.
