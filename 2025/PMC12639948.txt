Background
Staphylococcus aureus
can cause a spectrum of infections, ranging from minor superficial infections to severe invasive diseases. This species ranks among the most frequently isolated pathogens in clinical settings and is the most prevalent gram-positive bacterium [
1
]. Methicillin-resistant
S. aureus
(MRSA) is a major nosocomial pathogen that poses substantial challenges to infection control due to its dynamic epidemiology and intricate resistance mechanisms [
2
].
Epidemic MRSA strains exhibit considerable regional diversity, influenced by factors including geographical location and host characteristics [
3
]. Currently, the predominant hospital-ascquired MRSA MRSA (HA-MRSA) clones circulating globally include clonal complex (CC) 22 - staphylococcal cassette chromosome
mec
(SCC
mec
) IV (EMRSA-15) and CC30-SCCmec II (EMRSA-16) in the United Kingdom [
4
]; CC5 and CC45-SCC
mec
IV in Germany [
5
]; and sequence type (ST) 239-SCC
mec
III in South America and Asia [
6
]. Analysis of Chinese multicentre surveillance data highlighted ST239-t030-III, ST239-t037-III, and ST5-t002-II as prevalent clones in 2011, with dominance shifting to ST59-t437-IV after 2016 [
7
,
8
]. A 2022 multicentre longitudinal study identified ST59-t437-IV, ST239-t030-III, and ST5-t2460-II as current epidemic clones [
9
]. Distinct geographical distributions were also evident across Chinese administrative regions. Specifically, ST59-t437-IV was predominant in Sichuan, Jiangxi, Fujian, and Zhejiang; ST5-t2460-II was prevalent in Guangdong, Shanghai, and Hubei; ST239-t030-III was dominant in Inner Mongolia; and ST45-IVa was the primary epidemic strain in Hainan [
10
]. These findings reveal substantial geographical variability in MRSA infection rates and epidemiological characteristics, highlighting pronounced regional disparities in clonal distribution. Moreover, the persistent emergence and dissemination of new clones drive the ongoing evolution of predominant epidemic strains. Therefore, elucidating the epidemiological characteristics and genetic underpinnings of MRSA strains is imperative for developing precise prevention strategies and mitigation of infection risks.
MRSA strains exhibit lineage-specific pathogenic characteristics, including unique antimicrobial resistance profiles and virulence-determinant distributions. These features undergo dynamic evolution in conjunction with clonal succession [
11
]. Studies have indicated that the ST239 lineage is resistant to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, ciprofloxacin, aminoglycosides, and macrolides. Conversely, ST5 lineages demonstrate inherent susceptibility to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole with generally lower resistance levels. Among the community-acquired MRSA (CA-MRSA) strains, ST59-t437 clones exhibited superior growth characteristics, including significantly higher survival rates in human blood, toxin production, and cytotoxicity, compared to ST239-t030 strains [
12
]. However, the ST239-t030 strains exhibit superior adhesion capabilities and biofilm formation [
13
]. Investigating the evolutionary patterns of MRSA lineages can inform targeted prevention and control strategies.
Xinjiang, a strategically important border region in northwestern China, is characterized by its extensive land area, proximity to multiple countries, ethnic diversity, and status as a primary livestock production base. These distinctive geographical and sociocultural factors collectively contribute to the formation of complex microbial transmission networks [
14
]. In this study, we systematically collected clinically isolated MRSA strains from different geographical regions (northern and southern Xinjiang) in Xinjiang, China. The objective of this study was to analyse the spatial distribution patterns, antimicrobial resistance profiles, virulence factor carriers, and molecular evolutionary relationships among MRSA strains. The results will establish a foundation for the precise management of MRSA infections in Xinjiang, thereby informing enhanced control strategies against multidrug-resistant healthcare-associated bacteria.
Methods
Collection of MRSA isolates
From January 1 to December 31, 2024, consecutive clinical MRSA isolates were collected from hospitals in Urumqi, Alaer, Tumxuk, and Yining in the Xinjiang region of China. Non-viable isolates and those with mixed bacterial growths were excluded. Duplicate isolates from the same patient and specimens were excluded. The specimen types primarily consist of sputum (
n
= 47, 38.52%), secretions (
n
= 50, 40.98%), bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (
n
= 5, 4.10%), whole blood (
n
= 8, 6.56%), drainage fluid (
n
= 5, 4.10%), feces (
n
= 1, 0.82%), biopsy samples (
n
= 1, 0.82%), and urine (
n
= 2, 1.64%), among others (
n
= 3, 2.46%).
Cultivation, identification, and antimicrobial susceptibility tests
After reviving the strains on blood agar plates (Zhengzhou Antu Bio, Zhengzhou, China), single colonies were grown in sodium chloride nutrient broth (Qingdao Haibo Bio, Qingdao, China). A mass spectrometry identification system (Bruker, Germany) was used to identify the samples. Bacterial suspensions with a turbidity of 0.5 McFarland units were then prepared. The susceptibility of the strains to the following antibiotics was tested using the VITEK 2 Compact System (Merieux, France): penicillin, oxacillin, tobramycin, kanamycin, amikacin, gentamicin, ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin, erythromycin, clindamycin, linezolid, vancomycin, quinupristin/dalfopristin, tetracycline, tigecycline, rifampin, and trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole. The minimum inhibitory concentrations were interpreted for susceptibility based on the 2023 Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) breakpoints [
15
]. Quality control strains ATCC 25923 and ATCC 43300 were purchased from the China Collection of Microorganisms. The results were compared with the national MRSA resistance levels reported in the China Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance Report (2024) for January–December 2024 (
http://www.chinets.com
).
Bacterial DNA extraction
Bacterial DNA was extracted using the boiling method. First, 1000 µL of the bacterial suspension was collected and centrifuged at 12,000 ×
g
for 10 min. Then, 200 µL of distilled water was added to the bacterial suspension and placed in a 100 °C boiling water bath for 15 min. Afterward, the bacterial suspension was cooled in an ice bath, and the mixture was centrifuged again at 12,000 ×
g
for 10 min. Finally, the supernatant was used as the template for subsequent PCR.
Detection of antibiotic resistance genes and virulence genes
PCR was performed to detect antibiotic resistance genes. The antibiotic resistance genes encompassed those conferring resistance to β-lactams (
mecA

16

Multilocus sequence typing
PCR technology was used to detect seven housekeeping genes in
S. aureus
:
arc
C,
aroE
,

gmk

https://pubmlst.org/

http://www.phyloviz.net/

Staphylococcal cassette chromosome
mec
(SCC
mec
) typing
SCC

18

Staphylococcal protein A (
spa

PCR was used to detect the

20
]. The amplified fragments were purified, subjected to Sanger sequencing by Xinjiang Youkang Biotechnology Co., Ltd., and analysed using the Spatyper website (
http://spatyper.fortinbras.us/
).
Statistical analysis
The WHONET5.6 software was used to analyse the antimicrobial susceptibility test data. SPSS v25.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.5 software were used to create graphs and analyse the data. Count data are expressed as n (%), and comparisons among the four cities were performed using the chi-squared test. A
P
-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Antimicrobial susceptibility analysis of MRSA isolates from different regions
Antimicrobial susceptibility testing of 122 MRSA isolates revealed complete resistance to both penicillin and oxacillin, while all strains remained susceptible to linezolid, vancomycin, quinupristin/dalfopristin, tigecycline, and rifampin, consistent with national antimicrobial resistance surveillance data. Among aminoglycosides, the resistance rates to tobramycin, amikacin, and gentamicin were generally low, whereas resistance to kanamycin was notably high. The antimicrobial resistance profile of this study differed significantly from the CHINET national surveillance data: clindamycin showed a higher resistance rate, whereas gentamicin showed a lower resistance rate (
P
< 0.05). For quinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, and moxifloxacin), the overall resistance rates were relatively low; however, significant regional differences were observed (
P
< 0.05). Specifically, resistance to quinolones in the Tumxuk region and to tetracycline in the Alaer region was markedly higher than in other regions (Table
1
).

mec

Notably, CC59 strains were primarily isolated from sputum and secretions, whereas CC22 strains were predominantly found in secretions, with CA infections serving as the primary epidemiological source (Table

Discussion
The clonal distribution of MRSA exhibits significant spatiotemporal heterogeneity, with its prevalence and epidemiological characteristics undergoing dynamic evolution [
21
]. In recent years, the continuous emergence of novel MRSA clones worldwide has highlighted the importance of sustained surveillance of molecular characteristics, host adaptability, and transmission mechanisms of new strains across different ecological regions [
22
]. The 2024 CHINET data, showing a marked decline in the national MRSA isolation rate from 69% to 29.2% over the past two decades (
http://www.chinets.com
), highlighted the effectiveness of nationwide infection control measures and antimicrobial stewardship programs. In the present study, the average MRSA detection rate in Xinjiang was found to be lower than the national average. However, regional disparities within Xinjiang suggest possible differencesin local infection control protocols or in the distribution ofcirculating MRSA clones.The results of this study conducted in the Xinjiang region indicate that ST59-t437-IV isolates have supplanted the previously dominant ST239-t030-III isolates, establishing themselves as the most prevalent epidemic type in the area. This observation aligns with the national trends of MRSA [
9
]. Notably, CC22, which has been sporadically reported in domestic studies, has emerged as the second most prevalent clone in this region. The predominant type within this clonal group is ST22-t223-IV, contrasting with ST22-t309-IV, which has been reported in other regions of China. These findingshighlight the distinctiveness of the MRSA population structure in Xinjiang [
23
,
24
].
ST59 has emerged as the dominant lineage in East Asia, particularly in China. Previous reports and the present study have shown that the ST59-IV-t437 clone exhibits high resistance to erythromycin, clindamycin, and kanamycin [
25
]. Comparative analysis of predominant ST59-t437-IV strains from Urumqi and Alaer revealed striking regional differences in tetracycline resistance patterns. Alaer isolates exhibited significantly higher tetracycline resistance (75.00%) than Urumqi isolates (7.15%). The elevated carriage rates of
tetK
and
tetM
correlated well with the observed resistance phenotypes. These differences suggest that variations in antibiotic usage strategies, including the selection of antimicrobial classes and the intensity with which they are applied, may lead to the differential acquisition of mobile genetic elements carrying resistance determinants even among strains that share the same core genome (as defined by MLST).Given that studies in the Hanzhong region of China have identified ST59-t437 as the dominant lineage in livestock-associated (LA) isolates, and considering that tetracycline resistance is a recognized hallmark of LA-MRSA [
26
], this study proposes that the ST59-t437 clone in this region may also have an LA-MRSA origin or may have acquired its resistance determinants via horizontal gene transfer from LA-MRSA. This hypothesis warrants further investigation.
The ST22 clone, particularly the prevalent EMRSA-15 strain (ST22-MRSA-IV), has been identified as a significant pathogen that causes hospital- and community-acquired infections worldwide. ST22-MRSA-IV has become a focal point of international public health concern owing to its high transmissibility and adaptability [
27
]. The ST22-t223-IV isolate, predominantly identified in the Xinjiang region and referred to as the “Gaza clone,” carries the
tsst-1
gene and has been reported in countries such as Kuwait, Egypt, and Saudi Arabia [
28
–
30
]. The ST22-t005-IV isolates exhibited regional dominance in Tumxuk and carried the
pvl
and
tsst-1
virulence genes. They also exhibited a multidrug-resistant phenotype against ten antibiotics. These combined characteristics endow the strain with enhanced pathogenicity and transmission risk. Notably, this strain has not yet been reported in China. Previous reports have indicated that the coexistence of
pvl
and
tsst-1
in MRSA is relatively rare [
31
,
32
]. Notably, ST22-t005-IV isolates harbouring
pvl
and
tsst-1
genes have been linked to fatal necrotising pneumonia and sepsis [
33
], with similar strains reported in Pakistan, Nepal (showing high prevalence), and other regions [
34
,
35
]. The border ports of Tumxuk (Kashgar region), which is adjacent to multiple Central Asian countries, create conditions for cross-border transmission of MRSA and may also drive the local adaptive evolution of the ST22 strain.
This study revealed distinct epidemiological features of LA-MRSA clones compared with those reported in other Chinese regions [
36
]. Although the dominant national clones CC398 and CC97 were relatively concentrated in Alaer, their overall isolation rates remained low, consistent with previous Xinjiang surveillance data [
37
]. Furthermore, these Alaer clones exhibited unique resistance profiles compared with Urumqi isolates, showing high-level resistance to tetracycline, tobramycin, kanamycin, and gentamicin. These findings suggest that localized antimicrobial pressure in Alaer may have selected for bacterial subpopulations with divergent resistance traits.
This study has some limitations. First, sample collection was confined to four regions of Xinjiang, with relatively small sample sizes in some areas. Second, the cross-sectional study design with single-timepoint sampling may restrict data representativeness and hinder a comprehensive understanding of the dynamic epidemiological characteristics and transmission patterns of MRSA in this region. Finally, whole-genome sequencing was not conducted on the isolates, which would have allowed for a deeper investigation into their evolutionary history and molecular characteristics.
Conclusions
In conclusion, this study systematically characterized the molecular epidemiology of MRSA in the Xinjiang region in China, identifying CC59-ST59-t437 and CC22-ST22-t223 as predominant clones with distinct regional variations in antimicrobial resistance and virulence profiles. These findings highlight the necessity for region-specific interventions: active surveillance of high-risk ST22 clones, evidence-based empirical therapy guided by local resistance patterns, and continuous monitoring of molecular evolution. The results provide critical scientific support for optimizing
S. aureus
infection control strategies in western China.
