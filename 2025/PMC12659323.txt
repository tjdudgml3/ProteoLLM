Introduction
According to global cancer statistics, the incidence of breast cancer continues to rise, posing a significant threat to women's health worldwide [
1
,
2
]. Breast cancer is classified into four subtypes: luminal A, luminal B, HER2-positive (HER2
+
), and basal-like [
3
]. HER2
+
breast cancer accounts for approximately 25% of all cases, and its overexpression or amplification drives abnormal tumor growth [
4
–
7
]. The use of HER2-targeted therapies in recent years has improved survival rates and patient prognoses, but resistance to these treatments remains a major clinical challenge [
8
–
11
].
Protein post-translational modifications are widely recognized as potential therapeutic targets in cancer treatment. HER2 is modified by the ubiquitin–proteasome system (UPS), phosphorylation, and neddylation, as reported in previous studies [
12
–
15
]. CHIP and c-Cbl are identified as E3 ligases for HER2 [
16
–
21
]. Previously, we demonstrated that HER2 neddylation increases its protein stability by inhibiting ubiquitination. The Cullin-RING ligase (CRL) family, the primary substrate for neddylation, comprises a large group of E3 ubiquitin ligases [
22
]. Neddylation of Cullins activates CRLs, enabling them to regulate the ubiquitination and degradation of key proteins [
23
,
24
].
CAND1 (Cullin-associated and neddylation-dissociated 1) is a critical regulator of the neddylation process [
25
,
26
]. Both CAND1 and NEDD8 bind to CRLs but have opposing effects on CRL activity, including regulation of CUL1, CUL2, CUL3, CUL4A, CUL4B, CUL5, and CUL7 [
25
,
27
]. When CRLs are dissociated from neddylation, CAND1 binds to CRLs to inhibit their E3 ligase activity. CAND1 has been implicated in the progression of various tumors. For instance, CAND1 is highly expressed in liver cancer tissues, promoting tumor progression via the caspase-8/RIP1 pathway [
28
]. In prostate cancer, high CAND1 expression correlates positively with Gleason scores [
29
]. Furthermore, CAND1 enhances proliferation and migration in lung cancer [
30
]. However, its role in breast cancer remains unclear.
This study investigated the role of CAND1 in HER2
+
breast cancer. The results showed that CAND1 expression is elevated in HER2
+
breast cancer tissues and positively correlates with HER2 levels. CAND1 knockdown (CAND1-KD) promotes HER2 degradation by activating CUL7. These findings suggest that CAND1 may serve as a potential therapeutic target for patients with HER2
+
breast cancer.
Experimental procedures
Cell lines and culture conditions
Breast cancer cell lines SK-BR3 were purchased from Procell Biologics, while BT474 and HEK293T were obtained from ATCC. Cells were cultured under standard conditions at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, as previously described [
12
]. BT474 cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). SK-BR3 cells were cultured in McCoy's 5A medium containing 10% FBS, and HEK293T cells were grown in DMEM with 10% FBS.
Antibodies and reagents
Details of the antibodies and chemicals used are provided in supporting information Tables S1-S3.
Transfection of plasmids and siRNAs
The transfection procedure followed previously described protocols [
12
,
31
]. For siRNA transfection, 500 μL of RPMI Opti-MEM, 5 μL of Lipofectamine RNAiMAX, and 15 μL of siRNA were mixed and incubated for 15 min. The mixture was then added to the petri dish, achieving a final siRNA concentration of 50 nM for 48 h.
For plasmid transfection in a 60 mm dish with a 2.5 mL medium system, the mixture included 500 μL of Opti-MEM, 2.5 μL of P3000, 2.5 μL of Lipofectamine 3000, and 2.5 μg of plasmid DNA. After mixing for 15 min, the cells were incubated with the transfection solution for 48 h before assessing transfection efficiency for subsequent experiments.
Western blot and Co-IP
Western blotting and co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assays were performed following standard procedures previously described [
32
]. Magnetic beads were incubated overnight with the appropriate antibody on a vertical shaker. The antibody-coated beads were then incubated with cell lysates for 2 h. After washing the beads with PBST, the supernatant was used for protein detection by western blot.
Real-time quantitative PCR assay
Total RNA was extracted from cells as previously reported [
33
]. First-strand cDNA was synthesized from 1000 ng of RNA using the PrimeScript™ RT Master Mix kit. The cDNA was amplified using the TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II kit (Takara Biotechnology, Dalian, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Primers for target genes are displayed in supporting information Table S4. mRNA levels were quantified by real-time PCR.
Immunofluorescence assay
Immunofluorescence assays were conducted as previously described [
32
]. Cells were seeded onto glass-bottomed dishes, washed, fixed, permeabilized, and stained according to established protocols. Primary and secondary antibodies were diluted based on the manufacturer’s recommendations. Fluorescence images were captured using a confocal microscope (Leica TCS SP8).
Cell proliferation assay
EdU staining experiments were performed as previously reported [
34
]. Cells were seeded onto slides in small chambers (Millicell, PEZGS0816), treated with drugs or transfected with siRNA, and stained using the Cell-Light EdU Apollo567 In Vitro Kit. Fluorescence images were captured with a fluorescence microscope for subsequent analysis.
Cell colony formation assay
Cell suspensions were prepared after treatment. Equal numbers of cells were seeded into new 6-well plates and cultured for 2–3 weeks. Colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, washed with PBS, and stained with crystal violet solution.
Cell viability assay
As previously described [
33
], MTS assays were used to measure cell viability. Cells were seeded into 96-well plates and treated with siRNA. Following the kit instructions, 20 μL of MTS solution was added to each well, and absorbance values were measured after two hours. The assay was performed in at least three independent experiments.
Cell cycle assays and apoptosis assays (Flow cytometry)
Cell cycle and apoptosis analyses were conducted using flow cytometry, as previously reported [
12
].
Animal model
All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Guangzhou Medical University (GY2021-024). Female BALB/c nude mice, aged 6–8 weeks and weighing 18–20 g, were used in the experiments. SK-BR3 cells stably transfected with lentivirus were subcutaneously injected into the dorsal side of each mouse (3 × 10⁶ cells in 100 μL PBS per mouse). Body weight and tumor volume were monitored regularly. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula: length × width
2
× 0.5.
Immunohistochemistry
Human tissue and xenograft tumor samples were paraffin-fixed, embedded, and sectioned following previously established protocols. Tumor sections were incubated with the MaxVision kit (Mixin Biotech) and stained immunohistochemically with specific antibodies. Fifty microliters of MaxVision™ reagent was applied, followed by staining with a solution of 0.05% diaminobenzidine and 0.03% H₂O₂ in 50 mM Tris–HCl. The primary antibody was visualized using DAB. Ethics approval for human subjects was provided by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Cancer hospital, Guangzhou Medical University(2022-SK07).
Statistics and analyses
Experimental data were obtained from three independent experiments and are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical analyses and visualizations were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 and SPSS 16.0 software. Depending on the data type and experimental design, either ANOVA or a t-test was used. A
p
-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.
Results
The amplification of CAND1 is associated with HER2
+
BC progression
To investigate the progression of HER2-positive breast cancer (HER2
+
BC) and the underlying protein regulation mechanisms, we collected three paired HER2
+
BC and adjacent normal tissue samples for differential protein analysis using DIA quantification proteomics. A total of 747 upregulated proteins and 16 downregulated proteins were identified (Fig.
1
A and B). Proteins differing consistently across all three sample sets were further screened using a fold-change > 3.5 and p < 0.05, resulting in the identification of 353 proteins (Fig.
1
C). Additionally, liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) was used to identify proteins interacting with HER2 (Fig.
1
D). By overlapping LC–MS/MS and quantitative proteomics results, 96 proteins were found to be both upregulated and associated with HER2 in HER2
+
BC (Fig.
1
E). Further analysis of these 96 proteins using public databases revealed that CAND1 was critically involved in HER2
+
BC progression (Fig. S1A-C). CAND1 mRNA expression was significantly elevated in HER2
+
BC (Fig.
1
F) and was also higher in breast cancer with lymph node metastasis (Fig.
1
G). Notably, CAND1 expression positively correlated with HER2 levels (Fig.
1
H). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis indicated that high CAND1 expression was associated with poor prognosis in HER2
+
BC (Fig.
1
I). Collectively, these findings suggest that CAND1 overexpression contributes to the malignant progression of HER2
+
BC.

CAND1 loss suppresses proliferation of HER2
+
breast cancer cells
Given the overexpression of CAND1, we first investigated its role in regulating the phenotype of HER2
+
BC. MTS assays demonstrated that siRNA-mediated CAND1 knockdown (CAND1-KD) inhibited cell growth in HER2
+
BC cell lines SK-BR3 and BT474 (Fig.
2
A). EdU assays further revealed a reduction in the number of proliferating cells following CAND1-KD (Fig.
2
B and C). To assess the long-term effects of CAND1 depletion, colony formation assays were performed, showing significant inhibition of cell proliferation after CAND1-KD (Fig.
2
D). Cell cycle analysis indicated that CAND1-KD induced G0/G1 phase arrest (Fig.
2
E and Fig. S2A). This was accompanied by downregulation of Cyclin D1 and upregulation of P21 (Fig.
2
F). Flow cytometry analysis demonstrated that CAND1-KD also triggered cell apoptosis (Fig.
2
G and Fig. S2B), with PARP and Bcl-2 levels changed as shown by western blot (Fig.
2

+
BC progression.

HER2 is a substrate of CAND1
Based on these findings, we hypothesized that HER2 might be a substrate of CAND1. To explore the molecular mechanisms, we examined whether CAND1 regulates HER2 expression. Western blot analysis showed that CAND1-KD led to a reduction in HER2 protein levels (Fig.
3
A). RT-qPCR results indicated minimal upregulation of HER2 mRNA upon CAND1-KD (Fig.
3
B), suggesting that CAND1 primarily regulates HER2 protein stability. Cycloheximide (CHX) tracking experiments revealed that CAND1-KD significantly accelerated HER2 degradation (Fig.
3
C and D). Immunofluorescence and confocal microscopy confirmed that CAND1-KD reduced HER2 expression in the cytoplasmic membrane of SK-BR3 cells (Fig.
3
E). Since HER2 degradation depends on the ubiquitin–proteasome system (UPS) [
35
,
36
], we tested whether CAND1 modulates HER2 via this pathway. Cells were treated with MG132, a proteasome inhibitor, following CAND1-KD. Western blot analysis showed that MG132 reversed HER2 degradation mediated by CAND1-KD in HER2
+
BC cells (Fig.
3
F and G). These findings indicate that CAND1 promotes HER2
+
BC cell proliferation by stabilizing HER2 protein levels through the UPS.

CAND1 regulates the stability of HER2 protein
To confirm that HER2 is a substrate of CAND1, Co-IP and western blot assays were performed using anti-CAND1 and anti-HER2 antibodies. The results demonstrated that endogenous CAND1 directly binds to HER2 in vitro (Fig.
4
A and B). Immunofluorescence and confocal microscopy were used to determine the subcellular localization of the CAND1-HER2 interaction, revealing that the interaction occurs primarily in the cytoplasm of SK-BR3 cells (Fig.
4
C). Next, we mapped the binding domains of HER2 and CAND1. Based on HER2 structure, the protein was divided into three fragments: full-length (F1), amino acids 1-653 (F2), and amino acids 654-1255 (F3) (Fig.
4
D). The data indicated that both the N-terminus and C-terminus of HER2 are required for binding to CAND1 (Fig.
4
E). Additionally, CAND1 knockdown (CAND1-KD) increased K48-linked polyubiquitination of HER2 (Fig.
4
F), whereas CAND1 overexpression reduced K48-polyubiquitination of HER2 in SK-BR3 and BT474 cells (Fig.
4
G). These results indicate that CAND1 interacts with and stabilizes HER2 by preventing its ubiquitination.

CUL7 is a E3 ligase of HER2
To explore how CAND1 regulates HER2 ubiquitination, we investigated the role of Cullin-RING ligases (CRLs), which include CUL1, CUL2, CUL3, CUL4A, CUL4B, CUL5, and CUL7. CAND1 regulates CRL activity by blocking their E3 ligase function. Western blot analysis showed that knockdown of CUL1, CUL2, CUL3, CUL4A, CUL4B, and CUL5 did not affect HER2 expression (Fig.
5
A–F), whereas CUL7 knockdown increased HER2 protein levels (Fig.
5
G). Conversely, CUL7 overexpression reduced HER2 expression (Fig.
5
H). To confirm that CUL7 acts as an E3 ligase for HER2, MG132 (a proteasome inhibitor) was used to block the ubiquitin–proteasome system. MG132 partially reversed the reduction in HER2 levels caused by CUL7 overexpression (Fig.
5
I and J). Moreover, CUL7 promoted K48-linked polyubiquitination of HER2 (Fig.
5
K), and CUL7 knockdown decreased HER2 ubiquitination (Fig.
5
L). Co-IP assays revealed that CUL7 interacts with both HER2 and CAND1 (Fig.
5
M), and immunofluorescence demonstrated that this interaction occurs in the cytoplasm (Fig.
5
N). Together, these results establish CUL7 as an E3 ligase for HER2, mediating its expression by increasing K48-linked polyubiquitination.

CUL7 is required for CAND1-induced HER2 ubiquitination
Given that CUL7 promotes HER2 ubiquitination, we investigated whether CAND1-stabilized HER2 depends on the E3 ligase activity of CUL7. CAND1 competes with NEDD8 for CUL7 binding. CAND1-KD increased the neddylation of CUL7 (Fig.
6
A), while CAND1 overexpression reduced neddylated CUL7 levels (Fig.
6
B). Since neddylation activates CUL7’s E3 ligase activity, overexpression of CUL7 compensated for the reduction in HER2 ubiquitination caused by CAND1 (Fig.
6
C). To test the role of CAND1’s binding capacity to CRLs, we used a mutant CAND1 plasmid (CAND1-MT) that cannot bind CRLs [
37
]. CAND1-MT enhanced HER2 ubiquitination, indicating that CAND1’s regulation of HER2 depends on its interaction with CRLs (Fig.
6
D). Immunofluorescence analysis showed that CAND1-KD increased the interaction between CUL7 and HER2 in the cytoplasm (Fig.
6
E). In summary, these findings demonstrate that CAND1 stabilizes HER2 protein expression by regulating the activity of CUL7, which mediates HER2 ubiquitination.

CAND1 regulates the proliferation of HER2
+
BC in a HER2-dependent manner
To further elucidate the role of CAND1 in HER2 + BC, in vivo experiments were conducted. Lentivirus constructs containing GFP-HER2 and shRNA targeting CAND1 were transfected into SK-BR3 cells. Western blot analysis confirmed successful knockdown of CAND1 and overexpression of HER2 (Fig.
8
A). Cell viability assays showed that HER2 overexpression reversed the growth suppression induced by CAND1-KD (Fig.
8
B). Similarly, colony formation assays demonstrated that HER2 overexpression promoted tumor growth and counteracted the inhibitory effects of CAND1 deletion (Fig.
8
C). Flow cytometry analysis revealed that HER2 overexpression rescued the apoptosis induced by CAND1-KD (Fig. S3A and S3B). To assess these findings in vivo, SK-BR3 xenografts were established in nude mice using stably transfected cells. Tumor growth, measured by size and weight, was significantly inhibited by CAND1-KD, while HER2 overexpression effectively restored tumor growth (Fig.
8
D–G). No significant differences in body weight were observed among the groups. Immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis showed that Cyclin D1 and Ki67 protein levels were downregulated by CAND1-KD, whereas HER2 overexpression restored their expression (Fig. S3C). Collectively, these results from in vivo experiments are consistent with in vitro findings, further supporting the conclusion that CAND1 decreases HER2 ubiquitination to drive HER2
+
BC proliferation (Fig.
9
).

Discussion
This study investigates the role of HER2 in BC, focusing on its post-translational regulation, particularly ubiquitination. BC is a highly heterogeneous disease characterized by drug resistance and high recurrence rates. HER2 is overexpressed in approximately 25% of BC cases. While Herceptin, a first-line therapy targeting HER2, has significantly benefited patients, the emergence of resistance within months highlights the urgent need for alternative therapeutic strategies. Understanding the cellular and molecular mechanisms of HER2 regulation is essential to develop more effective treatments for BC.
To identify key proteins regulating HER2
+
BC progression, we analyzed three paired HER2
+
BC and adjacent normal tissues using proteomics. A total of 353 proteins were found to be either upregulated or downregulated in tumor tissues under specific screening conditions. However, this analysis alone was insufficient to identify the most critical protein. Given the hypothesis that the protein of interest interacts with and mediates HER2 expression, we conducted LC–MS/MS analysis to identify proteins interacting with HER2. Combining proteomics data with LC–MS/MS results and excluding previously reported proteins, we identified CAND1 as a key protein. CAND1 was upregulated and found to interact with HER2 in HER2
+
BC.
The following data clarified that CAND1 plays a core role in HER2
+
BC: firstly, bioinformatics database analysis showed that the mRNA level of CAND1 was highly expressed in HER2
+
BC. CAND1 expression was positively related with HER2. The high expression of CAND1 showed a poor prognosis and promoted lymphatic metastasis in HER2
+
BC. Secondly, expanded clinical samples were applied for immunohistochemistry. As expected, CAND1 protein was increased and positively correlated with HER2 protein. Functionally, the knockdown of CAND1 inhibited SK-BR3 and BT474 cell growth via arresting cell cycle progression at the G0/G1 phase and inducing cell apoptosis. In addition to in vitro, CAND1 deletion significantly inhibited tumor growth in SK-BR3 xenografts.
Having clarified the importance of CAND1, we further explored the molecular mechanism of CAND1 in HER2
+
BC progression. Based on previous findings, CAND1 expression was related to HER2, prompting investigation into whether CAND1 regulates HER2 protein expression. Western blot analysis revealed that CAND1 knockdown reduced HER2 expression by shortening its half-life. Additionally, treatment with MG132, a proteasome inhibitor, reversed the downregulation of HER2 induced by CAND1 siRNA, and CAND1 depletion increased HER2 ubiquitination. Further Co-IP analysis demonstrated that CAND1 interacted with HER2. Both the N-terminal and C-terminal regions of HER2 were required for binding to CAND1. Additionally, CAND1 was found to bind the E3 ligase CUL7, inhibiting its activity and forming a CAND1-CUL7-HER2 complex. Importantly, CAND1 functioned as an inhibitor of CUL7, an E3 ligase responsible for accelerating HER2 ubiquitination and degradation. Notably, in vitro and in vivo experiments showed that HER2 overexpression partially abrogated CAND1-induced growth inhibition in HER2
+
BC.
Conclusions
In summary, this study identified CAND1 as a critical protein in HER2
+
BC by regulating HER2. Our findings demonstrated that CAND1 knockdown suppressed HER2
+
BC progression by recruiting CUL7 to induce HER2 ubiquitination and degradation. These data provide a potential novel strategy for treating HER2
+
BC.
