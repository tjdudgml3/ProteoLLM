Introduction
Vitamin D insufficiency and deficiency have been reported globally
1
–
3
, and these conditions are highly prevalent in athletes
4
and the elderly
5
. Inadequate blood vitamin D levels are associated with muscle weakness
6
,
7
, muscle atrophy
8
,
9
, sarcopenia
10
,
11
, and musculoskeletal injuries
12
. Upon muscle damage, a multi-stage repairing process occurs to restore normal muscle characteristics and functions. This includes degradation, inflammation, regeneration, and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodelling
13
. However, repetitive muscle injuries induce fibrous tissue deposition
14
, which impairs the muscle regenerative process, and ultimately leads to incomplete functional recovery due to overexpression of profibrotic factor, transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1)
15
. The negative impact of TGF-β1 in skeletal muscle atrophy and fibrosis development has been demonstrated previously
16
, and the latter could be associated with an induction of differentiation of myogenic cells into fibrotic cells after muscle injury
17
. Thus, strategies to minimize an excessive stimulation of TGF-β1 during the regenerative process to enhance myogenesis and alleviate fibrosis are crucial for skeletal muscle recovery after injury.
Previous studies have reported the antifibrotic effects of the active form of vitamin D
3
, namely calcitriol (1α,25-dihydroxyvitamin D
3
; 1,25D
3
) under profibrotic factor stimulation in hepatic stellate cells
18
,
19
and renal interstitial fibroblast cell line (NRK-49 F)
20
. These findings are in agreement with our recently published data that 1,25D
3
exerted antifibrotic effects on primary skeletal muscle fibroblasts
21
. Beyond the counteractive role of 1,25D
3
on fibrosis, 1,25D
3
and its precursor form namely calcidiol (25-hydroxyvitamin D
3
; 25D
3
) that can be converted to 1,25D
3
by 1α-hydroxylase enzyme (CYP27B1), also possess the antiproliferative effects on skeletal muscle cells
22
,
23
. Moreover, the effects of 1,25D
3
on myogenic differentiation are still controversial, as reports showed suppressive effects on the differentiation ability of skeletal muscle cells
24
–
29
. This led to a growing interest in the effects of cholecalciferol (vitamin D
3
; D
3
) which is a prohormone and its conversion to 25D
3
is regulated by the hepatic 25-hydroxylase enzymes (CYP27A1 and CYP2R1). Recently, the effects of D
3
on counteracting with interleukin 6- and angiotensin II-induced C2C12 myotube atrophy have been reported
25
,
26
. However, the knowledge and understanding related to the antifibrotic effects of D
3
and its metabolites in skeletal muscle cells are limited and still need to be elucidated.
Vitamin D receptor (VDR) is expressed in various tissues throughout the body including skeletal muscle
30
. Nevertheless, VDR protein expression was relatively low in uninjured muscle, and its expression was substantially increased in regenerating muscle
22
,
29
indicating the potential role of VDR on muscle regeneration. To support this notion, VDR protein expression has been reported to be expressed in resident myogenic stem cells
31
and skeletal muscle fibroblasts
21
which play essential roles in muscle repair and ECM remodelling, respectively. In addition, VDR possesses its biological activity in skeletal muscle, as shown by muscle atrophy in rats
32
and decreased myogenesis in mouse skeletal muscle cells following
Vdr
gene suppression
32
,
33
, and dysregulation of the energy system following VDR ablation in mice
34
. These findings suggest the critical role of
Vdr
gene expression related to myogenic regulation and muscle homeostasis.
Additionally, gene-sets related with ECM remodelling, integrin, and collagen formation were enhanced following the VDR overexpression in rat tibialis anterior muscle
35
, indicating the potential regulatory role of VDR on ECM remodelling. However, none of the previous studies examined the effect of
Vdr
gene suppression in skeletal muscle cells under TGF-β1 stimulation that play a crucial role during the muscle healing process after injury, and how vitamin D
3
/its metabolites could modulate these processes. Therefore, this study was designed to address the questions about how vitamin D
3
/its metabolites and VDR could regulate myogenesis and fibrogenesis in skeletal muscle cells under profibrotic factor stimulation.

Vdr
gene suppression induced impairment of myogenesis and mitochondrial regulation in skeletal muscle cells

Vdr

Vdr
gene silencing. MYMK is a protein that regulates myoblast fusion, while MAP1LC3A involves in mitophagy mediating damaged mitochondria removal. These results suggest impairment of myoblast fusion and mitochondrial regulation in skeletal muscle cells could be occurred under
Vdr
gene suppression.

Discussion
This study aimed to investigate the regulatory role of vitamin D
3
/its metabolites and
Vdr
gene silencing under TGF-β1 stimulation in skeletal muscle cells. The main findings of this study are (1) D
3
, 25D
3
, and 1,25D
3
could counteract the fibrosis development in TGF-β1-treated skeletal muscle cells via inhibiting ECM accumulation by suppression of COL1A1 protein expression (2) D
3
, but not 25D
3
and 1,25D
3
, could preserve the antifibrotic effect without a negative impact on skeletal muscle cell growth under profibrotic factor stimulation (3)
Vdr
gene silencing altered skeletal muscle cell proteome on myogenesis and mitochondrial regulation that were associated with an upregulation of α-SMA protein expression under TGF-β1 stimulation, these changes suggested VDR could modulate fibrogenesis in skeletal muscle cells regardless of ligand binding (4) Under TGF-β1 stimulation,
Vdr
gene silencing abolished the antifibrotic effect of 1,25D
3
but not D
3
supporting that D
3
action is largely mediated independently of VDR.
Vitamin D
3
has been reported to exert its effects on skeletal muscle regeneration that are crucial processes involved in the orchestrate events between myogenesis and fibrogenesis. Excessive fibrous tissue formation has been associated with fibroblast overactivation
36
, the transition of skeletal muscle cells to myofibroblasts
17
,
37
,
38
, and excess TGF-β1 stimulation
39
,
40
. However, how vitamin D
3
and its metabolites differentially regulate these processes under profibrotic stimulation in skeletal muscle cells is largely unexplored. In this study, COL1A1 protein expression was significantly increased following TGF-β1 treatment in C2C12 myotubes, suggesting an increase in ECM accumulation and fibrosis development. This result aligns with prior studies that reported an upregulation of
Col1a1
gene following TGF-β1 treatment in skeletal muscle cells
41
and collagen type I deposition after TGF-β treatment in skeletal muscle
16
, supporting that skeletal muscle is one source secreting collagen under profibrotic stimulation. In this study, all three forms of vitamin D
3
could suppress the high levels of COL1A1. The suppressive effects of 1,25D
3
co-treated with TGF-β1 on
Col1a1
gene were reported in renal interstitial fibroblast cell line (NRK-49 F)
20
and hepatic stellate cells
19
. The ability to inhibit collagen type I production by 1,25D
3
might be direct to gene transcriptional repression at
Col1a1
gene promoter which is similar to VDREs upon the binding of 1,25D
3
to VDR
42
. However, deletion of the vitamin D responsive region did not entirely eliminate the suppressive effects of 1,25D
3
43
. This supports the finding of this study that the degree of COL1A1 protein suppression was not associated with VDR activation. From this point of view, it raises the potential of D
3
that serves as a prohormone to act on suppression of COL1A1 without VDR activation.
The putative mechanism could be involved with a non-classical pathway of vitamin D
3
metabolism that mediates through CYP11A1 enzyme to hydroxylate D
3
to 20-hydroxylation D
3
[20(OH)D
3
] and further hydroxylates 20(OH)D
3
to other derivatives
44
. Several CYP11A1-derived vitamin D
3
metabolites have been shown to exert antifibrotic effects by suppressing collagen production comparable to 1,25D
3
in dermal fibroblasts and these effects were reversed following retinoic acid-related orphan receptor γ (RORγ) knockout
45
. This finding revealed the regulatory role of RORγ on fibrotic activity under vitamin D
3
action. Moreover,
Cyp11a1
mRNA was reported to be expressed in skeletal muscle cells and dystrophic muscle
46
. RORγ was also shown to be highly abundant in skeletal muscle
47
. This raises RORγ as the potential receptor mediating the antifibrotic effects of D
3
through the alternative CYP11A1 pathway in skeletal muscle cells. However, our study showed no alterations in
Vdr
,
Cyp11a1
,
Rora
,
and Rorc
mRNA expression following the co-treatment of TGF-β1 and D
3
. These findings demonstrate a non-classical pathway of D
3
on antifibrotic effects could be a cell-type specific regulation. Additionally, the unchanged mRNA expression of
Cyp11a1
observed in our study is possibly due to skeletal muscle cells have the DNA methylation on
Ad4BP/SF-1
transcription factor that subsequently repressed
Cyp11a1
expression level
48
. Nevertheless, RORγ is a ligand-activated receptor, and its mRNA expression might not directly reflect the function or activity. Therefore, the mechanistic study to verify the potential target receptor mediating antifibrotic effects of D
3
by siRNA-mediated knockdown of RORγ or using a RORγ-specific inhibitor on COL1A1 protein suppression should be further investigated.
In contrast, 25D
3
and 1,25D
3
but not D
3
could suppress α-SMA protein expression under TGF-β1 stimulation and the degree of suppression on α-SMA protein depended on VDR activation. This is in accordance with previous studies that reported the suppression of α-SMA protein in a dose-dependent manner following 1,25D
3
treatment in normal rat kidney interstitial fibroblasts
20
. This suggests differential regulations of VDR activation on ECM production (i.e., COL1A1) and fibrotic protein expression (α-SMA) in skeletal muscle cells. The inhibitory effect of 1,25D
3
on fibrogenesis has been reported to be associated with the suppression of TGF-β/Smad in primary skeletal muscle fibroblasts
21
and human peritoneal mesothelial cells
49
. However, TGF-β1 and the antifibrotic effects of D
3
and its metabolites were not associated with the Smad2/3 signalling pathway in this study. Although TGF-β1/Smad2/3 signalling has been reported as the canonical pathway inducing fibrosis development
50
,
51
, the pathway mediating the effects of TGF-β1 and D
3
/its metabolites in skeletal muscle cells could be potentially through non-canonical pathways, i.e., p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)
52
,
53
and AMPK
54
–
56
.
The inhibitory effects of TGF-β1 on myogenin and MHC protein expression are in line with previous studies demonstrated that TGF-β1 negatively impacts myogenic differentiation
17
,
57
,
58
. Additionally, we found that TGF-β1 increased AMPKα phosphorylation at 12 h after treatment. This activation of AMPK has been reported to impair myogenic differentiation and induce myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells
59
,
60
, which are associated with upregulation of protein degradation pathway through FoxO3a-muscle-specific ubiquitin ligases
61
–
63
. Moreover, AMPK activation could lead to inhibition of protein synthesis as reported previously
64
, which is in accordance with our findings that TGF-β1 treatment reduced puromycin incorporation, suggesting a decline in protein synthesis in skeletal muscle cells. Although D
3
and its metabolites did not inhibit AMPK activation and alter protein synthesis in this study after TGF-β1 stimulation, D
3
treatment was able to alleviate impairment of myogenic differentiation under profibrotic stimulation, suggesting its action could be partially counteracted with the suppressive effect of TGF-β1 on myogenesis.
To support the effect of D
3
on myogenesis, previous studies have been reported about the protective role of D
3
on myotube size against the interleukin 6 (IL-6)-induced atrophy
26
and angiotensin II-induced atrophy
25
. The differential effects of D
3
compared to 25D
3
and 1,25D
3
could be due to lacking local conversion of D
3
to its 25-hydroxymetabolites in skeletal muscle cells, as our study showed the undetectable CYP27A1 protein expression that was supported by the previous study demonstrated the undetectable
Cyp2r1
and barely detectable
Cyp27a1
mRNA levels in C2C12 myotubes
26
. However, skeletal muscle cells expressed CYP27B1 enzyme that can convert 25D
3
to 1,25D
3
, resulting in its inhibitory effect on myogenic differentiation similar to 1,25D
3
action.
Vitamin D-deficient people demonstrated lowering VDR protein expression in skeletal muscle
65
. To mimic in vivo reduction of VDR expression in vitro,
Vdr
gene silencing has been utilized to study the impact of VDR on skeletal muscle cells. Growing evidence has addressed the role of VDR in modulating fibrogenesis in various tissues and cell types [e.g., intestinal tissue
66
, dermal tissue and keratinocytes
67
, and hepatic stellate cells
68
. VDR ablation under colitis-induced mice enhanced fibrosis in the intestine and showed upregulation of fibrotic-related genes (e.g., α-SMA, collagen I, and fibronectin) and proteins (fibronectin and vimentin)
66
. In this study, the exacerbation of α-SMA protein expression was evident in differentiated myotubes transfected with
Vdr
siRNA and treated with TGF-β1, indicating the protective role of VDR against fibrogenesis in skeletal muscle cells. To confirm whether VDR suppression is directly associated with an increase in α-SMA expression in skeletal muscle cells, a luciferase reporter assay is needed to assess VDR regulation on the promoter activity of the target gene (i.e.,
Acta2
). Nevertheless, this is a limitation of the current study and requires further investigation.
An augmenting α-SMA protein expression after
Vdr
gene silencing could be associated with alterations in skeletal muscle cell proteome that relate to impaired myogenesis and mitochondrial dysregulation, subsequently inducing excess fibrosis development after TGF-β1 stimulation. In support of this notion, previous studies reported the association between mitochondrial dysfunction and fibrosis development in heart
69
,
70
, liver
71
,
72
, lung
73
–
75
, and kidneys
76
–
78
. In skeletal muscle cells, a recent study has reported the modulating role of secreted protein acidic and rich in cysteine (SPARC) on mitochondrial function and ECM component protein (COL1A1) through AMPK activation
56
. Moreover, VDR protein expression is important for mediating the suppressive effects of 1,25D
3
on α-SMA and MHC but not D
3
under
Vdr
gene silencing and TGF-β1 stimulation as shown in our findings, suggesting the differential effects of D
3
and its metabolites on skeletal muscle cells.
Conclusion
TGF-β1 stimulation induced fibrosis development and suppressed skeletal muscle cell growth by impairing myogenic differentiation. Nevertheless, D
3
and its metabolites can alleviate fibrogenesis in skeletal muscle cells. Specifically, D
3
possesses the antifibrotic effect without a negative impact on skeletal muscle cell growth, and VDR may not be significantly responsible for this effect under profibrotic factor stimulation. In addition, lowering VDR protein expression under TGF-β1 stimulation could enhance the fibrosis development in skeletal muscle cells. These suggest that vitamin D-deficient individuals are more susceptible to developing fibrosis in skeletal muscle cells under repetitive injuries. Therefore, the knowledge about the antifibrotic effects of vitamin D
3
and its metabolites could be beneficial for providing the strategy of vitamin D supplementation to vitamin D-deficient individuals to counteract fibrosis development after muscle injury.

Cell culture
Mouse skeletal muscle cell line (C2C12) [American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) (CRL-1772)] were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) (12800-017) (Gibco, NY, USA) and 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS) (A5256701) (Gibco) [Growth medium (GM)] supplemented with 100 U/mL of penicillin and streptomycin (15140-122) (Gibco) in 5% CO
2

®

3
cells/well in 24-well plate containing GM for 24 h. Cholecalciferol (C9756) (Sigma-Aldrich), calcidiol (9000683) (Cayman Chemical, MI, USA), and calcitriol (71820) (Cayman Chemical) final concentrations at 1, 10, and 100 nM were treated to the cells every 24 h for 48 h. For early and late myogenic differentiation phases, C2C12 myoblasts were cultured in GM at 1.2 × 10
5
cells/well in a 24-well plate for 24 h. Thereafter, cell differentiation was induced by switching culture media with DMEM + 2% horse serum (16050-130) (Gibco) (Differentiation medium, DM). Cells were treated with different forms/doses of D
3
and its metabolites on day 0 and day 1 of the differentiation phase for the early time point whilst the late time point was on day 3 and day 4. For assessment of fibrogenesis and myogenesis responses to the treatment of D
3
and its metabolites under TGF-β1 stimulation, differentiated C2C12 myotubes were treated with vehicle (20 mM citrate pH 3.0) (9871) (Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA), 10 ng/mL of mouse TGF-β1 recombinant protein (5231LF) (Cell Signaling Technology) or co-treatment of TGF-β1 with the effective dose (100 nM) of D

ImageJ (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD) was used to quantify the protein expression levels by measuring the band density of investigated proteins.
Proteomics analysis

Log10 was used to transform the data to achieve normal distribution where appropriate.
