Introduction
Pulmonary lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM) is a low-grade, understudied, and metastasizing sarcoma, predominantly affecting women, and manifesting as proliferation of tumor smooth muscle-like cells within the lungs, which ultimately leads to lung damage and failure
1
–
4
. It develops in association with tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC) or as a sporadic form. Both TSC and sporadic LAM result from germline or somatic
TSC1/TSC2
mutations, respectivly
5
,
6
that prevent the inhibition of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) by TSC1/TSC2 complex
7
,
8
. The metastatic potential of LAM, which we discovered, and the origin, possibly, from the uterus, renal angiomyolipoma, or from an unknown site, are now well-accepted
1
–
4
. However, the mechanisms regulating LAM metastasis remain unidentified.
EVs are released from cells, including cancer cells, to mediate cell-to-cell communication, in part, through their cargo. They also promote cancer metastasis. Intercellular communication is pivotal for coordinating homeostasis, as well as for pathological processes. EV biogenesis and uptake are regulated by the classical and non-classical endocytic pathways
9
–
17
. EVs released from lung cells, including endothelium, pulmonary alveolar macrophages, fibroblasts, and epithelial cells, contribute to asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), pulmonary hypertension, and lung cancer
18
–
21
. EVs from epithelial cancer (carcinomas) cells and epithelial cancer stem cells (CSCs) or from the tumor microenvironment (TME) influence CSCs, premetastatic niche, metastasis, and response to therapy
22
–
25
. These EVs transport growth factors, integrins, the non-receptor tyrosine kinase protooncogene c-Src, and focal adhesion kinase (FAK), and can regulate angiogenesis, vascular permeability, the pre-metastatic niche, and seeding of target organs by tumor cells
22
–
31
. EV-derived integrins (EV-ITGs) regulate anchorage-independent (i.e., in the circulation) growth of tumor cells and their organotropism
29
,
32
. The lung-tropic EV-ITGs, including ITGα6, ITGβ4, and ITGβ1
32

33
–
38
. EVs also regulate cancer cell plasticity, which is linked to stemness, anoikis resistance, and increased metastatic potential
31
,
39
–
46
.
Carcinoma cells oscillate between a proliferative/differentiated and an invasive/dedifferentiated phenotype (metastable/hybrid phenotypes)
31
,
41
–
46
. Cancer cell plasticity and hybrid metastable phenotypes are also observed in non-epithelial tumors
39

52
,

,

Despite advancements in understanding the roles of EVs in carcinomas, their functions in non-epithelial malignancies, especially sarcomas, including LAM, are understudied. Limited evidence defines potential roles for EV in regulating angiogenesis in non-epithelial tumors, and adhesion and migration of non-epithelial/mesenchymal malignant cells
59
–
63
. We previously reported that EVs from
Tsc1
–null neuronal progenitors block the differentiation of recipient wild-type progenitors via the activation of Notch1/mTOR pathways, phenocopying
Tsc1
-null cells, and that mTORC1 hyperactive LAM surrogate cells secrete EV, thereby affecting target cells via activation of the Notch1/mTOR
64
. Consistently, LAM surrogate cells have increased EV biogenesis and cargo that enhance VEGF secretion and the viability of recipient fibroblast
65
.
The release of distinct EV subtypes from the same cancer cells, as well as the mechanisms involved in cancer progression mediated by these different EV subtypes have not been reported. We discovered that primary tumor LAM cells and metastasizing LAM cells, despite identical genotypes, release functionally different EVs. Thus, we aim to unveil the heterogeneity of distinct EV populations and their significance for promoting LAM metastasis.

TSC-null EVs enhance CSCs and metastable phenotypes of 621-101 spheres
TSC-null EVs and TSC2 EVs were isolated from adherent 621-101 (TSC-null) or TSC2 addback cells, respectively, grown for 3 (nutrient-rich environment) or 7 (nutrient-low environment) days. In this experimental setting, EVs from adherent cells represent EVs released from primary tumor (tumor EV) cells, as these cells are anchored to the ECM-like environment. Although this 2D model does not ideally mimic the primary tumor environment, it ensures a pure fraction of tumor-derived EVs that are not contaminated with EVs released from other cell types present in the tumor microenvironment (TME). Thus, their function can be experimentally tested. To generate EVs that resemble those released from metastasizing or circulating LAM cells (metastasis EVs), we isolated EVs from 621-101 or TSC2 addback cells cultured in media without anchorage, as these conditions simulate micrometastases or circulating tumor cells/spheroids

Shuttling ATP synthesis to pseudopodia or activation of integrin adhesion complex signaling drives TSC-null EV subtypes mediated CSC metastable phenotypes of LAM cells

Discussion
The biological significance of the EV pathway in non-epithelial malignancies, especially sarcomas, including LAM, is unclear. Limited evidence supports potential roles for EVs in regulating tumor angiogenesis, adhesion, and migration of non-epithelial/mesenchymal malignant cells
59
–
63
. Therefore, this study broadens our understanding of the EV pathways in non-epithelial malignancies. Here, we provide evidence for the previously unknown functions of EVs in regulating LAM progression and metastasis that are, at least partially, fueled by increased biogenesis of LAM-EVs and their enrichment with proteins known to drive lung organotropic metastasis, including ITGα6/β1
32
. In addition, LAM-EVs are enriched with several metalloproteinases, c-Src, and CD44 that are the established players in LAM progression
66
–
70
. KEGG analyses of LAM-EVs identified top-enriched pathways for DEPs, including regulation of actin cytoskeleton, pathways in cancer, oxidative phosphorylation, metabolic pathways, estrogen signaling pathway, and endocytosis that are known to be involved in cancer progression, thus supporting a potential involvement of LAM-EVs in LAM progression. The analysis of TSC-null EVs and TSC2 EVs from LAM surrogate cells and isogenic controls demonstrated that loss of TSC2 increases EV biogenesis, alters physical and biochemical properties of EVs, and impacts EV cargo sorting. Thus, LAM-EVs from patients share several features with TSC null EVs derived from LAM surrogate cells used in the experimental settings to model LAM. Our data are consistent with previous reports indicating that ITG-β1 is enriched in EVs of melanoma cells
84
and that loss of TSC1/2 increases EV biogenesis
64
,
65
,
85
,
86
. Despite the impact of TSC1/2 deficiency on EVs, demonstrated by our data, the long-term rapamycin treatment had no conclusive effect on EV biogenesis
65
. However, the activation of mTOR in
Tsc1/2
-null mouse embryonic fibroblasts and hepatocytes inhibits EV release
87
, suggesting that mTOR possibly regulates EV biogenesis in a different manner, depending on cell type, physiological vs. pathological conditions, and experimental culture methods.
The 2D and 3D culture systems used in this study, model, at least to some extent, the primary tumor and metastasizing tumor cells, and at the same time allow detailed studies of cellular signaling. They were instrumental in the discovery of the distinct EV subtypes, reported here, which were released from genetically identical cancer cells.
These TSC-null EV subtypes enhance CSC and metastable phenotypes of LAM surrogate CSCs to different magnitudes and through different mechanisms. EVs mimicking EVs from primary tumor cells are more powerful in enhancing the accumulated distance and velocity of migrated sphere cells compared to EVs mimicking EVs from metastasizing circulating tumor cells. Our work provides evidence for the role of primary tumor EVs in regulating mitochondrial function, with respect to OXPHOS and ATP synthesis, which is increased in spheres treated with these EVs prior to the initiation of migration. This EV-dependent metabolic shift is likely mediated by increased accumulation of critical mitochondrial function regulator Nrf2
78
in EVs and associates with moderate whole-cell cytoplasmic AMPK activation. The clinical relevance of these findings is underscored by Nrf2 enrichment in patient LAM-EVs. This metabolic and EV-mediated switch in LAM cells, associated with increases in mitochondrial potentials, activation of DRP1, formation of elongated mitochondrial tracks in cellular protrusions with an increased number of branching nodes, suggesting increased mitochondrial fission and presence of singular mitochondria in the leading edge and protrusive structures of migrated 621-101 spheres. Consequently, these changes lead to the enrichment of activated AMPK, TFAM, ATP synthase, and increased levels of ATP in chemotactic pseudopodia. The AMPK function in this process is supported by data indicating that inhibition of AMPK alleviated the tumor TSC-null EV-mediated increase in mitochondrial potential
s
, activation of DRP1, and enrichment of pseudopodia with p-FAK, p-AMPK, and ATP synthase. These data point to a novel EV function in coupling local energy demands to the subcellular targeting of energy sources for the activation of migratory machinery facilitating faster and more distant cell migration. Our data are consistent with AMPK function as an essential energy sensor and metabolic regulator
79
and with AMPK-mediated subcellular targeting of mitochondria to the leading edge and protrusive structures in the response to local energy demands during cell migration and invasion
79
. They are also consistent with the previous report, indicating that cell protrusions of migrated cells are on high energy demand and that local AMPK activation fulfills these demands
79
. Thus, data on EVs from primary tumors reveal novel and previously unreported functions of EVs in regulating the plasticity of cell migration via localized AMPK activation and subcellular localization of mitochondria and ATP synthase, supporting the previous notion of heterogeneity in cellular energy balance
79
. In addition, these primary tumor EVs are more effective in enhancing lung seeding by circulating LAM cells in vivo compared to EVs from metastasizing cells, which is consistent with primary tumor EV superiority in promoting cell migration. While primary tumor EVs promote localized ATP synthesis, and thus, faster migration, the EVs from metastasizing LAM cells promote the formation of IAC through the delivery of an EV pool of IAC building blocks, including vinculin, paxillin, and ILK1 (Fig.
7
, graphic presentation of heterogeneous EV subtypes functions). These IAC EV delivered building blocks probably have a similar function to the cytosolic pool of these blocks
80
. The clinical relevance of this mechanism is underscored by the enrichment of paxillin and ILK1 in patient LAM-EVs compared to Normal-EVs. Talin, paxillin, and ILK1 are chief determinants in this process, as increased expression of these proteins is evident in IAC, which metastasis TSC-null EVs regulate.
Fig. 7
Signaling triggered by distinct EV subtypes in the recipient tumor cells.
Our mechanistic studies demonstrate that both TSC-null EV subtypes promote TSC-null cell migration via engaging the ITGα6/β1-c-Src-FAK-paxillin regulatory axis, which is alleviated by the blockade of EV uptake or biogenesis, or inhibition of c-Src. In integrin-dependent migration modes, different velocities result from different levels of adhesion strength
88
. The slightly lower velocities and accumulated distance of migrated sphere cells treated with EVs from metastasizing vs. primary tumor cells may be explained by the formation of IAC itself and stronger adhesions. Of note, the superiority of tumor EVs over EVs from metastasizing cells in promoting cell migration was alleviated when migration was examined using the transwell assay and after sphere dissociation. This could be explained by dissociation procedures interfering with mitochondrial function and localization, underscoring the necessity of experimental designs that mimic in vivo conditions. Our data are consistent with alterations in LAM surrogate cells-derived EV cargo, which enhances VEGF secretion and viability of recipient fibroblast
65
, and with roles of EV-ITG-β1/5 and c-Src in the regulation of cell adhesion and disease progression in human osteosarcoma
89
. EVs from metastasizing cells were superior in promoting the stemness of LAM CSC, which is consistent with the role of EVs of Ewing sarcoma in promoting CSC
90
,
91
. These data also suggest that the central role of this EV subtype is CSC protection, with a secondary but significant influence on CSC migration and lung seeding by circulating tumor cells. Both types of EVs shared alike contribution to CSC invasion, indicating their equal importance in this process.
In summary, this study reveals previously unreported heterogeneous functions of EV subtypes that are derived from genetically identical primary tumor cells or metastasizing tumor cells. The engagement of these different mechanisms likely depends on differences in the tumor microenvironment (i.e., primary tumor cells/EVs vs. metastasizing cells /EVs). Importantly, this heterogeneity in EV functions may possibly play a role in the growth of other malignancies. The reported here EV functions in the progression of LAM establish the EV pathway as a new potential target for LAM therapy, warranting future clinical studies.
Limitations of the study
There are several limitations in the present study. We recognize that size distribution data shows inconsistencies between methods of EV isolation. However, different EV isolation/concentration methods might yield EVs with different size distributions due to differences in method biochemistry or physics principles
92
. The DLS is not able to accurately resolve heterogeneous mixture of vesicles, as it collects light from all particles simultaneously. In contrast, NTA simultaneously measures individual particle size and scattering intensity, thereby providing a more accurate representation of the size distribution, especially for smaller exosomes
93
. Therefore, size distribution inconsistencies likely result from different principles of these two methods. Sample preparation, including aggregation or dilution, might also influence these results. In addition, the rarity of disease, limited availability of LAM tissue samples, and inability to separate plasma EV secreted from LAM vs. other cells are the main obstacles in strengthening the connection between our in vitro and clinical findings. We also recognize that the direct evidence linking EV cargo to observed phenotypes is lacking. Nonetheless, our studies strongly support the EV-ITGα6/β1-c-Src-FAK axis in regulating LAM CSCs and metabolic plasticity, as well as metastable phenotypes.

In vivo bioluminescent reporter imaging
Ten minutes before imaging, mice were given D-luciferin (120 mg/kg, i.p., PerkinElmer Inc, 122799). Bioluminescent signals were recorded using the IVIS Spectrum System. The total photon flux of the chest regions was analyzed and quantified.
In vivo MMP study
The IVISense MMP-750 FAST Fluorescent Probe (MMPSense) from Revvity was used following the manufacturer’s instructions. The probe was reconstituted in 1.2 mL PBS and tail-vein injected into NCG mice 24 h post-EV inoculation at the dose of 2 nmol in 100 µL per mouse. The fluorescent signal was recorded using an IVIS imaging system.
Statistics and reproducibility
Data are expressed as mean ± SEM, unless otherwise indicated. Grubbs’ test was used to identify outliers. The significance of differences between groups was assessed using either a two-tailed Student’s
t
test, one-way ANOVA, or two-way ANOVA, as appropriate. Post-hoc comparisons were conducted with Tukey’s multiple comparison test. Differences were considered statistically significant at
p
values < 0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism, version 10.2.3, 10.4.1, and 10.5.0. All experiments were independently replicated at least three times unless otherwise noted. Replicates were defined as separate biological samples or independent experiments performed on different days. For experiments without statistical analysis, reproducibility was verified by consistent observations across multiple independent experiments. The sample sizes and number of replicates for these experiments are also specified in the figure legends.
