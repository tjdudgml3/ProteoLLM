Key Summary Points
Why carry out this study?
The role of autoantibodies in ND remains controversial, as they may contribute to neurological damage or, under certain pathophysiological conditions, exert protective effects
In this study, we aimed to analyze the clinical characteristics of patients with autoantibody-positive ND and investigate potential molecular mechanisms using proteomics
What was learned from the study?
Individuals with autoantibody-positive ND (ND +) exhibit superior frontal lobe cognitive function and better performance of activities of daily living
Proteomic analysis identified 162 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) associated with autoantibodies. These proteins were enriched in pathways related to endocytosis, endoplasmic reticulum protein processing, tight junctions, and cellular adhesion
ARRB1 and CAPN2 were positively correlated with Frontal Assessment Battery scores in the ND + group, indicating potential cognitive protective effects
Introduction
Neurodegenerative dementia (ND) is the most common form of dementia, characterized by an insidious onset and chronic, progressive worsening [
1
]. Despite ongoing research efforts, effective treatments to slow ND progression remain elusive, largely because of its complex etiology. The condition involves multiple pathological mechanisms, including neurotransmitter imbalances, inflammation, immune responses, and the accumulation of β-amyloid and tau proteins [
2
].
Recent evidence suggests that immune dysregulation plays a significant role in ND, with autoantibodies contributing to the pathophysiology [
3
]; however, patients with autoantibody-positive ND respond inconsistently to immunological interventions. For instance, a study by Flanagan demonstrated cognitive improvements in certain patients with ND with neural autoantibodies in their blood or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) following immunotherapy [
4
]. Similarly, a case report highlighted notable behavioral and cognitive improvements in a patient with frontotemporal dementia who tested positive for voltage-gated calcium channel antibodies following therapeutic plasmapheresis [
5
]. Conversely, other studies have shown no clinical improvement in patients with a semantic variant of primary progressive aphasia (svPPA) and anti-glial fibrillary acidic protein antibodies after three cycles of steroid therapy [
6
]. Studies have even found that some autoantibody-positive ND patients, who did not receive immunotherapy, exhibited higher Mini-Mental State Examination scores (MMSE) after 1 year compared with autoantibody-negative ND patients [
7
]. These mixed findings suggest that autoantibodies may also have protective roles against ND. The differential responses to immunotherapy observed in autoantibody-positive ND indicate that these autoantibodies may have either a pathogenic or protective function. Nonetheless, there is a notable deficiency in systematic research that thoroughly evaluates alterations in cognitive domains, activities of daily living, and mental status in this patient population. Specifically, it is yet to be determined whether these patients display distinct proteomic characteristics correlated with cognitive performance.
We propose the hypothesis that individuals diagnosed with autoantibody-positive ND demonstrate unique characteristics within particular cognitive domains, activities of daily living, or neuropsychiatric functions, accompanied by corresponding alterations in protein expression. In this study, we compared demographic data, laboratory results, and neuropsychological scores among patients with autoantibody-positive ND (ND + group), autoantibody-negative ND (ND– group), and healthy controls (HCs group). Proteomic analyses were also conducted to identify plasma protein changes in these groups. The findings aim to characterize autoantibody-associated ND, clarify the role of autoantibodies in neurodegenerative diseases, and provide insights for future personalized treatment approaches.
Methods
Study Participants
Between 2022 and 2023, all patients presenting with suspected neurodegenerative dementia to the Department of Neurology, Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University, underwent standardized clinical, neuropsychological, neuroimaging, and laboratory evaluations, which routinely included serum and/or CSF neuronal autoantibody testing. From this cohort, 13 patients with a confirmed diagnosis of ND who demonstrated serum and/or CSF neuronal autoantibody positivity (ND +) were consecutively enrolled and retrospectively analyzed. Neurodegenerative dementia was defined as a neurodegenerative disease with dementia as the primary clinical manifestation, such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD), frontotemporal dementia (FTD), and dementia with Lewy bodies. The ND + group comprised 11 cases of AD [
8
] and 2 cases of behavioral variant FTD [
9
], each diagnosis biomarker-verified.
To ensure comparability with the ND + cohort, the ND − and HC groups were established using an age- and sex-matched design. For each ND + patient, one ND – patient with the same diagnosis and one cognitively unimpaired healthy control were selected, with efforts made to approximate matching for age, sex, and education level. Specifically, the ND − cohort comprised 11 patients with AD and 2 with FTD admitted between 2022 and 2023, all confirmed negative for neuronal autoantibodies. HCs were drawn from a previously established control cohort of our research group during the same time frame (Fig.
1
).

Clinical data were systematically collected for all patients enrolled in the study, covering disease onset, clinical presentation, neurological and neuropsychological assessments, brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) findings, and laboratory analyses of CSF and peripheral blood. Diagnoses of ND were established according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition (DSM-5) [
10
] and supported by descriptions provided in Neurodegenerative Diseases and Dementia [
11
]. The etiological diagnosis of ND was established through standardized clinical assessments and advanced imaging modalities, including fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (FDG-PET) and amyloid PET, in line with internationally accepted diagnostic criteria and confirmed by experienced neurologists [
8
,
9
], thereby minimizing the likelihood of misclassification. Non-ND causes of dementia were excluded through systematic evaluation for endocrine, infectious, inflammatory, metabolic, nutritional, psychiatric, neoplastic, iatrogenic, toxic, and vascular disorders. None of the 26 patients exhibited warning signs of autoimmune encephalitis (AE), such as rapid progression, atypical features, or accompanying psychiatric symptoms/seizures.
HCs underwent the same clinical, neuropsychological, neuroimaging, and laboratory assessments as the patient cohort, with the exception of serum and CSF neuronal autoantibody testing. HCs had no subjective cognitive complaint, no history of mental or neurological diseases, showed no objective evidence of cognitive or functional impairment on clinical assessment, and were not taking medications known to affect cognition. Cognitive performance was within the normative range, defined as a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score ≥ 27 [
12
] and a Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) score ≥ 26 [
13
].
This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University, China. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their guardians. All clinical investigations were conducted in accordnce with the principles set forth in the Declaration of Helsinki.
Neuropsychological Assessments
Neuropsychological test results were available for all 39 participants. Patients with severe depression and anxiety were initially screened using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale [
15
] and the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale [
16
], respectively. The Mini-Mental State Examination [
17
], Montreal Cognitive Assessment [
13
], and Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) scores were used to assess global cognitive function. The Alzheimer’s Disease Cooperative Study–Activities of Daily Living (ADCS-ADL) scale was employed to evaluate self-care and instrumental activities of daily living [
18
]. The Neuropsychiatric Inventory (NPI) [
19
] and Frontal Behavioral Inventory (FBI) [
20
] were used as indicators to characterize behavioral and psychological changes. The NPI evaluates 12 behavioral domains commonly associated with dementia, whereas the FBI specifically assesses behavioral symptoms linked to frontal lobe dysfunction. Frontal Assessment Battery (FAB) was used to estimate frontal executive function with high sensitivity [
21
]. The following four specific cognitive domains were evaluated: (1) episodic memory (Auditory Verbal Learning Test immediate recall (AVLT), long-delayed free recall [
22
]); (2) language (the Boston Naming Test (BNT) [
23
], Verbal Fluency Test-categories of animals and fruits [
24
]); (3) attention (Digit Span Test [DST]-forward [
25
] and Trail Making Test [TMT] A [
26
]); (4) executive function (DST-backward [
25
] and TMT B [
26
]). To ensure consistency in interpreting intergroup differences across neuropsychological assessments, measures were categorized as either positively or negatively oriented. For positively oriented tests (e.g., MMSE, MoCA, CDR, ADCS-ADL, AVLT immediate and delayed recall, BNT, verbal fluency [animals, fruits], DST-forward, DST-backward, TMT-A, TMT-B), higher scores indicated better cognitive function or daily living abilities. In contrast, for negatively oriented tests (NPI, FBI), higher scores reflected worse behavioral or psychiatric symptoms. In intergroup comparisons, a group is deemed to demonstrate cognitive preservation if it achieves higher scores on positively oriented tests or lower scores on negatively oriented tests.
Autoantibody Measurement
Neuronal autoantibody profiles in blood and CSF were analyzed in 26 patients from the ND + and ND – groups as part of their routine clinical evaluation. The patients' neuronal autoantibody profiles were detected by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and cell-based assays. These assays targeted surface antigens, such as α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid receptors 1/2 (AMPAR1/2), contactin-associated protein-link 2 (CASPR2), dipeptidyl peptidase-like protein-6 (DPPX), gamma-aminobutyric acid type-B receptor (GABABR), immunoglobulin-like cell adhesion molecule IgLON family member 5 (IgLON5), leucine-rich glioma inactivated protein 1 (LGI1), N-methyl-d-aspartate receptor (NMDAR), and glycine receptor subunit alpha-1 (GlyRα1), in addition to intracellular antigens, such as amphiphysin, CV2, glutamic acid decarboxylase of kDa65 (GAD65), Hu, Ma-2/Ta, Ri, TR, Yo, titin, zinc finger protein of the cerebellum 4 (Zic4), recoverin, and sex-determining region Y box 1 (SOX1). Patients were classified as autoantibody-positive if any autoantibody was detected and autoantibody-negative if none were present. Notably, patients in the ND + group who did not show central nervous system (CNS) involvement linked to AE or paraneoplastic neurological syndromes were immunotherapy naive.
Molecular Markers
CSF core biomarkers for AD were analyzed using SIMOA technology on a single-molecule array platform provided by Quanterix Corp at the Beijing Kingmed Clinical Laboratory [
27
]. The Neuro-4-plexp assay was used to measure Aβ and tau levels, with detailed information available on the company’s website (
https://www.quanterix.com/
). A ratio of Aβ42/Aβ42 < 0.1 was deemed Aβ positivity per the manufacturer's guidelines. Furthermore, both ND + and ND– groups underwent amyloid positron emission tomography (PET) scans, with amyloid-positive PET scans confirmed visually by two experienced nuclear medicine physicians. A diagnosis of AD was supported by either CSF Aβ positivity or a positive amyloid PET scan.
Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) Function Evaluation
The CSF-serum albumin quotient (QAlb) was calculated using the formula QAlb = AlbCSF [mg/dl]/Albserum [mg/l] × 10
–2
to evaluate BBB function. Additionally, the age-adjusted upper reference value of the QAlb was calculated following the method proposed by Reiber et al.: QAlb(lim) = (4 + age/15) × 10
−3
[
28
]. BBB dysfunction was defined as QAlb > QLIM (Alb) [
29
].
Sample Preparation
Highly abundant proteins were depleted from plasma samples using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II liquid chromatography system coupled with the Multi Affinity Removal Column, Human-14 (Agilent part nos.5188-6557). Protein concentrations were measured using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Scientific catlog#23225). Low-abundance fractions were precipitated with trichloroacetic acid, reduced with 20 mM tris (2-carboxyethyl) phosphine, and alkylated with 40 mM iodoacetamide. The mixture was digested overnight with trypsin protease at 1/100 (w/w) trypsin protease-to-protein ratio at 37 °C. The lysate was desalted using a Monospin C18 column (GL Science, Tokyo, Japan) and vacuum centrifuged to dryness. The dried peptides were reconstituted prior to analysis.
High-pH Reversed-Phase Fractionation
Approximately 70 µg of mixed peptides for spectral library construction was fractionated using a high-PH reversed-phase HPLC system (1290 Infinity, Agilent). Briefly, peptides were fully resolved in Buffer A (20 mM ammonia, 2% ACN, pH 10.5) and loaded onto an Accucore C18 column (2.1 × 150 mm, 2.6 μm particles; Thermo Fisher Scientific) using a 56-min linear gradient of 5‒80% Buffer B (20 mM ammonia, 98% ACN, pH 10.5) at a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min. The fractions were pooled and combined into 22 groups and completely dried using vacuum centrifugation. Dried peptides were dissolved in 10 µl Milli-Q water with 0.1% formic acid (FA) and then spiked with iRT peptides for chromatographic correction.
Nano-Liquid Chromatography Coupled to Tandem Mass Spectrometry (nLC-MS/MS) Analysis
High-definition MS was performed using a Bruker timsTOF Pro system coupled to a nanoElute ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system. A reversed-phase C18 column (25 cm × 75 μm, 1.6 μm) was employed with a 120-min gradient (2‒95% acetonitrile, both containing 0.1% FA) and an integrated CaptiveSpray Source. The MS operated in parallel accumulation serial fragmentation data-dependent acquisition (PASEF-DDA) mode with the following settings: mass range: 100 to 1700 m/z, 1/K0 range: start 0.6 Vs/cm
2
, end 1.6 Vs/cm
2
, ramp time: 100 ms, capillary voltage: 1500 V, dry gas: 3 L/min, dry temperature: 180 ℃, PASEF settings: 10 MS/MS frames (1.16 s duty cycle), charge range 0‒5, target intensity 20,000, intensity threshold 2500, and higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD) 10 eV. Sample analysis was conducted in data-independent acquisition-parallel accumulation serial fragmentation (DIA–PASEF) mode, with the DIA–PASEF experiment acquired at defined 50 × 25 Th isolation windows spanning 400 to 1200 m/z. The collision energy was ramped linearly as a function of mobility, from 59 eV at 1/K0 = 1.6 Vs/cm
2
to 20 eV at 1/K0 = 0.6 Vs/cm
2
.
Spectral libraries were generated using Spectronaut version 17.6 (Biognosys) against the Uniprot-Swissprot reviewed database (Uniprot-Swissprot, release 2017-05) [
30
,
31
]. All parameters were processed in default mode. A hybrid peptide spectra library was constructed by combining data from the DDA analysis of 22 fractionated samples with 39 DIA raw files using Spectronaut Pulsar. Protein identification and label-free quantification were performed using MaxQuant software (
https://www.biochem.mpg.de/5111795/maxquant
).
Statistical Analysis
Numerical data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while counts and percentages describe categorical data. All patient characteristics and patient-related outcomes were tested for normal distribution using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed variables were compared using Student's
t
-test or analysis of variance (ANOVA) with the least significant difference test. For data that did not meet normality assumptions, non-parametric tests such as the Kruskal-Wallis test, followed by pairwise Mann-Whitney tests, were used. A
p
value < 0.05 after false discovery rate (FDR) correction was considered as statistically significant. Multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to investigate the effect of protein levels on cognitive function and to explore the interaction between protein levels and autoantibody status. These analyses were adjusted for age and sex. All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software (version 4.4.0).
Omicsbean software (
http://www.omicsbean.cn/
) was used for data analysis, including imputation, normalization, and principal component analysis (PCA). The imputation algorithm is detailed in a previous study [
32
]. Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were identified based on a significance threshold of
p
< 0.05 and |log2 (fold change) |> 0. General information on DEPs was obtained from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Protein Database, UniProt, and in-depth literature reviews. Throughout the article, DEPs are referred to using NCBI gene symbols. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway and Gene Ontology (GO) analyses were performed using DAVID (
http://david.abcc.ncifcrf.gov/
). GO analysis included three categories: biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions. Functional enrichment of the KEGG pathways was determined using the STRING database and KEGG classification (
https://string-db.org/
).
Search Strategy
Three electronic databases (PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science) were examined for articles published before December 2024. To identify KEGG pathways relevant to dementia and cognition, we employed a standardized search strategy of the form (KEGG pathway name) AND (Neurodegenerative Dementia OR Alzheimer’s disease OR Cognition). The term “KEGG pathway name” was used as a placeholder; in practice, each of the candidate pathways was entered individually into the search formula and queried separately. We also screened references from the included articles to identify potential missed articles. There were no language restrictions.
Results
General Characteristics of Participant Groups
This study included 39 participants divided into three groups: 13 patients in the ND + group, comprising 4 with anti-SOX1, 3 with anti-Ri, 2 with anti-titin, and 1 each with anti-recoverin, NMDAR-Ab, GABABR-Ab, and GlyR1; 13 patients in the ND– group; and 13 HCs. The demographic, laboratory, and neuropsychological characteristics of the three groups are summarized in Table
1
. The groups had no significant differences regarding sex, age, disease duration, or educational level. Furthermore, inflammatory markers in the blood, CSF, CSF biochemistry, CSF AD biomarkers, or QAlb (a marker of BBB permeability) did not differ significantly between the ND + and ND– groups. Neither the ND + nor the ND– groups exhibited autoimmune symptoms, including decreased consciousness, seizures, faciobrachial dystonic seizures, autonomic dysfunction, central hypoventilation, hyponatremia, infectious prodrome, new-onset headache, adverse reactions to psychiatric medications, other autoimmune diseases, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, early treatment resistance, or fluctuating psychopathology.

GO and KEGG Pathway Enrichment of Autoantibody-Related Proteins
Functional enrichment analysis using DAVID was conducted to elucidate the underlying biological processes and pathways associated with autoantibody-related proteins. Enriched ontological terms and pathways with
p
-values < 0.05 were selected for further analysis. Among the 162 DEPs, 8 significant GO biological process terms, 20 significant GO cellular component terms, four significant GO molecular function terms, and 13 significant KEGG pathways were identified (Fig.
3
). Biological process terms primarily involved cell communication, cell adhesion, cell motility, and migration. Cellular component terms indicated that the enriched proteins were predominantly localized in the cytoplasm. The enrichment of molecular function terms was mainly associated with protein binding and modification. Furthermore, KEGG analysis revealed that autoantibody-related proteins were primarily implicated in endocytosis, tight junctions, protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, proteoglycans in cancer, cell adhesion molecules, gap junctions, extracellular matrix-receptor interactions, tyrosine metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, and degradation of other glycans and glycosaminoglycans.

Differential Effects of Proteins on Cognitive Function in the ND + and ND – Groups
The availability of plasma proteomic data alongside cognitive assessments enabled us to examine proteomic alterations associated with intergroup cognitive differences between ND + and ND − patients. Through a literature review, we focused on six KEGG pathways closely associated with dementia: endocytosis, proteoglycans, protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, tight junctions, gap junctions, and cell adhesion. The role of proteins within these pathways in cognitive function differences was examined using interaction terms in linear regression models. We examined the differential effects of 34 proteins across these pathways using two cognitive assessment tools, the FAB and ADCS-ADL. Notably, in the ND + group, beta-arrestin-1 (ARRB1) and calpain-2 (CAPN2) showed significant positive correlations with FAB scores (
β
= 3.31, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.56 to 5.05;
β
= 0.32, 95% CI: 0.09‒0.55, respectively), while no such association was observed in the ND – group. In the ND – group, UDP-glucose:glycoprotein glucosyltransferase 1 (UGGT1) exhibited a negative correlation with FAB (
β

Discussion
This study is the first to our knowledge to investigate the clinical features and proteomic changes in patients with autoantibody-positive ND who demonstrate superior performance in activities of daily living (ADL) and better frontal lobe function. It identified four proteins potentially influencing neuropsychological performance linked to pathways such as endocytosis, proteoglycan metabolism, endoplasmic reticulum protein processing, tight junctions, and cell adhesion. These findings offer novel molecular insight into the cognitive benefits observed in these patients.
A comparative analysis of cognitive domains in patients with autoantibody-positive and -negative ND revealed that individuals with autoantibody-positive ND exhibit superior frontal lobe cognitive function and better ADL. These results suggest that autoantibodies may exert protective effects on cognitive function. Although existing literature does not fully support the protective role of autoantibodies in cognition, some studies have shown that autoantibodies targeting the NMDAR subunit NR1 can alleviate depressive and anxiety-related behaviors and enhance neuropsychiatric symptoms in murine models [
33
]. Based on these findings, we hypothesize that unidentified immune regulatory mechanisms may allow paraneoplastic or autoimmune brain autoantibodies to exhibit protective effects under certain conditions. However, due to the small sample size, this result should be interpreted cautiously as an exploratory finding requiring further research to validate its implications.
A previous study found that patients with cognitive impairment and autoantibodies had poorer cognitive performance than those without these conditions [
34
]; however, our study provides contrasting findings for several reasons. This study also provides an initial analysis of proteomic alterations in patients with autoantibody-positive ND and cognitively normal older adults. A comprehensive screening identified proteins with differential expression among the ND + and ND – groups, as well as the HCs, resulting in the identification of 162 significant DEPs. Subsequent KEGG enrichment analysis highlighted distinct proteomic signatures and elucidated six pathways implicated in neurodegeneration [
35
–
40
]. Among the six pathways analyzed, it was observed that four proteins exhibited inconsistent effects on FAB and ADCS-ADL within the ND + and ND – groups. This observation implies that the cognitive disparities between the ND + and ND – groups might be attributable to different effects of these proteins. Additionally, these proteins may be associated with the regulation of autophagy pathways, protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, and other related processes.
Specifically, our analysis revealed that ARRB1 and CAPN2 were positively correlated with FAB scores in the ND + group, indicating potential cognitive protective effects. Previous research has demonstrated that ARRB1 mitigates cognitive impairment by facilitating amyloid-beta clearance and reducing Aβ25-35-induced cellular apoptosis [
41
]. CAPN2, a calcium-regulated non-lysosomal thiol protease, is critical for synaptic plasticity and plays a role in preserving learning and memory by restoring protein homeostasis [
42
]. Conversely, UGGT1 was negatively correlated with FAB scores exclusively in the ND – group, while no correlation was observed in the ND + group. UGGT1 is commonly associated with endoplasmic reticulum stress, which can trigger the phosphorylation and aggregation of tau proteins, thereby worsening cognitive impairment in individuals with AD [
37
,
43
].
These proteins associated with cognitive preservation in patients with autoantibody-positive ND are involved in diverse but complementary domains of neurobiological processes. ARRB1 functions as a signaling adaptor regulating GPCR desensitization and intracellular cascades, thereby modulating synaptic plasticity and apoptosis. CAPN2 supports cytoskeletal remodeling and protein homeostasis but may also mediate excitotoxic injury when overactivated [
44
]. UGGT1, an ER-resident chaperone, reflects proteostatic control and ER stress, processes strongly implicated in neurodegenerative pathology. PPP2R1A, a scaffolding subunit of protein phosphatase 2A, governs MAPT dephosphorylation and microtubule stability, with impaired activity linked to neurodevelopmental disorders [
45
]. Together, these proteins represent pathways of signal transduction, proteostasis, synaptic plasticity, and cytoskeletal/microtubule integrity. Their altered expression in ND + patients may therefore reflect a range of biological processes, including inflammatory signaling, proteostatic stress, or compensatory repair mechanisms—processes that in combination may contribute to better cognitive performance. An alternative explanation is that vascular damage, blood-brain barrier dysfunction, or incidental antibody presence in the brains of patients with neurodegenerative dementia could also account for these proteomic changes, without any direct causal link to cognition. Nonetheless, we explicitly emphasize that these interpretations remain speculative and require validation in larger, mechanistically focused studies.
Modulation of these pathways offers valuable insights into the complex pathophysiological processes associated with ND in the presence of comorbid autoantibodies. Nevertheless, this study has several limitations. First, it included only 13 participants in each group, limiting the statistical power and generalizability of the findings. Consequently, the external validity of our results requires cautious interpretation. In particular, because the study was conducted at a single center with small group sizes and specific inclusion criteria, the findings may be context-specific and not fully generalizable to other ND cohorts or clinical settings. Therefore, replication in larger, multicenter samples with broader demographic and clinical diversity will be necessary to establish the robustness of these observations. Second, consistent with prior studies [
7
,
46
], patients were classified as autoantibody-positive if either serum or CSF testing was positive. Repeating the neuronal autoantibody testing, ideally using confirmatory assays such as tissue-based analysis, may further strengthen the reliability of our findings. Third, the cross-sectional design restricts the ability to infer causal relationships among autoantibody status, proteomic changes, and cognitive performance. Longitudinal studies would be more effective in elucidating the temporal dynamics and causality of these associations. Fourth, the proteomic and bioinformatics findings have not yet been validated using independent methods or replication in larger cohorts.
Conclusion
This study represents an initial exploratory analysis of proteomic alterations and their correlations with cognition in patients with autoantibody-positive ND, providing preliminary evidence to inform future research on therapeutic targets for cognitive preservation.
