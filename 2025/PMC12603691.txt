•
Aid the identification of key metal ions important for
K. pneumoniae
to counteract the antimicrobial impact of h-plasma, irrespective of the clinical isolates studied, i.e., shed light on metal ion-dependent virulence factor regulation.
•
Aid in developing/designing a rational antimicrobial strategy by targeting metal ion-related transporters, enzymes to combat bacterial infection, i.e., directing towards metal-dependent functioning of antibiotics.

Background
Difficult-to-treat antimicrobial resistant bloodstream infections caused by
K. pneumoniae
have emerged as a significant public health concern, contributing substantially to both mortality and morbidity [
1
]. The growing problem of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) has rendered many last-resort antibiotics ineffective, creating an urgent need for the discovery and development of new therapeutic/strategies to treat these pathogens [
2
]. Metal ions have been known to play an important role in bacterial physiology, augmenting bacteria's ability to adapt and survive in the host environment [
[3]
,
[4]
,
[5]
]. Studies indicate metal availability may drive evolutionary changes in bacterial metalloenzymes [
6
,
7
]. Besides, various metal ions have shown antimicrobial efficacy against K. pneumoniae. For example, platinum, gold, and palladium have shown significant antimicrobial activity, with platinum being particularly effective even at low concentrations (3.90 mg/L) [
8
]. Zinc, copper, and iron are reported to be associated with
K. pneumoniae
growth, virulence, and interaction with host and serve as major cofactors for bacterial proteome [
9
]. Thus, investigating metal-associated processes in
K. pneumoniae
may reveal new targets for combating difficult-to-treat drug-resistant infections. Despite this, studies reporting intracellular metal ion dynamics during bloodstream conditions are scarce. This dataset was, therefore, compiled to address this gap by using h-plasma as a host factor and to support future research in this area.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods
4.1
Bacterial culture, growth evaluation, and pellet generation
Pure colonies of
K. pneumoniae
were obtained by incubating bloodstream-isolated 7 clinical strains of
K. pneumoniae
and one ATCC strain (33495 ™) on Mueller-Hinton agar plates at 37 °C overnight, followed by an additional overnight preparatory broth culture at 37 °C in MHB with shaking at 250 rpm at 37 °C, confirmed using MALDI-TOF MS. The secondary cultures were then inoculated (1: 500 v/v) in fresh MHB and incubated under aerobic conditions at 37 °C until the mid-exponential phase of growth (∼0.4–0.6 OD600nm post-inoculation). 1*10
7
cells of exponentially grown
K. pneumoniae
cells (different clinical isolates) were collected using centrifugation at 6000 RPM and seeded in 1 ml of MHB consisting different concentrations (1, 5, 10, 25, and 55 % (vol/vol)) of h-plasma, incubated for 4 h., followed by plating (10 µl) on MH agar plate for CFU/ml calculation. Furthermore, incubated cells were collected as a pellet using centrifugation at 10,000 RPM, followed by 2 washes using PBS containing 5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, and twice with PBS to remove residual MHB/h-plasma content from the pellet [
11
] and subjected to sample preparation for metallomics.
4.2
Sample preparation for ICP-MS
Bacterial pellets were desiccated for 6 h at 95 °C and then digested in 250 µL 65 % (vol/vol) HNO
3
at 95 °C for 20 min. Samples were centrifuged at 18,000 g for 25 min, and supernatant (200 µL) was diluted to a final volume of 1.5 ml using MilliQ-H2O (ICP-MS grade). Elemental content was quantitatively analysed using ICP-MS XSERIES 2 Thermofisher Scientific using the parameters mentioned in “Acquisition Method Supplementary file 2” and Element Standard (Element Standard Curves Supplementary file 3).
4.3
Data analysis
Based on colony count (CFU/ml) data, the impact of h-plasma (in different concentrations, i.e., 0, 1, 5, 10, 25, 55, and 100 % (vol/vol) in MHB) on the growth of
K. pneumoniae
strain was assessed. And based on growth behavior, the bacterial strains were divided into 2 groups, i.e., h-plasma sensitive and insensitive, consisting of 4 strains each group. Next, the individual metal ion concentration/cell (total concentration of metal divided by CFU/ml for each strain) for each group of bacterial strains was plotted w.r.t h-plasma concentration and analysed to shortlist/identify metal ion profiles reflecting/associated with bacterial growth. The data is presented as Mean ± SD of the concentration of metal ions from each strain in a group. For statistical analysis, GraphPad Prism 10.1.0 software was used to analyse the data. Multiple unpaired T-test was used to analyse the results. For all tests, a
P
-value of <0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.
Limitations
1.
The study intends to evaluate the intracellular metal ion profile, but unfortunately, ICP-MS cannot differentiate between the metal adsorbed on the bacterial surface and internalized (cytosolic or organelle-localized) metal ions. Although washing steps were included in the method of pellet generation still this can be a limitation.
2.
The duration of h-plasma exposure can be a factor for variability. In case of exposure for a shorter duration, the bacteria may not have fully adapted their metal uptake and efflux mechanisms. However, longer incubations might induce stress responses that alter metal homeostasis differently. This data is acquired after incubating the bacterial cell for 4 h (single time point).
