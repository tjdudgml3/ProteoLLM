1
Introduction
Abdominal aortic aneurysm
(AAA)
is
a common vascular disease characterized by localized dilation and
weakening of the aortic wall, which can ultimately lead to aortic
rupture and severe clinical consequences.
The pathogenesis of AAA is complex, involving multifactorial interactions
among genetic, inflammatory, and immune factors.
−
Although progress has been made
in the early detection and clinical management of AAA, the pathobiological
mechanisms underlying its development and expansion remain incompletely
understood and effective therapeutic strategies for small AAAs remain
lacking. Therefore, elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying
AAA, particularly identifying potential therapeutic targets, has become
a significant focus in cardiovascular research.
In the formation
of AAA, immune-mediated inflammatory responses,
metabolic dysregulation,
and vascular
smooth muscle cell (VSMC) phenotype modulation
play critical roles. Increasing research has identified that post-translational
modifications (PTMs), such as acetylation, are pivotal in these biological
processes. As a common PTM, acetylation has been shown to play essential
roles in regulating cellular function, signal transduction, metabolic
pathways, and immune responses.
−
In particular, some of the pathological processes
involved also play a key role in AAA.
Recent studies on the
role of acetylation in AAA have deepened
and revealed potential regulatory networks. For example, acetylation
not only regulates the recruitment and activation of inflammatory
cells in the vascular wall
but also indirectly
affects the mechanical stability of the aortic wall by reshaping VSMC
phenotypes and influencing VSMC differentiation, proliferation, and
apoptosis.
,
Moreover, acetylation is also
important in metabolic regulation, as it modulates the activity of
key metabolic enzymes and impacts the local inflammatory microenvironment.
−
These findings suggest that acetylation may be a central regulatory
factor in the pathological mechanisms of AAA, providing new theoretical
support for the further exploration of its pathophysiological mechanisms.
More importantly, histone acetylation is an important epigenetic
modification. Studies have shown that histone acetylation and deacetylation
are dynamic and reversible processes, primarily regulated by three
protein families: histone acetyltransferases (HATs), histone deacetylases
(HDACs), and bromodomain-containing proteins (BRDs). As a key component
of epigenetic modifications, histone acetylation plays a critical
role in the progression, treatment, and prognosis of many diseases.
−
However, systematic and in-depth studies of the specific roles of
histone acetylation in different pathological stages and microenvironments
of AAA are still lacking. Further analysis is required to unravel
the completeness and interaction mechanisms of the molecular network.
In this study, proteomic and acetylomic analyses were performed
on abdominal aortic tissues from control and AAA mouse models, leading
to the identification of numerous differentially expressed proteins
and acetylation sites. Our research findings indicated that histones
played an important role in the inflammatory response and immune regulation
pathways involved in AAA formation. Notably, a substantial number
of acetylated histones involved in this process were regulated by
HATs and HDACs. This study aimed to clarify the key regulatory networks
of acetylation in AAA development and identify potential therapeutic
targets.

Methods
A detailed description of the
methods is provided in
Supporting Information
.
2.1
Animal Experiments
All animal treatments
and experimental protocols were approved by the Animal Care and Use
Committee at the Institute of Basic Medical Sciences, Chinese Academy
of Medical Science and Peking Union Medical College (ACUC-A01-2025-006).
All animal experiments were conducted in compliance with Directive
2010/63/EU of the European Parliament on the protection of animals
used for scientific purposes. Mice were maintained on a 12:12 h day/night
cycle and provided constant access to food and water.
2.2
Mouse Model of AAA
Eight-week-old
male Apoe knockout (Apoe–/−) mice on a normal chow diet
were used in this study. Mice were anesthetized by intraperitoneal
injection of avertin at a dose of 187.5 mg/kg of body weight, and
the surgery was performed only when the toe reflex disappeared. This
anesthetic is suitable for experimental scenarios lacking inhalation
anesthesia equipment. Its intraperitoneal injection characteristics
and rapid onset can meet surgical requirements under conditions without
an inhalation anesthetic machine. This method was conducted in accordance
with previously published studies and complied with standard operating
procedures.
,
The mice were infused subcutaneously
with Angiotensin II (Ang II, Solarbio, A9290) at a rate of 1.44 mg/kg/day
using osmotic pumps (Alzet, model 2004) for 28 days. Mice in the control
group were treated with saline via the same method. The procedure
was performed as described in previous studies.
,
In brief, Ang II was dissolved in sterile saline and delivered using
an Alzet osmotic pumps. Mice were anesthetized with an intraperitoneal
injection of avertin, and the pumps were implanted subcutaneously
through a small incision at the back of the neck, which was then sutured.
The incision healed quickly without infection in any of the mice.
2.3
Abdominal Ultrasonography in Mice
Aortic diameters were measured by a high-frequency ultrasound Vevo
2100 echography device (Visual Sonics). Briefly, mice were anesthetized
with isoflurane (3%) in oxygen for 2 min until they were immobile.
Each mouse was then taped on a heated (35–37 °C) procedure
board with isoflurane (1.5%) administered via nosecone on a supine
position during ultrasound. Inhalation anesthesia was chosen for the
ultrasound examination to maintain hemodynamic stability, which was
directly related to the special requirements for the accuracy of cardiac
function data in this detection method. The animal ultrasound examination
was conducted in a specialized animal ultrasound institution with
an inhalation anesthetic machine; therefore, isoflurane was used as
the anesthetic. The abdominal area was shaved and coated with an ultrasound
transmission gel before positioning the acquisition probe. These measurements
were performed on days 0 and 28 after Ang II infusion. As previously
described,
long-axis ultrasound scans
of suprarenal aortas were performed from the aortic hiatus to the
renal artery. Images of the abdominal aorta were obtained. Maximal
internal diameters of aortic images were measured with Vevo LAB software.
2.4
Sample Collection
At day 28 after
Ang II infusion, mice were deep anesthetized with avertin (187.5 mg/kg
body weight) by intraperitoneal injection. It was used in the animal
euthanasia process in this study, which was in line with the operational
norms. After the toe reflex disappeared, blood was collected, the
right atrium was cut open, and saline was perfused through the left
ventricle to remove blood in aortas. Then the abdominal aortas were
dissected, weighed, and photographed for documentation. After rapid
freezing with liquid nitrogen, they were transferred to −80
°C until proteome and acetylation analysis and detection were
performed. Tissues intended for morphological experiments were fixed
in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, embedded in paraffin, and stored
as paraffin blocks at 4 °C or room temperature until testing.
2.5
Proteomic and Acetylomic Experiments
Samples were retrieved from −80 °C, and an appropriate
amount was weighed into a precooled mortar with liquid nitrogen. Liquid
nitrogen was added, and the samples were thoroughly ground into a
fine powder. The samples were then collected, lysed with lysis buffer,
and digested with trypsin. To enrich the acetylated peptides, the
peptides were dissolved in prewashed resin (antibody resin, catalog
number PTM0104, provided by PTM Bio, Hangzhou Jingjie Biotechnology
Co., Ltd.). The peptides were separated by a Vanquish Neo UHPLC system
and then injected into an NSI ion source for ionization before being
analyzed by Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometry (ThermoFisher Scientific).
2.6
Database Search
The data-independent
acquisition (DIA) data were processed using Spectronaut (v.18) software
with default parameters. The database used was Mus_musculus_10090_SP_20231220.fasta.
A decoy database was generated to estimate the false discovery rate
(FDR) caused by random matches. Trypsin/P was specified as the digestion
enzyme, allowing for up to two missed cleavages. Carbamido­methylation
of cysteine (carbamidomethyl [C]) was specified as a fixed modification,
while oxidation of methionine and acetylation at the protein N-terminus
were set as variable modifications. The FDR thresholds for protein,
peptide, and PSM identification were all set to 1%.
Specifically,
for the acetylome data, trypsin/P was used as the digestion enzyme,
allowing up to four missed cleavages. Carbamidomethylation of cysteine
was set as a fixed modification, while methionine oxidation, protein
N-terminal acetylation, and lysine acetylation were set as variable
modifications. The PTM localization filter was enabled with the minimum
localization threshold set to 0.75. A decoy database was included
to estimate the FDR arising from random matches.
2.7
Statistics
The raw quantitative data
were processed by using the following steps: Proteins/Kac sites that
existed in >50% of the samples in three replicates were considered
quantifiable. The intensity of the quantifiable proteins and Kac sites
in each sample was divided by their corresponding median for normalization.
After that, the minimum value of all of the samples was used to fill
in the missing values. All samples passed quality control and were
used for further bioinformatics analyses.
Then, to calculate
the statistical significance of difference between groups, the Student’s
t
test was performed on the relative quantitative value
of each protein from the two groups. The corresponding
p
value was calculated as the significance index.
p
< 0.05 was usually considered as the threshold for significance.
Proteins or sites with
p
< 0.05 and fold change
of >1.5 were considered as significantly increased in abundance,
while
proteins or sites with
p
< 0.05 and fold change
of <1/1.5 were considered as significantly decreased in abundance.

3.1
Formation of the Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm
Model in Mouse
Ten mice were divided into two groups, and
five mice in each group were respectively treated with saline and
Ang II for 28 days. Ang II stimulation induced abdominal aortic aneurysms
in mice (
Figure
A).
Subsequently, it could be observed that compared with the control
group, the disease group mice showed a significant increase in systolic
blood pressure (

Meanwhile, aorta weight/body weight (

formed in mice. Of the five mice used for modeling, one did not develop
an AAA, whereas the remaining four developed AAA. No mortality was

from the remaining AAA-formed mice for proteomic analysis. For the
control group, physiological characteristics of all five mice were
similar, and following a 1:1 design principle, three mice (Saline_1,

3.6
Global Analysis of Histone Acetylation
Histone acetylation is a central element in cellular regulatory networks
and is important for understanding the regulation of gene expression,
cell fate decisions, and disease mechanisms.
We identified numerous histones and acetylated histones (
Tables S9 and S10
) and then counted the number
of Kac sites occurring on the lysine of H1 linker proteins and core
histones H2A, H2B, H3, and H4 (
Figure
A), with the highest number of modifications occurring
in H2B. Subsequently, we compared the acetylation levels of H1, H2A,
H2B, H3, and H4 as a whole between the two groups, and the results
showed that none of them differed significantly (
Figure
B). Then, we compared the acetylation
levels of specific histone modification sites between the AngII and
saline groups (
Figure
C). Among these, 28 sites showed significantly increased acetylation
in the AngII group with most located on H2B histones. As acetylation
is reversible and related to acetyltransferases, deacetylases, therefore
we explored the protein abundance of differentially acetylation-related
enzymes, BRDs, and histone in both AngII and saline groups (
Figure
D and
Tables S11–S13
). The results showed decreased
abundance of BRDs, acetyltransferases, and deacetylases, while histones
showed increased abundance. We did a Pearson correlation of the differential
histone modification sites in
Figure
C with the proteins in
Figure
D and plotted the correlation heatmap (
Figure
E and
Table S14
). Negative correlations were observed
between the abundance of Sirt5 and Sirt2 protein and most histone
acetylation site levels, suggesting that these deacetylases may influence
histone acetylation. To demonstrate which histone sites were most
affected by the key modifying enzymes, selected histone modification
sites and acetylation-related enzymes with significant correlations,
and plotted the scatterplots (
Figure
F). The sites significantly correlated with both sirt2
and sirt5 included Hist2h2bb-K13/K16, H2bc3-K12/K16/K17/K13/K21, H2bc4-K12/K13/K17/K16,
H2bc7-K12/K13/K17/K16, H2bc9-K12/K13/K16/K17, H3-3a-K123, and H4c1-K13/K17.
Additionally, sirt5 was also correlated to H1-1-K121, H2bc4-K121/K6,
H2bc7-K121/K6, and H4c1-K9. Overall, the majority of histone sites
were associated with deacetylases, which may modulate key pathways
in AAA through changes in histone acetylation, suggesting potential
therapeutic targets.

4
Discussion
In this study, we performed
a differential analysis of proteins
and acetylation modifications in the abdominal aortic tissues of control
and AAA mouse models and identified numerous proteins and acetylation
sites. KEGG enrichment analysis revealed many pathways related to
inflammation and immune response both in the proteome and acetylome
(
Figures
C and
C). AAA is a chronic inflammatory disease primarily
associated with destructive remodeling of the extracellular matrix
(ECM), VSMC apoptosis, inflammation, and oxidative stress.
−
The pathways enriched in this study also suggested that immune cells
and inflammatory factors play an important role in the progression
of AAA. Immune cells contribute to aortic dilation and rupture by
secreting inflammatory factors IL-1β and IL-18, which promote
apoptosis of aortic wall cells and drive the phenotypic transition
of VSMCs.
,
Additionally, they release proteases that
degrade the ECM, further weakening the aortic structure. Neutrophils,
the most abundant and rapidly responding immune cells in the human
immune system, can exacerbate AAA through the activation of matrix
metalloproteinases (MMPs) and the inactivation of tissue inhibitors
of metalloproteinases (TIMPs).
The involvement
of NETs is crucial in the formation of AAA,
as NETs activate NLRP3 in macrophages, further promoting the release
of IL-1β and IL-18, which enhance inflammation in the aorta.
Additionally, we found differentially acetylated proteins were
also significantly enriched in metabolic pathways, including cholesterol
metabolism, amino acid metabolism, glycolysis, and the TCA cycle (
Figure
C). Cholesterol and
its derivatives (such as oxidized cholesterol) are important molecules
in the regulation of inflammation regulation.
Inflammatory factors such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β
can also affect lipid metabolism. Enrichment of cholesterol metabolism-related
acetylated proteins may be associated with the generation of lipid
signaling molecules, potentially contributing to lipid peroxidation
and inflammatory signaling, for example, through activating macrophages
via oxidized cholesterol, which could further promote inflammation
and immune cell function.
Oxidized cholesterol
can also participate in MMP activation, accelerating vascular wall
degradation
and contributing to the pathological
progression of AAA. Acetylated proteins with decreased abundance were
significantly enriched in amino acid metabolism, glycolysis, and the
TCA cycle, reflecting a potential shift in metabolic priorities from
energy production to inflammatory signaling in the inflammatory environment.
,
Moreover, mitochondria in the vascular wall of AAA patients are
usually damaged by oxidative stress and inflammation, resulting in
a decreased TCA cycle efficiency and accumulation of glycolytic intermediates.
We performed PPI analysis on the pathways that were enriched in
both the proteome and acetylome, including Complement and coagulation
cascades, SLE, coronavirus disease COVID-19, and NET formation. The
complement system is an important component of the innate immune system
and is involved in processes such as inflammation, vascular remodeling,
and thrombosis. In the onset and development of AAA, the complement
system promotes local inflammatory responses and the degradation of
the vessel wall by activating the complement cascade, thereby influencing
the formation and progression of AAA.
Systemic lupus erythematosus is an autoimmune disease characterized
by abnormal activation of the immune system, leading to inflammation
and damage to blood vessel walls. The development of AAA is also
associated with chronic inflammation of the vessel wall, where the
activation of immune cells and the secretion of cytokines play important
roles in the pathological processes under both conditions. Coronavirus
binds to the ACE2 receptor on host cells through its spike glycoprotein,
potentially causing endothelial cell damage and dysfunction, thereby
increasing the risk of vascular diseases. The NET formation pathway
is closely related to AAA. Studies have shown that blocking thrombosis-dependent
NET can reduce the progression of AAA.
,
Inhibiting
the formation or function of NETs may serve as a new strategy for
the treatment of AAA.
We noticed that there were abundant acetylated
histones in the
NETs formation pathway. Histone acetylation, through regulating chromatin
structure and gene expression, plays a widespread and crucial role
in cellular functions and life processes.
−
This dynamic
modification mechanism is not only a switch for gene expression but
also involves processes such as metabolism, development, immunity,
and DNA repair. The acetylation modification sites on histone H2B
were the most abundant, likely due to the highly exposed lysine residues.
The N-terminal tail of histone H2B has multiple lysine residues that
are exposed to the surface of chromatin, making them major targets
for acetylation. A total of 69 differential histone modification sites
were identified (
Figure
A), with 28 showing significant differences, all exhibiting increased
abundance in the AAA group (
Figure
C). Since acetylation is reversible and associated
with acetyltransferases and deacetylases, we examined the abundance
of acetylation-related enzymes, BRDs, and histones in the Ang II/saline
groups (
Figure
D).
We found that histone deacetylases exhibited reduced expression in
the AAA model, and sirtuin family proteins Sirt2 and Sirt5 were significantly
negatively correlated with most differential acetylation sites (
Figure
E). We speculated
that promoting the expression of Sirt2 and Sirt5 may reduce histone
acetylation levels, potentially inhibiting the formation or function
of NETs. This could in turn suppress immune and inflammatory processes
in AAA, thereby limiting disease progression and highlighting Sirt2
and Sirt5 as potential therapeutic targets.
The sirtuin family
of proteins plays an important role in immune
regulation and inflammatory responses.
−
In recent years, numerous studies
have identified sirtuins as promising targets for the treatment of
aortic aneurysms.
,
,
−
For example, age-related reduction of Sirt1 in vascular smooth muscle
promotes vascular senescence and inflammation, thereby accelerating
AAA progression, suggesting that activation of Sirt1 or modulation
of its downstream pathways may help delay or prevent AAA development.
Similarly, SIRT6 acts as an epigenetic repressor
in thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) by inhibiting vascular inflammation
and cellular senescence, indicating that restoration of SIRT6 activity
may serve as a potential therapeutic strategy.
Calorie restriction markedly protects against Ang II-induced
AAA in Apoe
–
/
–
mice by upregulating
SIRT1 in VSMCs, which epigenetically suppresses MMP2 expression via
H3K9 deacetylation.
Currently, several
drugs have been reported to mitigate the aortic aneurysm formation
by activating sirtuins. The anticancer agent TIC10 reduces aneurysm
development in mice by activating mitochondrial ClpP and enhancing
SIRT1 activity in VSMCs, maintaining their quiescent differentiated
phenotype and suppressing inflammation and elastin degradation.
Licochalcone A attenuates Ang II–induced
AAA via the miR-181b/SIRT1/HO-1 signaling pathway.
Additionally, medium-dose Formoterol alleviates EPO-induced
AAA by activating the β2-adrenergic receptor/cAMP/SIRT1 pathway.
Based on the above studies, we propose that
targeting Sirt2 and Sirt5 activation may offer a novel therapeutic
strategy for AAA.
In this study, AAA was induced by Ang II injection
with saline-injected
mice as controls. We note that some studies use Ang II-treated mice
that do not develop AAA as controls. Li et al. reported that AAA formation
is age-dependent and exhibits interindividual variability, with certain
cell clusters promoting progression or providing protection.
Rush et al. further demonstrated that AAA development
relies on the activation of pathways including inflammation,
extracellular matrix remodeling, smooth muscle

However, our study also has certain limitations,
including the
small sample size, differences between mouse and human aortic tissues,
and the lack of experimental validation. Further research is required
to confirm the effectiveness of the identified targets. In addition,
previous studies have employed Ang II-induced nonaneurysmal aortas
as controls to reduce potential nonspecific effects arising from different
treatments. Future studies could further reveal AAA susceptibility
and potential therapeutic targets by simultaneously analyzing saline
controls, Ang II nonaneurysmal, and Ang II aneurysmal mice.

Conclusions
In summary, this study
provides comprehensive proteomic and acetylomic
data for the abdominal aortic tissue in the Ang II-induced mouse model
of an aortic aneurysm, significantly expanding the existing acetylation
database in mice. It emphasizes the importance of histones in the
inflammation response and immune dysregulation pathways. The results
suggest that Sirt2 and Sirt5 may potentially modulate immune and inflammatory
processes in the NETs, representing potential targets for AAA progression
and treatment, and they also provide new possibilities for exploring
therapeutic targets within the sirtuin protein family.
