Introduction
Protein phosphorylation is a type of post-translational modification (PTM) occurring on serine, threonine, tyrosine, histidine, arginine, lysine, cysteine, glutamic acid, and aspartic acid residues. Depending on the linkage between the modified residue and the phosphate group, it can be categorized into O-phosphorylation (Ser, Thr, and Tyr), N-phosphorylation (Arg, Lys, and His), S-phosphorylation (Cys), and COO-phosphorylation (Glu and Asp) [
1
]. However, research on protein N-phosphorylation has significantly lagged behind that on O-phosphorylation, mainly due to the acid lability of phosphoramidate bonds and the lack of specific antibodies [
2
]. Protein O-phosphorylation is involved in diverse biological processes and diseases [
3
], with its kinases [
4
] and phosphatases [
5
] serving as established various drug targets, particularly in cancer. While, publicly reported protein histidine phosphorylation are mainly limited to two-component systems in prokaryotes or lower eukaryotes [
2
]; only one kinase (i.e. McsB) of protein arginine phosphorylation has been identified in gram-positive bacteria [
6
]; and no kinase or phosphatase with protein lysine phosphorylation has been reported. Fortunately, advances in specific antibodies [
7
] and omics technologies [
8
] have increased the enthusiasm of researchers to study protein N-phosphorylation over the past decade, and its kinases and phosphatases are being continuously characterized, which accelerates the study of the biological functions of protein N-phosphorylation. In
Bacillus subtilis
, the McsB marks damaged proteins via arginine phosphorylation, facilitating their proteasomal degradation [
9
]. Protein histidine kinases in bacterial two-component regulatory systems have also emerged as targets for novel antibacterial agents [
10
]. Furthermore, kinases and phosphatases associated with protein histidine phosphorylation have been found to act as tumor initiators or suppressors [
11
]. Nucleoside diphosphate kinase B (NDPKB), a protein histidine kinase in higher eukaryotes, can activate the KCa3.1 channel by phosphorylating a histidine residue on KCa3.1, thereby activating human CD
4+
T cells [
12
,
13
]. Another study reported that NDPKB activates transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily V member 5 (TRPV5) channels to mediate Ca
2+
reabsorption in the distal tubules of the kidney, and this activation requires histidine phosphorylation at position 711 of the TRPV5 protein [
14
]. In addition, the histidine phosphatase activity of phospholysine phosphohistidine inorganic pyrophosphate phosphatase (LHPP) is crucial for the proliferation, metastasis, and anti-apoptosis of cervical cancer cells [
15
]; LHPP-mediated histidine dephosphorylation suppresses the self-renewal of mouse embryonic stem cells [
16
], and regulates stress-induced depression-like behaviors in mice [
17
]. Together, these findings suggest that protein N-phosphorylation plays an important role in regulating biological processes in mammals, and that its dysregulation may lead to a variety of diseases, including cancer and cognitive dysfunction.
Research on the sequence and structural patterns of protein O-phosphorylation suggests that although phosphorylation does not always occur in surface-accessible regions, surface-accessible amino acids are more readily modified [
18
]. This is because disordered regions increase their propensity to fold, allowing amino acid side chains to readily fold into the catalytic sites of modifying enzymes. Intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) contain short linear motifs that mediate protein–protein interactions and can be regulated by PTMs such as phosphorylation. Studies have found that 20% of pathogenic missense mutations in human proteome occur within IDRs [
19
]. Therefore, the analysis of phosphorylation sites within IDRs is crucial for elucidating the mechanisms of proteins and their interacting partners in disease. The structural environment of protein PTM sites is not random; incorporating solvent accessibility surface area [
20
], disorder [
21
,
22
], physicochemical properties [
23
] information significantly improves the performance of protein phosphorylation site prediction tools. Furthermore, protein O-phosphorylation kinases exhibit distinct sequence specificity towards their substrates [
24
,
25
]. Identifying and characterizing these specific motifs is of significant value for developing protein phosphorylation antibodies, kinase discovery, and mass spectrometry (MS) identification. Thus, the analysis and summarization of the sequence and structural patterns of protein N-phosphorylation may represent an important step toward advancing N-phosphorylation research.
As mentioned earlier, some achievements have been made in protein arginine and histidine phosphorylation, while these studies rely on a few identified protein kinases, phosphatases, and substrates [
26
,
27
]. In particular, single phosphoproteomics studies targeting N-phosphorylation typically detect a limited number of sites and proteins, and most of them only present and discuss basic functional and sequence analyses. Considering that systematic comparative analyses between protein N-phosphorylation and O-phosphorylation are still lacking, it is necessary to systematically integrate protein N-phosphorylation data and conduct large-scale, cross-species analyses of functional, structural, and sequence patterns, followed by in-depth comparison with O-phosphorylation. Therefore, we systematically analyze and compare the biological functions, sequence motifs, and structural features of proteins undergoing N-phosphorylation, utilizing experimentally validated protein N-/O-phosphorylation sites information collected from Nphos. This study aimed to explore the potential roles of protein N-phosphorylation modifications
in vivo
and to preliminarily characterize the sequence and structural features of the corresponding modification sites. We hope that our findings will provide new insights for MS identification, antibody development, and the discovery of protein N-phosphorylated kinases and phosphatases. This work may have also attracted more researchers to investigate the biological functions of protein N-phosphorylation and its potential roles in diseases mechanisms.

Distribution of N-phosphorylation sites of proteins
To investigate the relationship between N-/O-phosphorylation sites and their corresponding residues in proteins, we calculated the number of detected phosphorylated residues, the phosphorylation occurrence rate, and the amino acid usage (see Materials and methods) for the residues corresponding to three types of N-phosphorylation (His, Lys, and Arg) and three types of O-phosphorylation (Ser, Thr, and Tyr). The results showed that over 80% of protein N-/O-phosphorylation sites exhibited an occurrence rate below 1%, even when combining the occurrence rates for the three N-phosphorylated residues (93.81%) or the three O-phosphorylated residues (86.43%) (
Fig. 2
). And there was no significant correlation between the phosphorylation occurrence rate and the amino acid usage for the corresponding residues in either eukaryotes (
Fig. 2A
and
Supplementary Fig. S2A
) or prokaryotes (
Fig. 2B
and
Supplementary Fig. S2B
). In addition, analysis of the number of N-/O-phosphorylation sites per protein showed that single-site modification accounted for more than half of the total modifications observed, regardless of whether considering modifications on individual residue types or the combined N-/O-phosphorylation on their respective three residues (
Supplementary Fig. S3
). Specifically, 63.05% were single-site N-phosphorylated proteins and 54.56% were single-site O-phosphorylated proteins.

Sequence pattern of protein N-phosphorylation

We further analyzed the sequence patterns and extracted motifs from nearly one million collected protein O-phosphorylation sites (
Supplementary Fig. S5
and
Supplementary Table S5

Supplementary Fig. S5

Especially, when we matched the extracted N-phosphorylation motifs against the O-phosphorylation motif set, ~70% (32/46) of the eukaryotic N-phosphorylation motifs were also specifically identified within the O-phosphorylation dataset. And this phenomenon was also observed in motifs derived from human (27/37) and mice (7/11) (
Supplementary Tables S4
and

However, this overlap decreased to 30% (12/41) in prokaryotes, and it is unclear whether this discrepancy stems from the current scarcity of prokaryotic protein N-/O-phosphorylation research or reflects a genuine mechanistic divergence between the two systems in prokaryotes. The high motif overlap in eukaryotes could suggest crosstalk between protein N- and O-phosphorylation. One hypothetical mechanism involves phosphate transfer from P–N bonds to P–C (carboxyl) bonds and subsequently to P–O bonds on substrates [
54
,
55
]. And this hypothesis is further supported by the two-component signal transduction system [
56
]. Alternatively, the above results may indicate that protein N- and O-phosphorylation share common kinase or phosphatase systems in eukaryotes. For instance, the protein histidine kinase NME1, identified in higher eukaryotes, also exhibits kinase activity toward O-phosphorylation [
57

59

60

61

62

63

64

65

In brief, this work presents the systematic comparison of motifs derived from experimentally identified protein N-phosphorylation and O-phosphorylation sites. Our results show that motifs extracted from N-phosphorylation sites are also commonly present in O-phosphorylation sites across eukaryotes, including humans and mice. This finding provides new perspectives for advancing research on kinases, esterases, biological functions, and disease associations of protein N-phosphorylation in eukaryotic species. Moreover, the motifs identified from large-scale protein N-phosphorylation data can facilitate the development of sequence-specific antibodies targeting N-phosphorylation sites. And the sequence and structural features analyzed in our study can guide the design of N-phospho-specific antibody libraries. All of which will contribute to the development of detection tools for N-phosphorylation sites.
Structural pattern of protein N-phosphorylation site
Our study showed that protein N-phosphorylation substrate recognition exhibits distinct local amino acid sequence patterns. However, the structural determinants governing the local amino acid environment of N-/O-phosphorylation sites remain unexplored. To address this, we systematically analyzed key structural features at phosphorylation sites versus non-phosphorylation sites, including protein order/disorder propensity, secondary structure (q3 and q8 states), relative solvent accessibility (RSA), and position along the protein sequence (
Fig. 4
,
Supplementary Tables S6
and
S7
). The results showed that the ordered region was dominant at all three N-phosphorylation sites (pHis, pLys, and pArg). And compared to non-phosphorylated sites, pHis and pArg showed no specific enrichment in disordered regions (
P

Fig. 4A
and
Supplementary Table S6
). Intriguingly, O-phosphorylation sites were specifically enriched in disordered regions, and disordered regions predominated at pSer and pThr sites, while ordered regions predominated at pTyr sites (
Fig. 4B
and
Supplementary Table S6
). This preference for order might contribute to the well-established technical challenges in detecting pTyr [
66
]. In the single-species analysis, the results for human and mouse were consistent with the overall trends. However, in
B. subtilis
, ordered regions predominated at pSer and pThr sites, which contrasts with the overall analysis (
Supplementary Table S7
). Consequently, the difficulty in identifying N-phosphorylation sites may stem not only from the instability of the phosphoramidate bond but also from their predominant localization within ordered regions.

A systematic summary of predictors for protein phosphorylation sites showed that comprehensive feature analysis—including disorder, site conservation (e.g. Position-Specific Scoring Matrix: PSSM, Blocks Substitution Matrix 62: BLOSUM62), physicochemical properties, and sequence composition—improves model performance [
70
]. Therefore, these findings may provide valuable structural perspectives for developing improved phosphorylation site prediction models. And phosphorylation site predictors tailored to specific species may be of greater value based on the analysis of structural sequence patterns in individual species. Altogether, each phosphorylation type, influenced by its unique residue context and structural localization, exerts specific effects on protein structure, function, and cellular signaling. Deciphering this complexity maybe vital for unraveling the intricacies of cellular regulation and advancing therapeutic strategies for disease.
Conclusion
This study presents an in-depth analysis of large-scale experimentally identified N-phosphorylation data across diverse species, focusing on functional enrichment, structural characteristics, and sequence patterns. Our results further support that protein N-phosphorylation, like O-phosphorylation, is a widespread biological phenomenon involved in various cellular processes. The analysis suggests that single-site modification currently represents the dominant form for both N- and O-phosphorylation protein. The striking overlap in phosphorylation motifs between N- and O-phosphorylation in eukaryotes offers a promising perspective: insights gained from the well-studied O-phosphorylation system could inform and advance our understanding of N-phosphorylation. Analysis of sequence-structural patterns indicates that RSA may be a more informative predictor of phosphorylation sites compared to other structural-sequence features. As protein N-phosphorylation detection techniques continue to emerge and mature, more N-phosphorylation sites will be validated and characterized. This progress will likely help uncover the elusive functional roles of protein N-phosphorylation, particularly within eukaryotic systems. Our study provides valuable insights into the functional roles, sequence patterns, and structural contexts of protein N-phosphorylation
in vivo
. By elucidating the significance and characteristic patterns of protein N-phosphorylation, this work aims to stimulate broader research interest in this field.

Given the continuous updates of protein sequences in both UniProt and NCBI databases, we re-calibrated and consolidated the collected protein N-phosphorylation sites from the literature with the downloaded O-phosphorylation site.

Motif analysis
The background dataset extraction methodology followed Zhao
et al.
[
49
]: Proteome datasets from species with documented N-phosphorylation studies were downloaded. Sequences centered on histidine (H), lysine (K), arginine (R), serine (S), threonine (T), and tyrosine (Y) were separately extracted from eukaryotic and prokaryotic protein sequences. A previous study manifested that the phosphorylated specificity was closely relevant to the primary sequence surrounding the phosphorylation site [
84
], in which the 14 upstream/downstream amino acids around the phosphorylation site were conservative [
85
]. Hence, a phosphorylation site-centered window of 31 continuous amino acids was adopted [
86
]. Extracted sequences across species were then compiled. Subsequent identification of potential N-/O-phosphorylation motifs was performed using the “rmotif” [
87
] (v1.0; parameters: min.seqs = 10,
P
value cutoff = 1e-5). The overall sequential patterns were visualized with the online service pLogo [
88
] (
https://plogo.uconn.edu/
).
N-phosphorylation sites structural pattern analysis
Protein structure information prediction
Structural information of protein sequences plays a crucial role in protein PTMs. NetSurfP-3.0 is a method that accurately and rapidly predicts the structural features of proteins through protein language models and deep learning [
89
]. We employed NetSurfP3 to predict secondary structure, solvent accessibility, and disorder information for protein sequences. Python (version 3.11) was used to extract structural information corresponding to the modification sites and their flanking sequences. Based on residues’ RSA values, they were classified into three categories: Buried (RSA ≤ 0.1), Medium (0.1 < RSA ≤ 0.4), and Exposed (RSA > 0.4) [
90
]. Subsequently, the “ggseqlogo” [
91
] (v0.2) was utilized for the visualization of the extracted structural sequence patterns.
