Introduction
Pancreatic cancer is a common malignant tumor of the digestive system whose morbidity and mortality are gradually increasing worldwide.
1
Most patients have advanced and inoperable disease at the time of diagnosis. Patients with advanced-stage disease usually receive chemotherapy, with the aim of prolonging survival and reducing symptoms.
2
However, there is a paucity of effective and well-tolerated treatment options for pancreatic cancer, especially advanced disease, for which chemotherapy is still a treatment mainstay. The median overall survival (OS) is less than 12 months for first-line chemotherapy
3
,
4
and between 3.3 and 9.9 months for second-line treatment. Combination regimens comprising the taxane nanoparticle albumin-bound (nab)-paclitaxel and the prodrug deoxycytidine nucleoside analog gemcitabine (AG) are widely used worldwide.
5
,
6
However, limited survival of patients treated for advanced pancreatic cancer constitutes an urgent unmet medical need.
Elevated expression of inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (X-linked IAP [XIAP], cell-linked IAP-1 [cIAP-1], and cIAP-2) in pancreatic cancer cells has been found in many studies and is associated with a poor prognosis.
7
,
8
APG-1387 is an investigational, new-generation, dimer, mitochondrial, proapoptotic protein (second mitochondria-derived activator of caspases [SMAC] mimetic). It can block the activity of IAP family proteins (XIAP, cIAP-1, cIAP-2, and melanoma IAP [ML-IAP]) and induce tumor-cell apoptosis.
9
,
10
Gemcitabine resistance is a significant challenge in pancreatic cancer. One contributing factor is increased expression of inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (survivin or XIAP); inhibitors or gene silencing that downregulates these proteins can sensitize pancreatic cancer cells to gemcitabine.
11
,
12
,
13
A study based on quantitative proteomics revealed that potential mechanisms of action underlying the synergistic antitumor effects of birinapant, a bivalent SMAC mimetic, and paclitaxel in pancreatic cancer cells involves regulation of mitochondrial function, cell growth and apoptosis, and cell-cycle arrest.
14
As a single-agent, APG-1387 (up to 45 mg) demonstrated a manageable safety profile in 28 Chinese patients with solid tumors but not pancreatic cancer.
15
In a US study, APG-1387 monotherapy achieved stable disease in three of six patients with metastatic pancreatic cancer previously treated with multiline therapies, including one patient in the APG-1387 45-mg treatment cohort who was treated for >5 cycles and experienced tumor shrinkage (−18%).
16
An in-house preclinical study showed that adding APG-1387 to AG therapy potentiated the antitumor activity of AG, improved response rates, and prolonged the drug effect in pancreatic cell- and patient-derived xenograft animal models (data not shown). The foregoing evidence affords a rationale for combining an IAP inhibitor and chemotherapy to treat pancreatic cancer.
The aim of this phase 1 study was to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and efficacy of APG-1387 combined with AG in patients with advanced pancreatic cancer.
Results
Patient characteristics
Between March 30, 2021, and May 15, 2024, 28 patients were screened. Of these, 21 were enrolled and received at least one dose of investigational drug therapy (APG-1387 plus AG). Hence, 21 patients were included in safety and pharmacokinetic populations. Of these 21 patients, 15 who received at least one cycle of investigational drug therapy and post-treatment tumor evaluation were included in the efficacy-evaluable population. Four individuals did not receive at least one tumor evaluation, and two did not receive at least one cycle of investigational drug therapy. In addition, of the 21 patients, 12 were included in the DLT-evaluable population and 9 individuals did not receive minimal dose requirement of study drugs (
Figure 1
). Patients were enrolled into three dose cohorts for APG-1387, including 20, 30, and 45 mg. Baseline patient characteristics are summarized in
Table 1
. In short, among the 21 patients who received at least one dose of the study drugs, 3 (14.3%) had >3 metastatic organs, and 9 (42.9%) received ≥3 prior lines of systemic therapy. A total of 18 (85.7%) patients received prior AG treatment.

Safety
As of May 15, 2024, 21 patients with advanced pancreatic cancer had been treated with at least one dose of APG-1387 plus AG (3 in the APG-1387 20-mg cohort, 4 in the 30-mg cohort, and 14 in the 45-mg cohort). Only one DLT of grade 4 platelet count decreased occurred 5 days after the first dose and recovered 3 days after treatment was observed in the 45-mg cohort. Most TRAEs (any-grade and grade ≥3) were considered to be related to AG, especially myelotoxicity. Seventeen patients (17/21, 81%) had grade ≥3 TRAEs, including platelet count decreased (13/21, 61.9%), neutrophil count decreased (12/21, 57.1%), white blood cell count decreased (11/21, 52.4%), ALT increased (3/21, 14.3%), AST increased (2/21, 9.5%), hypokalemia (2/21, 9.5%), and anemia (2/21, 9.5%).
Efficacy
Among 15 efficacy-evaluable patients, 3 experienced confirmed PRs: 1 in the APG-1387 20-mg cohort (after treatment failure of leucovorin calcium (folinic acid), fluorouracil, irinotecan hydrochloride, and oxaliplatin [FOLFIRINOX] and 1 cycle of AG); 1 in the APG-1387 30-mg cohort (after having received 4 prior lines of systemic chemotherapies with 3 cycles of AG); and 1 in the APG-1387 45-mg cohort (after having received 9 prior lines of systemic chemotherapies without AG combination therapy;
Figures 2
A and 2B). In addition, 6 participants experienced SD and 6 PD. Therefore, the overall response rate (ORR) was 20%, DCR 60%, and DOR 7.36 months (95% CI, 4.86 months–not reached) (
Table 3
). Among the 3 patients with PRs, one patient who received 1 cycle of AG prior to enrollment received only 6 cycles of AG because of recurrent myelotoxicity, followed by 12 cycles of APG-1387 (20 mg monotherapy), with a PR occurring during maintenance treatment with APG-1387 (as a monotherapy) for 8 months (
Figure 2
C).

Pharmacokinetic parameters of APG-1387 in plasma after single and multiple doses in combination with AG are summarized in

0-t

Discussion
In prior study, APG-1387 monotherapy up to 60 mg weekly was well tolerated and preliminary clinical activity observed in patients with advanced pancreatic cancer.
16
XIAP is overexpressed in pancreatic cancer and contributes to chemoresistance; interfering with this pathway may have a potential therapeutic role in the treatment of this disease.
12
,
18
In this phase 1 study for safety exploration, one of three previously heavily-treated patients who had not received prior AG experienced a PR, resulting in an ORR of 33.3%; this value higher than that of second-line AG.
19
,
20
No objective response was observed in patients who had failed prior AG treatment. Partial response occurred in 2 patients with no prior AG resistance, and tumor control lasted for 1 year in patients receiving APG-1387 monotherapy after AG discontinuation due to intolerable fatigue and relapsed neutropenia and thrombocytopenia. These intriguing findings suggest that APG-1387 combined with AG or APG-1387 alone as a maintenance after AG treatment may be beneficial to control the growth of advanced pancreatic cancer.
In a study using the MPACT regimen
3
(AG; nab-paclitaxel 125 mg/m
2
plus gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m
2
on days 1, 8, and 15 every 4 weeks) as a first-line treatment for patients with pancreatic cancer (including Chinese individuals), common AEs were leukopenia, neutropenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, fatigue, peripheral neuropathy, nausea, alopecia, peripheral edema, diarrhea, fever, vomiting, decreased appetite, rash, and dehydration. In a Chinese study, 49% of patients had at least one nab-paclitaxel dose reduction, and 51% had at least one gemcitabine dose reduction, mostly because of AEs. Common AEs leading to dose reduction of nab-paclitaxel and gemcitabine were thrombocytopenia (14% and 20%), neutropenia (14% and 16%), and leukopenia (11% and 13%), respectively. At least one nab-paclitaxel or gemcitabine dose delay occurred in 37% of patients. The median dose intensities of nab-paclitaxel and gemcitabine were 79 and 627 mg/m
2
/week, respectively.
21
In US patients with solid tumors treated with APG-1387 monotherapy, frequently observed treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) included nausea (21.7%), fatigue (17.4%), decreased appetite (13.0%), and abdominal pain (13.0%).
16
Three grade 3 TEAEs, including elevated bilirubin, lipase increase, and shortness of breath, were also documented.
16
Further, single-agent APG-1387 in 28 Chinese patients with solid tumors was not associated with bone marrow toxicity; only one participant (3.6%) had a grade ≥3 TRAE of ALT elevation.
15
For context, common grade ≥3 AEs with birinapant displays preferential binding to cIAP1 over cIAP2 and XIAP (similar to APG-1387), include fatigue, headache, lymphopenia, elevated blood alkaline phosphatase, decreased appetite, dyspnea, pain in extremities, and aspiration pneumonia.
22
Fatigue, nausea, and vomiting are common TRAEs for AT-406 and ASTX660, which are orally administered IAP inhibitors.
23
,
24
However, in our current study, where APG-1387 plus AG was used as second-line or post-second-line systemic therapy, most AEs were myelotoxic-related, resulting in nearly 50% of participants discontinuing medication, thereby rendering some patients ineligible for DLT evaluation. This is consistent with MPACT studies,
3
,
21
especially in China. The results showed that the AG MPACT regimen of 3/4 weeks administration (nab-paclitaxel 125 mg/m
2
+ gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m
2
every 4 weeks on days 1, 8, and 15) was not feasible for Chinese patients, especially in posterior-line patients. This may be the reason for the 2/3 weeks dosing regimen that is currently popular in clinical practice and clinical studies.
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
In addition to the small number of patients, short follow-up, lack of hypothesis testing, and lack of control in this study, there are several limitations. First, the planned dose completion rate of phase 1 was low, possibly because of inappropriate AG dosing cycles in patients with advanced pancreatic cancer, especially for previously heavily treated patients in China. Second, although preliminary efficacy of APG-1387 plus AG treatment was observed in patients without prior AG therapy, a larger study is needed to determine the effect of this combination in this setting. Third, just as in patients previously untreated with AG, the utility of APG-1387 alone as maintenance therapy after AG treatment warrants confirmation in a large population.
In conclusion, weekly APG-1387 (45 mg IV) did not potentiate the toxicity of AG, and the safety profile was considered manageable. The ternary regimen showed encouraging clinical activity in patients initially treated with AG and also suggested that maintenance with APG-1387 monotherapy after withdrawal because of AG intolerance is beneficial to control the growth of advanced pancreatic cancer.
Limitations of the study
This phase 1 study of APG-1387 combined with nab-paclitaxel and gemcitabine (AG) in advanced pancreatic cancer has several limitations. The generalizability of our findings is restricted by the small sample size, short follow-up, lack of hypothesis testing, and absence of a control group. In addition, safety and efficacy assessments are potentially restricted by the low planned dose completion rate—likely due to inappropriate AG dosing schedule, especially for heavily pretreated Chinese patients. Evidence of preliminary efficacy in patients previously untreated with AG, as well as APG-1387’s potential as maintenance therapy, require validation in larger studies. These constraints warrant further investigation to confirm the regimen’s benefits and optimal dosing in this population.

Experimental model and study participant details
This study involved human participants with advanced pancreatic cancer. Adults were eligible for this study if they had histologically and/or cytologically confirmed advanced pancreatic cancer. During the dose-escalation stage, patients with disease that had failed standard treatment or who could not tolerate it were enrolled. Eligible patients also had: (1) life expectancy of at least 3 months; (2) at least one measurable lesion according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1; and (3) adequate bone marrow reserve and organ function.
Key exclusion criteria included: (1) anticancer treatment or any investigational treatment within 14 days before the first dose of APG-1387 combination therapy; (2) symptomatic active infection; (3) previous treatment with an IAP inhibitor; (4) allergy to APG-1387 or any component used in its preparation; (5) active central nervous system metastases; (6) active autoimmune disease or a history of autoimmune disease or immunodeficiency; (7) clinically significant cardiovascular, hepatic, renal, gastrointestinal, or metabolic disease; (8) pregnant or lactating status in female participants; or (9) other uncontrolled chronic comorbidities.
Baseline characteristics of the 21 enrolled participants (safety population) included: median age 58 years (range 40–73), 13 males and 8 females (sex determined by medical records; no gender reporting, as results were not based on self- or physician-reported gender identity), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0–1, and primarily metastatic disease with ≥1 prior lines of therapy (85.7% had prior nab-paclitaxel/gemcitabine [AG]). The influence of sex on study outcomes was not assessed, because the trial was not powered for subgroup analyses, and no sex-specific differences were observed in safety or efficacy endpoints. No genotype-based measures were used.
The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and applicable local regulations. The protocol (
Data S1
) was approved by the institutional review board of Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before enrollment.
No animals, cell lines, plants, microbe strains, or primary cell cultures were used. No cell line authentication or mycoplasma testing was applicable.
Method details
Study design and treatments
This phase 1 trial (
ClinicalTrials.gov
identifier:
NCT04643405
) evaluated APG-1387 combined with fixed dose nab-paclitaxel and gemcitabine (AG) in patients with relapsed or refractory metastatic pancreatic cancer. Nab-paclitaxel was administered by intravenous (IV) infusion at 125 mg/m
2
within 30–40 min (maximum infusion time, 40 min) on days 1, 8, and 15 per cycle for 3 weeks, followed by a 1-week rest period for 28 days per cycle. Within 30 ± 3 min after the end of an infusion of nab-paclitaxel, gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m
2
was administered by IV infusion on days 1, 8, and 15 of each cycle for 3 weeks, followed by a 1-week rest period for 28 days per cycle. APG-1387 was administered by IV infusion for 30 ± 3 min on days 1, 8, 15, and 22 of each cycle for 4 consecutive weeks for 28 days per cycle. The initial dose of APG-1387 was 20 mg, with dose-escalation to 45 mg based on an interval 3 + 3 (i3+3) design in a continuous enrollment of patients.
Participants
Enrollment occurred between March 30, 2021, and May 15, 2024. Of 28 screened patients, 21 were enrolled.
Assessments
Safety assessments included vital signs, physical examination, hematology, blood biochemistry, coagulation, electrocardiography, pregnancy tests for women, and clinical symptoms. Adverse events (AEs) were graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 at each clinic visit from the time of informed consent until either 30 days after the last dose of study drug or the participant started treatment with a new clinical regimen, died, or was lost to follow-up, whichever occurred first.
Any of the following AEs evaluated by the investigator as related to study drug was considered a dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) during the first treatment cycle (28 days): 1.) Hematologic: (i) grade ≥3 thrombocytopenia associated with clinically significant bleeding (as deemed by the investigator or resulting in platelet transfusion); (ii) grade 4 thrombocytopenia (platelet count <25.0 × 10
9
/L); (iii) grade 4 neutropenia lasting more than 7 days; (iv) grade 3 febrile neutropenia (defined as absolute neutrophil count [ANC] < 1.0 ×10
9
/L, single oral temperature >38.3°C, or oral temperature ≥38°C for >1 h); or (v) grade ≥3 neutropenia with grade ≥3 infection. 2.) Hepatic: (i) grade 4 elevated total bilirubin; (ii) grade 4 elevated alanine or aspartate aminotransferase (ALT or AST); (iii) grade ≥3 elevated ALT, AST, or total bilirubin (lasting >7 days); (iv) for patients with normal baseline AST, ALT, and total bilirubin values: AST or ALT >3.0 × upper limit of normal (ULN) and total bilirubin >2.0 × ULN; or (v) for patients with abnormal baseline AST, ALT, and total bilirubin: [AST or ALT >2 times the baseline value and >3.0 × ULN] or [AST or ALT >8.0 × ULN], whichever was lower, and [total bilirubin >2 times the baseline value]; in this setting, there was no evidence of cholestasis and no other reason to explain the elevated levels of AST or ALT and total bilirubin. 3.) Gastrointestinal: (i) grade ≥3 nausea and vomiting persisting for more than 3 days despite best antiemetic treatment; or (ii) grade ≥3 diarrhea persisting for more than 5 days despite optimal antidiarrheal treatment (including corticosteroids). 4.) Cardiac: grade ≥3 elevated troponins (I or T). 5.) Others: (i) other clinically meaningful toxicities; or (ii) grade ≥3 AEs that could not be controlled by daily management.
Dose-limiting toxicity in evaluable patients was assessed within 28 days of the first administration of study drug(s). For dose-escalation decisions, only DLTs that occurred within 28 days were assessed. Patients with the following conditions were not evaluated for DLT.
(1)
Incomplete safety assessments for the first cycle;
(2)
Did not receive ≥65% nab-paclitaxel, ≥65% gemcitabine, or ≥75% APG-1387 at the scheduled dose during the DLT evaluation window, unless they developed a DLT.
Tumors were evaluated by computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria version 1.1 during the screening period (within 4 weeks before the first dose), cycle 2, cycle 4, and day 1 of even-numbered cycles. The efficacy-evaluable population comprised all patients who received at least one cycle of investigational drug therapy after enrollment and had data for at least one post administration tumor evaluation.
Endpoints
The primary outcome measure of this study was the incidence of DLT events. Secondary endpoints included pharmacokinetic parameters of APG-1387 and total paclitaxel after combination therapy. Antitumor activity parameters included complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), progressive disease (PD), objective response rate (ORR, defined as the proportion of study participants who achieved a CR or PR), disease control rate (DCR; defined as the proportion of patients with a CR, PR, or SD), and duration of response (DOR, defined as the time from first documented CR or PR to the first assessment of disease progression or death, whichever occurred first). Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from the first administration of study treatment to the first record of disease progression (according to RECIST version 1.1) or the date of any-cause death.
Pharmacokinetic assessments
Plasma samples were collected on Cycle 1 Day 1 and Cycle 1 Day 15 at predose, the end of infusion of nab-paclitaxel, the end of infusion of APG-1387, and 1, 2, 6, and 24 h after starting the APG-1387 infusion. Plasma concentrations of APG-1387 and total paclitaxel were determined utilizing a validated liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) method, with assay ranges of 0.5–500 ng/mL and 2 to 2,000 ng/mL, respectively. Pharmacokinetic parameters were estimated by noncompartmental analysis using Phoenix WinNonlin Version 8.4 (Certara USA Inc., Princeton, NJ, USA).
Quantification and statistical analysis
No formal hypotheses were tested in this study (
Data S2
). Sample sizes were determined by discussion between the sponsor and investigators according to observed toxicities and the pharmacokinetic profiles of all patients who received at least one dose of APG-1387. Ninety-five percent confidence intervals (CIs) of the DCR and DOR were computed using the Clopper-Pearson method. For categorical data, numbers and percentages of individuals are presented. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC).
