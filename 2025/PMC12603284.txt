Introduction
The 2020 WHO Guidelines recommend that pregnant women engage in at least 150 min of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity per week
1
. In the past, physical activity was not recommended for pregnant women since it was thought that it might cause a miscarriage, premature delivery, disturb fetal development, or cause musculoskeletal injury; however, none of the mentioned risks has been confirmed
2
,
3
. Based on meta-analyses by Davenport et al.
4
,
5
. Physical activity is not associated with complications during pregnancy and childbirth, nor does it have a negative impact on the health of the fetus or newborn. Furthermore, physically active pregnant women have these issues, among others, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), gestational hypertension (GH), preeclampsia (PE)
6
, excessive weight gain
7
, lipid disorders
8
, and depressive symptoms
9
significantly less often.
Despite the growing benefits of physical activity during pregnancy, only 35% of pregnant women in the West meet the guidelines for physical activity
10
. The main intrapersonal reasons are fatigue, lack of time, and pregnancy discomforts
11
. However, it is important to note that physical exercise can help alleviate pregnancy discomforts
12
, such as back pain, and improve physical condition, resulting in less fatigue during daily routine activities and intensive exercise sessions.
Due to the inverse relationship between the intensity of the exercise and its duration, one can hypothesise that shorter but more intense workouts will be equally exerting to longer ones with moderate intensity. HIIT (high-intensity interval training) is one of many examples of that kind of training composed of short periods (< 60 s–8 min) of vigorous (70–90% of maximal heart rate) to near maximal (≥ 90% of maximal heart rate) exercises, which are interwoven with less active (40–70% of maximal heart rate) or passive recovery breaks (1–5 min)
13
. Anderson and co-workers reported that a single HIIT session was safe for the mother and fetus
14
. They continuously monitored fetal well-being during high-intensity interval resistance strength training using fetal heart-rate tracings and conducted umbilical artery Doppler velocimetry assessments both before and after exercise. The safety of the acute cardiorespiratory and glycemic responses to a one-time HIIT during pregnancy was confirmed by Wowdzia et al.
15
,
16
. In our previous work, we observed that an 8-week HIIT intervention had positive effects on both physiological
17
,
18
, metabolic
19
and psychological health indicators
18
,
19
. Moreover, participants considered HIIT to be an enjoyable modality of physical activity
14
. The emphasis on HIIT’s effectiveness, safety, and enjoyment during pregnancy can help reassure pregnant women and encourage them to participate in this exercise mode. However, there is a lack of data explaining the mechanisms by which HIIT regulates homeostasis and prevents pregnancy complications.
Thus, there is a need for research on changes in the proteomic profile induced by high-intensity physical activity during pregnancy. A proteome is a collection of all proteins in a cell or fluid at a particular time. Proteomes change in response to various factors, including the cell’s metabolic state, tissue type, and numerous interactions. The urinary proteome is a readily accessible, non-invasive matrix that reflects systemic physiological states, including those influenced by pregnancy and physical activity. Previous studies have shown that urinary proteome profiles differ between pregnant and non-pregnant women
20
. Exercise-induced shifts in urinary proteins, including those of low and medium molecular weight, are also well documented
21
. Moreover, studies in military cohorts have shown robust urinary proteomic responses to intense physical stress, particularly involving proteins related to immune and redox pathways
22
,
23
. Reviews confirm that urinary biomarkers effectively reflect exercise-related physiological processes
22
.
Proteomic research can provide detailed insights into the influence of exercise on the molecular processes of pregnant women. Therefore, our study focused on registering and identifying changes observed in the urinary proteome of pregnant women in response to either the progressive cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET) up to volitional exhaustion or the 8-week HIIT program implemented in women with uncomplicated pregnancies. We hypothesised that 8 weeks of HIIT during pregnancy would induce proteomic adaptations detectable in urine, reflecting improved physiological resilience to acute exercise stress (CPET), and offering a non-invasive means to evaluate the body’s adaptive response to a high-intensity exercise program during pregnancy.
Materials and methods
Study participants and biological samples
The pre- and post-intervention measurements and urine samples were collected at the Laboratory of Physical Effort and Genetics in Sport at the Gdansk University of Physical Education and Sport in Poland in 2021. All procedures were performed following the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki of the World Medical Association (WMA) and approved by the Bioethics Commission of the District Medical Chamber in Gdańsk (KB-8/21). The entire research protocol was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (
NCT05009433
) and was entitled “HIIT vs. MICT During Pregnancy and Health and Birth Outcomes in Mothers and Children (HIIT Mama)”. This research was partly funded by the National Science Centre, Poland (No. DEC2021/05/X/NZ7/01814). All women signed an informed consent at enrollment. The full study protocol was registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (
NCT05009433
) on 17/08/2021. No significant methodological changes were made after trial commencement.
The analysis was conducted on 18 healthy participants with uncomplicated pregnancies, aged between 24 and 42 years and in gestational weeks ranging from 13 to 28. Initially, the principal researcher randomly assigned 35 participants to the HIIT group (engaging in the HIIT program) and 34 to the EDU group (participating in the educational program), maintaining a 1:1 ratio based on the Pipeline Arm-Focused Randomisation (PAFR) model.
Since this pilot study focused on proteomic analyses within the multistage
HIIT Mama
project, the next step involved selecting 9 participants from each group to ensure comparable clinical characteristics (including age, gestational week, BMI, and parity). This selection aimed to enhance the reliability of data analysis, interpretation, and conclusions.
All participants identified as women; therefore, we use this term throughout the rest of the work to refer to the study group. The eligibility criterion was a course of pregnancy that allowed participation in physical activities adapted for pregnant women, confirmed by routine obstetric consultation. Exclusion criteria included contraindications to increased physical effort or other conditions that could compromise the health or safety of participants or significantly impact the quality of the collected data
24
. During the study, participants received standard obstetric care. Figure
1
shows a schematic diagram of the experiment.

Progressive cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET) up to volitional exhaustion before and after an 8-week intervention
Before and after the 8-week intervention, all study participants underwent CPET on a cycloergometer with an electronically regulated load (Viasprint 150P) and a respiratory gas analyser (Oxycon Pro, Erich Jäger GmbH, Germany), as previously reported
25
. It was calibrated before exercise tests according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Breath-by-breath data were averaged to provide a data point for each 15 s. Before the test, women sat on a chair for 5 min to adapt to breathing with a silicone face mask. After the adaptation phase, women began cycling to warm up for 4 min, with a relative load of 0.4 W/kg of body mass. Following the warm-up, the load increased by 0.2 W/kg every minute until the subject refused. We encouraged the women to cycle up to the limit of their exercise capacity. We considered volitional exhaustion when the participant refused to continue the exercise test due to the inability to engage in further physical effort. We also informed participants that they could stop the test at any time. When they stopped cycling, they sat on a chair and rested for 3 min. The highest oxygen uptake value maintained for 15 s was the peak oxygen capacity (VO
2peak
). The anaerobic threshold (AT) values, such as oxygen uptake at AT (VO
2
/AT) and heart rate at AT (HR/AT), were established using the V-slope method
26
.
Urine was collected before and after the 8 weeks of interventions at three time points: (a) fasting urine before CPET (T1), (b) up to 15 min after CPET (T2), and (c) 60 min after CPET (T3), and frozen at − 86 °C until analysis. Before and after the 8-week intervention, blood samples were also taken in fasting conditions to monitor glucose and lipid metabolism in both groups. To ensure that participants met the study inclusion criteria and had normoglycemia and normolipidemia, we measured their fasting glucose levels, glycated haemoglobin, and lipid profile (total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and triglycerides). These measurements were performed on the same day as blood collection in a commercial laboratory accredited by the Polish Centre for Accreditation (No. AM 002).
Additionally, the short form of the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ) was used to measure participants’ physical activity levels. The IPAQ provides information on weekly physical activity levels in multiples of the resting metabolic rate (RMR), also known as metabolic equivalents (METs).
Interventions
The HIIT group exercised for 60 min three times a week for 8 weeks. The HIIT program was developed based on our previous work
27
and aligned with Polish guidelines on physical activity during pregnancy
28
. The training consisted of 15–20 min of high-intensity interval exercises involving major muscle groups (e.g., squats, lunges, running in place). Low-to-moderate intensity resistance exercises, stretching, breathing, pelvic floor muscle and birth preparation exercises followed it. During the interval part of the exercise training, participants were encouraged to maintain a heart rate level above the anaerobic threshold (HR/AT). On average, it was determined individually based on the CPET at 88 ± 5% of the maximal heart rate. Intervals consisted of 30–60 s of exercise, followed by a 30–60 s rest break, in a ratio of 1:2, 1:1, and 2:1 (exercise: rest). The exercise intensity was measured using a heart rate monitor (Polar RS400, Finland), the Borg Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE) scale
29
, and the talk test
30
. During 8 weeks, participants from the HIIT group attended 17 ± 5 HIIT sessions.
Women in the EDU group participated in online, real-time sessions on healthy lifestyles and physical activity during the prenatal period. We encouraged them to participate in at least 150 min of moderate to vigorous physical activity per week, as recommended for pregnant women. Our previous papers
17
,
31
presented more details on the study interventions.
Significant differences between the HIIT and EDU interventions prevented participants and those conducting the interventions from being blinded regarding group allocation. However, the researchers responsible for data collection were unaware of the group assignment.
We did not observe any adverse effects of these two interventions, neither on the course of pregnancy and childbirth nor on the maternal or fetal health. Data on obstetric and neonatal outcomes were collected postpartum using an online questionnaire and based on medical documentation.
Protein precipitation
Urine amounts ranged from 1.3 to 1.0 ml. The urine was centrifuged for 5 min at 15,000 rcf to eliminate cell debris and other non-soluble compounds. After centrifugation, 1 mL of the obtained supernatant was further incubated with 100 µL of lysis buffer (1% SDS, 50 mM DTT, 100 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0) for 15 min at 96 °C. Next, 500 µL of the solution was transferred onto an Amicon Ultra (Millipore) filter with a molecular weight cut-off (MWCO) of 3 kDa and centrifuged at 15,000 rpm for 20 min. This step was repeated for another 500 µl of urine and centrifuged again [adjusted 32]. Filtrate was collected for peptidome analysis and stored at −20 °C. Amicon was placed upside down in a new Eppendorf tube and centrifuged at 1000 rcf for 1 min. Protein concentration was measured in the obtained samples using a Multiskan Sky Microplate Reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific) at a wavelength of 280 nm.
Enzymatic digestion of protein with the use of the filter aided sample preparation (FASP) method
Protein concentration in urine samples ranged from 0.01 to 0.02 mg/ml; since this amount was too low to obtain the minimal amount of protein required for the FASP procedure
32
, the whole protein fraction was transferred onto Microcon with MWCO 10 kDa. To each sample, 200 µl of urea buffer (UA, 8 mM urea in 100 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.5) solution was added and centrifuged for 20 min at 10,000 rcf. This step was repeated 4 times to lower the SDS concentration in the samples. Furthermore, 100 µL of a 55 mM iodoacetamide solution in UA was added to prevent the formation of disulfide bonds in proteins by covalently binding to their thiol groups, which are found in cysteine residues. The mixture was incubated in the dark for 20 min. Samples were then centrifuged for 15 min at 10,000 rcf. Then, 100 µl of UA was added to each sample and centrifuged for 15 min at 10,000 rcf. This step was repeated twice more, followed by washing with 100 µL of DB buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.5) twice. Next, the filter was transferred into a new Eppendorf tube, and 60 µL of 2 µg/mL trypsin was added. The mixture was then further incubated overnight at 37 °C. The following day, samples were centrifuged at 10,000 rcf for 15 min. Later, 125 µL of DB solution was washed at 10,000 rcf for 15 min, followed by analogous centrifugation after adding 100 µL of the mentioned solution, which was then further desalted and analysed. The peptide concentration was measured in the obtained filtrates.
Sample desalting by the STAGE tips procedure
Stage tips were prepared by placing 3 layers of C18 Solid Phase Extraction Disc 47 nm (EMPORE) into 100 µl tips
33
. The matrix was activated with 100 µL of methanol, centrifuged, and then further equilibrated with 100 µL of 1% acetic acid (AA) in a 60% acetonitrile (ACN) solution, followed by another centrifugation step. 100 µl of 1% acetic acid was washed down, followed by 100 µl of 1% AA and a sample volume containing 10 µg of peptides was centrifuged. Furthermore, 100 µL of 1% AA was added and washed down to remove compounds incompatible with MS. Then, the tips were transferred into new Eppendorf tubes. Tubes were placed in a centrifuge set at 15 min, 600 rcf, and 100 µl of 1% AA in 60% ACN was added to elute the cleansed peptides. After centrifugation, the obtained filtrates were evaporated in SpeedVac at 45 °C to a final volume of 30 µl.
High pH HPLC fractionation
A spectral library was constructed using urine samples from healthy pregnant women at 38 weeks of pregnancy; this was enriched with urine samples from each tested pregnant woman (10 µL of each). Those samples were treated in a manner analogous to the FASP procedure described in the previous paragraph to obtain peptide fractions. The peptide fraction was further lyophilised, and the collected peptides were resuspended in 160 µL of buffer A (100 mM NH
4
HCO
3
, pH 8.0). The final concentration of the peptide mixture was 0.073 µg/µL.
Further sample separation using the HPLC technique was performed on a Zorbax Extend C18 column (particle size 5 μm, 4.6 × 150 mm, Agilent) connected to a Nexera XR HPLC System with a PDA detector (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan)
34
. The column was maintained at 40 °C, and the flow rate was 1 mL/min, with each injection volume set to 20 µL. Buffers used in separation: A- 100 mM NH
4
HCO
3,
pH 8.0, B- 100% I. Separation was performed using a chromatic gradient of 0–40% ACN over 100 min, during which 1 mL fractions were collected every minute. All samples were then evaporated to 50 µl in a SpeedVac at 60 °C and then combined with two neighbouring samples within 2 min of retention time for each separation. Samples were further evaporated to 20 µl in SpeedVac at 45 °C. Fractionation was performed for 3 technical replications.
LC/MS-MS measurement
Library
Peptide fractions used for library construction were analysed with a TripleTOF 5600 + mass spectrometer in data-dependent acquisition mode (DDA). The DDA cycle consisted of accumulating precursor spectra over 100 ms, within the range of 400–1200 m/z, followed by fragmentation of the top 20 precursor ions and registration of their spectra in 50 ms, within the range of 100–1800 m/z. All cycles round up to 1.15s. Precursor ions that were fragmented earlier were further dynamically excluded. The obtained data were further processed using ProteinPilot 4.5 (SCIEX) to create a library, with the following parameters: alkylation of cysteine with iodoacetamide, instrument: TripleTOF 5600+, and trypsin digestion. The search was performed against the UniProt
Homo sapiens
(dated September 1, 2022) database, with the FDR (False Discovery Rate) set automatically to 1%.
SWATH MS
LC-MS/MS analysis was performed using a TripleTOF 5600 + mass spectrometer (AB Sciex LLC, Framingham, MA, USA) coupled with an Eksigent microLC 200 Plus System (Eksigent, Dublin, CA, USA) liquid chromatography system. Separation was performed with the use of ChromXP C18CL column (3 μm, 120 Å, 150 × 0.3 mm), with 11–42% gradient (buffer A: H
2
O + 0.1% formic acid; buffer B: 100% ACN + 0.1% formic acid) in 60 min for each MS run. The SWATH method was performed for the quantitative analysis of proteins in the samples. Each sample was analysed in three technical replications using data-independent acquisition mode (DIA). The 25 transmission windows of variable width were implemented in the SwathTUNER software. SWATH covered precursor ions with a mass range of 400–1200 m/z in 100 ms, with collision energy calculated for + 2 to + 5 charged ions centred upon window 2. SWATH-MS/MS spectra covered windows at the beginning of each cycle, with an accumulation time of 39.995 ms, with a 100–1800 m/z range. The whole cycle took up to 1.0998 s.
Data processing
SWATH-MS measurements of the pool and clinical samples were processed against the prepared spectral library. The library group file and DIA raw data were loaded into PeakView 2.2 to process and obtain the SWATH data using the given parameters: modified peptides were allowed, and shared peptides were excluded. The maximum number of peptides per protein was set to 6. Only proteins with identification at 1% FDR were filtered for further manual inspection for the presence of proteins with only one peptide, amino acid modifications or peptide repetitions to lower the chances of possible false protein identification.
Statistical data analysis
To present the characteristics of the study participants, most variables were expressed as means ± standard deviations. The normality of the study variables’ distribution was analysed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Between- and within-group mean differences in selected demographic, metabolic, and cardiopulmonary parameters were analysed using the Student’s t-test or analysis of variance (ANOVA) test, as appropriate. In cases where distributions are significantly different from the normal distribution, we used the non-parametric Mann-Whitney U and Wilcoxon tests to assess both between- and within-group differences. Additionally, Chi-square was used to evaluate the differences in frequencies. The statistical significance was set to
p
< 0.05.
Each urine sample was analysed in technical triplicate. The mean of these replicates was used in statistical analyses, and the reproducibility was assessed by calculating the coefficient of variation (CV) for selected proteins, yielding an average CV of < 20%, indicating acceptable analytical consistency.
Statistical analysis for proteome change was performed using Perseus 2.0.7.0
35
. Replicates were grouped and normalised to log2 values; any missing values were imputed from the normal distribution, inspected using histograms and scatter plots, and normalised by z-score. A t-test was performed to compare the samples. Proteins are statistically significant (q < 0.05).
Fold changes for samples after CPET and an 8-week HIIT program were calculated in relation to the control (baseline) values, and the obtained values were transformed to log2 values. Functional analysis (GO, KEGG, Reactome) was done using STRING (
https://string-db.org/
)
36
–
39
. The results of the quantitative analysis were visualised in the Cytoscape software
40
.
For the control (EDU) group, 54 samples (
n
= 54) were collected in a time-matched manner to the HIIT group. These were used in a paired analysis design to subtract matched EDU participant values from those in the HIIT group at equivalent gestational stages, thereby helping to isolate the proteomic effects induced explicitly by the HIIT intervention.

Proteomics analysis
Sample preparation
Fifty-four urine samples were obtained from 9 pregnant women participating in the 8-week HIIT program (HIIT group). Samples were collected from each participant before the CPET, up to 15 min after the CPET, and 60 min after the CPET, equalling 3 time points at the beginning of the 8-week HIIT program implementation. After 8 weeks of the exercise intervention, samples were collected again at 3 time points analogous to the beginning of the study. To account for changes in the urine proteome caused by pregnancy-related physiological alterations over the 8 weeks, each woman in the HIIT group was matched with a counterpart from the educational group at the same stage of pregnancy.
During the statistical analysis, the protein expression levels from the EDU group were subtracted from those of the matched HIIT participants to isolate the effects induced explicitly by the HIIT program. This subtraction was performed after normalisation and log₂ transformation of the data, using log₂-transformed protein intensity values. By aligning the gestational age between matched pairs and applying this normalisation strategy, we aimed to reduce confounding effects related to natural pregnancy progression and to highlight better proteomic changes directly associated with the HIIT intervention.
All samples were digested using the FASP protocol, followed by desalting and purification with STAGE Tips (Fig.
2
). Subsequently, purified and condensed samples were analysed using an LC-MS/MS system in DIA mode (Fig.
3
). Our method of choice was Sequential Window Acquisition of All Theoretical Mass Spectra (SWATH-MS), which allowed us to obtain accurate, high quantitative data without labelling beforehand. Three technical repetitions were made for each sample, summing up to the final number of 189 samples.

Identification of differently expressed proteins
Identification of proteins was performed against a spectra library composed of 1015 proteins, constructed according to the previous description. The intra-group comparison was conducted against the control (baseline) sample taken before the CPET for samples obtained 15 and 60 min after CPET at the beginning of the study. Proteins whose expression was found to be either upregulated or downregulated (Differentially Expressed Proteins-DEPs) in samples taken 15–60 min after CPET in comparison to the proteins found in a sample taken before the CPET were adjusted to q < 0.05. The number of differentially expressed proteins in intra-group comparison was significantly higher for measurements made before than after the 8-week HIIT program.

Functional analysis
Functional analysis of intergroup comparison
Gene Ontology (GO) was performed to identify the top 10 most critical biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components involving proteins with changed expression induced by CPET. Moreover, pathway analysis focused on significant groups of differently expressed proteins in Reactome and KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathways. GO analysis was conducted for proteins that exhibited alterations in at least 3 participants and were observed at both time points (15 min and 60 min after CPET). The same rules were applied for the group of proteins against which KEEG and Reactome analysis was performed.
The top 10 most important GO terms in each category influenced by proteins with changed expression were assessed based primarily on the lowest FDR (False Discovery Rate) value. Any results with FDR ≥ 0.02 for GO were omitted. Reactome and KEGG pathway analysis for the sample group obtained at the beginning of the study was held as described above.

Discussion
This is the first study to analyse changes induced by the exercise test up to volitional exhaustion and the HIIT program in the urine proteome of pregnant women using the SWATH-MS technique. Our study focused on identifying changes observed in the urinary proteome of pregnant women in response to either the progressive cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET) up to volitional exhaustion or the 8-week HIIT program implemented in women with uncomplicated pregnancies. The most interesting finding is that the scale of differentially expressed proteins after the CPET was significantly higher at the beginning of the study than after 8 weeks of regular HIIT. It may indicate that HIIT leads to positive proteomic adaptations.
Notably, the HIIT group presented values similar to those of the cardiopulmonary parameters obtained at the beginning of the study. Accounting for the development of pregnancy and related physiological adaptations, as well as weight gain, the sustained outcomes of CPET after 8 weeks reflect improved cardiovascular and respiratory system function. These results align with our previous work, which was performed in a larger group of participants subjected to an 8-week HIIT programme
17
,
18
. Other authors also observed positive effects of HIIT on cardiopulmonary outcomes after several weeks of interventions during pregnancy, both in human
44
,
45
and animal studies
46
,
47
. Unlike the HIIT group, the EDU group presented worse CPET results than the baseline. Notably, the level of cardiorespiratory capacity is a significant factor associated with the risk of metabolic syndrome, coronary heart disease, and other health disorders in the general population
48
and potentially during pregnancy as well. However, the molecular-level determinants of these processes remain unclear.
Acute proteomic responses to CPET before 8 weeks of HIIT intervention
Before the intervention, the main changes observed after CPET affected calcium ion binding and enzyme inhibitory activity, particularly endopeptidases. A significant part of those proteins was shown to be Serine-type or Aspartic-type endopeptidases. Regarding calcium ion binding, most proteins were also found to be involved in calcium-dependent protein binding, as well as in partially binding to glycosaminoglycans and S100 proteins.
The most significant group of pathways altered were those related to immune reactions, including Reactome pathways such as Neutrophil degranulation, Innate Immune System, and Immune System. Proteins with the most downregulation in the immune system group were ARSA, NPC2, MME, CTSC, GAA, B2M, CD177, IGKV1-33, LAMP2, QPCT, RAB3D, S100A11, and SERPINB1, while upregulation was observed for FABP5, ALB, and UMOD. Most proteins, such as Arylsulfatase A, are associated with neutrophil degranulation and are found in lysosomes and azurophilic granules in Neutrophils
49
. Interestingly, an increase in lysosomal enzyme activity was observed in humans after competing endurance exercise sessions; however, for ARSA, transient downregulation was observed for 2 h following cessation of physical activity
50
. We observed similar changes in our study 60 min after CPET. It is known that ACTH and cortisol contribute to the stabilisation of the lysosomal membrane, and it has been hypothesised that an increase in these hormones after exercise might explain the downregulation of ARSA
51
.
Furthermore, during pregnancy, an increase in the mentioned hormones has been shown to lead to even further stabilisation of the lysosomal membrane, explaining the downregulation of ARSA, as well as other proteins found in the lysosomes (NPC2, GAA) and neutrophil granules
52
. GAA is known for catalysing the degradation of glycogen in lysosomes
53
, while NPC2 is responsible for binding cholesterol in the process of its transportation from lysosomes together with the NPC1 protein
54
. Additionally, NPC2 is a protein primarily involved in binding and transporting cholesterol molecules
55
. However, there has been speculation that it might have been involved in regulating sodium reabsorption in the terminal nephron in the kidney
56
.
CTSC is a serine protease that can be found in a lysosomal acidic environment and is annotated for its role in neutrophil degranulation, as it is responsible for activating neutrophil granule proteases such as CTSG, NSP4, and PR3
57
,
58
. Most downregulated proteins were annotated to be associated with secretory granules found in mature neutrophils (B2M, CST3, RAB3D, S100A11, SERPINB1). Neprilysin (MME), a component of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, is a protein responsible for vascular homeostasis and the negative regulation of natriuretic and vasodilatory peptide activity, leading to a decrease in blood pressure and peripheral vascular resistance
59
. Proteins such as LAMP2 and CD177 were found on the membrane of neutrophils
60
61
. Furthermore, CD177 upregulation was noticed in extracellular regions after activation of these cells
62
.
Although neutrophil count was shown to increase in serum after exercise, their function was transiently impaired. This impairment is attributed to the increased release of immature neutrophils from the bone marrow, likely triggered by oxidative stress or stress hormones
63
,
64
. It may explain the downregulation of proteins within neutrophils, as observed in our research.
Interestingly, one of the proteins with the most significant downregulation fold change was TFF2, which corresponded positively with changes in insulin resistance and fasting insulin levels in obese men
65
. TFF2 is a protein expressed in GI tract cells involved in tissue repair, mucosal protection, and anti-inflammatory processes
66
. Furthermore, it was demonstrated that TFF2 is a gene induced by a high-fat (HF) diet, and mice with a knockout of the TFF2 gene were protected against HF-induced obesity
67
. This finding suggests the potential of HIIT to prevent gestational diabetes; however, further research is needed to confirm this.
Only four proteins were upregulated after the CPET: UMOD, FABP5, and IGKV3-20. High-intensity exercise has been reported to cause an increased concentration of ALB in the urine in healthy men, and a similar effect is observed in this study. Increased albumin is caused by a higher priority need for salt and water resorption to sustain the electrolytic equilibrium rather than albumin resorption, leading to transient albuminuria
68
. FABP5 is the lipid-binding protein produced in the proximal tubule, which is involved in fatty acid metabolism in mitochondria and peroxisomes, among others
69
. It has been demonstrated that urinary FABP5 reflects stress overload in the proximal tubule
70
. Urinary UMOD has been shown to reflect tubular mass and function
71
and is involved in sodium handling in the kidneys. Thus, upregulation may be correlated with prioritising salt and water reabsorption to sustain electrolytic equilibrium, as was the case for albumin
72
. However, it has also been demonstrated that long-term exercise may result in a decrease in UMOD
72
. Our research revealed a similar trend after 8 weeks of regular HIIT.
Acute proteomic responses to CPET after 8 weeks of HIIT intervention
After 8 weeks of regular HIIT, fewer changes were observed in the urine proteome of pregnant women following the CPET test. Specifically, only 15 proteins showed changes 15 min after CPET, and 30 proteins were altered 60 min post-test. Of these proteins, only one, DDAH1, appeared consistently across more than one participant sample, and it was found exclusively in samples taken 15 min after exercise. DDHA1 is responsible for ADMA degradation, thus controlling its concentration. ADMA is an inhibitor of NO synthase, which produces NO in endothelial cells
73
. Nitric oxide (NO) acts as a vasodilator, helping to regulate blood pressure
74
. Due to the limited number of altered proteins, only a partial functional analysis was possible, revealing changes only in cellular components. In the samples taken 15 min after CPET, most proteins were located in the cellular space and exosomes. In contrast, in the samples taken 60 min after CPET, most proteins were found in Vesicles.
Additionally, one molecular function was annotated in a pool of proteins from samples taken 60 min after CPET – structural molecule activity. One of these proteins is LTBP2, which is highly expressed in tissues such as muscle and arteries
75
,
76
. In these tissues, it plays a role in regulating elastic fibre assembly and interacts with collagen or fibrinogen in the extracellular matrix of connective tissue
77
. HMGN2 is responsible for the chromatin conformation, which it releases by binding to it, thus potentially enhancing transcription
78
,
79
. Despite the small number of altered proteins, some were also found in the pool of proteins from samples taken 8 weeks earlier, at the beginning of the research. Moreover, for a few proteins, we observed alterations that were opposite to those before the HIIT intervention. For example, as already mentioned, UMOD was upregulated before the HIIT program, and after 8 weeks of regular exercise, it was downregulated. In samples taken 15 min after CPET, opposite expression changes were observed for EGF, MIF, PRB1, and LEAP2, comparing pre- and post-intervention outcomes. EGF changed from being upregulated to downregulated after an 8-week period, which can be attributed to the higher weight of women further in the pregnancy. This change was observed only in women with the most advanced gestation week. It was shown that post-exercise EGF downregulation was negatively correlated with BMI
80
, which was attributed to the finalisation of ROS-induced defence in which EGF participates in the signalling pathway, as it has been proven that physical activity induces ROS production
81
.
Another protein, MIF, was upregulated after 8 weeks of regular HIIT. It can be released by many cells and has a variety of roles, such as inflammation response and metabolic regulation
82
. The downregulation of its expression during the exercise regimen was observed, contributing to increased uptake in the vascular wall and leading to an inflammatory reaction
83
. On the other hand, the upregulation of MIF was observed in individuals with type 2 diabetes as well as obese individuals after 8 weeks of HIIT
84
. Another protein whose levels are believed to be regulated by adiposity, among other factors, is LEAP2. Based on the animal data, some authors have linked a decrease in fat mass to a decrease in LEAP2
85
. In the samples taken 60 min after CPET, the proteins with opposite expression patterns to those observed at the experiment’s beginning were LTBP2, HMGN2, and UMOD. However, it is essential to note that only DDAH1 was detected in multiple samples from different participants, and this detection was limited to a single time point. Some physiological adaptation mechanisms to various HIIT interventions have been observed by other authors
22
, but not in pregnant populations previously.
Interpretation of data in the light of the prevention of pregnancy complications
In the analysed urine proteome, we observed auspicious results regarding potential biomarkers of several pregnancy complications, including GH and its often associated PE. In particular, prevention of PE is important, given that this pregnancy-specific disorder affects up to 8% of all pregnancies and remains a significant cause of maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality in all countries
86
. Above, we presented the results that at the beginning of the experiment, UMOD was substantially upregulated following CPET, and after the 8-week HIIT program, it was substantially downregulated. The obtained results correspond interestingly with the outcomes of other authors who observed a trend towards increased urinary UMOD excretion during pregnancy in rats with hypertension
87
. Presented urine proteome analysis confirms our previous results regarding blood pressure after the same intervention in pregnant women. After the 8-week HIIT program, we observed a statistically significant decrease in diastolic blood pressure, while systolic blood pressure remained unchanged, both within normal values
18
. Potentially, changes in UMOD levels may reflect the mechanisms that protect pregnant participants against hypertension.
Similar changes were seen in ALB secretion. Before the 8-week HIIT program, the level of this protein increased significantly 15 min after CPET, but a decreasing trend was visible after 60 min. After 8 weeks of HIIT, changes in ALB levels were not substantial, which again can be interpreted as an adaptation to intense exercise. These results should be related to the work of Bujold et al.
88
, who observed that ALB was upregulated in maternal urine before the diagnosis of preeclampsia. Another important observation was the downregulation of GDF-15 after 8 weeks of training, which was not observed at the beginning of the study. Cruickshank et al.
89
concluded that circulating GDF-15 levels were elevated in women more likely to develop PE or those already diagnosed with the condition. Therefore, the aforementioned changes in the levels of these proteins (UMOD, ALB, and GDF-15) appear beneficial and may partially explain how regular exercise reduces the risk of GH and PE.
Our analysis also provided interesting evidence for the prevention of GDM. In addition to the changes in TFF2 discussed above, we observed a significant decrease in LGR1. This protein was studied by Yao et al.
90
in exosomes in umbilical cord blood from both normal and GDM pregnancies. LGR1 was upregulated in GDM pregnancies and performed a largely pro-angiogenic function, which probably resulted in hypervascularisation in the GDM placenta. Inhibiting or stimulating the secretion of selected proteins through a few-week HIIT program seems to be crucial for maintaining carbohydrate homeostasis and proper placenta and fetal development.
A meta-analysis by Davenport et al.
6
revealed that exercise interventions may reduce the odds of developing GH by 39%, PE by 41%, and GDM by 38%. Nevertheless, most of the interventions included in their analysis were based on moderate-to-vigorous intensity exercise. In our pilot study, we explored whether higher-intensity exercise could provide an additional physiological stimulus. Although none of the participating women developed GH, PE, or GDM during pregnancy, the limited sample size does not allow us to draw conclusions regarding risk reduction. These preliminary observations highlight the feasibility and safety of our intervention and underscore the need for larger, adequately powered studies to confirm potential clinical benefits.
Strengths and limitations
The major strength of our study is the use of a structured, supervised HIIT programme, which is a promising mode of exercise in terms of potential health benefits for maternal and neonatal outcomes. Additionally, our program incorporated a variety of exercises targeting different muscle groups, which may enhance the adaptive effects of the intervention
20
. The main limitation is the small group of participants, which varied in their trimesters of study. Consequently, it is vital to conduct follow-up research with a larger group of participants at similar weeks of gestation and to collect data from early pregnancy through to birth. Additionally, we suggest that plasma analysis be conducted before and after every 8 weeks of HIIT cycles, enabling a more comprehensive analysis of changes visible at the molecular level and providing more information on the protective mechanisms of exercise regarding various pregnancy complications. Given the observed changes in proteins associated with the proper functioning of the cardiovascular system and maintaining carbohydrate and lipid homeostasis, it would be worthwhile in the future to conduct a similar intervention and proteome analysis in participants with complicated pregnancies. Research on the effects of exercise at various intensities, including high-intensity exercise, in individuals with GDM, GH, PE, and fetal growth restriction seems particularly valuable. To date, individuals with these pregnancy disorders have been advised to limit exercise, without evidence-based recommendations. A detailed analysis of proteomic responses in these populations could provide a new source of knowledge and thus support the treatment of many pregnancy complications, leading to better maternal health and neonatal outcomes.
In conclusion, CPET, performed up to volitional exhaustion during pregnancy, led to an innate immune response, mainly by decreasing Neutrophil degranulation and apoptosis-induced DNA fragmentation. Eight weeks of regular HIIT resulted in a significant decrease in the number of proteins altered by CPET, which might indicate proteomic adaptations of pregnant women to high-intensity exercise. Positive changes were observed in some proteins that are potential biomarkers of pregnancy complications, including GH, PE and GDM. Further research is needed to explain how these protein expressions regulate physiological and metabolic responses to intensive physical effort during pregnancy and how a few-week HIIT intervention may enhance these processes.
